**The directional story is right; the specific numbers are unsourced and internally questionable.**

**1. NVIDIA's dominance — broadly true, but check the math.**

NVIDIA's actual net income is verifiable. For FY2026 (ended Jan 2026), it posted $120 billion in net income on $215.9 billion revenue, and the company guides toward roughly $500B revenue in calendar 2026. If the "global AI net profit pool" is ~$637B and NVIDIA takes 32.5%, that implies NVIDIA's AI profit ≈ $207B. That's plausible only if you annualize the most recent run-rate — Q1 FY2027 alone was $58.3bn profit, up more than 200% year-on-year, which annualizes to ~$200B+. So the 32.5% figure isn't crazy, but it depends entirely on how you define the "pool" (whose profits count, hyperscalers' AI segments, etc.), and that definition is never stated.

**2. The 66% of US AI profit claim — internally inconsistent.**

This is where it breaks. If NVIDIA = 32.5% of the *global* pool and simultaneously = 66% of *US* AI profit, then total US AI profit ≈ NVIDIA / 0.66 ≈ 49% of the global pool. That would mean the US captures roughly half the global pool and the rest of the US AI economy (Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon, OpenAI, Broadcom, AMD) splits only ~16% of the global pool between them. That's implausible — those hyperscalers' AI-attributable profits are enormous. Google alone posted $132 billion profit in 2025, the largest annual profit ever recorded by a U.S. company. The 66% figure only works if you exclude hyperscaler AI profits from the "AI pool" entirely — a definitional sleight of hand. The rhetoric ("赢家通吞") oversells it.

**3. South Korea 35% via HBM — exaggerated.**

The HBM *market-share* number is real: South Korea's SK Hynix and Samsung control approximately 88% of the HBM market. But translating that into "35% of the global AI profit pool" conflates HBM dominance with profit capture. HBM is a fraction of total AI value, and DRAM margins, while currently elevated, are nowhere near NVIDIA's ~75% gross margins. A 35% profit share for Korea is far higher than any sober estimate would support.

**4. Taiwan/TSMC 7.4% — plausible as a floor.**

TSMC is the manufacturing chokepoint — Taiwan produces more than 60% of the world's semiconductors and nearly 90% of the advanced chips required for AI. But TSMC operates on foundry margins (~50-60% gross, lower net), capturing a small slice of the value it enables. 7.4% is directionally reasonable, arguably even low given how indispensable it is.

**5. Mainland China 4% / $26B — plausible directionally, hard to verify.**

Export controls have significantly impacted sales in the world's second largest economy, and China is locked out of the highest-margin layer (cutting-edge GPUs). A small *profit* share is consistent with that, even though China leads in deployment and patents (China holds an 82.4% global share of AI patents). But the specific $26B figure has no traceable source.

**Bottom line:** The *narrative* — value concentrating brutally in the compute layer (NVIDIA + HBM + foundry), with the US capturing the lion's share and China squeezed out at the high-margin top — is real and well-supported. The *specific percentages* ($637B pool, 32.5%, 66%, 35%, 7.4%, 4%) are not from any report I can locate, are internally inconsistent (the 32.5%-global vs 66%-US pair doesn't reconcile cleanly), and bear the hallmarks of a punchy social-media graphic engineered for the "赢家通吞" line rather than rigorous accounting. Treat the vibe as correct and the digits as unverified.
