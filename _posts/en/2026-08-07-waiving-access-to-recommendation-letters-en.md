**Question: What does the Buckley Amendment Waiver section mean in a US graduate application recommendation form?**

**Answer:**

This is about your **right to see recommendation letters** after you enroll at a US university.

The **Buckley Amendment** refers to the **Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA)**, a US law that gives students the right to access their education records, including recommendation letters in some cases.

You have two choices:

### 1. "I waive my right to access this report." ✅ (recommended)

Meaning:

> "I give up my legal right to read this recommendation letter in the future."

Why most applicants choose this:

* It makes the recommendation appear **more confidential and trustworthy**.
* Universities generally prefer recommenders to write honestly without worrying that the student may read it later.
* Many professors and employers expect applicants to waive access.

Example:

* Your professor writes a recommendation.
* You submit it.
* You enroll at the university.
* You cannot later request a copy of that recommendation letter.

### 2. "I do not waive my right to access this report."

Meaning:

> "I keep my legal right to request and read this recommendation letter later."

Possible downside:

* Some recommenders may feel less comfortable writing strongly.
* Some universities may consider waived letters more credible.

### "In place of my signature, please type your full legal name"

This is simply an electronic signature.

Example:

Your legal name:

```
Zhiwei Li
```

You type:

```
Zhiwei Li
```

It confirms that you made this waiver choice.

For US master's applications (CS/AI programs like Illinois Tech), the normal choice is:

```
☑ I waive my right to access this report.

Signature:
Zhiwei Li
```

Then your recommender submits the letter privately. This is the standard practice for US graduate admissions.
