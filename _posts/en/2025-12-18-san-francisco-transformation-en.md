Question: How has San Francisco been in the latest 100 years? Give some key milestones.

Answer: 

San Francisco has undergone remarkable transformation over the past 100 years (roughly 1925–2025), evolving from a recovering post-earthquake city into a global hub for culture, innovation, and technology. It has faced challenges like economic depressions, earthquakes, social upheavals, pandemics, and housing crises, but consistently demonstrated resilience through rebuilding, cultural movements, and economic booms. The city solidified its iconic status with landmark infrastructure, became a center for counterculture and civil rights, and emerged as a tech powerhouse, though recent years have seen struggles with downtown recovery and social issues.

Here are some key milestones, organized chronologically:

### 1920s–1930s: Growth and Infrastructure Boom
- **1920s**: The city experienced rapid modernization with new skyscrapers (e.g., the Pacific Telephone Building in 1925 and Russ Building), affirming its role as the financial capital of the West.
- **1934–1937**: Construction and opening of the Golden Gate Bridge (1937) and Bay Bridge (1936), iconic symbols that connected the city and boosted growth.
- **1930s**: Amid the Great Depression, the city embraced federal works programs and saw the completion of Coit Tower (1933).

### 1940s–1950s: Post-War and Cultural Shifts
- **1945**: Hosting of the United Nations Conference, where the UN Charter was drafted.
- **1950s**: Emergence of the Beat Generation, centered in North Beach with City Lights Bookstore publishing key works, marking the San Francisco Renaissance in literature and arts.
- Post-WWII: Suburbanization led to population decline, but the city remained a military and cultural hub.

### 1960s–1970s: Counterculture and Social Movements
- **1967**: The Summer of Love in Haight-Ashbury, drawing thousands of hippies and cementing San Francisco as the epicenter of the counterculture movement.
- **Late 1960s–1970s**: Rise of LGBTQ+ activism; the city became a major hub for gay rights, with early Pride events and growing community visibility.
- **1978**: Assassinations of Mayor George Moscone and Supervisor Harvey Milk, a tragic milestone in LGBTQ+ history.

### 1980s–1990s: Earthquakes and Urban Renewal
- **1989**: Loma Prieta earthquake caused significant damage, leading to the demolition of the Embarcadero Freeway and revitalization of the waterfront.
- **1990s**: Dot-com boom began, attracting tech entrepreneurs and sparking gentrification in areas like South of Market (SoMa).

### 2000s–2010s: Tech Explosion and Social Progress
- **2000**: Population surpassed 1950 peaks, driven by the internet boom.
- **2004**: "Winter of Love" – thousands of same-sex marriages issued, a landmark in marriage equality (later voided but influential).
- **2000s–2010s**: Mission Bay redevelopment, rise of social media companies, and transformation into a tech giant hub (near Silicon Valley influence).
- **2010s**: Continued tech growth, but rising housing costs and inequality.

### 2020s: Pandemic and Recovery Efforts
- **2020–2023**: Severe impact from COVID-19, with remote work emptying downtown offices and exacerbating homelessness and drug issues.
- **2025 (as of December)**: Ongoing "comeback" efforts under municipal leadership, focusing on downtown revitalization, tourism recovery, and addressing retail vacancies and social challenges.

San Francisco's history in this period reflects themes of innovation, diversity, and adaptation, making it one of the world's most dynamic cities.
