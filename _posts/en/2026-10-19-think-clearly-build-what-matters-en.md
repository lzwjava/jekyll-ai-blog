## 1. The full company philosophy

# Truth Software

**Think clearly. Build what matters. Play the long game.**

## Why We Exist

We exist to pursue truth in computer science, software engineering, product design, and business.

We believe the best way to understand something is to study it deeply, build it ourselves, use it in reality, and learn from the results.

We value intellectual independence over conventional wisdom, understanding over imitation, and simplicity over unnecessary complexity.

We build because we care about the work itself and the problems worth solving—not merely because something is fashionable or profitable today.

## Build for Ourselves. Build for Real Users

We believe great products often begin with people solving problems they personally experience.

We build tools we want to use ourselves, then bring them to real users, listen carefully, and improve them through actual use.

We do not confuse writing code with creating value. Software matters when it helps people do something useful, meaningful, or previously impossible.

We follow the spirit of the best startup philosophy: make something people want, release it into the world, learn from reality, and keep improving.

## Respect What Already Exists

We do not build software simply because we can.

When a good open-source solution already solves the problem, we would rather use it, contribute to it, or help our customers adopt it than charge them to reinvent it.

Our purpose is not to maximize the amount of code we write. It is to solve problems well.

We build something new when there is a genuine gap, a meaningful improvement, or a reason to create a better solution for ourselves and our users.

## Think in Years, Not Weeks

We are prepared for the long-term work of learning, building, debugging, serving users, and improving products.

We do not expect every worthwhile project to succeed immediately. We understand that trust, technical understanding, useful products, and strong relationships take time to develop.

We seek collaborators who share this attitude: people who care about the problem, respect the craft, keep their commitments, and are willing to build something valuable together over many years.

We value sustainable progress over the appearance of rapid success.

## Work With Empathy

Our customers are our collaborators, not merely sources of revenue.

We want to understand their problems, respect their constraints, explain our decisions honestly, and make choices that serve their long-term interests.

We welcome customers who care about what they are building, appreciate good engineering, communicate openly, and treat collaboration as a relationship rather than a transaction.

We do not expect every customer to share our interests or philosophy. But we work best with people who care about the product itself and want to create something genuinely useful.

## AI Should Expand What People Can Do

Artificial intelligence changes the economics of software creation.

We use models, agents, automation, and open-source tools to increase the capability of individuals and small teams.

We want to spend less time on repetitive work and more time understanding problems, designing systems, exploring ideas, and creating useful products.

We do not use AI merely to produce more code. We use it to make better things possible.

## Transparency Is Part of the Product

We believe customers should understand what they are paying for.

We aim for clear pricing, predictable costs, honest limitations, and straightforward commercial relationships.

We do not want customers to depend on us because our systems are mysterious or our bills are difficult to understand. We want them to work with us because we provide real value and earn their trust.

## A Company Worth Building

We want to build a company that we ourselves would be proud to work for and whose products we would be proud to use.

A company where people can pursue difficult technical questions, create things they care about, serve real users, and collaborate with people they respect.

We do not need to follow every trend. We do not need to pretend to know everything. We need to keep learning, keep building, and remain honest about what we discover.

**Truth in thought. Simplicity in design. Empathy in collaboration. Excellence in execution.**

**Build what matters. Build it well. Build it for the long term.**

This combines YC's emphasis on real customers and learning through execution with your own emphasis on truth, open source, technical depth, and long-term collaboration. YC's published advice explicitly emphasizes building something people want and talking to users while writing code.&#x20;

## 2. Your pricing philosophy

Your proposed pricing model is interesting because it makes the commercial relationship part of the company's philosophy: transparent, measurable, and understandable.

The preorder is a separate proposed commercial term. Whether it is a deposit, prepaid usage credit, or a commitment fee must be explicitly defined.

Before publishing this as a firm offer, settle three things:

1. What does the preorder buy? If the $500 is prepaid usage credit, say so and explain when it expires, if ever. If it funds product development, explain what the customer receives in return.
2. What counts as a token? Specify whether the rate applies to input and output tokens combined, and how cached tokens, image/audio tokens, and different models are counted.
3. Can the rate be sustained? Model provider costs, infrastructure, retries, and support before promising one fixed rate across all models. You can offer a transparent base rate while explicitly identifying any exceptions.

The principle should be simple: no hidden fees, no confusing pricing tiers, and no surprise bills.

## 3. Who you want to work with

I would express your ideal customer profile positively rather than saying you refuse to work with people who want quick success.

You want collaborators who:

- Care about the problem and the product, not only the financial return.
- Have genuine empathy for the people who will use the software.
- Respect existing open-source work and do not reinvent things without a reason.
- Are willing to invest in quality, learning, and improvement.
- Communicate honestly and respect the contributions of others.
- Want a relationship that can continue as the product and business evolve.

A business can pursue profit and still care deeply about its craft. In fact, sustainable revenue gives the team more time to keep improving a product. The distinction is not profit versus passion; it is short-term extraction versus long-term value creation.

## 4. The operating rule that ties everything together

I would make this the company's internal decision rule:

> Would we build this for ourselves? Does it solve a real problem for users? Is there already a good solution? Can we deliver it honestly and maintain it over the long term?

If the answer is no, investigate further before committing.

That doesn't mean every project must be personally exciting or open-ended. Consultancy work can be commercially motivated and still meet your standards, provided it solves a genuine problem and creates value for the customer.
