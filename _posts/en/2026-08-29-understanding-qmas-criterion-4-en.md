Question: What does QMAS Criterion (4) mean?

Answer: It means **you need to prove proficiency in two languages, with both written and spoken ability**.

For you, the obvious combination is **Chinese + English**.

### 1. Chinese

You generally need a recognized Chinese-language test **unless you qualify for one of the exemptions**.

For example:

* **PSC (Putonghua Shuiping Ceshi)** — pass
* **HSK** — Level 3 or above

However, there is an important exemption:

> If Chinese is your first language **and** you are a citizen/national of a place where Chinese is an official language, you don't need to provide a Chinese test.

So if you are a **Chinese citizen and Chinese is your first language**, this exemption should normally cover Chinese.

### 2. English

For English, the listed examples include:

* **IELTS ≥ 7.0 overall**
* **TOEFL iBT ≥ 94**
* **CET-6 ≥ 520**

The key point for you: **IELTS 6.5 does not meet the explicitly listed IELTS 7.0 threshold.**

So you cannot simply upload your IELTS 6.5 and say it satisfies this criterion.

But the page gives **four exemptions**, including:

> “you could provide a written explanation supported with evidence.”

That means there may be a route to satisfy the English requirement **without IELTS 7.0**, if you can convincingly document your English ability through other evidence.

For example, depending on what evidence you have, you might explain:

* your English-medium education/training;
* professional work conducted substantially in English;
* publications/blogs/technical work in English;
* other recognized English qualifications;
* evidence demonstrating both **written and spoken** English proficiency.

The wording **“could meet the language requirement for entry to an undergraduate programme with that language as the medium of instruction”** is also an exemption. This is potentially relevant if your academic background satisfies that condition.

### For your situation

I'd interpret your current position as:

| Language          | Your situation                      | Likely status              |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Chinese           | Native/first language + PRC citizen | **Exempt from test**       |
| English           | IELTS 6.5                           | **Below listed IELTS 7.0** |
| English exemption | Need supporting evidence            | **Potentially usable**     |

So **Criterion (4) is not necessarily saying you must have IELTS 7.0**. It says you must demonstrate proficiency in two languages, **but there are exemptions from submitting a test certificate**.

If you're preparing your QMAS application now, I would **not automatically retake IELTS yet**. First, we should examine which of the 4 exemptions you can use and build the strongest evidence package around your existing IELTS 6.5.
