Question: Should I apply for Hong Kong QMAS only for myself, or include my wife and two daughters?

Answer:

For your situation, I would **apply as the principal applicant and include your wife + two daughters as accompanying dependants**, assuming your goal is eventually moving your family to Hong Kong. ([Immigration Department][1])

QMAS structure:

```
You (main applicant)
        |
        +-- Wife (dependent)
        |
        +-- Daughter 1 (dependent)
        |
        +-- Daughter 2 (dependent)
```

QMAS allows a successful applicant to bring:

* spouse
* unmarried dependent children under 18

as dependants, if you can financially support and accommodate them. ([Immigration Department][1])

The online application also supports group submission. You do not need four separate independent applications; you can submit your dependants together with your own QMAS application. ([Government of Hong Kong][2])

### Why include them now?

**Pros:**

1. **Family immigration becomes easier**

   If you first get QMAS alone and later bring your family, you need another dependant application process. Applying together avoids that extra step. ([Government of Hong Kong][3])

2. **Your daughters can access Hong Kong life earlier**

   If your long-term plan is:

   * Hong Kong company
   * AI/software consulting
   * better international environment
   * possible permanent residence after years

   bringing your family together makes sense.

3. **Your wife can also live in Hong Kong**

   Dependants of QMAS entrants normally have stay linked to the sponsor's status. ([Immigration Department][1])

---

However, there is one strategic consideration:

### If your QMAS profile is borderline, consider applying alone first

Your QMAS strength:

* 11 years software engineering
* AI/LLM projects
* startup experience
* international clients possibility
* English ability

Potential weakness:

* no bachelor's degree (QMAS normally expects good academic background, normally a first degree, although exceptional technical ability can sometimes be considered). ([Immigration Department][4])

If you worry that:

* your case is weak,
* you want to minimize cost,
* you want to test Hong Kong first,

then applying alone is also reasonable.

But if your application is strong enough, I would include your family.

My suggested strategy for you:

```
2026:
Apply QMAS
    |
    +-- Include wife + 2 daughters
    |
2027:
Move to HK gradually
    |
    +-- Build AI consulting business
    +-- Find HK enterprise clients
    +-- Kids adapt to HK education
```

Given your previous idea (Hong Kong company + software consultancy), **family-inclusive QMAS matches your long-term direction better than a solo application.** ([Immigration Department][1])
