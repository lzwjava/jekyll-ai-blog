**Question:** Which competition is harder to win a gold medal in—IPhO (International Physics Olympiad) or IOI (International Olympiad in Informatics)?

**Answer:**

Both competitions are extremely prestigious, but they differ in difficulty and selectivity. Here's a comprehensive comparison:

## Gold Medal Selectivity

IPhO awards gold medals to the top 8% of contestants, while IOI awards gold medals to approximately 1/12th (about 8.3%) of participants. While the percentages appear similar, the actual difficulty differs significantly.

## Overall Difficulty Assessment

Based on expert and participant perspectives, IMO (International Mathematical Olympiad) is generally considered a much bigger achievement than IOI, with a gold medal at IMO being significantly more prestigious than a gold medal at IOI. IPhO typically falls between IMO and IOI in terms of prestige.

The key difference lies in the nature of the problems:

- **IPhO problems** require deep conceptual understanding of physics and creative problem-solving approaches
- **IOI problems** require strong algorithmic knowledge and flawless coding implementation

## Scoring Structure

IPhO classifies contestants by normalizing the mean points of the three best scores to 100%, with contestants receiving more than 90% earning gold medals. IOI in recent years has required gold medal thresholds of approximately 438-360 points out of a maximum 600 points, depending on the year.

## Expert Opinions

Based on competitive programming data, IOI problems at the level solved by ~45 people (top performers) correspond to approximately Codeforces difficulty 2600, requiring extraordinary algorithmic expertise and implementation speed. Meanwhile, 99% of IPhO problems are solved using a relatively limited set of physics ideas, making the challenge more about understanding fundamental concepts rather than novelty.

## Conclusion

**IPhO appears slightly easier to win gold in** compared to IOI, primarily because:

1. IPhO relies more on physics intuition and conceptual understanding
2. IOI demands both algorithmic insight AND perfect implementation with no coding errors
3. IOI participants must solve problems within strict time and memory constraints while coding

However, both are exceptionally difficult competitions with only the world's most talented high school students achieving gold medals. The relative difficulty depends on individual strengths—someone strong in physics concepts may find IPhO easier, while someone with deep algorithmic knowledge may find IOI more attainable.
