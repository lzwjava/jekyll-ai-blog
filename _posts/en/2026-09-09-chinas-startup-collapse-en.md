**Question:** What startups have died in China in recent years?

**Answer:**

China's startup scene has experienced a dramatic collapse in recent years, spanning multiple sectors. Here's an overview:

---

**The Scale of the Collapse**

China's once-booming venture capital industry experienced a severe downturn, with the number of new startups plummeting from 51,302 in 2018 to just 1,202 in 2023, according to data provider IT Juzi. A Beijing-based executive described the situation bluntly: "The whole industry has just died before our eyes. The entrepreneurial spirit is dead."

---

**EV Startups — The Biggest Graveyard**

The EV sector saw the most spectacular failures, driven by brutal price wars and the phasing out of government subsidies:

- **WM Motor (Weltmeister)** — WM Motor filed for bankruptcy in October 2023. It was the largest Chinese electric carmaker to become insolvent, having sold approximately 100,000 vehicles between 2019 and 2022. An estimated 160,000 Chinese car owners were left in the lurch.

- **HiPhi (Human Horizons)** — Production halted in 2024 due to funding and operational issues. The brand had only sold 4,520 vehicles in 2022.

- **Byton** — Byton, backed by Nanjing City government and state-owned carmaker FAW Group, filed for bankruptcy after failing to bring its first model, the M-Byte SUV, to production. It burned 8.4 billion yuan without producing a single car.

- **Singulato Motors & Aiways** — Both Singulato and Aiways left the market amid the wider EV shakeout.

- **LeEco EV** — Collapsed under massive debt linked to its parent tech conglomerate.

Overall, around 2018, China had more than 500 EV startups. By 2025, only around 100 remain active — meaning roughly 400 ceased operations.

---

**Tech & AI Unicorns**

- **Mininglamp (AI)** — Mininglamp, an AI unicorn invested by Tencent and Sequoia China, laid off thousands of people — about 70% of total employees.

- **Cloopen (SaaS/Communications)** — Once dubbed "the first Chinese SaaS company going to the US" with a market value of up to 50 billion yuan, it was ordered to suspend trading by the New York Stock Exchange, triggering a class action lawsuit by investors. Overnight, its market value plummeted to $55.

- **OPPO's ZEKU (Chip Design)** — OPPO, a mobile phone unicorn valued at 165 billion yuan, suddenly closed down its chip business subsidiary ZEKU in late May 2023.

---

**Why Did They Fail?**

Many Chinese investment strategists have been laid off and replaced with lawyers specialized in enforcing payment terms. Many founders who cannot repurchase shares have fled the country, leading VC firms to enact even more restrictive terms. The key causes include: government regulatory crackdowns, an exodus of foreign capital, phasing out of EV subsidies, fierce price wars led by BYD and Tesla, and a broader freeze in VC funding.
