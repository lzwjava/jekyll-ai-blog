**Answer:**

This is a live interview from the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, in January 2026. The Wall Street Journal's Editor-in-Chief Emma Tucker interviews Dario Amodei, CEO and co-founder of Anthropic.

### Main Themes and Key Points

- **Shift in AI Perception**
  Last year (2025), the public and media focused heavily on AI's exciting potential and opportunities. This year (2026), attention has shifted toward its societal impacts—what AI is doing *to* the world rather than just what it *can* do. Dario notes that public excitement and fear have oscillated wildly over the years, but the actual progress in AI capabilities has followed a remarkably steady exponential trajectory (he compares it to a "Moore's law for intelligence").

- **Balanced View on AI Impacts**
  Dario emphasizes a consistent, balanced stance (shared by Anthropic): AI has extreme positive *and* negative potential.
  - **Upside** — He still fully believes in the radical benefits outlined in his earlier essay "Machines of Loving Grace" (written ~mid-2024), such as curing cancer, eradicating tropical diseases, accelerating scientific discovery, and driving economic development in underserved regions.
  - **Downside** — Serious risks also exist, including misuse and major societal disruption. He is increasingly focused on warning about these.

- **Economic Disruption as a Central Concern**
  The signature economic effect of advanced AI will likely be **very high GDP growth combined with very high unemployment and inequality**—a combination history has almost never seen before. Past technologies that drove growth also created more jobs overall. AI may break this pattern.

  - Concrete example: AI coding capabilities
    Recent models (e.g., Claude Opus 4.5 mentioned) allow some engineering leads at Anthropic to stop writing code entirely—they only edit AI-generated code.
    Tools like "Claude Code" and the new "Claude Co-work" (built almost entirely by Claude in ~1.5 weeks) show software becoming dramatically cheaper or essentially free to produce. Small, custom apps could cost pennies instead of requiring large user bases to amortize development.
    However, this also means entire job categories and careers built over decades could disappear or shrink dramatically. Even if engineers shift to higher-level tasks, the displacement will accelerate as models improve further.

- **Societal Preparation and Awareness**
  Dario believes businesses, policymakers, and governments are **not doing enough** to prepare for these changes. Society can adapt, but there is currently very little awareness of the scale and speed of what's coming.

- **Anthropic's Efforts to Track Impact**
  Anthropic publishes the "Anthropic Economic Index" (updated several times over ~1 year). It anonymously and statistically analyzes real Claude usage across conversations to track:
  - Which tasks and industries the model is used for
  - Whether it is automating vs. augmenting human work
  - Geographic diffusion (U.S. states and countries)
  This serves as an early real-time signal of how AI is affecting the economy.

The interview highlights Dario's view that AI progress remains steady and powerful despite swings in public sentiment. He expresses both excitement about unprecedented productivity gains and serious concern about economic and social disruption that current institutions and thinking are unprepared to handle.
