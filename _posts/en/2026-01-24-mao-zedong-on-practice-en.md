Question: Can you provide an introduction to Mao Zedong's philosophical work, *On Practice* (《实践论》)?

Answer:

### Overview

Written in **1937**, *On Practice* is one of Mao Zedong's most significant philosophical essays. It was originally delivered as a series of lectures at the Anti-Japanese Military and Political College in Yan'an. The work serves as a foundational text for **Maoism**, aiming to reconcile Marxist-Leninist theory with the specific realities of the Chinese Revolution by emphasizing the importance of lived experience and practical application.

---

### Core Philosophical Arguments

The essay focuses on the **dialectical relationship** between knowledge and practice. Mao argues that knowledge is not innate but is derived entirely from social practice.

* **The Primacy of Practice:** Practice is the source, the driving force, and the ultimate criterion of truth. One cannot truly understand a thing without attempting to change it.
* **The Two Stages of Knowledge:**
1. **Perceptual Stage:** The initial phase where we gather raw data through our senses (seeing, hearing, feeling).
2. **Conceptual Stage:** Through reflection and analysis, perceptual data is transformed into concepts, theories, and laws.


* **The Cycle of Learning:** Knowledge does not end with theory. It must be "returned to practice" to see if the theory actually works. This creates an infinite spiral: **Practice  Knowledge  Again Practice  Again Knowledge.**

---

### Why It Was Written

At the time, the Communist Party of China was split between two internal factions:

1. **The "Dogmatists":** Those who followed Soviet instructions or Marxist texts blindly without considering China's unique rural landscape.
2. **The "Empiricists":** Those who relied solely on their limited personal experience without understanding broader revolutionary theory.

Mao wrote *On Practice* to criticize both, arguing that **theory is useless if it doesn't solve real-world problems**, and **action is blind without a theoretical guide.**

---

### Key Takeaways

* **Truth is Objective:** Whether a thought is "true" is determined by whether it achieves the predicted results in social practice.
* **Adaptability:** As the world changes, our theories must evolve. There is no "final" or static truth.
* **Famous Quote:** *"If you want to know the taste of a pear, you must change the pear by eating it yourself."*
