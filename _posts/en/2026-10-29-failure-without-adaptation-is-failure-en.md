**Question:** Do unsuccessful people simply try, fail repeatedly, and eventually give up?

**Answer:**

Partially — but the real picture is more interesting.

The pattern you're describing is actually two different failure modes:

**1. They stop trying (quit)**
Yes, many people abandon their efforts after repeated failure. Discouragement, fear, and exhaustion are real. Giving up is the most obvious way to guarantee failure.

**2. They keep trying — the same way (grind without learning)**
This is subtler and arguably more common. They don't quit; they just repeat the same approach and expect different results. Effort without reflection doesn't compound — it just accumulates frustration.

The distinction that separates outcomes isn't really *trying vs. not trying* — it's **trying + adapting**. Successful people fail too, often more dramatically. What differs is what happens *between* attempts:

- They extract a lesson from each failure
- They adjust their approach, not just their effort
- They distinguish between "this isn't working" and "I'm not working"

So the more accurate version of your observation might be:

> Unsuccessful people try and fail, try the same way and fail again, until they either quit — or eventually try *differently* and succeed.

The tragedy isn't failure. It's failing to notice *why* you failed.
