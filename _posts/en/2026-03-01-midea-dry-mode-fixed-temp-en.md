**Question: Why can't the temperature settings be adjusted for high or low in the extract humidity mode (dehumidify/dry mode) on a Midea air conditioner?**

**Answer:**

In most **Midea air conditioners**, the "extract humidity" mode (commonly called **Dry mode** or **Dehumidify mode**) is designed primarily to remove moisture from the air rather than to provide strong, adjustable cooling like in Cool mode.

Here is why the temperature cannot be adjusted (or is fixed/not changeable) in this mode on many Midea models:

- Dry mode prioritizes **dehumidification** over temperature control. The unit runs the compressor at a low speed or in cycles optimized for condensing moisture on the evaporator coils, with only incidental (mild) cooling as a side effect.
- The system automatically manages the operation based on room conditions (like humidity and sometimes temperature), so user-set temperature targets are not used or needed. Allowing manual temperature adjustment would interfere with the dehumidification-focused logic.
- In many Midea user manuals (for window, portable, and split-system units), it is explicitly stated that in Dry/Dehumidify mode, **the temperature cannot be adjusted**, and often the fan speed is also fixed or automatic.

This is a standard design for Dry mode across many air conditioner brands (not just Midea), as the goal is efficient humidity removal with lower energy use and minimal over-cooling, especially useful in humid but not extremely hot conditions.

Some older or specific Midea models might allow limited temperature setting in Dry mode, but this is uncommon. If your model behaves differently, check the user manual for your exact model number for confirmation.
