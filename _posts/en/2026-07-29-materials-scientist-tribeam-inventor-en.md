Question: Who is Tresa M. Pollock?

Answer: Materials scientist, not CS/ML — flagging that since it's outside your usual stack.

She holds a B.Sc. from Purdue (1984) and a Ph.D. from MIT (1989), working under advisor Ali Argon. She's the Alcoa Professor and chair of the Materials Department at UC Santa Barbara, after stints at GE Aircraft Engines (1989–91), Carnegie Mellon (1991–99), and University of Michigan (2000–10). Her research focuses on structural materials, coatings, and ultrafast lasers for microfabrication and materials diagnostics.

Core work: mechanical and environmental performance of materials in extreme environments — advanced design techniques and heat-resistant materials that improved jet aircraft efficiency and safety. She invented an in-situ tomography platform called "TriBeam," which captures a material's chemistry and structure in 3D at high resolution.

Recognition: elected to the National Academy of Engineering (2005), 2009 TMS Fellow, ASM International Fellow, and 2005 TMS President — the first woman to hold that office. Also a member of the German National Academy of Sciences Leopoldina.

The TriBeam angle is the interesting one from a systems/tooling perspective — it's basically an instrument pipeline (femtosecond laser serial sectioning + SEM/EDS/EBSD) for automated 3D microstructure reconstruction, which is closer to a data-acquisition/automation problem than pure metallurgy. If you're thinking about it from an "ICME" (Integrated Computational Materials Engineering) angle — she chaired the NRC study that pushed that field — that's essentially materials science trying to do what simulation + ML now does for alloy design: replace expensive physical trial-and-error with computational models.
