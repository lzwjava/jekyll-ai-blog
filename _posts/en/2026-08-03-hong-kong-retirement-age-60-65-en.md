Question: When do Hong Kong employees usually retire?

Answer:

Hong Kong is different from many countries because **there is no universal legal mandatory retirement age for private-sector employees**. Employers and employees generally agree on retirement arrangements in employment contracts. ([GovHK][1])

Typical situation:

| Group                           |                                                            Common retirement age |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------: |
| Private company employees       |                                    Around **60–65** (varies by company/industry) |
| Banking / professional workers  |                                                Often **60–65**, sometimes longer |
| Self-employed / business owners |                                                     Often continue beyond **65** |
| Hong Kong civil servants        | Usually **60–65** depending on joining date and role ([Civil Service Bureau][2]) |

For retirement savings:

* Hong Kong's **Mandatory Provident Fund (MPF)** generally allows withdrawal at **65**.
* Early withdrawal is possible in some cases, including **early retirement at 60** under MPF rules. ([Government of Hong Kong][3])

In practice, many middle-class Hong Kong workers aim for:

* **60:** financially independent if they have enough savings/property
* **65:** normal retirement point
* **65+:** continue working if they enjoy work or need income

Compared with mainland China, Hong Kong workers often retire later because there is no fixed statutory retirement age for private employees. ([GovHK][1])

For tech workers in Hong Kong (software engineers, AI engineers, finance IT), staying productive into the **50s and 60s** is quite common, especially in banks, consulting, and specialist roles.
