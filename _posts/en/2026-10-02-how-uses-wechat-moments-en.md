Question: How should I understand 豪车毒's marketing strategy, especially its heavy WeChat Moments posting, which some people would delete a contact over?

Answer:

The posting volume isn't the core of the strategy. Moments is where the whole system shows up, so it helps to see how the pieces fit together.

**1. Who they are.** 豪车毒 is an online luxury-car sourcing team run by 老纪 (Ji Wenhua) with no 4S store, no inventory and no showroom. Roughly 30 people reportedly sold about 1.5 billion RMB a year through WeChat. A business with no storefront has to build trust through its feed, so Moments works as the showroom.

**2. Customers become the sales force.** The founder's underlying idea is to treat customers as salespeople to cultivate, not just buyers. The tool for this is over-the-top service. For example, after a deep-clean they send customers before-and-after photos and videos, which makes it easy for them to post to Moments. By their own figures, 98 out of 100 customers post about it. Each of those posts brings in new, well-targeted leads.

**3. The "showing off" is deliberate.** In an interview, 老纪 said the wealth-flaunting on his Moments is a marketing method meant to stimulate his customers' spending. The feed sets a standard for the lifestyle and service level the buyer is joining. So a high-volume feed is closer to a managed content channel than to someone oversharing.

**4. Public platforms feed a private funnel.** Douyin traffic is moved to his personal WeChat through private messages, and sales then close through Moments marketing and referrals. The stated end goal is to get all followers onto personal WeChat accounts. One breakdown puts the referral rate above 90%, with referrals making up nearly half of sales.

**5. Event marketing builds the founder's profile.** One example was a Douyin creator's viral reaction after buying a Rolls-Royce from 老纪, which was followed by a "looking for 老纪" meme and a red-envelope campaign. This gave him a recognizable personal brand, so the Moments feed is read as a person's life, not a catalog.

**How to read it critically**

- The numbers (15亿 in sales, 98% posting, 90% referrals) are self-reported or come from marketing case studies, so treat them as claims.
- The sources are a few years old, so I can't say how the strategy has changed.
- The model suits a high-ticket, trust-driven product, where an audience of potential buyers tolerates or even likes the flaunting. For an ordinary account, posting 100 times a day mostly gets you deleted.
- Deleting someone over their posting is a normal reaction. Their approach works because the audience that stays is a self-selected set of prospects, and the people who delete were never going to buy.
