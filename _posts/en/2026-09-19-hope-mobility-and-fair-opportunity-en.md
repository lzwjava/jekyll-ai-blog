Question: Is a society good when it gives people hope and a realistic chance to succeed through hard work?

Answer: **Broadly, yes — but “work hard → succeed” is not enough by itself.**

A healthy society tends to have several properties:

* **Opportunity:** ordinary people can improve their situation through education, work, entrepreneurship, or other productive effort.
* **Mobility:** your parents' wealth or social position doesn't completely determine your future.
* **Reward for contribution:** productive skills, risk-taking, innovation, and hard work can translate into better living standards.
* **Fair rules:** people can trust contracts, property rights, courts, and institutions.
* **Hope:** people believe that their actions can meaningfully affect their future.
* **Second chances:** failure doesn't permanently destroy someone's ability to participate in society.

The important distinction is:

> **A good society doesn't promise that everyone who works hard will succeed. It gives people a credible path where effort, skill, and good decisions can substantially improve their odds.**

There will always be randomness: someone can work extremely hard and fail; someone else can become wealthy partly through luck or inheritance. What matters socially is whether **the system is sufficiently open that people can see a connection between what they do and what happens to their lives.**

Conversely, when people repeatedly experience:

> work hard → no improvement
> take risks → punished
> build something → blocked
> follow the rules → others bypass them
> fail once → permanently trapped

then **hope deteriorates**. Even if GDP or material wealth remains high, people can become cynical, disengaged, or unwilling to take productive risks.

This is also why entrepreneurship matters beyond individual wealth: a society where someone can start with little, build something useful, and substantially change their life demonstrates that **agency is real**.
