Question: What does "国家安全" (guójiā ānquán) mean in the context of Chinese politics?

Answer: **国家安全** (guójiā ānquán) literally translates to **"national security"**, but in the context of Chinese politics it carries a much broader and more expansive meaning than the Western conception of the term.

**Core meaning:** It encompasses the security of the state, the Communist Party's political power, territorial integrity, sovereignty, and ideological stability — all bundled together. Crucially, the CCP treats *party security* and *state security* as essentially synonymous.

**Key dimensions in Chinese political usage:**

- **Political security** — protecting the CCP's ruling position from internal or external threats
- **Territorial security** — sovereignty over Taiwan, Xinjiang, Tibet, Hong Kong, the South China Sea
- **Ideological security** — guarding against "Western values," separatism, or subversive ideas
- **Economic security** — protecting strategic industries, technology, and supply chains
- **Cyber/data security** — control over information flows and digital infrastructure
- **Social stability (社会稳定)** — suppressing dissent, protests, or anything deemed destabilizing

**Legal embodiment:** The concept was codified in the **National Security Law (国家安全法)**, including the sweeping 2015 version and the 2020 Hong Kong National Security Law. These laws define national security so broadly that criticism of the government, foreign journalism, or activism can fall under its scope.

**Political significance:** Under Xi Jinping, 国家安全 has become a governing framework unto itself — elevated in the 2022 Party Congress report as a top priority, with Xi declaring that security must underpin all development. The **National Security Commission (国家安全委员会)**, chaired by Xi personally, coordinates this across all state functions.

In short, it functions as both a legal tool and ideological justification for the Party to expand control over virtually any domain of public or private life.
