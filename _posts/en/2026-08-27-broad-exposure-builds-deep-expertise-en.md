**Question:**
If learning English by consuming many different materials naturally improves ability, does the same apply to learning hardware/software? In the AI era, is a generalist or specialist better, and which fits me?

**Answer:**

Yes. You are pointing at a very important learning pattern: **skill growth through a large amount of exposure + feedback**, even when the activities look random.

English is a good analogy.

A person reads:

* news
* novels
* technical docs
* Reddit
* YouTube comments
* papers

It feels unfocused. But the hidden common structure is:

> Every day the brain sees English tokens, grammar patterns, vocabulary, cultural context, and communication goals.

After 1-3 years, the person becomes good because the **distribution of experience is wide**.

The same happens in engineering.

Someone who:

* repairs GPUs
* builds PCs
* writes CUDA code
* trains models
* reads papers
* debugs Linux drivers
* writes backend services
* plays with Arduino

may look unfocused.

But the hidden common layer is:

> understanding computation systems from atoms → circuits → kernels → models → products.

That creates strong engineers.

### Specialist vs generalist in AI era

Historically:

```
Industrial era:
specialist > generalist

Software era:
specialist + broad software knowledge

AI era:
deep specialist + wide system understanding
```

The reason is AI changes the cost of breadth.

Before AI:

Learning many fields was expensive.

You needed:

* books
* mentors
* years of experience

Now:

```
Question
   ↓
LLM
   ↓
explanation
   ↓
experiment
   ↓
feedback
   ↓
new knowledge
```

The bottleneck is no longer information.

The bottleneck is:

* taste
* curiosity
* ability to connect domains
* ability to build things

So generalists become more valuable, but only if they have a "spine".
