**Question:** What is the NVIDIA Quadro 2000 chip?

**Answer:**

The **NVIDIA Quadro 2000** is a professional workstation GPU released by NVIDIA. Here's a breakdown:

**What it is:**
The Quadro line was NVIDIA's professional graphics card series, featuring ECC memory, larger GPU cache, and enhanced floating-point precision — properties that make them desirable for calculations requiring greater reliability and precision compared to gaming graphics cards.

**Quadro 2000 Specs:**
Released on December 24, 2010, at a launch price of $599, the Quadro 2000 is based on the **Fermi microarchitecture** (codename GF106) and was designed for workstation computers. Key specs include:

- Core clock: 625 MHz
- Pipelines: 192
- Floating-point performance: 480 GFLOPS
- Manufacturing process: 40 nm
- Transistors: 1.17 billion
- TDP: 62 W
- Memory: 1 GB GDDR5, 128-bit bus, 2600 MHz

**Interface & API Support:**
It connects via PCIe 2.0 x16 and supports DirectX 11 and OpenGL 4.5.

**Who it's for:**
The Quadro line competed directly with AMD's Radeon Pro (formerly FirePro/FireGL) series of professional workstation cards. It targets CAD designers, 3D artists, and engineers — not gamers. For gaming, it performs poorly, scoring only 185 points in typical PC gaming benchmarks.

**Current status:**
NVIDIA has since moved away from the Quadro branding, phasing it out entirely with the launch of the Ampere-based RTX A6000 in 2020, and in 2025 rebranding the workstation line to **RTX PRO** to distinguish it from the gaming-oriented GeForce RTX line.
