Question: So hot drying right after washing is better because we need to do it immediately. In a humid environment like this, hanging or delaying drying makes little progress and helps bacteria or mold grow, right?

Answer:

Yes, exactly — doing hot drying **right after washing** (as soon as the wash cycle finishes) is much better and more effective in a high-humidity place like Guangzhou in March (or really any time of year there).

**Why immediate hot drying is important:**

- **Humidity slows or stops natural drying**
  When relative humidity is 75–85%+ (typical for Guangzhou rainy season / spring), the air already holds a lot of moisture. Wet clothes hanging indoors or even outside release water very slowly — sometimes they stay damp for 24–48 hours or more. This creates the perfect warm, moist, still-air environment where bacteria and mold multiply quickly, producing that musty / sour / mildew smell.

- **Delay = more bacterial growth**
  Bacteria and mold spores are already on most fabrics from normal wear/sweat/previous washes. They need moisture + time + moderate warmth (around room temperature) to grow explosively. If clothes go from wet → washer → sit in a pile/basket/dryer (even for just a few hours), the smell can become noticeably worse. The longer the delay, the deeper the odor embeds into fibers, making it harder to remove later.

- **Hot dryer defeats humidity**
  A heated dryer (like your L-2 hot mode) blows warm, dry air through the clothes, forcing moisture out even when the room air is humid. It overcomes the ambient humidity problem that hanging can't. The heat also kills many bacteria/mold directly, and fast drying prevents them from having time to regrow. That's why hot drying right away is the key to preventing (or stopping) smells in humid climates.

**Practical rule for humid weather:**
- Finish wash → transfer to dryer **immediately** (within 10–30 minutes max).
- Use a heated mode (not cool/air-dry) from the start.
- Don't overload — smaller loads dry faster and more evenly.
- Make sure dryer lint filter and vent are clean so hot air moves freely.
- Only take clothes out when they are 100% dry (check thick seams, waistbands, etc.).

If you ever have to delay (power cut, etc.), at least spread clothes out in a single layer with good airflow (fan helps a lot) to slow bacterial growth — but it's still not as good as immediate hot drying.

So yes, your understanding is spot on: in a humid environment, **prompt hot drying after washing** is the single best way to avoid or eliminate those stubborn mildew smells. Waiting almost always makes things worse.
