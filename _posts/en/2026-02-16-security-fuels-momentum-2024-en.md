Question: Does the same principle—that financial security improves focus and performance—also explain why financial crises in companies (especially startups) often end badly, and why success breeds more success while struggles make things harder, due to the importance of momentum?

Answer:

**Yes, exactly—the same underlying mechanism of security vs. insecurity driving focus, decision quality, and momentum applies at both the individual and company/startup levels.**

When a startup (or person) **appears successful**—with growing revenue, funding rounds closing easily, strong metrics, positive press, or cash in the bank—the psychological and practical environment becomes highly conducive to further achievement:

- **Reduced cognitive load and stress**: Founders and teams aren’t constantly firefighting cash-flow crises or fearing imminent shutdown. This frees mental bandwidth for creative problem-solving, strategic thinking, hiring top talent, and executing at high quality.
- **Positive feedback loops**: Success attracts better talent, easier customer acquisition, favorable vendor terms, media coverage, and investor interest. Each win makes the next one easier → momentum builds.
- **Confidence and risk-taking**: With a safety buffer, teams can afford to take calculated risks (new features, markets, hires) that accelerate growth. Confidence becomes self-reinforcing.
- **Social proof & network effects**: Perceived success makes partnerships, deals, and recruiting dramatically easier.

When things **appear not well**—cash is running low, metrics are stalling, fundraising fails, key people leave, or bad press emerges—the spiral reverses:

- **Chronic stress and tunnel vision**: Founders and teams enter survival mode. Decisions become short-term and reactive (cutting costs aggressively, delaying payments, overpromising to customers/investors). Long-term strategy and innovation suffer.
- **Negative signaling**: Struggles scare away talent, customers hesitate, investors disappear, suppliers demand cash upfront. Each problem compounds the next → downward momentum.
- **Psychological toll**: Anxiety, burnout, blame culture, and loss of confidence lead to poorer execution, higher mistake rates, and “fake work” (busyness without progress).
- **Liquidity death spiral**: In startups especially, running out of cash forces desperate moves (mass layoffs, fire sales, pivots under duress), which often destroy morale, brand, and remaining value—making recovery extremely hard even if the core idea was good.

This is why many startups that look promising on paper still fail catastrophically once the runway shortens: **the loss of perceived security destroys focus and momentum faster than the actual numbers would suggest**. The company enters a self-reinforcing failure loop, just as the individual full-time exam taker enters a failure loop under financial/psychological pressure.

Momentum matters enormously because human systems (individuals, teams, companies) are not purely rational machines. **Perceived security creates virtuous cycles of focus → performance → more security**, while **perceived insecurity creates vicious cycles of distraction → poor performance → more insecurity**.

In short:
Success → ease → more success
Struggle → difficulty → more struggle

The financial and psychological state is not just a background condition—it actively shapes capability and outcomes.
