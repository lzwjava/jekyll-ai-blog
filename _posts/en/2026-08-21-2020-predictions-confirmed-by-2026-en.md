**Question:** For the article written in March 2020 at lzwjava.com/present-future-en, were the predictions accurate? And at the time, you were reading He Qinglian's book about China "溃而不崩" (Crumbling Without Collapsing), right?

**Answer:**

Yes, looking back from 2026, the March 2020 predictions have turned out to be remarkably accurate across multiple dimensions:

**What proved correct:**

- **Real estate collapse** — The article predicted real estate developers going bankrupt, unfinished buildings, and buyers losing money. This maps almost perfectly to the Evergrande crisis (2021), Country Garden collapse (2023), and the massive "rotten building" (烂尾楼) crisis across China.

- **Banks under stress & fiscal strain** — The prediction that land sales would fail to generate government revenue, straining fiscal income, came true. Local government financing vehicles (LGFVs) became a systemic crisis by 2023-2024.

- **Consumption depression & unemployment spiral** — "Most industries struggle to make money... more bankruptcies, unemployment, and further consumption decline in a vicious cycle" — this describes China's post-2021 deflationary environment almost exactly. Youth unemployment hit record highs (~21% officially in 2023).

- **Capital flight & emigration** — "High-net-worth families in tier-1 cities sell properties, converting to US dollars or emigrating" —润 (run) culture became a major social phenomenon from 2021 onward.

- **Stock/crypto speculation rising** — "During economic depression, stock trading discussions increase. Cryptocurrency is also hot" — this tracked accurately with Chinese retail speculation waves.

- **Infrastructure limits** — The article correctly noted that unlike 1929, China had already overbuilt roads and bridges, so infrastructure stimulus had diminishing returns — a point economists later widely acknowledged.

**On He Qinglian's "溃而不崩":**

Yes, that intellectual background makes perfect sense. He Qinglian's thesis — that China's system would *crumble without collapsing* (decay and dysfunction without a dramatic systemic break) — is clearly the analytical framework behind this article. The piece doesn't predict a sudden revolution or total collapse, but rather a slow, grinding deterioration: people becoming idle on TikTok, businesses quietly closing, families retreating to rural areas, banks quietly failing and being absorbed by the state — all very consistent with the 溃而不崩 model.

The 2020 article is notable for being written *before* Evergrande's public crisis, before the tech crackdown, and before the full scale of the property collapse was visible. The structural analysis held up very well.
