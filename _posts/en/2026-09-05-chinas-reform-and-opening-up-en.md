**Question:** What is 改革开放 (Gǎigé Kāifàng) in China?

**Answer:**

改革开放, literally meaning **"Reform and Opening-Up"**, is one of the most transformative policy shifts in modern Chinese history.

**Background:** Before Reform and Opening-Up, China had just experienced the 10-year internal turmoil of the Cultural Revolution. The country was closed off, social productivity was low, the economy was stagnant, and science, technology, and education lagged far behind.

**When & Who:** The reforms were led by the Communist Party of China and Deng Xiaoping, starting in December 1978. The starting point is widely considered to be the Third Plenum of the 11th Central Committee of the CPC, held from December 18–22, 1978.

**What it meant:** It changed the planned economic model of China to what is officially termed a "socialist market economy." The specific direction was "reform internally, open up externally." Deng Xiaoping also proposed a "three-step" modernization strategy: solving food and clothing issues by 1990, achieving a moderately prosperous society by end of the 20th century, and reaching the level of a medium-developed country by the mid-21st century.

**Key Results:**

- Over 40 years (1978–2018), China upgraded from a low-income country to the upper-middle income bracket, with the poverty rate falling from 97.5% to just 3.1% by 2017.
- The private sector grew remarkably, becoming 70% of China's GDP by 2005, and from 1978 to 2013, the economy grew at an unprecedented rate of 9.5% per year.
- Reform and Opening-Up was incorporated into the CCP constitution in 1997 and written into the state constitution in 1999.

In short, 改革开放 turned China from an isolated, centrally-planned economy into the world's second-largest economy and a major force in global trade.
