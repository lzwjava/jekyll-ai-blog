Question: What does "multimode" mean in the context of optical fiber?

Answer:

"Multimode" refers to an optical fiber that can carry **multiple light rays (modes) simultaneously**, each traveling at slightly different angles through the core.

**Key characteristics:**

- **Large core diameter** — typically 50 µm or 62.5 µm, making it easier to couple light into the fiber
- **Short distances** — best suited for distances under 2 km (usually within buildings or campuses)
- **Light source** — uses LED or short-wavelength laser (850 nm), which are cheaper
- **Modal dispersion** — because different modes arrive at slightly different times, the signal degrades over long distances

**Compared to Single-mode fiber:**

| Feature | Multimode | Single-mode |
|---|---|---|
| Core diameter | 50 / 62.5 µm | 8–10 µm |
| Light source | LED / 850nm laser | 1300–1550nm laser |
| Max distance | ~550m (Gigabit) | Up to 100+ km |
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Use case | LAN, data centers | WAN, long-haul |

**Simple analogy:** Think of multimode fiber like a wide highway where multiple cars (light rays) travel in different lanes simultaneously — efficient for short trips but prone to traffic (signal dispersion) over long distances. Single-mode is like a single narrow lane, only one car at a time, but with no congestion over very long distances.
