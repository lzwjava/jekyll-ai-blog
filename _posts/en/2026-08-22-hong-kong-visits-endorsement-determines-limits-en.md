Question: With 港澳通行证 (Mainland Chinese Exit-Entry Permit for Hong Kong/Macau), how many times can a mainland citizen visit Hong Kong and how long can they stay each time?

Answer:

It depends on the **签注 (endorsement)** attached to your 港澳通行证. The permit itself is not enough; you need a valid Hong Kong endorsement. ([NIA][1])

For a normal mainland resident visiting Hong Kong for tourism:

| Type                                      |                                  Number of visits |     Stay per visit |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------: | -----------------: |
| 香港个人旅游签注 (G)                              | Usually 1 time, 2 times, depending on endorsement |           ≤ 7 days |
| 香港团队旅游签注 (L)                              |                         Usually 1 time or 2 times |           ≤ 7 days |
| Multi-entry (some eligible cities/people) |             Multiple times during validity period | ≤ 7 days each time |

([NIA][2])

Examples:

* **3个月一次签注** → enter Hong Kong once within 3 months, stay up to 7 days.
* **3个月二次签注** → enter twice within 3 months, each stay up to 7 days.
* **1年一次签注** → one trip within one year, max 7 days.
* **1年二次签注** → two trips within one year, max 7 days each. ([Liaoning Public Security Department][3])

For someone living in Guangdong (for example Guangzhou/Shenzhen), Hong Kong is relatively easy:

* You can apply for Hong Kong personal travel endorsement.
* Guangdong residents generally have access to individual travel policies. ([NIA][1])
* Shenzhen residents may have special multi-entry arrangements under certain conditions. ([Liaoning Public Security Department][3])

If your purpose is **doing business / meeting clients in Hong Kong**, tourism endorsement is not the correct route. You normally need a **商务签注 (S)**, which can allow multiple entries depending on eligibility, usually with each stay up to 7 days. ([NIA][1])

For your situation (Guangzhou + AI/software consulting), a practical pattern is:

1. Get 港澳通行证 + 香港签注.
2. Visit Hong Kong for networking/client meetings.
3. If you start doing regular paid business there, consider a Hong Kong company + proper business/work arrangements.

Hong Kong is close enough that many mainland entrepreneurs treat it almost like a "nearby international business hub" rather than a one-time travel destination.
