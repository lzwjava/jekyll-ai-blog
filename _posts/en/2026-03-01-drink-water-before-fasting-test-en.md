Question: Can we drink plain water before a health check, and after waking up that morning (suppose 7am, with the health check around 8am)?

Answer: 

**Yes, you can — and should — drink plain water** before your health checkup, even on the morning of the appointment (e.g., after waking up at 7am before an 8am check).

This applies to most routine health checkups that include **fasting blood tests** (such as glucose, lipid profile/cholesterol, triglycerides, etc.), which are the main reason for the "no eating/drinking after evening" rule.

### Key points from standard medical guidelines:
- **Fasting means no food and no beverages except plain water.**  
  You must avoid anything with calories, sugar, caffeine, flavorings, or additives (coffee, tea, juice, milk, soda, flavored/sparkling water with lemon/artificial sweeteners, gum, etc.), usually for 8–12 hours before the test.
- **Plain water is explicitly allowed and recommended.**  
  - It does **not** affect blood test results (glucose, lipids, etc.).  
  - It keeps you **hydrated**, which makes your veins fuller and easier to locate → easier and less painful blood draw.  
  - Dehydration from not drinking anything can make veins harder to find and sometimes affect certain measurements slightly (e.g., blood concentration).
- **In your example scenario (wake up 7am, appointment 8am):**  
  You can drink plain water right after waking up and up until you leave for the appointment (or even right before the blood draw if needed). Drink a normal amount — a glass or two is fine and helpful. Just avoid large volumes if you have a urine test soon after (to not dilute it too much), but for blood tests, hydration is a benefit.

### Exceptions to note:
- Very rare tests (e.g., some hormone tests, specific electrolyte panels, or water deprivation tests) might restrict water — but these are uncommon in standard health checkups.  
- If your checkup includes **only non-fasting tests** (e.g., just CBC, liver/kidney function without glucose/lipids), water is always fine anyway.  
- Always follow your specific doctor's or lab's instructions — if they say "nothing by mouth" (NPO), confirm whether water is included (most allow it for blood tests).

In summary: Stick to **plain, unflavored water only** that morning — it's not only permitted but actively encouraged for better vein access and comfort during the blood draw.
