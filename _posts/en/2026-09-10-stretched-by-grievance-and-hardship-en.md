**1. Steve Jobs — Fired from his own company**

In 1985, Steve Jobs was humiliatingly fired from Apple, the very company he had built in his parents' garage. In his famous Stanford commencement speech, Jobs recalled the devastating public humiliation of being ousted, and admitted he even considered fleeing Silicon Valley entirely. Only years later did he see how the blow had actually helped him — saying that getting fired turned out to be the best thing that ever happened to him, because the heaviness of being successful was replaced by the lightness of being a beginner again, which freed him to enter one of the most creative periods of his life. He went on to found NeXT and Pixar — and eventually returned to rescue Apple, leading to the iPhone and everything that followed. Pure 委屈撑大.

**2. Ray Dalio — Publicly wrong, then broke**

In a defining moment of hubris, Dalio made a bold, public prediction about an imminent U.S. recession. When markets instead embarked on a prolonged bull run, Bridgewater's portfolio suffered devastating losses. The situation deteriorated so badly that Dalio was forced to lay off his entire staff and seek financial assistance from his father — a humbling experience for someone who had prided himself on market foresight. From that humiliation, he rebuilt Bridgewater around "radical transparency" and turned it into the world's largest hedge fund. His now-famous principle: "Pain plus reflection equals progress."

---

**3. Walt Disney — Told he lacked creativity**

Disney's humiliations repeatedly served as inflection points, forcing him to adapt, innovate, and institutionalize lessons that sustained long-term growth. One of his most enduring statements was: "I think it's important to have a good hard failure when you're young. I learned a lot from that. Because it makes you aware of what can happen to you." He was early in his career fired by a newspaper editor for supposedly lacking imagination — a grievance that, in retrospect, stretched him into building the most imaginative entertainment empire in history.

---

**4. Oprah Winfrey — Fired and told she was unfit for TV**

After moving to become a news anchor in Baltimore, Oprah was fired for not being "fit for TV" and demoted to reading headlines on a morning program. She then took on a low-rated morning talk show in Chicago, and within less than a year made it the number one talk show in the city. The humiliation of being publicly deemed inadequate became the fuel for one of the most remarkable media careers ever built.
