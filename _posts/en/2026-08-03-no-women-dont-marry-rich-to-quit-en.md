Question: Is it common in the UK for women to want to marry a rich partner specifically so they can stop working?

Answer: No solid data supports that framing as a widespread female "goal" in the UK — but a few real, related patterns exist and probably feed the perception:

**What the data actually shows:**
- UK employment rate for women is ~72% (ONS, all women 16-64), and marriage/motherhood does reduce it — a lot of the drop is driven by childcare, not a plan to "not work." Once kids are in school, most women return, often part-time.
- Part-time work is heavily skewed female in the UK (~38% of employed women work part-time vs ~13% of men) — this is the actual mechanism people sometimes mislabel as "not wanting to work."
- On partner income preference: a 2017 Pew survey found 71% of women said it's "very important" for a male partner to have the capacity to provide financially — that's about wanting a *capable* partner, not a "don't need to work" plan. It's also a US survey, not UK-specific.
- Women still tend to marry men who earn more than they do — in the US, 63% of recently married women were outearned by their husbands as of 2023. No direct UK equivalent I found, but UK gender pay gap dynamics likely produce something similar.

**What this isn't:**
- There's no UK survey I found showing women explicitly targeting rich partners to quit working. That's a stereotype (often pushed in manosphere/red-pill content) that takes real patterns (part-time work after kids, mild income hypergamy) and reframes them as a coordinated female strategy.
- Individual variance is huge — plenty of high-earning UK women marry lower-earning men, plenty of dual-income-no-kids couples, etc.

**Bottom line:** the underlying economic pattern (women's paid work drops around childbirth, income hypergamy exists at the margin) is real and measurable. The "girls want to marry rich to avoid working" framing is a caricature of that pattern, not something surveys support as a common stated goal.
