### 1. Mainland → Hong Kong

A person in Shenzhen/Guangzhou:

```text
Customer in HK
      ↓ sends order + money
Mainland buyer
      ↓
Shenzhen → Hong Kong
      ↓
buy / deliver goods
      ↓
HK customer
```

Typical things historically include:

* Mainland specialty foods
* Shenzhen electronics/accessories
* Chinese e-commerce products
* Certain lower-priced consumer goods

The person makes money from:

```text
selling price
- purchase price
- transport
- customs/tax costs
- payment fees
= margin
```

### 2. Hong Kong → Mainland

This direction is probably what you are thinking about when people say **HK 代购**.

For example:

```text
Mainland customer
      ↓
"Buy me X in HK"
      ↓
HK buyer
      ↓
Hong Kong store / pharmacy / electronics shop
      ↓
carry to Shenzhen
      ↓
Mainland customer
```

Common historical categories:

* cosmetics
* watches / luxury goods
* medicines and health-related products
* electronics
* baby products
* imported food
* clothing
* branded goods

Hong Kong is attractive for this because it is a **free port with no general tariff on imported goods**; only specific categories such as tobacco, certain alcohol, hydrocarbon oil and methyl alcohol are subject to excise duties. ([Hong Kong Customs][1])

### 3. The important distinction: personal shopping vs commercial import

This is where the business gets complicated.

Someone carrying a couple of legitimate personal purchases is very different from:

```text
20 phones
50 bottles of something
100 cosmetics
大量 identical products
```

The second starts looking like **commercial importation**, rather than ordinary passenger baggage.

And some goods are specifically controlled. For example, Hong Kong Customs says passengers exceeding the applicable tobacco/liquor exemptions need to use the Red Channel and declare them. ([Hong Kong Customs][2])

So legitimate 代购 operators generally need to think about:

```text
product legality
      +
quantity
      +
origin/destination customs rules
      +
tax/duty
      +
transport
      +
commercial import requirements
```

### 4. Why Shenzhen ↔ Hong Kong makes this particularly interesting

The geography is extremely favorable:

```text
Guangzhou
    │
    │ ~2h
    ▼
Shenzhen
    │
    │ border
    ▼
Hong Kong
```

Someone living in Shenzhen can potentially make a same-day trip:

```text
Shenzhen
   ↓
Futian / Luohu
   ↓
Hong Kong
   ↓
purchase
   ↓
cross border
   ↓
Shenzhen
```

That's why historically there has been a whole ecosystem of **parallel traders / personal shoppers / cross-border purchasing**, rather than everything needing international shipping.

### 5. There is also a modern version

You don't necessarily need to physically find customers one by one.

A small operator can run:

```text
Xiaohongshu / WeChat / Taobao / private group
                 ↓
             order form
                 ↓
        aggregate 20 orders
                 ↓
             HK purchase
                 ↓
        cross-border logistics
                 ↓
             customers
```

This is essentially a tiny **cross-border commerce operation**.

The interesting economic variable isn't really "Can I buy something cheaper in HK?"

It's:

> **Can I create enough price difference to cover my transportation, payment, customs/tax, inventory, return and fraud risk while still leaving margin?**

For example:

```text
HK retail price       ¥700
Mainland market price ¥850

gross spread          ¥150

transport             -¥30
payment               -¥10
logistics             -¥20
tax/customs risk      -¥20
customer acquisition  -¥20
--------------------------------
potential margin       ¥50
```

At that point you need **volume**, not just individual arbitrage.

One particularly important point: don't accept sealed/unknown packages from strangers to carry across the border. Hong Kong Customs explicitly warns travelers not to carry unknown items for other people or accept delegation to carry controlled items. ([Hong Kong Customs][3])
