Question: How should I create a WeChat group for more interaction with close friends who align with my long-term focus, such as English, global perspective, USA, Singapore, Hong Kong, machine learning, deep learning, and strong engineering skills? Should it include young and promising people, or others who share similar interests and have helped each other in the past?

Answer:

Yes, your intuition is correct. To make a WeChat group truly active and meaningful, the members should be carefully selected based on both alignment and potential for mutual value. Here’s a structured way to approach it:

1. **Alignment with Long-Term Goals**

   * Include people who share your long-term focus: global exposure, strong engineering or ML/DL skills, interest in English-language discussions, or experience in target regions (USA, Singapore, HK).
   * This ensures conversations stay relevant and constructive toward your personal or professional growth.

2. **Age and Growth Potential**

   * Young and promising members can bring energy, new ideas, and willingness to experiment or share openly.
   * Experienced members with domain knowledge are also valuable but should complement rather than dominate the group.

3. **Shared Interests and History**

   * Members who have collaborated with you before, or share overlapping interests, are more likely to engage actively.
   * Past mutual help builds trust and a sense of community, which encourages ongoing participation.

4. **Diversity within Focus**

   * While alignment is crucial, a bit of diversity (different backgrounds, perspectives, or complementary skills) can spark more interesting discussions and ideas.

5. **Interaction Encouragement**

   * Set a culture of active sharing: questions, resources, personal updates, or challenges.
   * Consider weekly themes, challenges, or short “show-and-tell” sessions to stimulate engagement.

6. **Selective Invitation**

   * Avoid adding too many passive members who are unlikely to contribute; quality over quantity matters for keeping the group vibrant.

If you focus on these principles, the group becomes not just a chat space but a small professional-learning network aligned with your growth path.
