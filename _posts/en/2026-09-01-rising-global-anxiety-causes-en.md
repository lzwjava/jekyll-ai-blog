**Question:** Why are people so scared / anxious in recent years?

**Answer:**

Global anxiety has been climbing steadily and measurably. In 2024, 43% of adults said they felt more anxious than the year before — up from 37% in 2023 and 32% in 2022. By 2026, nearly half of Americans reported feeling more anxious than the previous year. Several forces are converging to drive this:

**1. Economic Pressure**
The economy was the top source of anxiety, with 71% of people reporting they were very or somewhat anxious about it. Concerns about rent, groceries, debt, childcare, retirement, and job stability create a chronic background strain — 40% of employed adults said they were worried about job security.

**2. Global Conflicts & Current Events**
Major anxieties include the 2026 Iran war, healthcare costs, gun violence, international conflict, and hate speech — each cited by 60%+ of respondents.

**3. Non-Stop News Cycles**
News updates now arrive continuously through multiple devices and platforms, which helps explain why many people feel mentally exhausted even when nothing is happening directly in front of them.

**4. AI & Technological Uncertainty**
The percentage of people anxious about the impact of emerging technologies, including AI, rose from 44% in 2025 to 53% in 2026.

**5. Loneliness**
Loneliness experienced at least once weekly increased from 33% in 2025 to 42% in 2026, with 16% experiencing it every day.

**6. The COVID Hangover**
The COVID-19 pandemic triggered a 25% jump in anxiety and depression rates during its first year alone — the biggest mental health disruption ever recorded.

The core pattern is that uncertainty — financial, political, technological, and social — triggers the brain's threat-detection system, which evolved for physical dangers but now runs continuously in response to abstract, never-ending modern stressors.
