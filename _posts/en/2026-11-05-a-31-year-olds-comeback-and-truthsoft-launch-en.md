All of these are real parts of your life.

But a potential customer might finish reading and still wonder:

I would establish one central positioning:

# TruthSoft

## An AI-native software engineering company.

Think clearly. Build what matters. Play the long game.

From model training and inference to AI agents and production software, we help businesses turn technical ideas into working products.

Then make the four business lines supporting capabilities rather than four equally important businesses.

My recommended priority:

| Priority | Business                                     |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | AI agents and AI application development     |
| 2        | AI engineering and transformation consulting |
| 3        | Model training and inference                 |

Why?

Because your immediate commercial objective is to generate revenue. Model training is an important long-term research direction, but probably not the easiest service to sell to ordinary businesses today.

Keep researching LLMs. Don't pretend that all four business lines have equal commercial maturity.

## 3. Five things I would change before publishing

### A. Remove the medical advice about eyesight

This paragraph is problematic:

> If you wear glasses, use glasses 150 degrees below your prescription...

You are presenting a personal experiment as general medical advice.

Even if your own experience has been positive, it doesn't establish that this approach is safe or effective for other people.

I'd change it to a personal experience, or remove it from the introductory section entirely.

Your article is about entrepreneurship, engineering, AI, and starting again. The eyesight discussion distracts from that message.

The hydrogen peroxide foot-soaking detail is also unnecessary.

### B. Be more careful with the 200 million figure

You write:

> My code and content have reached more than 200 million people.

This is a very large claim.

The problem isn't necessarily that the underlying numbers are wrong. It's that different categories of impact are being aggregated:

- Direct users.
- SDK developers.
- End users of products using those SDKs.
- Project customers' users.
- Blog visitors.
- Video views.

These aren't equivalent.

I'd use:

"My software and content have contributed to products and platforms reaching hundreds of millions of users."

Then provide a breakdown with methodology.

Avoid implying that you personally acquired 200 million users.

### C. Reduce the emotional complaints

Your article has a few passages that could be interpreted as resentment:

- The world is too pragmatic.
- Good engineers are stuck in outsourcing.
- Foreign companies are affected by the economic environment.
- Conflicts with your father.
- The housing market and debt.

I wouldn't remove the history.

But I'd make sure the emphasis is on what you learned, rather than who or what caused your difficulties.

For example:

> 世界真势利，那么多能力好、英语好的人，只是在大公司的外包体系里工作。

This sounds like a complaint about the industry.

A stronger message would be:

You discovered that technical ability alone doesn't guarantee freedom, recognition, or financial independence. You now want to build something where your work and its value are more directly connected.

That's a much more powerful insight.

### D. Don't oversell the one-person laboratory

Your current company description says:

> One person can also operate a laboratory.

I like this philosophy.

But distinguish between:

- What you personally built.
- What you experimentally demonstrated.
- What the company can deliver commercially.
- What external collaborators can help deliver.

For example, training a GPT-2 model is evidence of engineering ability. It doesn't automatically demonstrate the ability to pretrain competitive foundation models.

Be technically precise. Your engineering audience will appreciate that.

### E. Shorten the final company profile

The article has two distinct sections:

1. Your personal story.
2. A company website / professional portfolio.

Both are useful, but the second is enormous.

I would keep the complete resume, project portfolio, and influence statistics on TruthSoft's website, linking to them from the article.

The article should create interest and trust. The website should provide evidence and convert that trust into business.
