Question: Does reading aloud help with focus, and why is it effective compared to passive consumption like tweets or short videos?

Answer:

Yes, reading aloud (朗读) is genuinely powerful for focus, and there's good reasoning behind why it works so well.

**Why reading aloud helps focus:**

- **Multi-sensory engagement** — you're using your eyes (reading), mouth (speaking), and ears (hearing) simultaneously. More senses engaged = harder for your mind to wander.
- **Forces a pace** — you can't skim or rush. Your brain has to process each word, which builds deeper comprehension.
- **Active production vs. passive consumption** — speaking is an *output* act. It keeps you in the driver's seat mentally, unlike scrolling where content just washes over you.
- **Phonological loop activation** — working memory has a verbal rehearsal system. Saying words aloud strengthens this loop, improving retention.

**Why tweets/short videos break focus:**

- They're engineered for **dopamine micro-hits** — each swipe resets attention, training the brain to expect novelty every few seconds.
- They reward **shallow processing** — no need to hold a thought for more than a moment.
- Over time, this shrinks your **attention span** by conditioning your brain to be restless.

**Reading aloud is essentially the opposite** — it's slow, deliberate, and demands sustained attention. It's one of the oldest study techniques precisely because it works.

Many people also find that reading aloud helps with comprehension in a second language (like English for Chinese speakers), since it connects written text to spoken rhythm and sound.
