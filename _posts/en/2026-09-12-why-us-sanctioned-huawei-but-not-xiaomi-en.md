Question: Why hasn't Xiaomi been cut off by the US like Huawei was?

Answer: Great question — and the answer comes down to three key differences: **what they make, who they're seen as, and what they depend on.**

**1. Huawei was on the Commerce Entity List; Xiaomi was not**

These are two completely different lists with completely different consequences. Huawei's placement on the **US Commerce Department's Entity List** was the nuclear option — it cut off access to US suppliers, chips, software (Google/Android), and manufacturing (TSMC). That's what destroyed Huawei's smartphone business. Xiaomi was never placed on that list.

**2. Xiaomi's brief blacklisting was quickly reversed**

When Xiaomi's name surfaced in early 2021, the context was different. The US Department of Defense designated Xiaomi as a "Communist Chinese military company" (CCMC). The immediate consequence was a prohibition on US persons from investing in Xiaomi's publicly traded securities — not a technology supply ban. Xiaomi scored a rare legal victory four months later, getting its name scraped off the list — one of the first Chinese companies to do so. A US District Court judge described the original blacklisting as "arbitrary and capricious."

**3. Xiaomi doesn't threaten US national security in the same way**

The core reason Huawei was targeted so aggressively is that it was the **world's dominant 5G infrastructure provider** — meaning it was supplying the actual network backbone of allied countries. That's a direct national security concern. Xiaomi is primarily a **consumer electronics** company that buys chips from Qualcomm (a US company), which means sanctioning Xiaomi would actually *hurt* US chip revenue, not protect US security. Unlike Huawei, Xiaomi is not on any US trade blacklist and is thus able to source the latest chips from Qualcomm, which are important to remain competitive in the global smartphone market.

**4. Fear of it happening again**

Interestingly, Xiaomi founder Lei Jun said his fear of US sanctions in 2021 pushed him to build an electric vehicle (EV) business from scratch, revealing for the first time a key motivating factor in his decision to venture into a notoriously challenging business segment. So even though Xiaomi escaped, the threat was real enough to reshape the entire company's strategy.

**In short:** Huawei was a geopolitical target because of 5G infrastructure dominance. Xiaomi sells consumer gadgets using American chips — sanctioning it would hurt US companies more than China.
