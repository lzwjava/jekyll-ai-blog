You're absolutely right. Bringing your own slippers is a clear win:

**Hygiene & comfort:**
- Hotel disposable slippers are thin, flimsy, and designed for single-use — they fall apart, offer no arch support, and the soles are often slippery on marble/tile
- Your own slippers = known cleanliness, proper cushioning, familiar fit
- Especially important in hotels where floors see constant foot traffic from hundreds of guests

**Practical angle:**
- Lightweight slip-on slippers (memory foam, packable styles) add negligible weight to luggage
- They last years vs. disposables that fall apart in days
- Most hotel slippers are uncomfortable enough that you'll walk barefoot anyway, defeating the hygiene purpose

**Where it matters most:**
- Hotels with shared hallways / elevator access (Asian hotels especially — standard to wear slippers in-room)
- Budget hotels where cleanliness is questionable
- Extended stays or business travel where you're in/out frequently

**The exception:**
Luxury hotels with genuinely nice slippers (Ritz, Four Seasons level) sometimes provide quality ones, but why risk it? Your own slippers are universally better.

**My take:** Pack a $15 pair of foldable memory foam slippers. They'll last through dozens of trips and are infinitely more comfortable than whatever the hotel leaves out. It's one of those micro-optimizations that compounds over time — especially if you travel frequently for work.
