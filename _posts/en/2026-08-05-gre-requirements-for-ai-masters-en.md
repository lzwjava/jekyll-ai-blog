**Question: Do you need the GRE for a US AI/Computer Science master's?**

**Short answer:** **Usually no, but it depends on the university.** Since around 2020, many US CS programs made the GRE optional or stopped requiring it altogether.

Here's the current landscape:

| Program type                              | GRE                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Top CS MS (Stanford, CMU, Berkeley, etc.) | Often **Not Required** or **Not Accepted**               |
| Mid-tier CS MS                            | Often **Optional**                                       |
| Some Engineering programs                 | Sometimes **Required**                                   |
| PhD                                       | Usually Optional or Not Required, but check each program |

The trend is toward **less emphasis on GRE** and more emphasis on:

* GPA
* Academic preparation
* Research experience
* Publications
* Recommendation letters
* Statement of Purpose
* GitHub/open-source contributions (especially in AI systems)
* Relevant industry experience

For someone with your engineering background, a strong portfolio (e.g., contributions to SGLang/vLLM, training models, AI infrastructure projects) is likely to be more valuable than spending months trying to maximize a GRE score.

### What about English tests?

These are much more commonly required:

* **TOEFL iBT**
* **IELTS Academic**

Some universities waive the English test if you earned a degree in English, but that generally doesn't apply to applicants from China.

### Your situation

If your goal is an **MS in AI/CS**:

* Don't start with GRE.
* First identify 10–20 target universities.
* Check each program's admissions page.
* Only prepare for the GRE if one of your target schools actually requires or strongly recommends it.

That approach saves a lot of unnecessary effort.
