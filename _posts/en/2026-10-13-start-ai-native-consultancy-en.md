**Question: Should I start a software consultancy company again (AI, agents, mobile, full stack, etc.)?**

**Answer:**

Given your background, **yes, a consultancy-style AI company is one of the more realistic startup paths for you**, but I would not restart it as a traditional software outsourcing company. I would build an **AI-native engineering consultancy**.

Your previous startup experience matters:

- You already built a product with users (Fun Live, 30K users).
- You have 12 years engineering experience.
- You have real LLM infrastructure experience (training, agents, CLI tools, deployment).
- You understand enterprise systems (HSBC, banking, Java, DevOps).
- You can deliver, not just prototype.

The market today is different from 2016. A small team can produce much more output because AI coding agents reduce labor cost.

A traditional software agency:

```
customer
   |
requirements
   |
10 developers
   |
6 months delivery
   |
large cost
```

is becoming less attractive.

An AI-native consultancy:

```
customer problem
       |
AI engineer + agents
       |
prototype in days
       |
production system in weeks
       |
high-value delivery
```

has a stronger advantage.

Examples of services:

### 1. Enterprise AI automation

Companies have:

- thousands of documents
- internal knowledge bases
- repetitive workflows
- legacy systems

You can build:

- RAG systems
- internal AI assistants
- agent workflows
- AI testing automation
- migration tools

A small team can charge serious money because the value is business impact, not code volume.

### 2. AI engineering contractor / FDE model

A new category is emerging:

> "Give us your hardest AI engineering problem, we integrate it into your product."

Not:

> "We provide 5 Java developers."

More like:

> "We build your AI coding agent, evaluation pipeline, inference stack, or automation system."

This matches your strengths.

### 3. Content + reputation as distribution

For a normal employee:

```

```

For a founder:

```
skill
 |

```

Your AI notes, open-source projects, GPU experiments, and engineering writing are actually valuable distribution assets.

---

However, there are risks.

Your previous startup had a common founder problem:

```
build product
      ↓
hope users arrive
```

For consultancy, reverse the order:

```
find painful customer problem
      ↓
sell solution
      ↓
deliver
      ↓
reuse technology
      ↓
turn service into product
```

The first goal is not "build a company".

The first goal is:

**get 1 customer paying 10k-50k RMB/month.**

Then:

```
1 customer
   ↓
3 customers
   ↓
small team
   ↓
repeatable service
   ↓
product opportunity
```

This is closer to how many successful companies started. For example, Palantir Technologies initially grew by solving difficult problems for specific customers rather than selling a generic SaaS product.

Your current situation also favors this path:

- You dislike slow corporate promotion.
- You prefer direct feedback from users/customers.
- You enjoy building.
- You already have technical credibility.
- AI reduces the capital needed.

The main thing you need is **sales and customer discovery**, not more technical learning.

A reasonable 6-month strategy:

```
Month 1:
- define 2-3 AI consulting packages
- publish technical demos
- contact potential customers

Month 2-3:
- close first paid project
- deliver quickly
- collect case study

Month 4-6:
- repeat
- hire contractors
- build reusable AI infrastructure
```

I would avoid positioning as:

> "mobile + web + backend outsourcing"

because that competes with thousands of cheap teams.

Position as:

> "AI engineering partner helping companies deploy agents, automation, and LLM systems."

That is a much rarer skill set.
