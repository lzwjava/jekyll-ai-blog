I'd push back on this a bit. While AI chatbots are incredibly powerful learning tools, they complement books rather than replace them. Here's why I think keeping your books still makes sense:

**Books have unique strengths:**

- **Deep, structured learning**: Books take you through a carefully architected argument or body of knowledge. A chatbot conversation is more reactive and fragmented—you might miss connections or foundational concepts you didn't know to ask about.
- **No knowledge cutoff**: Especially for current events and recent research, but also because authors synthesize knowledge in ways that might not be captured in training data.
- **Focus and immersion**: Reading a book creates sustained engagement without the temptation to jump around. That deep focus builds different kinds of understanding.
- **Reliability for specialized knowledge**: In technical fields, legal texts, or academic subjects, authoritative sources matter. Chatbots can make mistakes or hallucinate details.

**Where AI excels:**

- Clarifying confusing concepts from books you're reading
- Exploring tangential questions quickly
- Getting multiple perspectives on topics
- Interactive problem-solving and practice
- Customizing explanations to your level

The sweet spot is using both: read books for structured, deep learning, then use AI to explore further, test understanding, and fill gaps. Think of it like having a brilliant study partner for your reading, not a replacement for the curriculum.

That said, if you have books you'll genuinely never read again, sure—declutter. But I wouldn't sell off your library just because AI exists. The best learners tend to use multiple modalities.
