Here’s a comprehensive introduction to the life of Li Ka-shing, one of Asia’s most influential business magnates and philanthropists:

---

### **Early Life and Humble Beginnings**
- **Born**: June 13, 1928, in Chaozhou, Guangdong province, China.
- **Family Struggles**: At age 14, after his father’s death from tuberculosis, Li dropped out of school and moved to Hong Kong with his family. He worked as an apprentice in a teahouse and later at a watch company, learning business and self-reliance from an early age.

---

### **Rise to Business Prominence**
- **First Venture**: At 22, Li founded Cheung Kong Industries, a plastics manufacturing company specializing in plastic flowers. His business acumen and hard work led to rapid expansion.
- **Real Estate Empire**: In the 1960s, he entered the real estate market, eventually becoming Hong Kong’s leading private developer. By 1979, only the colonial government owned more real estate in Hong Kong than Li.
- **Acquisition of Hutchison Whampoa**: In 1979, Li made history as the first Chinese businessman to acquire a major British trading company, Hutchison Whampoa. Under his leadership, it became the world’s largest independent operator of ports and a global conglomerate with interests in retail, infrastructure, telecommunications, and more.

---

### **Business Empire and Global Influence**
- **CK Hutchison Holdings and CK Asset Holdings**: Li’s flagship companies are now global giants, with operations in over 50 countries. His conglomerate’s market capitalization once represented 4% of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange.
- **Diversification**: Beyond real estate and ports, Li invested in technology (Horizons Ventures), social media (Facebook, Spotify), and international assets, including significant stakes in Canadian and European companies.
- **Nickname**: Known as “Superman” for his sharp business instincts and ability to navigate crises, Li’s influence extends to politics, philanthropy, and global markets.

---

### **Philanthropy and Legacy**
- **Li Ka Shing Foundation**: Established in 1980, the foundation has donated over $3.8 billion, primarily to education, healthcare, and disaster relief in Greater China and beyond. Li pledged to give away one-third of his wealth to charitable causes.
- **Recognition**: Awarded the Grand Bauhinia Medal (Hong Kong’s highest honor) and the Malcolm S. Forbes Lifetime Achievement Award, among others.

---

### **Personal Life and Retirement**
- **Family**: Li’s sons, Victor and Richard, are actively involved in the family business. Victor succeeded him as chairman of CK Hutchison in 2018, though Li remains a senior advisor.
- **Retirement and Beyond**: After retiring, Li focused on philanthropy and global investments, while maintaining influence in Hong Kong and international business circles.

---

### **Recent Challenges and Controversies**
- **Geopolitical Tensions**: Li’s business dealings, especially the sale of Panama Canal ports, have drawn scrutiny from both Beijing and Washington, reflecting the complex interplay between global business and politics.
- **Market Adaptation**: In response to changing political and economic landscapes, Li has diversified investments overseas and reduced reliance on mainland China.
