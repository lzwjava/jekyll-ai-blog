Question: **Whose entrepreneurial story is similar to Zhao Deli — someone who nearly lost everything, kept building, and eventually broke through?**

Answer: **Yes — Elon Musk is probably the closest famous example.** But there are several different flavors of the same pattern.

### 1. Elon Musk — probably the closest

This is the strongest parallel.

In **2008**, Musk had simultaneously pushed almost all his wealth into **Tesla + SpaceX**. Tesla was struggling badly, while SpaceX had three consecutive Falcon 1 launch failures. Both companies were approaching collapse. ([Fortune][1])

The interesting similarity:

```text
Zhao Deli                         Elon Musk

make money first                 Zip2 / PayPal exit
        ↓                                ↓
bet personal wealth              bet personal wealth
        ↓                                ↓
crazy technical project          Tesla + SpaceX
        ↓                                ↓
almost no money                  almost bankrupt
        ↓                                ↓
keep building                    keep launching
        ↓                                ↓
one breakthrough                 Falcon 1 succeeds
        ↓                                ↓
capital arrives                  financing arrives
        ↓                                ↓
XPeng AeroHT                     SpaceX / Tesla
```

And both had a **physical technology that had to actually work**.

Zhao couldn't bullshit a flying machine into flying.

Musk couldn't bullshit a rocket into orbit.

That's an important difference from many startup stories.

---

### 2. Steve Jobs — similar psychologically, different financially

Jobs is another very strong example.

He wasn't broke in the same way Zhao was. Instead, at **30 years old he was effectively expelled from the company he had co-founded**.

He then built NeXT and acquired Pixar. NeXT itself wasn't a commercial hit, but Apple's eventual acquisition of NeXT brought Jobs back to Apple in 1997. ([Inc.com][2])

The pattern:

```text
huge success
    ↓
catastrophic personal setback
    ↓
start again as outsider
    ↓
build new capability
    ↓
return to the original battlefield
    ↓
much bigger success
```

What's particularly interesting is that **NeXT's failure wasn't wasted**. Its technology eventually became part of the foundation of modern macOS/iOS.

So Jobs is less:

> "I went broke and kept going"

and more:

> **"I lost my position and rebuilt myself."**

---

### 3. Henry Ford — extremely similar to Zhao

This one is underrated.

Ford had **two failed automobile companies** before Ford Motor Company became successful. ([Business LibreTexts][3])

That's conceptually very close:

> **build → fail → lose capital/reputation → try again with accumulated technical knowledge**

Ford also had something Zhao had:

**obsession with the physical product.**

He wasn't primarily a financial entrepreneur. He wanted to make the machine work.

---

### 4. Walt Disney — another crazy one

Disney's early businesses failed badly, including bankruptcy.

He eventually rebuilt and created Disney.

But there's a key difference from Zhao:

Disney's breakthrough was more **creative/product/IP-driven**, whereas Zhao's was deeply hardware/engineering-driven.

---

### 5. Jensen Huang — a very interesting modern example

Huang's story is actually useful for you because it's closer to **engineering entrepreneurship**.

NVIDIA's early product **NV1** was a commercial failure. NVIDIA subsequently had to pivot, and the company came close to collapse around the NV2/NV1 era. Huang later described NVIDIA as being roughly 30 days from going out of business during one particularly dangerous period.

But instead of abandoning graphics, NVIDIA kept iterating until the architecture/business direction worked.

That pattern is:

```text
technical conviction
       ↓
wrong implementation
       ↓
company nearly dies
       ↓
pivot
       ↓
technical breakthrough
       ↓
massive compounding
```

It's very different from someone who simply gets lucky with a consumer app.

And **Jack Ma is actually quite different**. His famous story is primarily rejection and lack of initial resources, not *“I made a fortune and then bet almost all of it on an insane technical project.”* ([Wikipedia][4])

### What makes Zhao/Musk especially interesting

There is a particular category of founder that I think is more interesting than the generic **"never give up"** story:

> **They become personally financially exposed to a technical thesis before the market believes it.**

```text
"I think this technology should exist."
                    ↓
             nobody believes it
                    ↓
              build prototype
                    ↓
             prototype actually works
                    ↓
             still nobody believes it
                    ↓
               build better
                    ↓
             demonstrate publicly
                    ↓
                capital arrives
```

Zhao's flying car and Musk's rockets are almost textbook examples.

And there's a brutal selection effect here: **for every Zhao or Musk, there are probably thousands of people who went broke pursuing a technically fascinating idea and never recovered.** The fact that these stories are famous is itself survivorship bias.

So I wouldn't extract **"bet everything and never give up."**

I'd extract:

> **Keep increasing the amount of objective evidence that your crazy idea actually works.**

That's the part that separates Zhao's story from pure stubbornness.
