**Question:** What has been the flight/passenger volume trend at Hong Kong International Airport (HKIA) over the past decade?

**Answer:**

Here's the decade-long passenger volume picture:The decade breaks down into three distinct phases:

---

**Phase 1 — Pre-pandemic growth (2015–2018)**
HKIA was on a consistent upward trajectory, growing from ~63 million passengers to a record peak. HKIA reached its all-time record of 74.67 million passengers in 2018.

**Phase 2 — Collapse (2019–2022)**
2019 was already disrupted by social unrest, then COVID hit catastrophically. Traffic dipped to 71.54 million in 2019 amid late-year social unrest. The pandemic caused an unprecedented collapse — the airport served only 8.84 million passengers in 2020, then just 1.35 million in 2021. At the height of the pandemic, this represented a 98% collapse from pre-pandemic highs. 2022 remained largely paralyzed due to Hong Kong's strict quarantine policies.

**Phase 3 — Recovery (2023–present)**
The turning point came in late 2022, when the government began lifting border restrictions. A full reopening in early 2023, followed by a high-profile "Hello Hong Kong" campaign that included free airline tickets, sparked an immediate resurgence in travel.

- **2023:** HKIA handled 40 million passengers, a six-fold increase from 2022 and a new post-pandemic high.
- **2024:** HKIA served over 53 million passengers, a sharp increase of +34.3% compared to the previous year, though still 26% lower than pre-pandemic 2019 levels. The airport also commissioned its Three-Runway System in November 2024, on time and within budget.
- **2025:** HKIA handled 61 million passenger trips in 2025, representing double-digit growth of 15%, with flight movements rising 8.7% to 394,730.
- **2026 (YTD):** Over the first eight months of 2025 (reported on a rolling basis), passenger volume saw 15.4% growth year-on-year to 40.3 million, while flight movements increased by 9.9% to 260,830.

**Still not fully recovered vs. rivals:** Compared to rivals like Singapore and Seoul, both of which reached or exceeded 2019 traffic levels by 2024, Hong Kong's recovery was delayed — however, its growth trajectory is now among the steepest. Projections suggest HKIA will near 2019 figures by end of 2026.
