Question: Is it better to create a situation where opportunities come to us, rather than constantly chasing and persuading people to collaborate?

Answer: **Yes — especially for software/AI consulting, this is a powerful asymmetry.**

Think of the two funnels:

```text
Outbound:
You → find person → explain yourself → persuade → negotiate → build trust → project
       ↑ lots of friction

Inbound:
Person → sees your work → trusts you → contacts you → discusses project → collaborate
          ↑ much less friction
```

The important distinction is that **you cannot eliminate sales; you can move the sales effort earlier and make it mostly implicit.**

For you, your existing technical work can become the "sales machine":

```text
Open source / AI experiments
        ↓
technical notes / demos
        ↓
people discover your capability
        ↓
"Can you build this for us?"
        ↓
paid project
        ↓
successful project
        ↓
referrals / reputation
        ↓
more inbound
```

That is much better than:

```text
"Hi, I'm an AI engineer. Do you have any projects?"
"Can I help you?"
"Please give me a chance."
```

And there is another important effect: **the power relationship changes.**

If someone comes to you with:

> "We have this software problem. Can you build it?"

you are no longer trying to convince them that AI/software development is valuable. **They already have a problem and have already identified you as someone worth talking to.**

Then your job becomes:

```text
understand problem
→ estimate
→ propose solution
→ execute extremely well
→ communicate clearly
→ deliver
```

That's a much easier collaboration problem.

But I wouldn't go all the way to **"just wait for people to find us."** Early on, you want a hybrid:

```text
                 ┌── inbound ──→ clients
Your work ───────┤
                 └── outbound ─→ distribution
```

Use outbound primarily to **increase the surface area for inbound**.

For example, instead of cold-selling an AI project:

```text
"Do you need an AI agent?"
```

publish:

```text
"I built an agent that turns X into Y."
"I benchmarked X vs Y."
"Here's how I reduced inference cost by 70%."
"Here's the architecture I used for a production agent."
"Here's a real failure and how I fixed it."
```

Now potential clients can self-select.

And this connects strongly to your idea of building an AI/software factory. The long-term asset isn't merely your ability to code. It's:

**capability + public evidence + distribution + trust.**

Once those compound, you can get into a very nice loop:

```text
ship
 ↓
show
 ↓
people trust
 ↓
people contact
 ↓
solve expensive problem
 ↓
case study
 ↓
more people trust
 ↓
more inbound
```

So yes: **"make ourselves easy to discover and easy to work with" is much more scalable than "constantly persuade people to work with us."**

The deepest point is probably:

> **Don't optimize only for finding opportunities. Build a system in which your work creates opportunities.**
