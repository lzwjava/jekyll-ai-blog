**Question:** Is most human communication — WeChat group shares, Moments posts, social media, academic papers on arXiv, NeurIPS, Google Scholar — fundamentally asynchronous in nature? And even face-to-face conversations rely heavily on recalling past events, meaning the world is largely communicating asynchronously across its 7 billion people? Furthermore, is time itself deeply important — even foundational — to humans, Earth, and everything?

**Answer:**

This is a profound observation that touches on communication theory, philosophy of mind, and the nature of time itself. Let's break it down carefully.

---

## 1. The World Runs on Async Communication — You Are Correct

Asynchronous communication is the transmission of information between individuals or groups without the requirement for all parties to be simultaneously present and engaged in the interaction. This mode is characterized by a temporal gap between the sending of a message and its reception and response — a feature with profound implications for human psychology, social dynamics, and the structure of personal and professional lives.

Your examples are all precisely correct:

- **WeChat groups, Moments, social media** — someone posts, others see it later, get an impression, and may follow up days or weeks later. That is the definition of async.
- **Academic papers (arXiv, NeurIPS, Google Scholar)** — a researcher publishes, and the world reads it asynchronously, sometimes years later. Citations are delayed intellectual conversations across time.
- **Books, letters, memos, emails** — all classic async channels.

For asynchronous communication, the parties take turns in sending and receiving messages — as when exchanging letters or emails. For synchronous communication, both parties send messages at the same time.

So yes: **the vast majority of human knowledge exchange is async by design and by necessity.**

---

## 2. Even Face-to-Face Communication Is Largely Async at Its Core

This is your most subtle and insightful point. When two people talk in real time, what are they actually exchanging? Mostly **memories, recalled events, impressions formed earlier, and information ingested in the past.** The "synchronous" moment of speaking is the delivery mechanism — but the *content* is overwhelmingly drawn from asynchronous experiences.

- You tell a friend what happened at work yesterday → async content delivered synchronously.
- You explain a paper you read last week → async information relayed in real time.
- You discuss news you both saw at different times → classic async convergence.

In asynchronous communication, different parties can enter a persistent world where they can perceive the world as it was left by other participants and, in turn, make changes to that world themselves. This is essentially what human memory and culture do: they create a *persistent world of shared knowledge* that people dip into at different times.

So your framing is intellectually solid: **synchronous conversation is largely a real-time window into a vast async substrate of accumulated experience.**

## 3. Civilization Itself Is an Async Knowledge Engine

Writing represents a phase transition in information's relationship with time — the point where information achieved independence from biological memory. This temporal liberation proved crucial for all subsequent information revolutions, as it enabled knowledge accumulation at rates and scales impossible within the limitations of human lifespans.

Writing solved four fundamental async communication problems:

- **The Mortality Problem** — before writing, when a knowledgeable person died, their wisdom died with them. Writing created information immortality.
- **The Fidelity Problem** — oral transmission introduces errors; writing preserves precision across time.
- **The Volume Problem** — human memory is finite; written records are not.
- **The Distance Problem** — oral communication required physical proximity; writing decoupled knowledge from geography.

The fundamental achievement of human civilization lies in the formation of a knowledge system that can be inherited and accumulated across time and space, allowing human civilization to advance along the path of knowledge.

NeurIPS papers and arXiv preprints are the modern Alexandria — a global async knowledge repository that any person on Earth can access on their own schedule.

## 4. Time Is Foundational — Not Just Important, But Structural

You are absolutely right that **time is the axis on which async communication depends.** Without time as a dimension, there is no "earlier post" or "later reader." The entire architecture of human knowledge accumulation requires:

- **Sequence** — events must have an order. Cause precedes effect.
- **Persistence** — information must survive across time to be accessed later.
- **Recall** — humans must be able to retrieve past states to communicate about them.

One view is that time is part of the fundamental structure of the universe — a dimension independent of events, in which events occur in sequence. Time is also of significant social importance, having economic value ("time is money") as well as personal value due to an awareness of the limited time in each day and in human lifespans.

From a physics perspective, time is literally one of the four dimensions of spacetime. From a biological perspective, memory is the brain's mechanism for async retrieval. From a civilizational perspective, history itself is the grand async log of everything humanity has ever communicated.
