Question: How can I overcome fear and anxiety about debt and mortgage, even when the amount (800K CNY) feels manageable but still overwhelming?

Answer:

Fear of debt is extremely common — even when the numbers are objectively manageable, the psychological weight can feel enormous. Here's how to work through it:

**Understand the Fear First**

Debt anxiety usually comes from one of these roots:

- Loss of control / uncertainty about the future
- Upbringing or cultural messaging that "debt = failure"
- Fear of worst-case scenarios (job loss, illness, market crash)
- Abstract numbers feeling unreal and therefore scary

**Reframe How You See the Mortgage**

- A mortgage on property is *leverage on an appreciating asset*, not the same as consumer debt. You're not "losing" money — you're converting rent payments into equity ownership.
- 800K CNY spread over 20–30 years is roughly **2,200–3,300 CNY/month**. Framed monthly, it becomes much less daunting.
- Banks wouldn't lend it to you if they didn't believe you could repay it. Their risk assessment is a form of external validation.

**Build a Psychological Safety Net**

- Keep 3–6 months of expenses as an emergency fund — this alone dramatically reduces anxiety because you know you have a buffer.
- Run a simple "worst case" scenario on paper. Write down what you'd actually do if income dropped. Having a *plan* kills abstract fear.
- Automate your mortgage payment so you never have to "decide" to pay it each month.

**Shift Your Relationship With Numbers**

- Track your net worth (assets minus liabilities), not just your debt. Watching it grow monthly is motivating.
- Celebrate each payment — every month you pay, your debt shrinks and your equity grows.

**Work on the Mindset Layer**

- Read or listen to stories of people who successfully paid off similar loans — normalization helps.
- Limit doom-scrolling about economic crashes and housing collapses. Curate your information diet.
- Talk to someone who has been through it — a trusted friend, family member, or financial advisor. Isolation amplifies fear.

**When to Seek More Help**

If the anxiety is affecting sleep, relationships, or daily functioning, consider speaking with a therapist or counselor. Financial anxiety is a recognized psychological pattern and responds well to CBT (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy).

The fear usually doesn't disappear immediately — it fades as you build a track record of successful monthly payments and watch your balance drop.
