Question: **Has the core of Hong Kong life stayed basically the same, even though the technology and products have changed?**

Answer: **Yes — your intuition is quite good.** The *surface layer* changed enormously; the underlying economic/social pattern has much more continuity.

Think of it as:

```text
1985 Hong Kong                 2026 Hong Kong
────────────────────────────────────────────────
Cantonese                     Cantonese
dense city                    dense city
small shops                   small shops / chains
wet markets                   wet markets
restaurants                   restaurants
property                      property
finance                       finance
trade                         trade
shipping                      shipping
international business        international business
MTR / buses                   MTR / buses
consumer culture              consumer culture
long working hours            long working hours
China ↔ world gateway         China ↔ world connection
```

The **objects** changed:

```text
CRT TV       → OLED TV
film camera → iPhone
Toyota      → EV
fax         → WhatsApp
bank branch → mobile banking
CD shop     → Spotify
newspaper   → online media
```

But the basic human loop is remarkably persistent:

```text
work → earn money → eat → shop → socialize
     → commute → rent/buy housing → raise family
     → try to improve your economic position
```

### But there is one *huge* difference

I wouldn't say the **business/economic core** is unchanged.

The *function* is similar, but the **economic engine underneath Hong Kong has changed substantially**.

In 1985, you could walk down a street and encounter a lot of businesses that were actually **making physical things** or directly facilitating trade:

> factory → wholesaler → exporter → shipping → overseas customer

Today it's much more:

> finance → property → professional services → retail → tourism → logistics → technology/services

Hong Kong manufacturing essentially moved across the border into the Pearl River Delta.

So there is an interesting illusion:

**The street still looks like a marketplace.**

But the marketplace is sitting on top of a completely different production system.

And that's actually why Hong Kong feels so continuous.

Hong Kong has always been less about *producing the physical object* and more about **moving money, people, goods and information efficiently**.

That's the deeper invariant.


So when you walk through Mong Kok today and see:

**restaurant → pharmacy → phone shop → clothing → market → bank → jewelry → convenience store**

it can feel almost like the 1990s.

The **technology of exchange** changed.

The **economic behavior of exchange** didn't.

That's why old Hong Kong footage can feel surprisingly familiar rather than like a completely alien historical period.
