Question: How does a Chinese national (international student) apply to college in the US in 2026?

Answer:

**1. Pick target schools (12-18 months out)**
No centralized system in the US — apply per-school, mostly via Common App or directly on the university's site. Filter by: SEVP-certified (required for F-1 eligibility), and whether they accept international financial aid.

**2. Standardized tests (9-12 months out)**
- Academic: SAT or ACT (many schools still test-optional post-COVID, check per-school policy)
- English: TOEFL or IELTS (waived if you did high school instruction in English)
- Grad school instead: GRE/GMAT/LSAT/MCAT depending on program

**3. Application materials**
- Transcripts + GPA conversion to 4.0 scale (schools often do this themselves, or use WES/ECE for credential evaluation)
- Personal essay (Common App essay + school-specific "Why us" essays — these matter as much as the main essay)
- 2-3 recommendation letters — give recommenders 4-6 weeks notice
- Application fee or fee waiver if low-income

**4. Financial aid**
FAFSA is US-citizens-only. International students use the **CSS Profile** for need-based aid at schools that offer it. Need-blind-for-internationals schools are rare (MIT, Harvard, Yale, Princeton, a handful of others) — check per-school.

**5. Admission → F-1 visa**
Once accepted: use the acceptance letter to apply for the F-1 student visa. School issues Form I-20 → pay SEVIS I-901 fee → schedule visa interview at US consulate → DS-160 form.

**⚠️ Watch for in 2026:**
- DHS has proposed replacing "Duration of Status" with a fixed admission period capped at 4 years, requiring extension applications for longer programs like a PhD, and the post-completion grace period may drop from 60 to 30 days — still proposed, not final law.
- Students from high-demand countries like India and Vietnam should expect interview wait times of 90+ days.
- Form DS-160 now requires disclosing all social media accounts used in the past five years, including inactive ones, and consular officers review your online presence. Don't lie on this form.
- Check current Presidential Proclamations — students from certain countries face processing pauses or restrictions.

**Practical for you specifically:** if this is grad school for CS/ML given your background (GPT-2 training, MI300X work), GRE is often waived now at many CS programs — check per-school, and your production ML/training experience + Fun Live traction is a stronger differentiator in an SOP than test scores.
