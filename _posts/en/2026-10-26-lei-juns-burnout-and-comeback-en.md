**Question:** Did Lei Jun experience burnout during his years working at Kingsoft?

**Answer:**

Yes — Lei Jun's burnout at Kingsoft is one of the most well-known chapters of his career story.

**The grueling years at Kingsoft (1992–2007)**

After joining Kingsoft as its sixth employee in 1992, Lei slept only four to five hours a day and was regarded as the "Exemplary Worker of Zhongguancun." He helped transform Kingsoft from a struggling company focused primarily on word-processing programs into a financially stable firm with products that also included video games and computer security software.

However, the road was brutal. He called the stretch before Kingsoft's 2007 IPO the most painful period of his life — a run of years where the company came close to folding more than once, and he carried that weight personally.

**Resignation due to "health reasons"**

In October 2007, Kingsoft Software was listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. In December, citing health reasons, Lei Jun stepped down as CEO of Kingsoft Software and entered the angel investment industry.

He later explained his departure: "I had given Kingsoft 16 years of my life. I was exhausted and needed to step back to reflect on what I truly wanted to accomplish." His wife also recalled that Lei was "completely consumed by his work at Kingsoft, often working until 3 or 4 in the morning."

**The recovery period (2008–2010)**

Lei Jun stepped down worn out, and has said since that he genuinely believed his best working years were behind him. He took a year mostly doing nothing, by his own account, before deciding that quitting the fight wasn't the same as being finished with building things.

By 2009, he had invested in over ten internet companies, gained a deep understanding of the internet, and achieved substantial returns — before going on to found Xiaomi in April 2010.

In short, the 16-year grind at Kingsoft — including near-bankruptcies, competition from Microsoft, and an exhausting push to the IPO finish line — drove Lei Jun to a genuine physical and mental breaking point, making his eventual comeback with Xiaomi all the more remarkable.
