Question: What startups or companies were created because of unfairness experienced by the founder?

Answer: Here are some of the most compelling examples, organized by the type of unfairness that sparked the company:

---

### 🥊 Workplace Sexism & Harassment

**Bumble (2014)**
Whitney Wolfe Herd was harassed and forced out of her co-founder role at Tinder — she alleged she was stripped of her co-founder title, excluded from meetings, and subjected to verbal abuse by executives. From the start, one of the driving forces behind Bumble's success has been its founder's dedication to creating a safer dating app for women, guided by her personal experience. In 2021, Wolfe Herd became the youngest female founder in history to take a company public.

---

### 🏠 Racial Discrimination

**Noirbnb (2016)**
The founders tried to book an Airbnb and were told the property wasn't available. A white friend requested the same dates and was approved right away. So they built a company in response. Both startups were riding a powerful wave of anger about racial discrimination in the home-sharing market — discrimination their founders claimed to have personally encountered.

---

### 👓 Overpriced & Unfair Markets

**Warby Parker (2010)**
Co-founders Neil Blumenthal, Andrew Hunt, David Gilboa, and Jeffrey Raider identified the high prices of prescription glasses as a pain point for many. They launched Warby Parker to sell stylish glasses directly to consumers at a fraction of the price of traditional retailers.

**Stripe (2010)**
Patrick and John Collison were frustrated that accepting money on the internet required a weeks-long process of talking to banks and submitting paper forms. They built Stripe to make internet payments simple.

---

### 💼 Gender Bias in Tech

**WORK180**
Founders Gemma Lloyd and Valeria Ignatieva started WORK180 after personally experiencing significant discrimination in the technology industry.

**digitalundivided**
Kathryn Finney believed her prior business accomplishments and experience would help her advance in the tech industry. She later realized that would not be the case. Her experience with racial and gender bias led her to create digitalundivided, a social enterprise that aims to bridge the digital information gap for women and minorities.

---

### 🚕 Everyday Frustration Turned Empire

**Uber (2009)**
The vision was born from a shared miserable experience — Camp and his friend Travis Kalanick were in Paris for a conference and couldn't get a cab on a cold night. That one frustrating night sparked a company now worth hundreds of billions.

---

### 🏷️ The Broader Pattern

Some of the most successful startups didn't start with a business plan. They started with a problem — more specifically, a personal pain point. You skip the focus groups, the theoretical personas and the assumptions. You already understand the problem deeply because you live it.

The common thread: **unfairness is a powerful fuel**. When founders feel wronged — by discrimination, bad systems, or being overlooked — that anger often becomes the most authentic motivation to build something better.
