Question: How does a tech company grow differently from a personal IP/brand?

Answer: Great distinction. They're fundamentally different models:

**Tech Company (Corp)**
- Grows by adding people — 10 → 100 → 1,000 → 10,000 employees
- More people = more capacity, more products, more revenue
- But also: more costs, more coordination overhead, more management complexity
- Growth is *linear to multiplicative* — depends on how well teams compound

**Personal IP / Brand**
- One person (or tiny team), but scales through *leverage*
- A blog post, YouTube video, book, course, or open-source repo reaches millions without hiring anyone
- Revenue comes from licensing, royalties, ads, audiences — not headcount
- Growth is *non-linear* — a single viral piece can outperform a 100-person company's marketing team

**The key insight:**
- A company grows by *hiring more people to do more things*
- A personal brand grows by *creating assets that work while you sleep*

**In the AI era, the gap is narrowing** — one person with Claude/AI tools can now do what previously required a team of 10–50. That's why you see tiny companies (like Cursor with ~150 people) generating billions in revenue.

So both models are valid — they just answer different questions: do you want to *build an organization*, or *build an audience and assets*?
