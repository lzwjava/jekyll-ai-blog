Question: What if you are bullied or unfairly treated at work — doing hard work but getting criticism, no reward, no promotion for years?

Answer:

This situation is common, especially in large companies, legacy teams, and contractor roles. The key is to separate **temporary unfairness** from a **broken system where your growth is blocked**.

A useful mental model:

```
Your career value = Skill growth × Visibility × Leverage × Market demand
```

Hard work alone is not enough.

Many engineers fall into this trap:

```
More work
    ↓
More responsibility
    ↓
More hidden tasks
    ↓
Manager thinks "he can handle it"
    ↓
No promotion, no raise
```

The company benefits from your reliability, but your career does not move.

## 1. First identify: bullying or bad incentives?

### Case A: Difficult manager but fixable

Signs:

* Criticism comes with specific feedback
* You get opportunities
* Manager advocates for you sometimes
* Promotion criteria are clear

Example:

> "Your design docs need improvement. Write two proposals this quarter."

This is painful but useful.

---

### Case B: Exploitation

Signs:

* You always receive urgent tasks
* Others get credit
* Your mistakes are highlighted, others ignored
* No clear promotion path
* Salary frozen for years
* You are "the guy who fixes everything"

This is not a performance problem. It is an incentive problem.

---

### Case C: Workplace bullying

Signs:

* Public humiliation
* Personal attacks
* Deliberate exclusion
* Threats
* Impossible deadlines designed to make you fail

Document everything.

Example:

```
Date:
Person:
Situation:
What was requested:
Impact:
Witness:
```

Do not rely on memory.

---

## 2. Stop playing the "hero engineer" game

A common mistake for strong engineers:

```
Problem appears
        |
        v
You solve it quickly
        |
        v
Nobody knows complexity
        |
        v
Next problem goes to you
```

Instead:

Make work visible.

Example:

Bad:

> Fixed production issue.

Good:

> Investigated payment timeout issue.
> Root cause: DB connection pool exhaustion.
> Reduced timeout errors from 3% → 0.1%.
> Added monitoring and runbook.

Same work. Different career value.

---

## 3. Build leverage outside your manager

Your manager is not the only market.

Create:

### Technical reputation

* GitHub projects
* Blog posts
* Open-source contributions
* Technical talks

For example:

You already have stronger evidence than many engineers:

* trained GPT models
* built AI agents
* worked on LLM infra
* wrote technical notes

That is career capital.

A bad manager can block internal promotion, but cannot erase external reputation.

---

## 4. Have a promotion conversation

Do not ask:

> "Why didn't you promote me?"

Ask:

> "What exact evidence is required for the next level?"

Force measurable criteria.

Example:

```
Current level:
Backend Engineer

Target:
Senior Engineer

Gap:
1. Architecture ownership
2. Mentoring
3. Business impact

Timeline:
6 months
```

If they cannot answer, that itself is information.

---

## 5. Set a deadline

A dangerous pattern:

```
Year 1: Maybe next year
Year 2: Need more time
Year 3: Budget issue
Year 4: Market bad
```

After 12-18 months without movement, assume the system will not change.

Your options:

```
A. Change team internally
B. Change company
C. Build your own income
D. Accept the tradeoff consciously
```

Do not stay passively.

A good engineer eventually learns:

> Companies reward the problems they can see, not the effort they cannot measure.

Your goal is not to "win" against a bad manager. Your goal is to become someone who has enough leverage that a bad environment cannot trap you.
