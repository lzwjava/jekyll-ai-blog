Question: What is "founder anxiety"?

Answer:

Founder anxiety refers to the chronic stress, worry, and psychological pressure experienced by startup founders and entrepreneurs as a result of the unique demands of building and running a company. It's not a clinical diagnosis, but a widely recognized phenomenon in the entrepreneurship community.

**Key characteristics include:**

- **Existential pressure** — the weight of knowing that thousands of decisions, large and small, could make or break the company
- **Financial stress** — personal financial risk, runway concerns, investor expectations, and payroll responsibilities
- **Impostor syndrome** — persistent self-doubt about whether one is truly capable of leading the company
- **Isolation** — founders often feel they can't fully share their fears with employees (who might panic), investors (who might lose confidence), or family (who might not understand)
- **Always-on mentality** — difficulty switching off, since the company's fate feels constantly tied to one's personal effort
- **Identity fusion** — the startup becomes so intertwined with the founder's sense of self that any business setback feels like a personal failure

**Why it's particularly intense:**

Founders face an unusual combination of high stakes, high uncertainty, and high visibility — all at once. Unlike most professionals, they often carry the livelihoods of their team members on their shoulders, which amplifies the emotional burden significantly.

**Growing awareness:**

The mental health conversation in Silicon Valley and the broader startup world has grown considerably, with founders like Ben Horowitz (*The Hard Thing About Hard Things*) and others speaking openly about the psychological toll of entrepreneurship.
