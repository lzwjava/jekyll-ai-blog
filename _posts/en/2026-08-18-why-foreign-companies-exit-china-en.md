**Question:** Why are foreign companies like Microsoft, Citibank, and others laying off workers in China or reducing their presence there?

**Answer:**

There are several overlapping reasons driving this trend:

---

**1. US-China Geopolitical Tensions & Trade War**

About 40% of 130 US companies surveyed by the US-China Business Council reported being negatively affected by export controls. Fewer than half now plan to invest in China, down sharply from 80% just a year prior. Companies like IBM and Microsoft have had to comply with US restrictions that make it legally risky or impossible to continue certain R&D operations in China.

---

**2. Rising Local Competition**

Foreign firms are facing growing competition from Chinese rivals, and the share of surveyed companies reporting greater market share in China declined to just 18%. Chinese tech companies (Huawei, Alibaba, ByteDance, etc.) now dominate markets that foreign firms once held.

---

**3. China Is No Longer the Cheapest Manufacturing Base**

Ten years ago, China's labor, land, and supply-chain advantages were unrivaled. Today, Vietnamese workers earn less than RMB 2,000/month, while factory wages in China's Yangtze River Delta exceed RMB 6,000 — squeezing margins in low-profit industries. Thailand and Mexico also enjoy tariff advantages for US exports.

---

**4. Political Risk, Data Security & Rule-of-Law Concerns**

Political tensions, cybersecurity concerns, and rule-of-law concerns are increasingly driving exit decisions. For financial firms like Citibank, operating under China's data localization and security laws creates compliance conflicts with their home-country regulations.

---

**5. Shrinking Market Opportunity**

Honda closed gasoline-vehicle plants, Mitsubishi fully exited vehicle production, Samsung suspended its Huizhou factory, and Sony formally exited the mainland smartphone market. Slowing Chinese consumer demand — caused partly by the property market collapse — is reducing the attractiveness of the market.

---

**6. AI Replacing Headcount**

AI adoption has emerged as a key driver of job cuts. Alibaba's workforce fell from 254,941 in 2022 to 204,891 in 2024, as AI takes over labor-intensive tasks — mirroring what Amazon and Google have done globally.

---

**7. Supply Chain "China+1" Strategy**

Companies are diversifying production into Vietnam, India, Indonesia, and Mexico to reduce dependency on China. India's electronics exports have tripled since 2018, and J.P. Morgan estimated a quarter of all iPhones would be made in India by 2025.

---

In short, the combination of **geopolitics, cost increases, fierce local competition, regulatory risk, and slower growth** has ended the decades-long era when China was the obvious choice for foreign companies.
