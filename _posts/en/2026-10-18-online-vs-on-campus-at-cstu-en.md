## 5. What I would do in your position

My decision process would be:

**Step 1: Contact CSTU and request a formal evaluation of your academic history.**

Explain that you have incomplete undergraduate education, extensive professional engineering experience, and self-study coursework. Ask whether you qualify for the BSCSE and what transfer credits you might receive.

**Step 2: Get the actual cost of completing the degree.**

Don't compare online and onsite study using tuition alone. Calculate:

\\[
C_{\text{online}} = T + F
\\]

\\[
C_{\text{onsite}} = T + F + L + R + O
\\]

Where:
- \\(T\\) = tuition
- \\(F\\) = fees and books
- \\(L\\) = additional living expenses
- \\(R\\) = relocation and travel
- \\(O\\) = other costs, including any income lost while studying

Ask for the total cost based on your transfer credits, not just the advertised annual price.

**Step 3: Verify the outcomes that matter.**

Ask CSTU:
- Can I complete the entire BSCSE online from outside the US?
- Can my existing coursework count toward the degree?
- What is the expected graduation date and total tuition after transfer credits?
- What proportion of graduates complete the program and obtain relevant employment?
- Which specific degree programs are currently eligible for STEM OPT, and what are the applicable conditions for international students?

**Step 4: Choose based on your primary objective.**

If your priority is building a profitable AI company, online study is the more natural fit.

If your priority is establishing yourself in the US, compare CSTU onsite with other eligible universities before committing. Don't choose a school solely because it is located in Silicon Valley.

If your priority is simply obtaining a recognized bachelor's degree as efficiently as possible, compare CSTU's total cost and completion time with the Chinese bachelor's route you have already started.
