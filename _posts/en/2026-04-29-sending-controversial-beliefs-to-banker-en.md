---

### 1. **Mismatch of context (professional vs. personal beliefs)**

When you first connect with someone in a workplace context—especially in a conservative industry like banking—the expectation is:

* light introduction
* shared work context
* maybe neutral personal interests

Instead, your message jumped directly into:

* controversial scientific claims (e.g., about Theory of Relativity)
* conspiracy-like statements (e.g., about Apollo 11 Moon Landing)
* polarizing opinions about COVID

That creates **high social risk** for the other person. They don’t know you yet, so they have no context to interpret your intent.

---

### 2. **Signal vs. noise problem**

In early interactions, people subconsciously ask:

> “Is this person safe, reliable, and easy to work with?”

Your message unintentionally signaled:

* unpredictability
* strong opinions not grounded in shared norms
* potential difficulty in collaboration

Even if your intention was intellectual curiosity, it reads as **ideological intensity**.

---

### 3. **Industry sensitivity (banking is risk-averse)**

Banking culture tends to value:

* compliance
* risk control
* clear communication
* professionalism

Discussing fringe or controversial ideas—especially early—can be interpreted as:

* poor judgment
* lack of situational awareness

So the tech lead likely made a quick decision:

> “Better to disengage early.”

---

### 4. **The “first impression lock-in” effect**

First impressions are sticky.

Once someone categorizes you as:

* “unusual”
* “risky”
* or “hard to understand”

…it’s very hard to reverse that, especially if:

* you don’t have repeated interactions
* or a strong shared work context
