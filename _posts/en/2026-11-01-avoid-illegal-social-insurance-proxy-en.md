## ⚠️ 1. Legal Boundary — The Most Important Thing

This is the biggest risk area. There are **two very different scenarios**:

**✅ Legal use of an HR agency:**
According to social insurance regulations (effective September 2025), the only legal scenario for third-party social insurance handling is: the employer has a genuine employment relationship with the worker (signed formal labor contract), contributions are based on actual salary, and the agency acts merely as an administrative conduit — not a "virtual employer."

**❌ Illegal "proxy payment" (代缴挂靠):**
Strictly prohibited situations include: fabricating a labor relationship to pay social insurance through a third-party agency (even if the individual pays the full amount themselves); flexible/self-employed workers commissioning a non-employer to pay their social insurance; and companies routing employee social insurance through third parties to evade employment responsibilities — violations carry fines of 1–3x the unpaid amount under Article 84 of the Social Insurance Law.

So: using 51社保 to **administer** your real employees' social insurance = fine. Using it to pay social insurance for someone with no actual employment relationship = illegal.

## 🔍 2. Check Their Credentials

When choosing a service provider, make sure they hold a **《人力资源服务许可证》** (HR Service License) and/or **《劳务派遣经营许可证》** (Labor Dispatch License), and sign a formal written service agreement.

Ask specifically:
- Do they have a Guangzhou local operating license?
- Are contributions paid directly to the social insurance bureau under your company's name, or under theirs?
- Can you verify payments on the government portal (广东政务服务网)?
