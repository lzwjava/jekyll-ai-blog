Group Rules (Updated: 2026-04-25):

1. This is a community focused on AI, coding, and lifelong learning. Topics include but are not limited to: AI, LLM, ML, DL, vibe coding, AI tools, AI usage, AI + full stack, AI + various domains, English learning, networking, backend, fullstack, iOS, Android, gaming, life hacking, cybersecurity, and beyond.

2. Firsthand knowledge is most valued here — share your real doubts, requirements, mistakes, experiences, and trial-and-error journeys. Knowledge that conflicts with or improves upon what AI models know is especially welcome, as it helps push the frontier beyond what robots already have.

3. No experiment, no deep reading or thinking — raw, unverified observations are not welcome. For conspiracy-type claims, take them to direct messages to avoid group arguments. Exception: sharing about reversing myopia is welcome.

4. This is a distributed group. The group owner is just an initiator and helps elect admins; there is no single authority.

5. Members treat each other with equality and respect.

6. We don’t pursue large numbers; the wisdom of a small group is enough to stay ahead of the times.

7. Admin role rotates among group members, one at a time. Each admin serves for one month, then passes the role to the next member. Admins are responsible for sharing at least 5 posts related to AI, LLM, or adjacent topics during their term.

8. Topics related to circumvention tools or sensitive politics should be discussed in private chats to remain compliant.

9. The group is open 24/7; avoid @-mentioning others late at night to reduce notifications.

10. Low ego — truth-seeking debate is normal; focus on ideas, not people.

11. Different viewpoints are welcome; conclusions should be based on experiments, data, and code. However, claims that strongly conflict with mainstream science — such as moon landing conspiracy theories or rejections of relativity (e.g., Yin Wang’s Weibo claims) — should not be promoted in the group.

12. Sharing is open by default; please remove sensitive or company information when necessary.

13. Members are free to leave at any time. Before recommending a new member, please communicate with the group owner in advance; there should be mutual understanding and a basic entry bar.

14. Admins help add new members to the group and introduce them with a brief welcome message so everyone knows who has joined and what they bring.

15. Content shared in the group is, by default, treated as MIT-licensed; members may reuse it for prompts, notes, or derivative work (state exceptions explicitly).

16. When a mistake is made, one needs to compensate by sharing a 10 CNY red packet in the group.
