Question: How does the Xianyu OpenClaw installation service work, where engineers and technologists post ads to help with installation, charging around 200–300 CNY or more?

Answer:

## The Xianyu OpenClaw Installation Service Economy

### What is OpenClaw?

OpenClaw (nicknamed "lobster" 🦞 by Chinese users, due to its claw-shaped logo) is an open-source AI agent framework created by Austrian developer Peter Steinberger in November 2025. Unlike simple chatbots, OpenClaw can actually perform tasks on behalf of the user — such as making restaurant reservations, monitoring investment portfolios, and sending messages automatically. It has become a viral tech phenomenon in China.

---

### Why Is There a Service Market for Installation?

OpenClaw has a certain learning curve in terms of installation and usage, which is why programmers have become the main force for "on-site installation." Non-technical users — including elderly people, housewives, students, and office workers — want to use it but simply cannot set it up themselves.

---

### How the Xianyu Installation Service Works

**Where listings appear:**

Searching for "Lobster/OpenClaw on-site installation" on platforms like Xiaohongshu and Xianyu reveals that this service has established a market price. Xianyu is Alibaba's secondhand marketplace, functioning somewhat like China's Craigslist/eBay hybrid, and it became one of the primary platforms for these gig-style listings.

**Two main service tiers:**

1. **Remote Installation (cheaper):** Remote installation is significantly cheaper, priced around 50–100 yuan. The technician connects to your computer via screen-sharing software and sets everything up for you.

2. **On-Site / In-Person Installation (more expensive):** On-site installation services range from 300 to 800 yuan, with 500 yuan per session being the most common price. The technician physically comes to your home or office.

**What you get for the price:**

The "on-site" service typically includes: OpenClaw installation, model debugging, and hands-on training, with the entire process taking more than 2 hours.

**Extra perks to stand out from competitors:**

Some advertise "guaranteed to teach and learn," others offer "free 24/7 problem solving," and some even offer "one free cooking service, including cooking common dishes" in addition to "on-site deployment" — an amusing sign of how competitive this gig economy has become.

---

### Who Are the Providers?

Like Feng, most providers of these services are early adopters with some technical ability who are looking for a side gig. But as demand has surged, some have found themselves overwhelmed.

Besides individual business owners, many "brokers" have also started a business connecting customers with "on-site installation" services. Some organizations previously engaged in computer repair and network maintenance have begun recruiting "on-site installation personnel" on social media, gathering them in community groups for unified order dispatch.

---

### How Big Is This Market?

On Taobao and Xianyu, paid installation services appeared overnight. The top-selling Taobao store logged over 1,000 orders.

In one RedNote post, a user said some installers had earned as much as $36,000 in just a few days helping others set up and configure the software.

---

### The "Uninstall" Side Economy

Interestingly, once Chinese authorities began restricting OpenClaw use in government and banks, a new service emerged: **paid uninstallation**.

Business Insider found numerous listings offering paid OpenClaw uninstall services on Xianyu, with sellers charging about 299 yuan ($44), some going up to $87. Some promise to remove leftover files and viruses, while others offer in-home services for a higher fee.

As one Chinese netizen summarized it: "Loading lobsters costs 599, unloading them costs 299" — and the person offering those services is "making a killing."
