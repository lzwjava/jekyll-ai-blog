Question: What are the top places to visit in Beijing, and how to plan a 7-day trip?

Answer:

Beijing offers an incredible mix of ancient history, imperial grandeur, modern landmarks, and vibrant culture. Here are some of the **top places to visit** in Beijing (based on consistent popularity and recommendations in recent travel guides):

- **Great Wall of China** (especially Mutianyu section – less crowded, with cable car and toboggan options)
- **Forbidden City** (Palace Museum – the heart of imperial China)
- **Tiananmen Square** (world's largest city square, with surrounding historical sites)
- **Summer Palace** (stunning royal garden and lake)
- **Temple of Heaven** (beautiful park with iconic architecture)
- **Hutongs** (traditional alleyways, e.g., Nanluoguxiang or Houhai area for local life and rickshaw tours)
- **Lama Temple** (Yonghe Temple – active Buddhist temple)
- **Olympic Park** (Bird's Nest Stadium and Water Cube from 2008 Olympics)
- **Beihai Park** or **Jingshan Park** (for panoramic views and relaxation)
- **Wangfujing Street** (famous for street food and shopping)

### Suggested 7-Day Beijing Itinerary

This balanced plan assumes you arrive on Day 1 and depart on Day 7 or after. It prioritizes major sites, includes rest, and accounts for travel time (use metro, Didi rides, or guided tours). Book tickets in advance for Forbidden City and Great Wall to avoid long lines.

**Day 1: Arrival & Central Beijing Introduction**

- Arrive and settle into your hotel (central areas like Dongcheng or near Wangfujing recommended).
- Afternoon/evening: Explore **Tiananmen Square** (free entry), walk around, and see the area at night.
- Stroll **Wangfujing Street** for street food (try jianbing crepes or Peking duck).
- Relax and adjust to jet lag.

**Day 2: Forbidden City & Surroundings**

- Morning: Visit the **Forbidden City** (Palace Museum) – arrive early (opens ~8:30 AM). Spend 3–4 hours exploring the massive complex.
- Afternoon: Climb **Jingshan Park** for panoramic views over the Forbidden City.
- Evening: Wander nearby **hutongs** or enjoy dinner (Peking duck is a must-try in Beijing).

**Day 3: Temple of Heaven & Summer Palace**

- Morning: **Temple of Heaven** – beautiful halls, gardens, and local activities (tai chi, etc.).
- Afternoon: Head to **Summer Palace** – enjoy Kunming Lake, Longevity Hill, and gardens (boat ride if time allows).
- Evening: Relax or explore a nearby area.

**Day 4: Great Wall Day Trip**

- Full day: Visit **Mutianyu Great Wall** (recommended over Badaling for fewer crowds and better scenery).
  - Take a tour bus or private transfer (~1.5–2 hours each way).
  - Hike, use cable car up/toboggan down.
- Return to Beijing in late afternoon; rest or light evening activity.

**Day 5: Cultural & Spiritual Sites**

- Morning: **Lama Temple** (Yonghe Temple) – explore vibrant Buddhist halls.
- Afternoon: Nearby **Confucius Temple** or **hutong tour** (rickshaw ride through traditional neighborhoods).
- Evening: Head to **Houhai Lake** or **Shichahai** area for bars, lakeside walks, and nightlife.

**Day 6: Modern Beijing & Olympic Sites**

- Morning: Visit **Olympic Park** – see the Bird's Nest (National Stadium) and Water Cube (from outside or enter if interested).
- Afternoon: Optional – **National Museum of China** (near Tiananmen) for history/art, or shopping at **Silk Market** / **Panjiayuan Antique Market**.
- Evening: Enjoy local food or a show (acrobatics/Kung Fu if interested).

**Day 7: Flexible Day & Departure**

- Morning: Free time – revisit a favorite spot, shop for souvenirs, or try a cooking class/hot pot experience.
- Afternoon: Depending on flight time, visit any missed site (e.g., Beihai Park) or relax.
- Depart Beijing.

**Tips for Your Trip**

- Best time: Spring (April–May) or autumn (September–October) for pleasant weather.
- Transport: Beijing metro is efficient and cheap; get a transport card. Use apps like Didi for taxis.
- Food: Must-tries include Peking duck, dumplings, hot pot, and street snacks.
- Tickets & crowds: Book major sites online in advance. Early mornings help avoid crowds.
- Visa/entry: Check current China entry requirements as of 2026.

This itinerary covers Beijing's highlights without rushing too much, blending history, nature, and modern vibes.
