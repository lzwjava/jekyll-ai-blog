Question:
How can I introduce Scratch to my 5-year-old daughter in a way that uses interest-driven learning (e.g., watching 1–2 tutorials first, then letting her create her own ideas), especially after setting up Linux Mint, Scratch Desktop, and YouTube access?

Answer:

Your friend’s idea is basically aligned with how young children actually learn well: **short exposure → curiosity → hands-on play → guided support when stuck**. For a 5-year-old, the key is not “teaching Scratch systematically”, but **making it feel like play where she is in control**.

Here’s a practical way to structure it based on what you already set up (Linux Mint + Scratch Desktop + YouTube):

---

## 1. Start with “very small inspiration”, not a course

Don’t treat it like lessons.

Instead:

* Watch **1 short Scratch animation (2–5 min)** together
* Or 1 very simple tutorial (like “move cat with arrow keys”)

Then stop immediately.

At this age:

> One idea is enough. More videos = passive consumption, not creation.

---

## 2. Ask one open question (this is important)

After watching:

* “你想做一个什么？（What do you want to make?）”
* Or even simpler: “小猫要做什么？跳？跑？说话？”

If she doesn’t know, you can give 2–3 choices:

* “跑来跑去的小猫”
* “会说话的小猫”
* “躲猫猫游戏”

This avoids overwhelming her with infinite possibilities.

---

## 3. You become “button helper”, not instructor

At age 5, the best role is:

* She decides what she wants
* You help click/drag when needed
* She observes cause → effect

Avoid:

* Explaining menus
* Teaching programming concepts

Instead:

* “试试这个块拖到这里看看发生什么”

---

## 4. Keep sessions extremely short (10–20 min)

Even if she is engaged, stop while it’s still fun.

Better pattern:

* 10–15 min Scratch
* Stop → leave it “unfinished interesting thing”

This creates natural anticipation.

---

## 5. Build from “copy → modify → create”

Good progression:

### Stage 1: Copy

* Make cat move
* Make it say something

### Stage 2: Modify

* Change speed
* Change text
* Add sound

### Stage 3: Create

* “Make your own mini game”

This is exactly how Scratch learning naturally works.

---

## 6. Use Scratch as storytelling, not programming

For a 5-year-old:

Scratch is closer to:

* Toy animation studio
* Cartoon maker
* Digital Lego

Not “coding”

So focus on:

* characters
* stories
* funny effects

---

## 8. One important warning (based on your setup)

Since you mentioned YouTube access + proxy:

Be careful that:

* YouTube doesn’t become passive scrolling
* Scratch time doesn’t turn into “watching tutorials only”

Rule of thumb:

> 80% doing, 20% watching
