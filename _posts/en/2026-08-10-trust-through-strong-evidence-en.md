Question: Is it true that most relationships only need one strong meeting, or that repeated meetings lose value if trust is not built?

Answer:

Yes, there is a lot of truth in this, especially for **high-value relationships** (hiring, investment, recommendation, partnerships). The key factor is not the number of meetings; it is whether the other person updates their **belief about you**.

A simple model:

```
Trust = f(competence, integrity, reliability, shared history, evidence)
```

Meeting someone 10 times does not automatically increase trust:

```
10 × casual meetings ≠ trust
```

But one strong interaction can create a large update:

```
1 × strong evidence → big trust jump
```

Examples:

* You show a working product.
* You solve a hard technical problem.
* Someone sees you handle pressure.
* A respected person recommends you.
* You deliver something promised.

Those create **evidence**, not just familiarity.

---

For jobs:

A hiring manager usually does not think:

> "I met this person five times, so I trust him."

They think:

> "I have enough evidence that this person can solve my problems."

This is why strong engineers can get opportunities quickly after one technical discussion, while others can meet many times without progress.

---

For investment:

Investors often spend hours with founders, but the important part is not the hours. It is:

* Is this person unusually capable?
* Do they understand reality?
* Do they execute?
* Do they have a track record?
* Do other smart people trust them?

A founder with previous execution history can get trust faster.

Regarding Lei Jun hiring people: stories about him personally persuading key employees for hours show another mechanism.

For extremely valuable people, sometimes the problem is not trust but **alignment**:

```
Person A:
"I know you are capable."

Person B:
"I know you are capable."

But:
"Why should we build this together?"
```

The long conversation is to convince them about:

* mission
* timing
* upside
* mutual respect
* future possibility

It is less "building basic trust" and more "making both sides commit."

---

A useful framework:

### Low-value relationship

Many small interactions matter.

Example:

* casual friends
* community members
* networking

Frequency helps.

---

### High-value relationship

One or two deep interactions matter.

Example:

* hiring
* investment
* co-founder
* recommendation

Signal quality matters.

---

Also, time gaps can sometimes increase value.

If you meet someone after 3 years and you have:

* shipped products
* grown skills
* built companies
* achieved something

then the second meeting is powerful because they see **trajectory**.

The opposite is also true:

If every meeting gives no new evidence, repeated contact can even reduce interest.

A simple equation:

```
Relationship value ≈ Trust × Evidence × Time consistency

not:

Relationship value ≈ Number of meetings
```

For builders (especially entrepreneurs and engineers), the highest-leverage networking strategy is usually:

```
Build → publish → create evidence → reconnect
```

rather than:

```
Meet many people → hope they remember you
```

The work itself becomes the trust-building mechanism.
