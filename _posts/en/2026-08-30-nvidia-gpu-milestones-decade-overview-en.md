**Question:** What are the biggest, most powerful Nvidia GPU milestones over the last decade (and beyond)?

**Answer:**

Here's a chronological tour of the landmark Nvidia GPUs — from gaming dominance to AI supercomputing:

---

**🎮 GTX 980 (2014) — Maxwell Architecture**
The flagship GTX 980 wiped the floor with both AMD's R9 290X and Nvidia's own previous-gen GTX 780 Ti. Maxwell brought massive power-efficiency improvements.

---

**🎮 GTX 1080 / 1080 Ti (2016–2017) — Pascal Architecture**
The 16nm Pascal architecture was well ahead of the competition, so much so that GeForce GTX 10 cards remained in contention for several years — almost an anomaly in the graphics market. The GTX 1080 Ti delivered yet another 30% performance improvement over the 1080, further cementing Nvidia's dominance of the high-end market.

---

**✨ RTX 2080 (2018) — Turing Architecture**
The Turing-powered RTX 20-series implemented real-time hardware ray tracing for the first time in a consumer-oriented video card. A historic first, even if adoption was initially slow.

---

**🚀 RTX 3080 / A100 (2020) — Ampere Architecture**
The RTX 3080 delivered a sizeable 30% performance bump at 4K over the RTX 2080 Ti. On the data center side, Ampere brought dramatic scaling and sparsity improvements to AI workloads.

---

**🤖 H100 (2022) — Hopper Architecture**
Volta introduced Tensor Cores in 2017; Hopper and Blackwell expanded transformer acceleration and rack-scale computing. The H100 became the backbone of modern AI infrastructure, powering ChatGPT-scale training. The H100 features 80 GB HBM3 and 3.35 TB/s memory bandwidth.

---

**🧠 H200 (2024) — Hopper Refresh**
The H200 improves on the H100 by using newer HBM3e memory and increasing total VRAM to 141 GB, enabling larger batch sizes and higher throughput during inference. It delivers ~241.3 TFLOPS FP16 and ~4.89 TB/s memory bandwidth.

---

**⚡ B200 / GB200 NVL72 (2024–2025) — Blackwell Architecture**
The current reigning champion. The B200 uses a dual-die chiplet design with 208 billion transistors on TSMC 4NP, delivering 20 petaFLOPS of FP4 AI performance and 192 GB of HBM3e memory per GPU. The GB200 NVL72 pools 72 B200s into one 13.4 TB liquid-cooled rack built for trillion-parameter models. The B200 delivers 2.3× the compute and 1.7× the memory bandwidth of the H200.
