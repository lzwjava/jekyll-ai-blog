Question: What is infrared (红外线)?

Answer: Infrared (红外线) is a type of electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths longer than visible red light but shorter than microwaves — roughly 700 nanometers to 1 millimeter. It sits just beyond the red end of the visible light spectrum, which is why it's called "infrared" (meaning "below red").

Key points:

- **Invisible to the human eye**, but we perceive it as **heat**
- Emitted by all objects with a temperature above absolute zero
- Divided into near-infrared (NIR), mid-infrared (MIR), and far-infrared (FIR)

**Common applications:**
- Thermal imaging cameras
- TV/device remote controls
- Night-vision equipment
- Medical therapy (heat lamps)
- Astronomy and satellite sensing
- Fiber-optic communications
