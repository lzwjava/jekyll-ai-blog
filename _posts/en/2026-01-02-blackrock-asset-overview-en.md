# BlackRock: A Comprehensive Introduction

BlackRock, Inc. is the world's largest asset management company, headquartered in New York City. As of the third quarter of 2025, it manages approximately **$13.5 trillion** in assets under management (AUM), making it a dominant force in global finance.

## History and Founding

BlackRock was founded in 1988 by eight partners, including Larry Fink (current Chairman and CEO), Robert S. Kapito (President), Susan Wagner, and Barbara Novick. It began as part of the Blackstone Group, focusing on risk management and fixed-income institutional asset management. The founders aimed to prioritize client needs through innovative risk assessment.

In 1992, it separated and adopted the name BlackRock. It went public on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE: BLK) in 1999. Key growth milestones include:

- Merger with Merrill Lynch Investment Managers in 2006, expanding its reach.
- Acquisition of Barclays Global Investors in 2009, including the iShares ETF platform, which propelled BlackRock to the top of the industry.

Over the years, BlackRock has grown through organic expansion and strategic acquisitions, evolving from a fixed-income specialist to a comprehensive asset manager.

## Business Operations

BlackRock provides investment management, risk management, and advisory services to institutional and retail clients worldwide. Its key offerings include:

- **iShares ETFs**: The world's largest ETF provider, with significant assets in index-tracking funds.
- **Aladdin Platform**: Proprietary technology for portfolio management, risk analysis, and trading, used internally and licensed to other institutions.
- Diverse asset classes: Equity, fixed income, multi-asset, alternatives, cash management, and private markets.

The firm operates in over 30 countries with offices in 70 locations, serving clients in 100 countries. It emphasizes fiduciary responsibility, helping clients (including many retirement savers) achieve financial well-being.

## Leadership and Size

- **Larry Fink**: Chairman and CEO, a prominent voice on topics like sustainable investing.
- **Robert S. Kapito**: President.
BlackRock employs over 21,000 people globally. Its scale gives it significant influence through proxy voting on behalf of clients.

## Controversies and Criticisms

BlackRock has faced scrutiny on several fronts:

- **ESG Investing**: Promoted environmental, social, and governance factors, but criticized from both sides—accused of "woke capitalism" by conservatives (leading to divestments by states like Florida and Texas) and greenwashing by environmental groups for continued fossil fuel investments.
- **Investments**: Holdings in fossil fuels, arms manufacturers, and companies linked to deforestation or human rights issues have drawn criticism.
- **Influence**: Its vast AUM leads to perceptions of excessive corporate power, fueling conspiracy theories (often unfounded) about controlling global events or markets.

Despite these, BlackRock maintains it acts as a fiduciary for clients and has adjusted some ESG approaches in response to feedback.

BlackRock remains a pivotal player in shaping global investment trends, technology in finance, and debates on sustainable capitalism.
