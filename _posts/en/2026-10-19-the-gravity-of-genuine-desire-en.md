Question: How do you understand the idea that "what your heart does not truly desire (内心不渴望的东西) can never truly draw close to you / become part of you"?

Answer:

This is a profound insight that operates on several levels:

**1. The motivational gap**
When you don't genuinely want something deep down, you will unconsciously withhold the full energy needed to reach it. You go through the motions, but the commitment is hollow. The body moves toward it; the soul doesn't. Effort without heart is like rowing with one oar — you circle, but never arrive.

**2. The identity barrier**
"靠近自己" (drawing close to *yourself*) is the key. Even if you *obtain* something you don't truly want — a degree, a relationship, a title — it sits *beside* you, not *inside* you. It never integrates into who you are. You wear it like a costume, not a skin.

**3. Self-sabotage as honesty**
Psychologically, when the conscious mind pursues what the heart rejects, the unconscious finds ways to destroy it. Procrastination, forgetting, "bad luck," repeated mistakes — these are often the inner self saying *no* louder and louder.

**4. The reverse is also true (and more useful)**
The statement implies its opposite: **what you genuinely, deeply desire has a way of finding you** — because you notice opportunities others miss, you persist where others quit, and you shape your identity around it. Desire is a kind of gravity.

**Practical takeaway:**
Before chasing something, ask honestly: *Is this mine, or is this someone else's wish wearing my face?* The heart knows before the mind admits it. Authentic desire is a compass; borrowed desire is noise.
