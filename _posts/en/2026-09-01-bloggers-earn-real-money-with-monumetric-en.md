**Question:** Do people actually earn money from Monumetric as bloggers?

**Answer:**

Yes, bloggers do earn real money from Monumetric. Here's a breakdown of how it works and what to expect:

---

**What is Monumetric?**

Monumetric (formerly The Blogger Network) is an all-in-one display ad partner that automatically generates dynamic display ads based on your content and visitors' interests — covering in-article ads, dynamic video ads, and banners.

---

**Who qualifies?**

Monumetric has four program tiers based on monthly pageviews: **Propel** (10K–80K), **Ascend** (80K–500K), **Stratos** (500K–10M), and **Apollo** (10M+). So you need at least 10,000 monthly pageviews to get started.

---

**How much can you earn?**

Bloggers report earning anywhere between **$5 to $30 per 1,000 pageviews (RPM)** with Monumetric, though results vary depending on your niche and audience.

Real examples from bloggers:

- One blogger reported RPMs ranging between **$14 and $22** depending on the time of year.
- Another blogger reported an RPM of **$7.8**, earning **$333 for ~50,000 pageviews**.
- Some bloggers have reported a **400% increase in revenue** after switching to Monumetric from self-service platforms.
- Some publishers have reported even more dramatic gains — one noted a 10× RPM increase, another shared a 700% revenue jump in the first month.

---

**Revenue model**

Monumetric supports two revenue models — **pay-per-click (PPC)** and **pay-per-view (PPV)** — so bloggers earn both when visitors view ads and when they click them.

---

**Things to watch out for**

Payment is on **net-60 terms**, meaning you get paid 60 days after the month ends — much slower than AdSense (21 days) or Ezoic (26 days). There are also reports of less publisher control over ad placements compared to other networks.

---

**Bottom line:** Monumetric is a legitimate and popular choice for mid-level bloggers (especially those with 10K–80K monthly pageviews) who want to earn more than AdSense offers, but it's not instant money — your traffic volume and niche heavily impact earnings.
