Question: What does "chronic" mean?

Answer: 

**Chronic** means **long-lasting, persistent, or continuing for a long time** (usually months or years), often used in these main contexts:

1. **Medical/health context** (most common usage)  
   A **chronic** condition is a disease or health problem that  
   - lasts a long time (typically 3 months or more)  
   - often cannot be completely cured  
   - may slowly get worse over time or stay relatively stable  
   - usually requires long-term management  

   Examples:  
   - chronic pain  
   - chronic fatigue syndrome  
   - chronic kidney disease  
   - chronic asthma  
   - chronic depression  
   - chronic hepatitis  

   Opposite → **acute** (sudden, short-term, often severe but usually temporary)

2. **General / everyday language**  
   Something that continues for a very long time, keeps happening repeatedly, or is very habitual.  
   Examples:  
   - chronic lateness (= always late)  
   - chronic liar (= someone who lies very frequently)  
   - chronic underfunding of schools  
   - chronic traffic jams in the city  
   - chronic procrastination  

3. **Slang / informal usage** (especially in some English-speaking communities)  
   Sometimes used to mean "very bad", "extreme", or "intense" (often negative).  
   Examples:  
   - "He's got chronic body odor." (= very strong / terrible)  
   - "That was chronic service." (= really bad service)
