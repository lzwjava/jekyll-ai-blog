## Yin Wang (王垠) — Who He Is

Yin Wang graduated from Sichuan University's class of 1997, was admitted directly to Tsinghua University's computer science PhD program, and in 2005 was lead author on a paper that won Best Paper Award at ASP-DAC — the first time a paper from mainland China won that award. He later studied at Indiana University, Cornell, and MIT, and worked at companies like Google and Intel. His blog "Surely I Am Joking" at yinwang.org became a cult phenomenon in Chinese tech communities.

## Core Pillars of Yin Wang's Philosophy

### 1. Independent, Critical Thinking Over Dogma

His writing often challenges mainstream dogma in programming and academia. He prioritizes functional programming principles (e.g., Scheme/Lisp) over popular paradigms and dismisses hype in tech trends.

People who consistently question authority, challenge conventional wisdom, or point out flaws often do so because they have a deeper engagement with the subject. Their critical stance usually comes from analytical thinking — examining assumptions, logic, and evidence rather than accepting claims at face value.

### 2. Simplicity and Depth Over Surface Breadth

Yin Wang's emphasis is on self-directed clarity, without blindly following the crowd. His approach is a manifesto for real learning — intellectual independence and ruthless simplicity, cutting through noise like a laser.

Wang Yin strongly criticizes rote, exam-oriented education. He emphasizes deep understanding of fundamentals, learning through curiosity, beauty, and personal interest, and thinking independently rather than memorizing or doing endless practice problems.

### 3. Refine Relentlessly, Not Accumulate Blindly

Wang Yin argues that the most effective way to improve programming skill is to repeatedly revise and polish code, not just write more of it. He compares this to a writer who discards more than they publish — great programmers delete more code than they keep. You cannot write perfect code in one shot; insights come gradually over time.

### 4. Focused, Deep Thinking (One Problem at a Time)

Yin Wang described his mentor Daniel P. Friedman as someone who would fixate on one single difficult problem for an entire day (or even longer), turning it over in his mind continuously. Immersing deeply in one problem for a long time (without constant context switching) often produces faster real progress than doing many shallow things.

### 5. Reject Hype, Seek Real Value

Yin Wang consistently attacks hype and dishonesty in tech — including Silicon Valley startup culture, AI overreach, and credentialism. He prioritizes truth over social approval, which takes real courage. He emphasizes deep understanding over superficial tool-chasing, arguing that most programmers learn frameworks without understanding fundamentals.

### 6. Anti-Materialist / Anti-Grading-Culture

His core claim is that mainstream success is a conformist trap rooted in a lifelong "grading" culture, that it leads to servility to external validation, and that many celebrated tech entrepreneurs and companies are overhyped bubbles lacking real value.

### 7. The "Knowledge-First" Model

Yin Wang is a compelling example of the knowledge-first model: his blog consistently challenges mainstream programming dogmas and he has been teaching online computer science lessons and publishing long-form technical memos on Substack, covering topics from programming language research to AI cognition. Monetization becomes natural — courses, consulting, books, services follow naturally from trust.

## Applying Yin Wang's Philosophy to a Startup

| Yin Wang Principle | Startup Application |
| --- | --- |
| **Independent thinking** | Don't copy competitors blindly — find what's *actually* true about your market |
| **Reject hype** | Don't chase trend-driven ideas (AI for AI's sake); solve a real, verifiable pain |
| **Simplicity over complexity** | Build the simplest product that works; fight feature bloat |
| **Deep focus** | Spend sustained time on one core problem rather than spreading thin |
| **Refine relentlessly** | Iterate the product deeply, not just ship more features |
| **Truth over approval** | Listen to real user feedback, not validation-seeking metrics |
| **Knowledge-first trust** | Build credibility through transparency and genuine expertise |

### Key Tensions to Be Aware Of

By learning to separate Yin Wang's brilliant technical insights (like his work on compilers) from his personal grievances, you develop a mental filter. He is famous for debunking industry myths but is also known for highly subjective personal takes.

The most valuable thing to copy is not his conclusions — but his method: question everything, read original sources, build experiments, write clearly.

In short: Yin Wang's philosophy is best used as a **thinking discipline** for a founder — push past hype to find genuine problems, build with depth and simplicity, and earn trust through real expertise rather than marketing noise.
