Question:
Do we need to read important materials carefully (like frequent exam questions, interview topics, or work-related content)? Otherwise we forget, repeat mistakes, and in the long term this leads to low performance and low efficiency, so we cannot become the person we want to be — is that correct?

Answer:

Yes — your reasoning is correct. This is a classic **learning efficiency vs. shallow exposure** problem.

If important material is not studied carefully:

* You **recognize** it but do not **understand** it
* You **understand** it but do not **retain** it
* You **retain** it but cannot **apply** it
* You **apply** it incorrectly and repeat mistakes

This creates a loop:

Shallow reading → forgetting → mistakes → relearning → forgetting again → low efficiency

Over time this becomes **very expensive cognitively** and professionally. 📉

### Why careful reading matters

Important materials (exam questions, interview topics, work knowledge) usually have:

* High reuse frequency
* High impact on results
* Conceptual depth
* Hidden traps / edge cases

So each careless read causes **compound loss**:

* lose time now
* lose time relearning
* lose confidence
* reinforce wrong understanding

This is why careful study gives **compounding returns** 📈

### Long-term comparison

Careful learner:

* reads once slowly
* builds correct mental model
* remembers longer
* applies correctly
* improves continuously

Shallow learner:

* reads fast
* forgets fast
* repeats mistakes
* relearns repeatedly
* stagnates

After months or years, the gap becomes huge.

### The deeper truth

You're also pointing to something important:

> The result we get will not match who we want to become.

Because identity is shaped by **what we repeatedly do**:

* careless learning → careless thinking
* precise learning → precise thinking
* repeated mistakes → unstable performance
* deep understanding → reliable performance

So yes — **careful reading of important material is not just about memory, it's about shaping competence**.

### Best practical rule

Not everything needs deep reading. Only:

* frequent exam questions
* repeated mistakes
* core concepts
* interview fundamentals
* daily work knowledge

These must be studied **slowly and deliberately** 🎯

Everything else can be skimmed.

This is **high-performance learning strategy**:

* skim most things
* deeply learn the few important ones
* review mistakes
* build long-term memory

That leads to:

* higher efficiency
* fewer mistakes
* stable performance
* becoming the person you want to be
