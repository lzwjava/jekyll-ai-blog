**Question: What does 着想 mean, especially in some “金刚经” (Diamond Sutra) context?**

**Answer:**

**着想 (zhuó xiǎng)** means **to be attached to a thought / to cling to an idea / to take a concept as real**.

It comes from Buddhist terminology:

* **着 (zhuó)** = attach, stick to, cling to
* **想 (xiǎng)** = thought, perception, mental image, concept

So **着想** literally means:

> the mind becomes attached to a certain thought or mental image.

In the context of the **Diamond Sutra (金刚经)**, the related idea is:

> **“应无所住而生其心”**
> "One should give rise to the mind without abiding anywhere."

Meaning: the mind should function, but it should not become trapped by any particular concept.

Examples:

### 1. 着相 (zhuó xiàng) — more common in 金刚经

The Diamond Sutra often uses **着相**:

> “凡所有相，皆是虚妄。”

Meaning:

> All appearances/forms are ultimately empty; don't cling to them.

A person who **着相** sees a phenomenon and thinks:

* "This identity is absolutely me."
* "This success defines my value."
* "This failure destroys me."
* "This opinion must be true."

The problem is not seeing things. The problem is **attachment**.

---

### 2. 着想

Similar idea, but focusing on **thought** rather than external appearance.

Example:

普通想法:

> "I failed this interview."

着想:

> "I am a failure."

The first is a fact. The second is the mind attaching an identity/concept.

Another example:

普通想法:

> "This person criticized me."

着想:

> "This person hates me forever."

The mind adds a fixed story.

---

From a modern cognitive science perspective:

* Thought = a generated representation by the brain.
* 着想 = treating that representation as reality itself.

It is similar to:

```
thought -> belief -> identity -> attachment
```

The Buddhist practice is:

```
thought -> observe -> let go -> act clearly
```

So **着想 does not mean "don't think"**. It means:

> Think, but don't become imprisoned by your thoughts.

This is close to the modern idea of **cognitive defusion** in psychology: "I am having the thought that X" instead of "X is absolutely true."
