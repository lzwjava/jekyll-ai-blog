## Question: What does XTX Markets, the UK company, do?

**Answer: XTX Markets is a London-based quantitative trading firm that uses machine learning, mathematics, and massive computing infrastructure to make money in financial markets.**

Think of it as an AI company whose product is financial trading, rather than selling AI software to customers.

Its official website is [xtxmarkets.com](https://www.xtxmarkets.com/). [XTX Markets](https://about.xtxmarkets.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 1. What exactly does XTX Markets do?

XTX operates across three main areas.

**A. Algorithmic trading**

It uses mathematical models and machine learning to predict prices and execute trades across financial markets.

Its markets include:

- Foreign exchange (FX).
- Stocks and ETFs.
- Bonds and other fixed-income instruments.
- Commodities.
- Cryptocurrency markets.

According to its official website, XTX generates price forecasts for more than 53,000 financial instruments. [XTX Markets](https://www.xtxmarkets.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**B. Providing liquidity to financial institutions**

This is a particularly important part of its business.

Imagine a bank wants to exchange €10 million for US dollars.

The bank needs a counterparty willing to trade at a competitive price.

XTX can quote prices and execute the transaction, earning money from the spread between its buying and selling prices, while managing the risk of its positions.

It provides liquidity to institutional clients, including banks and brokers. Its business is not primarily selling trading software to retail investors. [XTX Markets](https://about.xtxmarkets.com/clients/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**C. Using AI to gain a trading advantage**

XTX uses machine learning to estimate the fair value of financial instruments and identify trading opportunities.

For example, suppose its models estimate that a currency pair should trade at 1.1002, while the market price is 1.1000.

That difference might represent a potential opportunity. However, the model must account for transaction costs, market movements, execution quality, and risk before the trade makes economic sense.

At scale, even tiny advantages can become valuable.

The core idea is:

\\[
\text{Profit} \approx \text{Trading volume}
\times \text{Net edge per trade}
\\]

This is an approximation, not a complete accounting identity. Real profitability also depends on inventory risk, hedging, operating costs, and losses.

Consider the engineering implications.

**1. Massive data pipelines**

The company needs to process market prices, order-book information, historical data, and other signals.

**2. Machine learning research**

Researchers build models that extract predictive signals from financial data.

**3. High-performance computing**

The company needs substantial computing resources for research, model training, experimentation, and potentially inference workloads.

**4. Low-latency systems**

Once a model identifies an opportunity, the trading infrastructure must execute orders efficiently. In competitive markets, milliseconds can matter.

**5. Production reliability**

A model that performs well in an experiment but fails under real market conditions is not useful. Data quality, risk management, execution, and monitoring are essential.

### 3. How does XTX make money compared with an AI consultancy?

The business models are fundamentally different.

| Dimension | XTX Markets | AI consultancy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | Trading and liquidity | Software and AI solutions |
| Customers | Banks, brokers, and institutional market participants | Businesses needing technical solutions |
| Main advantage | Predictive models, execution, data, and infrastructure | Engineering capability, domain knowledge, and delivery |
| Revenue source | Trading profits and market-making economics | Project fees, retainers, and software contracts |
| Scalability | Can increase trading activity without proportionally increasing headcount | Often requires additional engineers as projects grow |
| Main risks | Market losses, model degradation, execution, and regulation | Customer acquisition, delivery costs, and competition |

An AI consultancy typically earns money by helping customers solve problems.

XTX uses AI and computing to compete directly in financial markets.

**The important difference is that XTX monetizes its own technological advantage rather than primarily selling engineering labor.**

That distinction is worth understanding when designing an AI business.

### 5. My take: Why you should pay attention to XTX

Given your interests in AI infrastructure, GPU computing, and building an AI company, I would study XTX for one specific reason.

**It demonstrates how a relatively small technical organization can operate at enormous scale when its software creates measurable economic value.**

A conventional software services company might need hundreds of engineers to generate substantial revenue.

A quantitative trading firm can instead invest heavily in researchers, computing, data, and trading systems, then monetize the resulting advantage across enormous trading volumes.

But there is a catch: this model requires a difficult-to-replicate combination of financial expertise, proprietary data, research talent, execution infrastructure, and risk management.

You cannot reproduce XTX's economics merely by renting GPUs and training a model.

For your own company, the more practical lesson may be to move gradually from selling engineering services toward building reusable AI systems that customers pay to use.

For example:

1. Start with AI agent and software projects for paying customers.
2. Identify recurring problems across multiple clients.
3. Build reusable agents, tools, and infrastructure.
4. Turn the most valuable components into products or usage-based services.
5. Let software, rather than headcount alone, drive revenue growth.

That is a different business from quantitative trading, but it applies a similar principle: **use technical capabilities to create an advantage that scales beyond the number of hours you personally work.**
