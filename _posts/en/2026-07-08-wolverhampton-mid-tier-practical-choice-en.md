**University of Wolverhampton** — a public university in the West Midlands, England (main campus in Wolverhampton city, with additional sites in Walsall and Telford).

**Short answer: it's a mid-tier, non-selective UK university — decent for specific practical subjects, not a name that carries weight internationally.**

Key facts:

- **Global rankings**: QS World University Rankings 2027 places it at #1001-1200, and Times Higher Education 2026 has it at 801-1000. US News ranks it #1667 in Best Global Universities.
- **UK standing**: Roughly 85th in the UK by EduRank's metric — solidly in the bottom half of UK universities, well behind Russell Group schools (Birmingham, Warwick, etc. are ~20 mins away by train and rank far higher).
- **Where it's actually decent**: Subject-level rankings for business, computer science, and engineering are consistently stronger than the overall institutional number — Computer Science sits inside the UK top 100-110 on the Complete University Guide, backed by accredited modules in cybersecurity and AI. There's also a quirky historical highlight: its mathematicians (Statistical Cybermetrics Research Group) were ranked #1 in the world 2017-2019 in the Shanghai/ARWU ranking for citation impact of articles — a narrow, specific win, not a signal about the university as a whole.
- **Practical strengths**: ranked third in England for social mobility, with 87% graduate employability (2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey). It's a Bronze-rated institution under the UK's Teaching Excellence Framework — meaning "satisfactory," not "excellent," by that framework's own scale.
- **Fees**: notably cheaper than Russell Group peers for international/postgraduate study, which is its actual competitive angle rather than prestige.

**Bottom line**: if you're asking because of an admissions offer, exchange program, or hiring a grad from there — treat it as a low-cost, teaching-focused UK university with a genuinely solid CS/business subject program, but it won't open doors the way a Russell Group name would in a CV screen. If you're evaluating a specific course (e.g., CS, AI) rather than the brand name, that's the more meaningful comparison to make — the subject tables tell a different story than the institutional rank.
