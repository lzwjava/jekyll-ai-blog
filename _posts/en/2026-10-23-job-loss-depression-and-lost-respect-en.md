Question: When someone loses their job, falls into depression, and is under mortgage pressure, does their spouse often start to pull away or treat them poorly — and does the world in general stop respecting them?

Answer:

Yes — and it's one of the more painful, quietly understood truths about human society. What you're describing is real, and many people have lived it.

**On the spouse/partner side:**

A partner's frustration in this situation is extremely common. It doesn't always mean lack of love — it often comes from their own fear (about money, the future, stability), and that fear comes out sideways as irritability or coldness. But the effect on the person already struggling is devastating, because the one place they expected safety starts to feel like another front. It compounds everything.

Some partners grow closer under pressure. Many don't, especially when the financial stress drags on for months.

**On the "world stops respecting you" side:**

This is also real. Much of social status — quietly, uncomfortably — is tied to employment and financial stability. Invitations thin out. Conversations become awkward. Even friends don't know what to say, so they drift. The person who was confident at a dinner table starts to feel invisible. It's not always malicious; people are just uncomfortable around visible struggle.

**What's important to hold onto:**

- This dynamic is a reflection of social conditioning and fear — not your actual worth.
- The people who stay, or who treat you the same, are the ones worth knowing. Hard times are a filter, and the filter, while brutal, is honest.
- Depression distorts the lens further — it makes the coldness feel like total rejection when reality is more mixed.
- Men especially are socialized to derive identity from work, so job loss hits identity at the root. Recognizing that mechanism doesn't fix it, but it helps to name it.

If any part of this is personal — if you're in this situation right now — I'm genuinely glad to talk through it. You don't have to frame it as "one." It's okay to just say what's going on.
