Question: Is customer acquisition a key CEO responsibility, and did Jensen Huang actively reach out to partners to drive CUDA and GPU adoption?

Answer:

Yes on both counts.

**CEO as Chief Customer/Partner Officer**

Getting customers — and in B2B contexts, *partners and ecosystem players* — is arguably the most critical CEO job, especially in the early and growth stages. A CEO sets the vision, but without revenue and adoption, nothing else matters. Many great CEOs (Jobs, Ellison, Huang) were known to be deeply personally involved in major deals and partnerships.

**Jensen Huang's CUDA/GPU Evangelism**

Jensen Huang is a textbook example of a CEO-as-salesperson. His outreach was strategic and relentless:

- **Early CUDA push (mid-2000s):** When NVIDIA launched CUDA in 2006–2007, almost no one saw GPUs as general-purpose compute tools. Huang personally championed it to researchers, universities, and labs — before there was obvious money in it.

- **Scientific & HPC community:** He courted high-performance computing (HPC) researchers, national labs, and academics — seeding adoption in physics, chemistry, and biology simulations. This built a loyal developer base.

- **AI/Deep Learning moment:** When Geoffrey Hinton's team used NVIDIA GPUs to win ImageNet in 2012, Huang recognized it immediately and doubled down — personally engaging the AI research community.

- **Cloud & hyperscalers:** He built deep relationships with AWS, Google, Microsoft, and Meta to make NVIDIA GPUs the default AI compute infrastructure.

- **Automotive & robotics:** He personally drove partnerships with Toyota, Mercedes, and others for autonomous driving (DRIVE platform).

The result is that CUDA now has **millions of developers** and is nearly impossible to displace — a classic *platform lock-in* achieved largely through deliberate, CEO-led ecosystem building.

> Jensen Huang is often described as one of the best salespeople in tech history — not just a chip designer, but an ecosystem architect.
