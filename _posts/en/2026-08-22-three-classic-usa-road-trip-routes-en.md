**Question:** What are the typical driving routes for a cross-country road trip across the USA (East to West or West to East)?

**Answer:**

There are three classic highway corridors most drivers choose from:

---

### 🛣️ 1. Northern Route — ~2,800 miles
**I-90 / US-2**
New York → Chicago → Minneapolis → Billings → Spokane → Seattle

- Passes through the Great Lakes, Great Plains, and Pacific Northwest
- Highlights: Mount Rushmore, Glacier National Park, Spokane
- Best season: Late spring to early fall (avoid winter snow)

---

### 🛣️ 2. Central Route — ~2,800 miles (Most Popular)
**I-70 / Historic Route 66**
Washington D.C. → St. Louis → Kansas City → Denver → Salt Lake City → Las Vegas → Los Angeles

- The classic American road trip corridor
- Highlights: Gateway Arch, Rocky Mountains, Arches NP, Grand Canyon (detour), Las Vegas
- Route 66 (Chicago → LA) is the iconic, nostalgic alternative with small-town Americana

---

### 🛣️ 3. Southern Route — ~2,500 miles
**I-10**
Jacksonville (FL) → New Orleans → San Antonio → El Paso → Tucson → Los Angeles

- Flattest and fastest; fewest elevation changes
- Highlights: New Orleans, Texas Hill Country, Saguaro NP, Joshua Tree NP
- Best for winter travel — warmest and least snow risk

---

### 🕐 General Tips

| Factor | Details |
|---|---|
| **Total distance** | ~2,500–3,000 miles coast to coast |
| **Driving time** | ~40–50 hours nonstop; plan 7–14 days for a leisure trip |
| **Fuel stops** | Every 200–300 miles is typical; plan ahead in Nevada/Wyoming |
| **Best season** | May–September for most routes |
| **Direction** | East→West or West→East makes no major difference in difficulty |

Most first-timers recommend the **Central Route via I-70 + a Route 66 segment**, as it balances scenery, iconic stops, and road quality.
