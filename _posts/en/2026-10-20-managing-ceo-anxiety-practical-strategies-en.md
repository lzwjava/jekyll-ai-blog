**Question:** How can a CEO reduce worry and manage anxiety in their role?

**Answer:**

Being a CEO means carrying enormous responsibility — the worry is real and often legitimate. Here's what actually helps:

**1. Separate signal from noise**
Most CEO anxiety comes from conflating *urgent* with *important*. Build a weekly rhythm: distinguish what needs your attention today vs. what just *feels* urgent. A clear operating cadence (weekly reviews, monthly strategy checks) reduces the ambient dread of "am I missing something."

**2. Delegate aggressively — and mean it**
Worry often spikes when you feel like everything depends on you. If you're mentally carrying work your team should own, you've under-delegated. Real delegation means accepting their execution, not redoing it in your head.

**3. Define "good enough" in advance**
CEOs often have no clear threshold for success, so the bar drifts infinitely upward. For each major initiative, explicitly write down what "done well" looks like. Without that anchor, anxiety fills the gap.

**4. Build a peer support system**
Loneliness amplifies worry. A CEO peer group, a coach, or even one trusted founder friend dramatically reduces the mental load. Talking through fears with someone who gets it is more effective than most productivity hacks.

**5. Separate decision-making from outcomes**
You can make great decisions and still get bad outcomes. Getting clear that your job is the *process* (good information, thoughtful judgment, fast iteration) — not omniscience — reduces catastrophizing.

**6. Physical basics, non-negotiably**
Sleep deprivation and poor physical health multiply anxiety by 2–5x. Protect sleep, limit alcohol, and get consistent movement. It sounds basic because it is foundational.

**7. Scheduled worry time**
Counterintuitively, setting aside 20 minutes daily to write down all your concerns — and nothing else — trains your brain to stop leaking worry throughout the day. What's on paper is no longer stuck in your head.

**8. Accept irreducible uncertainty**
Some worry won't go away, and that's appropriate. The goal isn't to eliminate worry, it's to stop it from being *paralyzing*. Good leaders act despite uncertainty — you develop a tolerance for it over time.
