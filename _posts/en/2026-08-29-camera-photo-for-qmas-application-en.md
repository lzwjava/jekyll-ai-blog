Question: **For a Hong Kong QMAS online application, can I upload a photo taken directly with a camera instead of scanning a physical passport photo?**

Answer: **Yes.** You do **not** need to scan a physical photo.

The Hong Kong Immigration Department explicitly says that for online visa applications, **if you don't have a scanner, you can use the camera on an electronic device to take pictures of the required photos and documents and upload them**. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][1])

For the QMAS application itself, the required document is simply **“Applicant’s recent photograph”**, with one file allowed. Accepted upload formats include JPEG, PDF, GIF, PNG and TIF, with a **5 MB maximum per file**. ([GovHK][2])

So I would do:

```text
Phone/camera
    ↓
Take a proper passport-style photo
    ↓
Crop/prepare as JPEG
    ↓
Upload to QMAS
```

For the safest result:

* plain **white background**
* face directly toward camera
* centered
* good lighting, no shadows
* no sunglasses/hat
* recent photo
* high resolution
* JPEG, preferably well below 5 MB

The ImmD's current photo guidance gives **40 × 50 mm** as the physical-photo size and, for digital-camera capture in its passport-photo system, specifies at least **1200 × 1600 px**. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][3])

**Important distinction:** the QMAS page doesn't explicitly say “1200 × 1600 px” for the QMAS upload itself; that specification is from ImmD's digital-photo guidance for passport applications. So I would treat it as a **good practical target**, rather than claiming it is a QMAS-specific mandatory resolution.
