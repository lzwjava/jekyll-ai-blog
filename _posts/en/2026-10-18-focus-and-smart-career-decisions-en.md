Question: How to stay focused and make smart career decisions — should I work at a company, start a startup, or pursue something else? And how do I earn money and develop my career long-term?

---

Answer:

## 1. How to Stay Focused

The core problem isn't motivation — it's **clarity**. You can't focus on something you haven't clearly defined.

**Practical tactics:**
- **One thing at a time.** Use the "MIT" method (Most Important Task): pick 1–3 things that *must* get done today, do those first.
- **Time-blocking.** Schedule deep work blocks (90–120 min) with no notifications. Treat them like meetings you can't skip.
- **Eliminate, then optimize.** Cut low-value activities before trying to be more efficient. Say no more often.
- **Weekly review.** Every Sunday (or Monday), ask: *What moved the needle this week? What didn't? What should I stop doing?*

---

## 2. How to Decide: Startup vs. Job vs. Something Else

Use this framework — answer these three questions honestly:

| Question | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| What stage of life am I in? | Early career → learn fast at a job first. Have savings/skills → startup risk is lower. |
| What's my risk tolerance? | Startup = high variance (big win or fail). Job = stable income, slower growth. |
| Do I have a real insight or just excitement? | Startups need a specific problem + unfair advantage, not just the *idea* of a startup. |

**General guidance:**
- **Go to work first** (especially 20s) — build real skills, earn money, observe how businesses actually run, build a network.
- **Start a startup** when you have: a specific problem you deeply understand, some savings (6–12 months runway), and ideally a co-founder with complementary skills.
- **Freelance/consulting** is a smart middle path — more freedom than a job, less risk than a startup.

> The best startup founders usually worked somewhere first. They saw a problem from the inside, then went to solve it.

## 3. How to Earn More Money

Think in three levers:

**Lever 1 — Increase your value (skills)**
- Identify the 2–3 skills that are highest-paid in your field and go deep on them.
- Learn at the intersection of two fields (e.g., engineering + design, finance + coding) — rare combinations command premium pay.

**Lever 2 — Increase your market (audience/network)**
- Most money comes from who knows you, not what you know. Build a professional reputation publicly (writing, speaking, building things in public).
- Every year, intentionally expand your professional circle.

**Lever 3 — Increase your leverage (systems)**
- Trade time for money → low ceiling. Build things that work while you sleep: products, content, investments, businesses.
- Even in a job: automate your work, delegate, lead teams — you get paid for impact, not hours.

---

## 4. How to Develop Your Career Long-Term

**The 3-horizon model:**

- **0–2 years (Now):** Master a specific skill. Become known for one thing. Execute extremely well.
- **2–5 years (Next):** Build a track record. Take on bigger problems. Start managing people or projects.
- **5–10 years (Future):** Build a platform — reputation, network, possibly a company or audience — that compounds.

**Compounding principles:**
1. **Learn in public** — share what you're doing and building. Opportunities find people who are visible.
2. **Work with the best people you can find** — your network shapes your trajectory more than almost anything else.
3. **Optimize for learning rate over salary** (especially early on) — a job that pays $20k less but teaches you 2× more is the better deal.
4. **Take career risks early, not late** — the cost of failure at 25 is almost nothing. At 45, it's much higher.
