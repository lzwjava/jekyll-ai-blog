Question: What are Glenn Gould's best works?

Answer:

Glenn Gould (1932–1982) is widely regarded as one of the greatest pianists of the 20th century, particularly renowned for his interpretations of Johann Sebastian Bach's keyboard music. His playing emphasized clarity, precision, contrapuntal detail, and often unconventional tempos, making his recordings both iconic and polarizing.

His most acclaimed and influential works are his recordings of Bach, with the **Goldberg Variations** standing as his signature achievement:

- **Bach: Goldberg Variations, BWV 988 (1955 recording)**: This debut album launched Gould to international fame. Known for its brisk tempos, crystalline articulation, and exhilarating energy, it transformed an obscure harpsichord piece into a piano repertoire staple and became one of the bestselling classical recordings ever.

- **Bach: Goldberg Variations, BWV 988 (1981 recording)**: Gould's final studio recording, more reflective and introspective with slower tempos. Many consider it a profound artistic statement, contrasting sharply with the 1955 version while showcasing his evolved interpretation.

Other highly regarded recordings include:

- **Bach: The Well-Tempered Clavier (selections or Books I & II)**: Praised for definitive counterpoint and clarity.
- **Bach: Partitas (especially Nos. 1–6)**: Dynamic and insightful performances.
- **Bach: Italian Concerto and French/English Suites**: Highlighting his structural mastery.
- **Bach: Keyboard Concertos (e.g., No. 1 in D minor)**: Often with orchestras, showing rhythmic vitality.
- **Beethoven: Late Piano Sonatas**: Gould's analytical approach shines in these complex works.
- **Schoenberg and Hindemith Piano Works**: Gould excelled in 20th-century modernists, bringing lucidity to atonal music.

While Gould recorded some Romantic composers (e.g., Brahms, Beethoven concertos), his Bach interpretations remain his enduring legacy and are most frequently cited as his finest contributions.
