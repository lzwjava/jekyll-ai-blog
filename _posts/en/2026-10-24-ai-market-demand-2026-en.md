**Question:** What does the market actually need right now?

**Answer:**

Cutting through the noise, here's what businesses are genuinely paying for in 2026:

---

### 🔥 High Demand (pay right now)

**1. AI Agents & Workflow Automation**
Companies want repetitive work automated — approvals, emails, data pipelines, reports. Not research, but *shipping working agents*. The AI services that win in 2026 are the ones that attach directly to expensive pain, removing friction rather than showcasing technology.

**2. RAG Systems (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)**
Businesses want their own documents, databases, and knowledge bases connected to LLMs. Legal, finance, healthcare — everyone wants a chatbot that knows *their* data. This is the most repeatably sold product in AI right now.

**3. AI Integration into Existing Software**
Not new apps — plugging AI into what companies already have. CRMs, ERPs, support systems. 51% of companies are using generative AI for content creation, customer support, and process automation.

**4. MLOps / AI in Production**
The most in-demand AI skills in 2026 include retrieval-augmented generation, MLOps, AI governance, and AI security — the "last mile" of getting AI from prototype to reliable production is where most companies are stuck.

**5. AI Video & Content Generation**
The fastest-growing AI skill by hiring demand is AI video generation and editing, up over 329% year-over-year. Unglamorous but real money.

---

### ⚠️ Growing but Harder to Monetize

**6. AI Governance & Compliance**
Gartner named AI security platforms a top investment area for 2026. As AI moves into regulated industries like healthcare and finance, governance skills are becoming table stakes. Slow sales cycle but large contracts.

**7. Domain Fine-tuning**
Companies in law, medicine, finance want models tuned on their data. Real need, but commoditizing fast with services like OpenAI fine-tuning.

---

### ❌ What the Market Does NOT Really Need

- Another AI chatbot SaaS
- Pretraining models from scratch (frontier labs do this better)
- AI education courses (massively oversupplied)
- Generic "AI consulting" without a specific industry focus

---

### The Honest Summary

| What sells | Why |
|---|---|
| Working agents that replace a specific job/task | Measurable ROI |
| RAG over a company's own data | Every company has documents |
| AI embedded in existing tools | Low switching cost |
| Production reliability (MLOps) | Prototypes are easy, prod is hard |
| AI video/content pipelines | Huge creative demand |

**The pattern:** the market pays for *outcomes*, not technology. "I built a RAG system that cut your support tickets by 40%" beats "I trained a language model" every time. The opportunity for someone like you or Zhiwei is connecting deep technical skill to a specific industry problem — finance, legal, healthcare — rather than selling AI in the abstract.
