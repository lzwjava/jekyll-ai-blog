Question: How has the Meiri Youxian (每日优鲜, Everyday Freshness, also known as Missfresh) app platform in China performed in recent years?

Answer:

Meiri Youxian (Missfresh), a pioneering online fresh grocery delivery platform in China known for its front-warehouse model promising fast delivery (e.g., 1-2 hours), experienced rapid growth in its early years but has faced severe decline and near-collapse in recent times.

**Early Success (2014–2021):**
- Founded in 2014, it quickly became a leader in the fresh produce e-commerce sector.
- Pioneered the "front-warehouse" model with distributed mini-warehouses for quick delivery.
- Attracted massive funding (over $1.7 billion total) from investors like Tencent and Tiger Global.
- Went public on Nasdaq in June 2021 as the "first fresh e-commerce stock," raising about $273 million, with a peak valuation around $3 billion.

**Decline Starting in 2022:**
- Post-IPO, the company struggled with heavy losses due to high operational costs, intense competition from platforms like Dingdong Maicai, Meituan, and Pinduoduo's community group buying.
- In July 2022, it announced closure of its core speedy delivery business (accounting for ~85-95% of revenue), leading to rumors of dissolution, unpaid salaries, and supplier debts exceeding 2 billion RMB.
- Faced allegations of financial irregularities, delayed reports, and Nasdaq warnings for low share price.

**Recent Status (2023–2026):**
- Delisted from Nasdaq in late 2023 (suspended November 2023, formally delisted February 2024).
- Core operations largely ceased; sold off assets cheaply (some businesses for nominal amounts).
- Involved in multiple lawsuits, including securities class actions in the US (settled in 2024) and debts/executions in China.
- As of 2025-2026, the company exists legally but with significant risks (over 1,200 risk items noted on business registries), ongoing legal disputes, and no major active operations reported. The app/website may show limited activity (e.g., some product listings), but the platform is effectively defunct compared to its peak.
- The broader fresh e-commerce sector continues to grow, but Missfresh failed to achieve profitability amid subsidy wars and mode inefficiencies.

Overall, after burning through billions in funding, Missfresh represents a cautionary tale of over-expansion in China's competitive online grocery market.
