Question: Do we need to brag about achievements, or is calm, honest disclosure more trustworthy?

Answer: **Yes. Your instinct is basically right.** You usually don't need to *brag*; you need to make the evidence easy to see.

There is an important distinction:

* **Bragging:** “I’m an elite AI engineer. I’m exceptionally talented. I’m one of the best.”
* **Evidence:** “I trained GPT-2 760M on MI300X.”
  “Fun Live reached 30K users and generated ~3M CNY revenue.”
  “I contributed ~X agents/scripts at HSBC.”
* **Letting people conclude:** Give the facts, show the work, and let the reader update their belief.

The third is often much stronger.

### Why this works

People don't have a perfect “brag detector,” but humans are quite sensitive to **inconsistency between confidence and evidence**.

Suppose you say:

> “I'm an amazing AI engineer.”

That's a claim. The reader has to trust you.

But:

> “I trained a 760M-parameter model from scratch on MI300X and built the training/inference pipeline.”

Now the reader can reason:

```text
claim
  ↓
evidence
  ↓
reader evaluates evidence
  ↓
trust
```

The strongest personal branding is therefore often:

```text
do impressive things
        ↓
document them accurately
        ↓
show the artifacts / numbers / code
        ↓
let other people form the conclusion
```

This is especially powerful for an engineer because **the work itself is unusually inspectable**: GitHub commits, benchmarks, papers, systems, demos, revenue, users, technical writing, etc.

### But don't hide achievements either

There's an opposite failure mode:

> “I don't want to brag, so I'll barely mention what I've accomplished.”

That's also bad.

If you did something difficult, **state it plainly**.

For example:

> “Built Fun Live, a knowledge livestreaming product that reached 30K users and generated ~3M CNY in revenue.”

That's not bragging. It's useful information.

You don't need:

> “I'm incredibly proud to say that through extraordinary entrepreneurial ability, I successfully…”

Just give the fact.

A good rule is:

**Don't optimize for looking impressive. Optimize for being accurately understood.**

And over a long time, that compounds into something much more valuable than hype: **trust**.

This is also why publishing technical notes can be powerful. Someone who reads 20 of your posts and sees you consistently explain things accurately doesn't need you to tell them you're technically strong. They've already formed that belief themselves.
