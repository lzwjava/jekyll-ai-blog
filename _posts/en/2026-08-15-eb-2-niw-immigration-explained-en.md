## Question: What is the USA EB-2 NIW immigration program?

## Answer:

**EB-2 NIW (Employment-Based Second Preference — National Interest Waiver)** is a U.S. green card pathway for highly skilled people where you can **self-petition without a U.S. employer sponsor** if you can prove your work is in the **national interest of the United States**. ([USCIS][1])

It is popular among:

* AI researchers / engineers
* Scientists
* Startup founders
* Entrepreneurs
* Doctors
* Engineers
* Researchers with publications/patents

because it does **not require a traditional job offer**. ([USCIS][2])

---

## 1. EB-2 basic category

EB-2 has two main qualification routes:

### Route A: Advanced Degree Professional

Usually:

* Master's degree or higher, OR
* Bachelor's + 5 years progressive experience

Examples:

* MS Computer Science
* PhD researcher
* Senior software engineer with equivalent experience

### Route B: Exceptional Ability

You prove expertise significantly above normal in:

* science
* arts
* business

Evidence can include:

* awards
* publications
* patents
* high salary
* professional memberships
* recognition from industry

([USCIS][1])

# 2. What makes NIW special?

Normally EB-2 requires:

```
Employer
   |
   v
Job offer
   |
   v
PERM labor certification
   |
   v
I-140
   |
   v
Green Card
```

NIW removes:

```
❌ Employer sponsorship
❌ Job offer
❌ PERM labor certification
```

You can file yourself:

```
You
 |
 |-- I-140 NIW petition
 |
 v
USCIS
 |
 v
Green Card process
```

([USCIS][2])

# 3. The 3 NIW tests (Matter of Dhanasar)

USCIS evaluates three things: ([USCIS][3])

## Test 1: Your proposed work has substantial merit + national importance

Bad:

> "I want to work as a software engineer."

Too generic.

Better:

> "I will develop open-source large language model infrastructure improving AI efficiency and reducing computational costs for U.S. companies."

Examples:

AI:

* LLM optimization
* AI safety
* semiconductor AI acceleration
* robotics
* healthcare AI

Technology:

* cybersecurity
* cloud infrastructure
* distributed systems

---

## Test 2: You are well positioned to do it

USCIS looks at your track record:

Examples:

Strong:

* published papers
* GitHub projects with adoption
* patents
* startup success
* research citations
* industry recognition
* major engineering achievements

Weak:

* only job title
* only degree
* only years worked

---

## Test 3: America benefits by waiving normal requirements

Argument:

> "The U.S. should not force this person to find one employer because their contribution benefits the country broadly."

Examples:

* entrepreneur building AI company
* researcher advancing AI technology
* engineer solving national infrastructure problems

([USCIS][4])

# 5. Can someone without a bachelor's degree get EB-2 NIW?

This is the difficult part.

EB-2 usually expects:

* advanced degree, or
* exceptional ability

A person without a degree may try the **Exceptional Ability** route, but the evidence burden becomes much higher. ([USCIS][1])

For a self-taught engineer, possible evidence:

* successful startup
* large-scale software impact
* open-source influence
* publications
* patents
* industry awards
* strong recommendation letters

A normal senior developer profile is usually not enough.

# 6. EB-2 NIW vs EB-1A

|                           | EB-2 NIW             | EB-1A           |
| ------------------------- | -------------------- | --------------- |
| Difficulty                | Medium               | Hard            |
| Employer needed           | No                   | No              |
| Degree                    | Usually helpful      | Not required    |
| Research required         | Helpful              | Often important |
| Extraordinary achievement | Not required         | Required        |
| Typical candidates        | Strong professionals | Top experts     |

EB-1A is more like:

> "I am among the top people in my field."

EB-2 NIW:

> "My future work is valuable to the United States."

# 7. Timeline

Typical process:

```
Prepare evidence
    |
    v
I-140 NIW petition
    |
    v
USCIS decision
    |
    v
Priority date becomes current
    |
    v
Green card interview / adjustment
```

The biggest variable is often **visa backlog**, especially for applicants from countries with high demand.
