Question: How do you think about approaching SHL tests and practice, considering past experiences with exams and coding practice?

Answer:

It sounds like your approach is thoughtful and realistic. Here’s a structured way to think about it:

1. **Flexibility vs. Sequence**

   * You don’t always have to follow a strict step-by-step order. If a section is easy or particularly interesting, you can tackle it first. This helps build momentum and keeps engagement high.
   * At the same time, for more difficult sections, it’s good to systematically practice to avoid gaps in core skills. So a hybrid approach—jump around for interest but return to ensure full coverage—works well.

2. **SHL as a Learning Tool**

   * Treat SHL not just as a test but as a platform to learn about your own problem-solving speed, accuracy, and weak spots.
   * Pay attention to **environmental factors**: website quirks, test timing, and interface issues. These often affect performance more than raw ability. Your experience with TOEFL shows that external factors can significantly impact results.

3. **Preparation Strategy**

   * Warm up with sections you’re confident in to reduce stress and familiarize yourself with the SHL platform.
   * Take note of **where mistakes occur**: Is it knowledge gaps, misreading questions, or technical glitches? This informs targeted practice.
   * Simulate test conditions where possible—strict timing, no interruptions, same device—to train both skill and mental endurance.

4. **Coding & Algorithm Background**

   * Your previous algorithm practice (1000 problems, Codeforces 1400) gives you a strong foundation, even if rusty.
   * Brief refreshers on key patterns and commonly tested problem types are often more effective than grinding many new problems right before the test.
   * Since SHL focuses on logic, numerical reasoning, and verbal reasoning more than complex coding, your coding practice mainly helps your problem-solving mindset and speed.

5. **Mindset & Efficiency**

   * Think of SHL as **practice in high-pressure decision-making**. Each mock test builds both skill and confidence.
   * Small wins—completing a section successfully—help reduce stress for the next sections.

6. **Learning from Experience**

   * You already have experience with exam pitfalls (network issues, environment, interruptions). Make sure the SHL mock sessions simulate real conditions as much as possible.
   * Track patterns over multiple practice tests: where do errors consistently happen? Then target those areas.

Overall, your strategy of treating SHL as a **learning platform**, being flexible with what you do first, and focusing on the interface and test logistics is smart. Combined with a light refresher on key problem types, this should maximize your performance and reduce stress.

You could even make it a mini “research project”: log each attempt, note errors, and improve both skills and test-taking strategy.
