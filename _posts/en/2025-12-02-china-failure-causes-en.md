### Prompt
Why do people fail in their lives in China?

### Answer

"Failure" in China is often defined narrowly as **not achieving high socioeconomic status** (stable job, house, marriage, financial security for family) rather than personal fulfillment. The most common reasons people end up in long-term hardship or feel they have "failed" are structural and cultural, not just individual shortcomings. Here are the main factors (backed by data and widely discussed in Chinese society):

1. **The gaokao (college entrance exam) bottleneck**
   - One exam at age 18 largely determines your university tier, which strongly predicts lifetime income and social class.
   - In 2024, ~13.4 million took gaokao; only ~4–5 million got into Tier-1 (“985/211”) universities that feed into good jobs.
   - Failing to enter a decent university → limited access to urban hukou, white-collar jobs, and the marriage market → lifelong disadvantage. Many describe this as “one exam decides your fate” (一分定终身).

2. **Hukou (household registration) system**
   - Rural hukou holders (still ~40% of population) have severely restricted access to urban public services, good schools for kids, and social welfare even if they work in cities for decades.
   - Without local urban hukou, buying a house, getting your child into good public schools, or qualifying for many jobs is almost impossible → intergenerational poverty trap.

3. **Real-estate and marriage market pressure**
   - In most cities, a man is considered “not marriageable” without owning an apartment (often 100–200 m², costing 30–100× annual salary).
   - Parents frequently go into massive debt or bankrupt themselves for the son’s “wedding house.” Those who cannot afford it often stay single (bare branches / 光棍), which is seen as personal failure in Chinese culture.

4. **996 work culture + layoffs in tech/private sector**
   - Many middle-class professionals who survived gaokao and got good jobs face sudden layoffs (especially 2021–2025 tech crackdown and property crisis).
   - At 35+ you are considered “old” and unemployable in many industries (“35-year-old curse” 35岁危机). Thousands of ex-BAT (Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent) engineers now drive ride-hailing or deliver food.

5. **Extreme competition and winner-take-all economy**
   - Top 1–2% capture most wealth and opportunities (Beijing/Shanghai/Top universities → civil service or state-owned enterprise “iron rice bowl” or finance/tech).
   - Everyone else fights for shrinking middle-class spots. The middle class itself has been contracting since ~2018.

6. **Family expectations and “lying flat” backlash**
   - Strong filial piety: many feel they failed if they cannot financially support parents or give them grandchildren in a decent city.
   - When the effort/reward ratio becomes unbearable, millions choose tangping (lying flat) or runxue (escape-ology, emigrating), which society labels as “personal failure” even though it is a rational response.

7. **Demographic and economic timing**
   - Being born in the wrong cohort matters:
     - Post-1990s born into one-child policy + skyrocketing housing → hardest-hit generation.
     - Post-2000s now face youth unemployment rates that peaked above 21% in 2023 and still hover around 15–18% in 2025.

In short, in China “failure” is less about laziness or bad choices and more about **failing to win in a series of high-stakes, zero-sum tournaments** (gaokao → job market → housing/marriage → keeping the job past 35) under institutional constraints that most developed countries do not have at this severity.
