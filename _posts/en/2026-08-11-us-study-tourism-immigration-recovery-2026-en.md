Question: In 2026, are U.S. study, tourism, and immigration opportunities from China recovering?

Answer:

Short answer: **yes, there is some recovery compared with the COVID period, but it is not a full return to the 2018–2019 era.** The situation is more like **"reopening + selective competition + higher uncertainty."**

### 1. Tourism: recovering faster

Chinese travel to the U.S. has clearly improved after the pandemic collapse. Flights, tourism demand, and business travel are gradually coming back. Some data showed Chinese arrivals had a strong rebound in early 2026 compared with previous weak periods. ([Skift][1])

But:

* Air routes are not fully back to pre-pandemic convenience.
* Costs are higher.
* Geopolitical uncertainty still affects confidence.

So tourism is probably the easiest category to recover.

---

### 2. Studying in the U.S.: recovering, but more selective

For Chinese students:

**Good news:**

* The U.S. still has the strongest AI/CS ecosystem.
* Chinese students remain one of the largest international groups in U.S. STEM programs.
* Universities still need international tuition revenue.

China remains a major source of U.S. STEM graduate students. In 2025, China and India together accounted for a large share of international STEM graduate enrollment. ([NCSES][2])

**Bad news:**

* Visa screening is stricter.
* Some fields (especially sensitive technology areas) face more scrutiny.
* The U.S. political environment is less predictable.

Recent reports show international student visa issuance declined significantly in 2025, with Chinese students also affected. ([The Times of India][3])

So the profile that wins is increasingly:

* strong technical background
* clear study purpose
* good financial proof
* clean application history
* valuable STEM direction

AI is actually one of the stronger areas because the U.S. wants global technical talent, although sensitive AI/security areas can have extra considerations.

---

### 3. Immigration: not "open", but still possible

The U.S. is not in a broad immigration expansion phase.

Compared with 2010–2015:

* easier to enter universities? maybe
* easier to stay permanently? harder

The common path:

```
F-1 student
    ↓
OPT (especially STEM OPT)
    ↓
H-1B
    ↓
Green Card
```

still exists, but competition is high.

For Chinese AI engineers, another route is:

```
build strong AI profile
        ↓
US company / research collaboration
        ↓
O-1 / H-1B / employer sponsorship
```

The value is less "getting a degree" and more "entering the ecosystem."

---

### 4. For someone with your background

Your situation is different from a normal Chinese applicant:

* 10+ years engineering experience
* startup experience
* LLM training experience
* open-source/projects
* IELTS 6.5

The question is not:

> "Can I get into America?"

The bigger question is:

> "Does spending $50k–80k + opportunity cost create enough network/value?"

A master's degree can buy:

```
1 year in US
+
legal student status
+
university network
+
internship possibility
+
local credibility
```

But you already have assets many students don't:

```
real production engineering
AI projects
users/revenue history
technical blog
GPU training experience
```

So your ROI calculation is different.

A possible strategy in 2026:

```
China base
    |
    | build AI products / consulting / open source
    |
    +----> US master's as network accelerator
    |
    +----> US customers / partnerships
```

rather than:

```
go US
    |
    find normal job
    |
    repay debt
```

The second path is riskier.

---

My view: **2026 is better than 2021–2023 for Chinese people going abroad, but harder than 2015–2019.**

The window is not closed. It has shifted from:

> "any international student can go"

to:

> "high-skill people with a clear reason can still create opportunities."

For AI engineers, the opportunity is still relatively strong. ([NCSES][2])
