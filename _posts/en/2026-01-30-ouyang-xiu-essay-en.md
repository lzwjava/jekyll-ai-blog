## **Background**
- **Historical Context**: The **Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period (907–960 CE)** was a chaotic era in Chinese history following the fall of the Tang Dynasty. It was marked by political instability, frequent wars, and the rise and fall of five short-lived dynasties in northern China.
- **Purpose**: Ouyang Xiu wrote this preface as an introduction to the biographies of court musicians (伶官, *línguān*) in his historical work, *New History of the Five Dynasties (新五代史)*. The preface serves as a philosophical commentary on the rise and fall of states, the role of fate, and the dangers of complacency.

## **Key Themes**
1. **The Role of Fate and Human Agency**:
   Ouyang Xiu explores the tension between destiny (天命, *tiānmìng*) and human effort. He argues that while success and failure are influenced by fate, human actions—especially virtue, wisdom, and diligence—play a crucial role in shaping outcomes.

2. **The Rise and Fall of Dynasties**:
   The preface reflects on why some rulers succeed while others fail. Ouyang Xiu attributes the downfall of the Later Tang Dynasty (后唐) to the arrogance and complacency of its ruler, **Zhuangzong (庄宗)**, who became overconfident after his military victories and neglected governance.

3. **The Paradox of Success and Failure**:
   Ouyang Xiu uses the story of Zhuangzong’s rise to power—with the help of court musicians—and his eventual downfall to illustrate how success can sow the seeds of failure. He warns that prosperity often leads to arrogance, which in turn leads to ruin.

4. **The Importance of Humility and Vigilance**:
   The preface emphasizes the need for rulers (and individuals) to remain humble and vigilant, even in times of success. Ouyang Xiu argues that complacency and overconfidence are the enemies of longevity, whether for a dynasty or a person.
