**Question:** What's hot in AI hardware right now, including Plaud and other notable devices?

**Answer:**

## 🔧 Data Center / Chip Level

NVIDIA continues to dominate with its Blackwell platform and the upcoming Rubin architecture. The B200 GPUs deliver massive performance leaps, while AMD fights back with the MI350/MI355 series, offering competitive memory and inference speeds that appeal to cost-conscious cloud providers.

The flagship NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 rack-scale solution scales up to 72 GPUs per rack, with AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, and Oracle Cloud among the first deployers. On AMD's side, their new Helios rack-scale solution also supports up to 72 MI455X GPUs per rack, directly competing with NVIDIA's NVL72.

Intel's Gaudi 3 GPU competes with NVIDIA's H100, training models 1.5× faster and using less power. Its successor, Jaguar Shores, is still set to launch in 2026 with a focus on energy efficiency. Apple and Broadcom are also jointly developing an AI-specific server chip called Baltra for internal inference tasks.

## 💻 Consumer / Local AI Hardware

The 2026 memory shortage has dramatically reshaped the local AI build market — consumer DRAM is up roughly 300–600% from its 2024–2025 lows. A used NVIDIA RTX 3090 with 24GB VRAM currently offers the best value per gigabyte for local AI GPU builds.

In 2026, hardware development is no longer just about higher clock speeds or more cores — it's about efficiency, parallelism, and real-time inference, enabling devices to run sophisticated models locally and reducing reliance on remote data centers.

## 🎙️ Plaud — AI Note-Taking Hardware

Plaud's existing lineup includes the **Plaud Note** — pocket-sized, card-shaped recording devices that attach to the back of a phone — and the **Plaud NotePin**, which can be worn as a brooch, neck pendant, or wristband. Devices start at $159 and include up to 300 minutes of transcription per month without a subscription.

At CES 2026, Plaud launched the **NotePin S** ($179), which adds a physical button for start/stop recording and tap-to-highlight, plus a new desktop app for transcribing digital meetings without a bot joining the call.

Plaud's **upcoming new wearable** (later in 2026) marks a strategic shift — moving from reactive transcription tools to hardware designed to **feed data directly into autonomous AI agents**, with a target 8–12 hour battery life and potential cellular connectivity.

Plaud projects **$500 million in 2026 sales** from over 2 million users, with the US, Japan, and Europe accounting for close to 80% of its business.

Plaud's key differentiator over software-only competitors like Otter or Fireflies is capturing **in-person conversations** — doctor visits, site visits, field work — where software meeting tools simply don't reach.
