Question: How did **Matthew Gallagher** use AI tools to build a billion‑dollar company selling weight‑loss related products and services?

Answer:

Matthew Gallagher (sometimes reported in news about the company *Medvi*) became notable because a telehealth startup he co‑founded was described as nearly reaching **$1.8 billion in projected 2026 revenue after rapid growth**, drawing attention for how lightly staffed and AI‑driven its early operations were. ([Techmeme][1])

Here’s what’s known about how he and his team built the business:

* **Startup and market focus:** The company, often referred to in recent reporting as *Medvi*, operates in the telehealth space — connecting people to virtual consultations and prescriptions for GLP‑1 weight‑loss medications (a category of drugs that includes popular weight‑loss treatments). ([Techmeme][1])

* **AI‑assisted development:** According to reporting based on *The New York Times*, Gallagher used **AI tools to build significant parts of the business very quickly**. This included writing software code, generating website content and marketing materials, and streamlining other startup tasks. It’s said that *a dozen or more AI services were used* to help develop the product and infrastructure on a small initial budget (~$20,000) and in a short time (about two months). ([Techmeme][1])

* **Lean team model:** In many descriptions, Gallagher’s operation initially had **very few full‑time employees** (reported as just him and, later, one other person), relying on automation and external service providers rather than a traditional large team. ([Techmeme][1])

* **Business model:** The core value proposition wasn’t developing new medical therapies itself, but acting as a **consumer‑facing platform** that simplifies access to telehealth consultations and prescriptions for existing GLP‑1 medications. The AI tools helped run technical and marketing functions that would normally require many more staff. ([Techmeme][1])

* **Controversy and discussion:** Many observers have noted that the success attributed to AI is partly because the underlying business is in a *very high‑demand healthcare niche (weight loss medications)* rather than purely because of AI innovation. Some also criticize the approach as largely outsourcing key services and not building entirely proprietary technology. ([Hacker News][2])

In summary, Gallagher’s story illustrates how generative AI and automation tools can dramatically reduce development costs and staffing needs for online businesses. By leveraging those tools along with a lucrative telehealth market, the company scaled to very large revenue figures with minimal traditional staff — though boundaries between technical automation and outsourced services blur in how “AI‑built” it truly is. ([Techmeme][1])
