Question: **What happened to Zhao Deli (赵德力), the founder of XPeng AeroHT? Did he actually go broke once?**

Answer: **Yes — essentially, he did.** His story is much more extreme than the usual “startup struggled” narrative.

### The short version

赵德力's path was roughly:

**worker → restaurant owner → drone/RC aircraft entrepreneur → made tens of millions → bet everything on a manned flying vehicle → went nearly bankrupt → company reduced to 2 people → sold his house → survived by flying/demoing the prototype → attracted investors → met He Xiaopeng → XPeng AeroHT.**

The important part is that **he didn't start with VC money**. He initially made money himself and then burned almost all of it on the flying-machine idea.

### 1. He wasn't originally an aerospace guy

He grew up in Hunan and had a very non-elite background. He worked various jobs, including factory work, security, real estate, insurance and running a restaurant.

Around 2008 he became obsessed with RC aircraft/drones.

He eventually started an aircraft-model business and reportedly made **tens of millions of RMB** from it. By 2013, he had accumulated roughly **RMB 20+ million** and decided to go all-in on a much crazier idea:

> Build an electric manned flying machine.

This was before the current eVTOL boom.

([Sina Finance][1])

### 2. Then he basically burned his fortune

This is the crazy part.

From 2013 onward, he gradually abandoned the profitable drone/model business and poured the money into the flying-machine project.

By **2016**, the money was basically gone.

The company had started with around 10–20 people, but eventually:

**20-ish people → 2 people**

The other person was reportedly an engineer / family member depending on the account.

He later described the situation as:

* funding chain broken
* personal savings exhausted
* debts everywhere
* credit cards / consumer loans maxed out
* creditors calling
* almost nobody contacting him

One report specifically describes him as having **“all debts”** and his credit cards, Huabei and Jiebei all maxed out. ([CE][2])

Another interview is even more direct: **he had gone bankrupt / lost everything** and sold his house to keep development going. ([South China News][3])

So when you say **“he broke right?”** — yes. Not literally a formal corporate bankruptcy necessarily, but **personally he was essentially financially wiped out**.

### 3. And he kept coding/building himself

This is the part I think is most interesting from an engineering perspective.

When everyone left, he didn't pivot to fundraising.

He basically became the entire company:

**hardware + flight control + testing + fundraising + sales**

He taught himself flight-control technology and continued testing.

From 2016–2018 he spent huge amounts of time in the lab. One interview says that during the worst period, it was literally **him + one engineer**, and he would eat a corn cob and a bun for lunch and go back to work. ([CLS][4])

This wasn't just theoretical R&D either.

Before his first successful manned flight, the team reportedly conducted **1,500+ unmanned/weighted flight tests**.

Then on **June 26, 2018**, he finally got into the machine himself.

It flew.

([CLS][4])

### 4. But he still had almost no money

This is another interesting entrepreneurial move.

After the successful flight, he didn't suddenly have a giant company.

He used the aircraft itself as a **fundraising/marketing machine**.

He went around doing demonstrations and letting people see the thing actually fly.

The successful flight generated media attention, which brought angels and investors.

So the loop became:

**prototype → demo → media attention → investor → more prototype → better demo**

rather than:

**pitch deck → VC → build product**

That's a huge distinction.

([Sina Finance][5])

### 5. Then came He Xiaopeng

The real turning point was **2020**.

He Xiaopeng became interested in Zhao's flying machines and eventually went to see the team in Dongguan.

Zhao literally **flew He Xiaopeng around in the prototype**.

That seems to have been the moment where the story changed from:

> “crazy guy building flying machines”

to:

> “maybe this is actually a company.”

In 2020, He Xiaopeng and XPeng invested in/combined with Zhao's company, creating **XPeng AeroHT (小鹏汇天)**.

([The Paper][6])

And that's basically how the guy who had almost nothing ended up running one of China's most prominent flying-car companies.

---

### The deeper lesson

I wouldn't summarize his story as simply **“never give up.”**

The more interesting pattern is:

```text
Make money from a real business
        ↓
Use accumulated capital to pursue an insane technical idea
        ↓
Burn almost everything
        ↓
Company collapses to ~2 people
        ↓
Founder becomes the engineer
        ↓
Build something that physically works
        ↓
Demonstrate it publicly
        ↓
Get attention
        ↓
Get angels
        ↓
Build a much stronger prototype
        ↓
He Xiaopeng notices
        ↓
XPeng AeroHT
```

And there's an important survivorship-bias warning here.

**Thousands of founders can follow “I bet everything on my dream” and simply disappear.** Zhao's story became famous because the technical bet eventually produced something spectacular enough to attract capital.

So the remarkable part isn't merely that he was willing to go broke.

It's that **before he went broke, he had already demonstrated an ability to make money; after going broke, he continued producing increasingly convincing technical evidence.**

That's a very different story from someone simply refusing to admit that a bad business isn't working.

([The Paper][7])
