**Question: Is Tritan also called PCT, and is it called changed PCT in China?**

**Answer:**

Tritan is **not exactly the same as plain PCT**, but it is frequently referred to as **"modified PCT"** (or "改性PCT" in Chinese), especially in China and some Asian markets.

- **PCT** stands for **Polycyclohexylenedimethylene Terephthalate**, a base copolyester.
- **Tritan** is Eastman's branded copolyester, which is a **modified version of PCTG** (Polycyclohexylenedimethylene Terephthalate Glycol-modified). It incorporates an additional monomer (2,2,4,4-tetramethyl-1,3-cyclobutanediol, or TMCD/CBDO) to improve heat resistance, impact strength, clarity, and other properties compared to standard PCTG.

In technical terms:
- Tritan belongs to the PCTG family but is an **enhanced/high-temperature version** of it.
- It is often described as "Tritan is PCTG, but not all PCTG is Tritan" (similar to how not all cola is Coca-Cola).

In **China**, the common names are:
- "Tritan" (the brand name, often used directly for bottles and products).
- "改性PCT" (modified PCT) — this is the official term in Chinese national standards (e.g., GB 4806.6-2016 and GB 4806.7-2016 list it as "modified PCT", with chemical name involving dimethyl terephthalate + 1,4-cyclohexanedimethanol + TMCD, CAS 261716-94-3).
- Sometimes simply called "PCT" or "PCTG" in product listings, but "改性PCT" is the most accurate and widely used translation for Tritan in the Chinese market, especially for food-contact items like water bottles, baby bottles, and tableware.

This naming comes from regulatory approvals and marketing: Eastman developed Tritan as a BPA-free alternative to polycarbonate (PC), and Chinese regulations classify it under "modified PCT" rather than using the English brand name alone.

In summary:
- Tritan is **not called plain PCT** — it's a specific, modified form.
- Yes, in China it is commonly called **"changed/modified PCT"** (改性PCT), and this is the standard term in standards, product labels, and industry discussions there.
