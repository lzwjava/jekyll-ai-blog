Question: What does "written into the Party Constitution (党章)" mean in the context of Chinese Communist Party governance?

Answer:

The term **党章 (dǎng zhāng)** refers to the **Constitution of the Communist Party of China (CPC Constitution)** — the party's foundational governing document, supreme behavioral code, and overarching rulebook. It holds the highest authority within the party's internal legal framework, above all other party regulations.

**What "written into the Party Constitution" means here:**

At the **19th National Congress of the CPC (2017)**, **Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era (习近平新时代中国特色社会主义思想)** was formally enshrined into the **General Program (总纲)** of the CPC Constitution as the party's official guiding ideology.

This is politically significant for several reasons:

- **Ideological legitimacy**: Only the most authoritative ideological frameworks get written into the General Program — previously, Mao Zedong Thought and Deng Xiaoping Theory were enshrined this way.
- **Binding on all members**: Once in the Constitution, adherence to this ideology becomes a formal obligation for all ~98 million CPC members.
- **Permanent institutional status**: It elevates Xi Jinping's thought from policy preference to foundational doctrine, making it far harder to revise or remove.
- **Historical parallel**: Mao Zedong was the only prior leader to have his name attached to a guiding ideology *while still in power* — placing Xi in exceptionally rare company.

In short, "writing into the Party Constitution" is the highest form of ideological codification within the CPC system.
