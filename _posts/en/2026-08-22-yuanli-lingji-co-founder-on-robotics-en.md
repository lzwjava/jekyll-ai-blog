**Question:** What does this article say?

**Answer:** This is an interview with Fan Haoqiang, co-founder of **Yuanli Lingji** (原力灵机), an embodied intelligence / robotics startup that spun out of Megvii (旷视). Here are the key points:

**Background**
- Fan joined Megvii at age 16 as its 6th employee, having won an International Olympiad in Informatics gold medal in high school. He became serious about robotics around 2016 after visiting a Stanford lab.
- Yuanli Lingji was formally founded in early 2025, raising nearly ¥1 billion across two rounds.

**Core Philosophy**
- **Models come first.** The model's capability determines which scenarios are viable, which in turn defines what the hardware should look like.
- They want to build both the AI model *and* the robot hardware — believing clients want a complete, end-to-end solution.

**Real-World Challenges**
- Fan emphasizes that real customer problems are far harder than lab benchmarks. Even the *simplest* task from a real factory client is harder than their hardest internal test.
- His single most important metric for a robot: **how quickly can it pay for itself?** Everything else is secondary.

**DFOL (Embodied-Native Mass Production Workflow)**
- Their approach to finding "embodied-native applications" — tasks where robots have a genuine advantage, like flexible production line changeovers that previously took 5 months to set up.
- They use human correction feedback in a closed loop to continuously improve the model during deployment.

**On the Industry**
- Different technical routes (simulation, real-data collection, world models) are fine — he likens it to "diverging at the mountain base but meeting at the summit."
- His view: technology doesn't need to be perfect to succeed — it just needs to be *good enough to be useful*, which is how most technologies historically spread.
