**Question:** Who are the fellows of the Computer History Museum?

**Answer:**

The Computer History Museum (CHM) recognizes distinguished technology pioneers through its **CHM Fellow Awards** program, which began in 1987 with the first Fellow, Grace Murray Hopper. The program honors individuals (and occasionally teams) for their outstanding contributions to computing and the evolution of the information age. Fellows are inducted into the museum's **Hall of Fellows**.

The complete and up-to-date list of all Fellows is maintained on the official CHM website at the **Hall of Fellows** page: <https://computerhistory.org/hall-of-fellows>. This page showcases all honorees, including recent classes.

Some notable past and recent Fellows include:

- **2024 Fellows**: Allan Alcorn, Nolan Bushnell, Elizabeth "Jake" Feinler, Jensen Huang, and Steven (likely Steven Sasson or another; recent announcements highlight contributions in video games, networking, AI, GPUs, etc.).
- Other prominent examples from various years:
  - Grace Murray Hopper (1987, first Fellow)
  - Tim Berners-Lee (inventor of the World Wide Web)
  - Steve Wozniak (Apple co-founder)
  - Gordon Moore (Intel co-founder, Moore's Law)
  - Katherine Johnson (NASA mathematician)
  - Margaret Hamilton (Apollo software engineer)
  - Ed Catmull (Pixar co-founder)
  - Doug Engelbart (inventor of the mouse and more)
  - Vint Cerf (TCP/IP co-inventor)
  - Lynn Conway
  - Ivan Sutherland
  - Guido van Rossum (Python creator)
  - And many others in areas like AI, microprocessors, robotics, software, and hardware.

The selection process involves public nominations and review by a panel of historians, researchers, industry leaders, museum staff, and prior Fellows. The program continues annually, with nominations open year-round for future classes (e.g., 2027 nominations are currently accepted).

For the full historical and current list, including bios and induction years, visit the official Hall of Fellows page linked above.
