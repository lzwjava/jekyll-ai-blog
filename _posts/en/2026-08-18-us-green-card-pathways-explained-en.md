**Question:** What are the ways to obtain a Green Card (permanent residency) in the USA?

---

**Answer:**

A Green Card (Form I-551, Permanent Resident Card) gives you the right to live and work permanently in the United States. It's the stepping stone to US citizenship. Here are the main pathways:

---

### 1. Family-Based Sponsorship
Family-based immigration is the most common path — roughly two-thirds of all green cards issued each year go to family members of US citizens and permanent residents. You file Form I-130 (Petition for Alien Relative), which establishes the relationship between the petitioner and beneficiary.

### 2. Employment-Based Sponsorship
The employer typically files Form I-140 (Immigrant Petition for Alien Worker). This requires a permanent full-time job offer and PERM labor certification. Categories include skilled workers (2+ years experience), professionals (requiring a bachelor's degree), and unskilled workers.

### 3. Diversity Visa Lottery (DV Lottery)
It's possible to get a Green Card by winning the Diversity Visa Lottery, which randomly selects applicants from countries with historically low immigration rates to the US.

### 4. Refugee or Asylum Status
Applicants can qualify through refugee or asylum status after meeting the required conditions and residing in the US for at least one year.

### 5. Investor Program (EB-5)
The investor program is another eligible immigration category. It requires a substantial capital investment in a US business that creates jobs for American workers.

---

### General Application Steps
The process involves: determining your eligibility, completing the required immigration forms, gathering supporting documents and evidence, submitting your application and paying fees, and attending biometrics appointments, interviews, and awaiting a decision.

### Important 2026 Update
USCIS has declared Adjustment of Status (Form I-485) an "extraordinary" discretionary benefit. Most green card applicants now in the United States are expected to leave and apply through consular processing at a U.S. embassy abroad. This applies to F-1 students, B-2 visitors, TN workers, and potentially H-1B/L-1 holders.
