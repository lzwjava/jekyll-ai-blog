**Question:** Who are some examples of people in the tech or IT sector who achieved major success later in life — in their 40s, 50s, or 60s?

**Answer:**

Here are notable late bloomers specifically in tech and IT:

---

**💻 In Their 40s**

- **Robert Noyce — Intel (age 41)**
  After working in a number of technological ventures, Noyce achieved his life-changing success when he co-founded Intel with Gordon Moore in 1968 at the age of 41. His estimated net worth reached $3.7 billion.

- **Eric Yuan — Zoom (age 41)**
  Eric Yuan created Zoom at 41. It later became one of the most widely used video conferencing platforms in the world, especially after the pandemic.

- **Liu Chuanzhi — Lenovo (success in his 40s)**
  Liu Chuanzhi is the founder of Lenovo, which is currently the largest computer maker in the world. Although he started his entrepreneurial endeavors as a college student, he only found success in his 40s, after repeatedly trying to launch his computer startup. Along with 10 colleagues and a budget of less than $30,000, he co-founded what was later named Lenovo.

- **Jack Ma — Alibaba (success in his 40s)**
  Jack Ma came from a humble background and failed his college entry test twice. After he managed to graduate, he was rejected from all kinds of jobs. He was well over 40 when his company Alibaba was recognized as a major entrepreneurial success, eventually entering the Forbes list of wealthiest people in the world.

---

**🖥️ In Their 50s**

- **Gordon Moore — Intel (influential leadership in his 50s)**
  Though he co-founded Intel at 41, his most impactful leadership came in his 50s, helping define the microprocessor age. He also gave the world "Moore's Law."

- **Tom Siebel — C3 IoT (late 50s)**
  After Oracle's buyout of Siebel Systems, Siebel founded energy company C3 in 2009 when he was in his late 50s, later relaunching it as C3 IoT — an internet of things-focused company that helps customers analyze data from IoT sensors.

- **Bill Porter — E\*TRADE (age 54)**
  Bill Porter started E\*TRADE at 54. It became one of the most well-known online brokerage platforms in the US.

---

**🏢 In Their 60s**

- **David Duffield — Workday (age 65)**
  David Duffield launched Workday at 65. Workday became a leading enterprise cloud application for finance and HR, valued at billions of dollars.
  He had previously founded PeopleSoft in his mid-40s, which gave Oracle a run for its money in the business software market — making him someone who succeeded *twice* later in life.

---

**📊 A Broader Trend Worth Noting**

The Kaufman Institute found that U.S.-born tech CEOs are far more likely to be over 50 than under 25. And a 50-year-old founder is twice as likely as a 30-year-old to build a company that goes public or gets acquired. Experience, networks, and industry knowledge are powerful advantages that accumulate with age.
