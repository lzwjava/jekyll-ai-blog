Question: What is 快速以太网 (Fast Ethernet), and how fast is it?

Answer: **快速以太网** is the Chinese term for **Fast Ethernet**, a networking standard that operates at **100 Mbps (megabits per second)** — exactly 10× faster than the original Ethernet standard (10 Mbps).

**Key facts:**

- **Official standard:** IEEE 802.3u (ratified in 1995)
- **Speed:** 100 Mbps
- **Common variants:**
  - **100BASE-TX** — runs over Cat 5 (or better) twisted-pair copper cable; the most widely deployed variant
  - **100BASE-FX** — runs over fiber optic cable
  - **100BASE-T4** — uses 4 pairs of Cat 3 cable (largely obsolete)
- **Maximum segment length:** 100 m for copper (100BASE-TX), up to 2 km for fiber (100BASE-FX)
- **Duplex:** Supports both half-duplex and full-duplex modes (full-duplex gives effective 200 Mbps aggregate)

**Speed comparison in context:**

| Standard | Chinese Name | Speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ethernet | 以太网 | 10 Mbps |
| **Fast Ethernet** | **快速以太网** | **100 Mbps** |
| Gigabit Ethernet | 千兆以太网 | 1,000 Mbps |
| 10 Gigabit Ethernet | 万兆以太网 | 10,000 Mbps |

Fast Ethernet was the dominant LAN standard through the late 1990s and 2000s. Today it has been largely superseded by **Gigabit Ethernet**, though 100BASE-TX ports are still found on older hardware and some embedded devices.
