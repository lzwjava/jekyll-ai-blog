Question: Is there a saying about not blaming others for one's own failures?

Answer: Yes, there are several well-known sayings that capture this idea:

**Most classic English proverb:**
> **"A bad workman always blames his tools."**
This means a person who lacks skill or fails will find excuses by blaming their instruments or circumstances rather than accepting personal responsibility.

**Other relevant sayings:**

- **"Don't blame the mirror if your face is ugly."** *(Russian proverb)* — You can't fault the messenger for reflecting reality.
- **"The fault, dear Brutus, is not in our stars, but in ourselves."** *(Shakespeare, Julius Caesar)* — Our failures stem from our own choices, not fate or others.
- **"When you point one finger at someone, three fingers are pointing back at you."** — A reminder that blame often reflects more on the blamer.

**The underlying principle** is sometimes called **"accountability"** or **"taking ownership"** — the idea that genuinely successful people focus on what *they* could have done differently, rather than assigning blame outward.

The Chinese equivalent concept is **怨天尤人 (yuàn tiān yóu rén)** — blaming heaven and other people — used as a criticism of someone who refuses to accept personal responsibility.
