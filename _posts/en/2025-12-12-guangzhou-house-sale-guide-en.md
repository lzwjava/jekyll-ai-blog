Question: How to sell a house in Guangzhou?

Answer: 

Selling a house in Guangzhou, China, involves a structured process governed by local real estate regulations. As of 2025, Guangzhou has relaxed housing purchase limits, but sellers must still comply with ownership verification, tax obligations, and online registration requirements to ensure a smooth transaction. The process typically takes 1-3 months, depending on buyer financing and document preparation. Below is a step-by-step guide based on standard procedures for second-hand (existing) residential properties. It's highly recommended to engage a licensed real estate agent and consult a lawyer, especially for foreigners or complex cases like inherited properties.

### Step 1: Prepare and Verify Ownership
- **Check eligibility for sale**: Confirm your property meets any local restrictions, such as a minimum holding period (e.g., 2 years for some properties to avoid taxes). Review your **不动产权证** (Real Estate Ownership Certificate) for details on ownership, area, and restrictions.
- **Obtain a property title search (查册)**: Visit a Guangzhou Real Estate Registration Center (不动产登记中心) to get a **广州市不动产登记查册表** (Property Title Search Report). This verifies clear title, no mortgages, liens, seals, or disputes. Required documents: Your ID, property address, certificate number, and land parcel number. Cost: Around 10-20 RMB; processing time: Same day.
- **Gather documents**: Prepare your ID (passport for foreigners), marriage certificate (if applicable), property certificate, tax payment records, and any power of attorney if selling on behalf of someone else.
- **Tip for foreigners**: If you're a non-resident, ensure compliance with foreign ownership rules; you may need notarized documents for repatriating sale proceeds.

### Step 2: List the Property and Find a Buyer
- **Hire a licensed agent**: Choose a registered intermediary (e.g., via the Guangzhou Real Estate Intermediary Association). Sign a **房地产中介服务合同** (Agency Agreement) through the **广州市存量房网上交易系统** (Guangzhou Second-Hand Housing Online Trading System). Specify price, commission (typically 1-2% of sale price, split between buyer/seller agents), and listing duration.
- **Set a realistic price**: Research market values using platforms like Anjuke, Lianjia, or SouFun. Average prices in 2025: 30,000-50,000 RMB per square meter for residential properties, varying by district (e.g., higher in Tianhe, lower in Panyu).
- **Market the property**: List on agent networks, WeChat groups, 58.com, or expat forums. Highlight features like location, amenities, and renovations. Conduct viewings and negotiations.
- **Sign preliminary agreement**: Once a buyer is found, sign a **房屋买卖意向书** (Intent to Sell Agreement) and receive a deposit (usually 1-5% of price, up to 20% max).

### Step 3: Sign Contracts and Online Registration
- **Draft the sales contract**: Use a standard template from the online system. Include price, payment terms, delivery date, breach penalties, and tax responsibilities. Both parties sign the **房屋买卖合同** (Sales Contract).
- **Complete net signing (网签)**: Within 24 hours of the contract, the agent submits it via the **广州市存量房网上交易系统** for official online registration. This prevents double-selling and locks in the price for tax purposes. Report the true transaction price to avoid penalties for under- or over-reporting.
- **Tip**: Review clauses carefully; disputes often arise from vague terms on repairs or utilities.

### Step 4: Handle Taxes, Fees, and Buyer Verification
- **Calculate and pay taxes**: As the seller, expect:
  - **Deed tax**: Paid by buyer (1-3% of price).
  - **Value-added tax (VAT)**: 5% if held <2 years; exempt if >2 years or primary residence.
  - **Individual income tax**: 20% on gains (or 1-2% of price if not primary home); exemptions for sole family home sold after 5 years.
  - **Land appreciation tax**: Up to 30-60% on profits exceeding 50,000 RMB (rare for individuals).
  Total seller costs: 2-5% of sale price. Pay via tax bureau; agent can assist.
- **Buyer checks**: The buyer verifies their purchase eligibility (no limits since 2024, but they may need proof for loans). If the buyer uses a mortgage, the bank appraises the property (value often matches market price).

### Step 5: Transfer Ownership and Payment
- **Submit for transfer registration (过户)**: Both parties go to the Real Estate Registration Center with contracts, IDs, tax receipts, and search report. Apply for **转移登记** (Title Transfer). If mortgaged, redeem or transfer the loan first.
- **Payment**: Buyer pays balance (often via escrow). For loans, the bank wires funds directly to your account after mortgage registration.
- **Issue new certificate**: Receive the updated **不动产权证** within 3-5 working days. Hand over keys, utilities transfer, and possession.
- **Processing time**: 5-10 working days; fees: 0.5% of price (split).

### Additional Tips
- **Total timeline and costs**: 30-90 days; seller fees ~3-5% (taxes + agent commission).
- **Common pitfalls**: Unclear titles, unreported mortgages, or tax evasion. Always use the online system to avoid "one house, two sales" risks.
- **For expats/foreigners**: Engage a lawyer for cross-border issues; proceeds can be repatriated via SAFE approval if originally foreign-funded.
- **Market context (2025)**: With limits lifted, sales are brisk, but consult current policies via 12345 hotline or Housing Bureau website.
