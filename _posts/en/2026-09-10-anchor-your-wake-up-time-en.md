Question: **How can you establish a regular sleep/rest clock?**

Answer:

The key is to **anchor your wake-up time**, not force your bedtime.

Your circadian rhythm is basically a feedback system. You want strong, repeated signals at roughly the same time every day.

### The simplest protocol

**1. Pick a fixed wake time**
For example:

```text
08:00 — wake up
08:00–08:30 — sunlight + walking
```

Keep 08:00 even on weekends, ideally within ~30–60 minutes.

**2. Let bedtime emerge naturally**

If you want ~8 hours:

```text
08:00 wake
↓
~00:00 sleep
```

Don't lie in bed at 22:00 just because "I should sleep." Go to bed when genuinely sleepy.

**3. Get bright light soon after waking**

This is probably the strongest practical lever.

```text
wake
  ↓
outdoor daylight 10–30 min
  ↓
normal daytime activity
  ↓
dim lights at night
  ↓
sleep
```

Morning light tells your brain: **"this is the beginning of the day."**

**4. Avoid shifting the clock dramatically**

The common failure mode is:

```text
Mon: 00:00 → 08:00
Tue: 01:00 → 09:00
Wed: 03:00 → 11:00
Thu: 05:00 → 13:00
```

Instead, if you're currently sleeping at 4 AM, don't try to jump immediately to midnight.

Move the schedule gradually:

```text
04:00 → 03:30 → 03:00 → 02:30 → ...
```

while keeping the **wake time increasingly stable**.

### Your computer/LLM work makes one thing particularly important

Don't make **"I'm still productive"** the criterion for staying awake.

LLM work is unusually good at creating this loop:

```text
interesting problem
      ↓
"one more experiment"
      ↓
2 AM
      ↓
still mentally stimulated
      ↓
"might as well finish this"
      ↓
5 AM
```

Instead, establish a **hard shutdown ritual**:

```bash
23:30  stop new experiments
23:30  write down unfinished thoughts
23:40  close laptop
23:45  shower / read
00:00  bed
08:00  wake
```

The important part is that unfinished work gets **externalized**. Write:

```text
TODO:
- reproduce kernel benchmark
- inspect attention.py line 183
- test batch=8
```

Then your brain doesn't need to keep holding the problem overnight.

### Also: don't use naps to compensate heavily

If you're trying to reset your clock:

* No nap, or
* ~20 min early afternoon

A 2-hour nap at 6 PM can effectively tell your brain:

> "The night hasn't started yet."

### Think of it as a control system

There are three major inputs:

```text
             ┌── morning light ──┐
             │                   ↓
wake time ───┼──────────────→ circadian phase
             │
activity ────┤
             │
             └── evening darkness → melatonin/sleepiness
```

And your goal isn't perfect sleep every night.

It's **low variance**:

```text
Wake:
08:00 08:05 07:55 08:10 08:00

Sleep:
00:05 00:20 23:55 00:15 00:00
```

rather than trying to hit an exact bedtime.

If you're currently sleeping very late, **fixing the wake time + morning light for 1–2 weeks** is usually a much better starting experiment than trying to force yourself to sleep early.
