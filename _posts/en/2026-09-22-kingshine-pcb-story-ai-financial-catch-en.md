Question: **How is 科翔股份 (300903), the Huizhou PCB company? What’s the story behind it?**

Answer:

If you mean **广东科翔电子科技股份有限公司 / Kingshine**, I think the interesting part is not the stock chart—it’s the **industrial story**.

广东科翔电子科技股份有限公司 is a Huizhou-founded PCB manufacturer, established in **2001**, listed on ChiNext in **2020** under **300903**. Its headquarters are in Huizhou Daya Bay. ([Gdkxpcb][1])

### 1. The origin story is actually pretty good

The founder/chairwoman **郑晓蓉** came into PCB manufacturing from the factory floor in the 1990s. She started 科翔 in **2001 in Daya Bay**, reportedly with only a dozen-ish people and a lot of second-hand equipment. ([CNStock Paper][2])

The early story is basically:

> **learn manufacturing → solve supply-chain bottlenecks → survive → accumulate process know-how → gradually move up the PCB complexity curve.**

One particularly telling episode: around 2002, an overseas key-material supplier reportedly raised prices by 30%. Instead of simply absorbing the increase, the company went around China looking for domestic chemical suppliers and eventually worked with a small chemical manufacturer on joint development. ([CNStock Paper][2])

That's a very **Pearl River Delta manufacturing entrepreneur** story.

Not "we invented a revolutionary technology."

More:

**factory → process → supply chain → scale → engineering capability → higher-end products.**

---

### 2. What does 科翔 actually make?

PCB is basically the physical substrate connecting chips/components together.

But "PCB" covers an enormous complexity range.

科翔 now makes:

* high-layer-count PCB
* HDI
* high-frequency/high-speed PCB
* thick copper
* metal substrate
* ceramic substrate
* rigid-flex
* IC substrates
* etc.

And targets:

**AI servers / data centers / optical modules / automotive electronics / communications / industrial control / medical / consumer electronics.** ([Sina Finance][3])

This is the important transition:

**ordinary PCB manufacturer → increasingly high-end PCB manufacturer.**

That's the investment/business story.

---

### 3. The AI angle is real, but don't confuse "AI PCB" with an AI company

This is where the current narrative gets interesting.

AI infrastructure needs enormous amounts of:

**GPU → board → high-speed interconnect → server → switch → optical module → rack**

And PCB requirements become substantially harder as bandwidth, layer count, signal integrity, thermal load, and power density increase.

科翔 says it has entered areas including:

* AI servers
* high-speed optical modules
* high-density interconnect
* precision control

and its 2025 R&D expenditure was **¥211M**, around **5.67% of revenue**. ([CS.com][4])

So the potential thesis isn't:

> "AI makes 科翔 an AI company."

It's:

> **AI capex increases demand for increasingly sophisticated PCB, and 科翔 is trying to move its product mix toward that higher-value portion of the PCB stack.**

That's a much more interesting thesis.

---

### 4. But financially, there's a huge catch

2025:

|             |       2025 |
| ----------- | ---------: |
| Revenue     | **¥3.72B** |
| YoY revenue | **+9.56%** |
| PCB revenue | **¥3.38B** |
| Net profit  | **-¥246M** |
| R&D         |  **¥211M** |

The company actually **lost money** in 2025 despite growing revenue. It did, however, reduce the loss by 28.32% YoY. ([CS.com][4])

And 2026 Q1 revenue was **¥954M, +9.39% YoY**. ([CS.com][4])

So the central question is not:

> "Does AI help PCB?"

Obviously it can.

The harder question is:

> **Can 科翔 convert higher-end AI/automotive/optical PCB demand into sustainable gross margin and ultimately net profit?**

That's the thing I'd watch.

### 5. The customer story is also becoming more interesting

The company says it has entered supply chains involving:

* BYD
* Aptiv
* Joyson
* Huaqin
* Luxshare
* DJI

and other major automotive / intelligent-device companies. Its top 10 customers accounted for **36.57% of 2025 revenue**. ([CS.com][4])

That's useful because PCB manufacturing has a nasty chicken-and-egg problem:

**high-end customer → certification → production validation → volume → process learning → more high-end customers**

Once you're qualified into an automotive or server supply chain, switching suppliers isn't necessarily trivial.

So customer qualification can become a moat.

But customer concentration also means you need to watch pricing power carefully.

### 7. Why Huizhou matters

This is also a very Huizhou story.

The company started in **Daya Bay**, and today describes production/operations across **Huizhou, Shenzhen, Jiujiang and Ganzhou**, with roughly **6,000 employees** and stated 2025 revenue of ¥3.7B. ([Gdkxpcb][1])

It's a classic Guangdong manufacturing trajectory:


That's why I find the company more interesting as an **industrial-history story** than as a pure "AI stock."

## My read of the business

I'd frame 科翔 as:

**"A 25-year-old Huizhou PCB manufacturer attempting its second transformation—from scale manufacturing into high-end PCB for AI, automotive and high-speed communications."**

The first transformation—**small factory → listed ¥3.7B company**—already happened.

The second one is happening now:

**traditional PCB → high-end PCB → AI infrastructure.**

And the financial evidence currently says **the transformation is underway, but the profitability outcome isn't proven yet**. ([CS.com][4])
