Question: In today’s society, for engineers being interviewed in China or globally, are the most important factors their current or recent job title, salary, abilities, and whether they fit the position and company values?

Answer:

Yes, generally those are the key factors, but there’s a hierarchy and nuance depending on the company and role:

1. **Current or recent job title** – This often signals your level of experience and the kind of responsibilities you’ve handled. For senior positions, companies usually check that your title reflects sufficient leadership or technical depth. However, some companies focus more on what you actually did rather than the title itself.

2. **Salary history** – This can indicate market value and expectations, but it’s more relevant in some countries than others. Many global companies avoid overemphasizing past salary to prevent bias. In China, it’s often used as a benchmark for negotiating, but not necessarily as a measure of ability.

3. **Ability/skills** – This is critical. Technical interviews, coding challenges, design exercises, or project discussions reveal your capability. Even with a great title, if you can’t demonstrate the skills needed, you’re less likely to succeed.

4. **Fit with position and values** – Cultural and functional fit matter a lot. “Position fit” ensures you can handle the tasks and team dynamics; “values fit” ensures long-term retention and alignment with company philosophy. For instance, some companies value innovation and autonomy, others value structured execution.

5. **Other factors** – Sometimes overlooked, but important: communication skills, problem-solving approach, adaptability, and growth potential. Especially in high-growth or startup environments, potential can outweigh past titles.

In short, titles and salary give context, abilities demonstrate capability, and fit determines sustainability and team harmony. The weight of each factor varies by company, role, and country, but all four elements together give the most complete picture for hiring decisions.
