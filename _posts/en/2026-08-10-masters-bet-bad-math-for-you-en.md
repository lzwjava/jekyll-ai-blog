Question: Given ~700k (assume CNY, ~$97k USD) existing mortgage/debt, is it rational to add US master's debt (family backstop available) to bet on a US degree?

Answer:

Run the actual numbers first, not the vibe.

**Cost side:**

- 2yr US MS in CS/AI: tuition $60-90k/yr at a name-brand school (CMU, Stanford, UIUC) + living $25-35k/yr = **$170-250k total**, on top of your existing ~$97k.
- Total exposure: **$270-350k USD**, family as backstop.
- Real cost isn't the tuition — it's **2 years away from your company**. You have 30K-user Fun Live history, GPU cluster experience, an AI consulting/training business running now. Stepping away from that for 2 years while competitors ship is the bigger bill.

**What a master's actually buys you at your level:**
It's near-zero technical ROI. You've trained a 760M GPT-2 on MI300X, run 2B tokens/mo across frontier models, built CLI agents. A US MS curriculum teaches things you already do in production. The degree isn't for skills — it's for three things: (1) visa/immigration optionality, (2) network/credibility with US investors and enterprise clients, (3) F-1→OPT→H-1B pathway.

**Current immigration reality (as of Aug 2026, verified just now):**

- The $100k H-1B fee is back in effect after a court fight (struck down June 8, then reinstated late June), still under appeal — as of late June 2026, the $100,000 H-1B fee is back in effect after a court briefly struck it down, but it applies mainly to petitions filed for workers who are still outside the country.
- Critically: petitions requesting a change of status, amendment, or extension of stay filed after September 21, 2025 are not impacted by this proclamation — meaning F-1 students who go through OPT and get H-1B sponsorship *while already in the US* are exempt from the $100k fee.
- But the lottery is still capped and now wage-weighted: the Wage-Weighted Selection Rule took effect on February 27, 2026, prioritizing higher-paid Level IV and Level III applicants, and USCIS reached the fiscal year 2027 H-1B cap including the master's cap by March 31, 2026. So the fee risk is avoided, but you're still gambling on a lottery, and the whole policy is headed toward the Supreme Court before it's settled — meaning the rules could change again mid-program.

**My honest read, given your specific profile:**
A generic MS bet is bad math for you. You're not a 22-year-old needing a US credential to get a first job — you're an operator with revenue, a shippable product history, and demonstrable infra work. That profile is a much stronger fit for **O-1 (extraordinary ability)** than for F-1→lottery→H-1B roulette. O-1 doesn't need a degree, doesn't go through the cap, and doesn't touch the $100k fee. It needs evidence: press, revenue, notable contributions, judging/speaking, GPU-scale training work — most of which you already have.

If the actual goal is "presence in the US market" (clients, capital, credibility), a master's is the slow, expensive, high-friction path. Cheaper paths: incorporate a US entity, do short B-1/ESTA trips for client work, hire a US-based rep, build the network through conferences/YC-adjacent circles — none of that requires putting your family's house on the line.

If the goal is genuinely "I want the degree/experience for its own sake, money is secondary" — that's a different, non-financial decision, and only you can weigh that against risking a mortgage.

**Bottom line:** as pure ROI, no — don't lever family collateral for a master's you don't technically need, into an immigration system that's still being litigated. If the target is US legal status/market access, look at O-1 first.
