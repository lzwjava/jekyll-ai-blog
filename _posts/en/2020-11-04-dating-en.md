This is advice from someone who has been through it himself. Sometimes, when chatting with younger friends, including middle school students, we end up discussing these topics, and I share a few suggestions. I’m writing this article partly to let loose, express myself freely, share my thoughts, and have a casual conversation with everyone.

When it comes to pursuing a girl, the first thing we need to do is understand women. What kind of creatures are women, really? They are not all that different from us men. Men and women are 99% alike. The more we understand ourselves, the more we understand the opposite sex. And the more we understand ourselves, the more we understand human beings in general.

I once wrote down the following passage:

How do we understand the world? The best way to understand the world is to understand ourselves.

Lately, after doing something, I often pause for a moment and ask myself why I did it. If I do something for a particular reason, plenty of other people must do it for similar reasons. Instead of imagining what other people might like, I observe what I like myself.

It’s like the scripts of many movies we imagine in our heads: they often have little to do with reality. What I come up with in my own mind is also frequently detached from the facts. So I’ve started observing instead.

Likewise, when we discuss how to pursue a girl, a better approach is to observe whom we ourselves are attracted to, how we judge other people, and which people we would agree to date if they pursued us.

Sometimes we men joke that we would date anyone, as long as she’s a woman. But is that really true? Deep down, we naturally want someone who is as attractive and accomplished as possible. We want to find the best person we can get. After all, there is only one spot available: once you find someone, you can’t keep dating other people at the same time.

As we grow older and gain more life experience, our thinking keeps changing. Eventually, we may decide that someone who looks reasonably attractive and is available will do. Later, we may feel that we should just let things happen naturally, and so on.

## My Own Experiences with Love

Let me share some of my own romantic experiences. Naturally, I’ll keep things anonymous and somewhat vague. I’m just telling a few stories.

When I was in middle school, I sometimes chatted with girls on QQ, and some of those conversations got pretty intense. With a few girls, we even arranged to meet up, watch movies, or eat at McDonald’s. At school, we would also send each other text messages.

Once, during a break between classes, a girl was laughing happily at me while we were fooling around. That left a deep impression on me. I kept replaying that beautiful moment in my mind. Yes, it was that feeling of having a crush on someone.

Later, we chatted on QQ, and I confessed my feelings to her there. When she didn’t reply, I called her on the phone and shyly confessed again. But she hung up.

At the time, I was living in the electrical room of a tunnel. I was afraid of disturbing my family while they slept, so I secretly went to the electrical room, some distance away from the bedroom, to make the call.

I was nervous and uneasy as I expressed my feelings, and then I was rejected. I was heartbroken.

I wonder whether my classmates might read this and try to guess who she was. There’s no particular person to identify here. Maybe she was in my class, maybe in another class in the same grade. Perhaps we’re still in touch; perhaps we aren’t. I don’t know.

I wasn’t sure whether I should tell these stories at all, or whether doing so would be disrespectful to the other person. But there’s nothing wrong with it. We both have clean consciences. If I can’t talk about this, and I can’t talk about that, then I don’t know what I can talk about anymore. So let’s carry on.

After the high school entrance examination, I also got very close to another girl through online chatting. We eventually met in person and went to see a movie together.

After the movie, we came down in the elevator. I really wanted to hold her hand, but I never found the courage to do it.

Later, we went to different high schools, and we never did hold hands. When I first started high school, we exchanged text messages and chatted occasionally, but gradually we lost touch.

In high school, quite a few girls in my class were very pretty. I had crushes on a few of them, and I also liked girls in other grades.

My thinking was simple: I wanted to experience being in a relationship while I was in high school. I wanted to see what dating felt like.

I knew lots of people in middle school and high school, both boys and girls. We greeted one another, chatted, and made friends. But when it came to romance, nothing really progressed. I never had a relationship.

When I got to university, I started dating. There were a few stories, a few romantic adventures, and eventually I met a girlfriend with whom I had a particularly strong connection. Three years later, we got married.

That’s all I’ll share for now. These stories aren’t particularly consequential; I’m just telling them casually to give everyone a sense of something real and beautiful.

## Am I a Womanizer?

Am I a womanizer? I don’t think so. I’m just an ordinary, unremarkable person.

If you don’t accept me, then you’re simply one person among thousands upon thousands who might not accept me. Personally, I think I’m still a kind and upright person.

Let’s return to the question: How do you pursue a girl?

Ask yourself: How do other people pursue me? How do I make my own decisions? What kind of person am I attracted to? What kind of person am I myself?

We’ll discover that many of the answers are uncertain and vague. Our answers change at different times. Our thinking changes, I change, and other people change, too.

In an uncertain world, the most effective approach is to experiment quickly and learn from the results.

We should experience more things and try pursuing people we’re attracted to. If we put in our best effort with one person and it doesn’t work out, we can move on and try with someone else.

Being brave enough to take the initiative, without being overly concerned about embarrassment, is an effective strategy for many problems. There are so many women in the world. Surely someone will like me. I might as well make a list of potential partners and keep trying until something works out.

When I went back through my old QQ Space posts and reminisced about the past, I realized something.

It wasn’t that no girls liked me when I was in middle school. It wasn’t that I was unattractive or that nobody wanted to date me. It was that I never even asked.

The girls I invited out might actually have wanted to hold my hand. The problem was that I never took the initiative.

This strategy of being brave and not worrying too much about embarrassment is so effective that I gradually started applying it to many other areas of life.

Suppose I want to make money. My skills are what they are. My code and articles are only as good as they are. Will those limitations prevent me from making money?

No. I can still find users and customers. As I get more users and customers, I receive more feedback and suggestions, and my code and writing gradually improve.

The history of humanity could be described as the history of the brave.

Of course, excessive boldness is not good either. Taken too far, it becomes evil. We should strive to be brave and upright people.

## Learn from Rejection

When we bravely pursue a girl and discover that no matter how hard we try, it simply isn’t working, that is precisely when we have an opportunity to learn.

We can learn a great deal by asking why things aren’t working. Why does this keep happening? Is the problem with me, or is it something about the other person?

Eventually, we discover that different people have different tastes. One person’s meat is another person’s poison.

There might be one girl I can never win over, yet another girl might agree to date me very quickly. I’m still the same person. I haven’t changed. So why is the outcome different?

It’s similar to the world in general. Every kind of job has someone willing to do it. Every kind of public account has people who follow it. All kinds of people find partners.

Why?

Because, broadly speaking, employers who successfully recruit people take the initiative. Writers who build audiences take the initiative. People who find romantic partners take the initiative.

Whether a single man can find a single woman to date depends on many factors: her life experience, values, views on relationships, and current circumstances; whether she wants a relationship at this particular stage of her life; her past romantic experiences; how she understands the world; how well the two people understand each other; her actual expectations for a partner; whether I have the courage to pursue her; and whether practical circumstances, such as living in the same city or a long distance apart, make things easier or harder.

So yes, it’s complicated. People call the combination of all these factors fate.

What should we do when there are so many uncertain variables?

Ask directly. Make your intentions clear. Give it a try.

We may never discover the full truth about why two people end up together. We may never completely understand what makes a relationship work. But by being brave enough to act, we can eventually make things happen.

As we continue taking the initiative, we also discover what kind of person we should become to improve our chances. What makes us more attractive to others? What kinds of people do others generally like?

One simple answer is this: be hardworking, brave, upright, sincere, and kind.

Internet celebrities often succeed because they know how to generate traffic, attract followers, and use proven tactics. We don’t necessarily need to learn from them. We can simply focus on being ourselves.

If we live this way, we can have a clear conscience and earn the respect of our friends. If we don’t, perhaps something is wrong with the world around us.

Gradually, we may discover that one reason a girl isn’t attracted to us is that she simply doesn’t know us well enough.

When we are open and willing to share our lives, even our shortcomings, more people can understand us. We don’t have to share everything publicly. We can open up to a girl privately instead.

If she is willing to keep talking with us, we can gradually understand each other better and build mutual trust.

As we talk, we can also sense how she feels about us. Of course, we don’t need to overthink every detail. If the conversation continues naturally and both people are engaged, there may be potential. We can then take things forward one step at a time. After chatting for a while, we can arrange to meet in person.

When someone is genuinely interested in us, we may discover that everything progresses surprisingly quickly. Pursuing a relationship can be much simpler than we imagined.

## We Don’t Need to Be a “Simp”

We don’t need to become a simp—someone who excessively caters to another person, puts them on a pedestal, and sacrifices their own dignity in the hope of winning their affection.

When we act like a simp, we’ve already lost psychologically. We see ourselves as inferior to the other person, which reflects a lack of confidence.

In Teacher Xiaolai’s new book, Ordinary People’s Entrepreneurship on WeChat Internet, I learned a concept: the long-term value of fair exchange.

It’s just like doing business. We don’t need to grovel before someone. We can simply engage in a fair exchange. That way, we don’t feel wronged, and by maintaining a healthy mindset, we can become more attractive to the other person.

My attitude is simple:

I like you, and I sincerely want to be in a relationship with you. If you agree, I’ll be very happy. If you don’t, I’ll be sad.

But I’ll understand your decision as your personal choice at this particular point in time, not as proof that there is something wrong with me.

I’ll respect your decision. I hope things go well for you, and I hope you have a happy and fulfilling life.

If we become reasonable and understanding people, try our best to understand everything in the world, respect everyone’s choices, consider other people’s perspectives, and make an effort to understand them, won’t it become much easier to work and get along with others?

Won’t more people want to work and spend time with us?

I’ve found that coercion never produces good results, and it certainly doesn’t produce lasting ones. Coercion implies unfairness, and unfairness cannot be sustained over the long term.

Take your time. Learn from every rejection and every experience.

Understand everyone. Be tolerant of everyone. Love everyone. Appreciate everyone. Be willing to talk to everyone and learn from everyone.

We can choose to become this kind of person. But how the world treats us, and how other people respond to us, is beyond our control.

How many people are willing to trust us? How many are willing to treat us the way we treat them? Those are matters for the world to decide.

Sometimes we do foolish things. Sometimes we act impulsively, complain, or criticize aspects of society. Sometimes our words are harsh, and some people may not like us because of that.

But what’s done is done. We don’t need to regret everything we’ve done in the past. We simply need to adjust our mindset and try to face the world with optimism.

Yes, we don’t need to be perfect people. We can just be ordinary people. As long as we don’t cause trouble for others, don’t hurt them, treat our friends kindly, and live our own lives well, that’s enough.

We’re all ordinary people.

## Dating Is a Learning Process

The process of pursuing a girl is itself a learning experience.

We might successfully win someone’s affection without fully understanding why she chose us. Likewise, we may live our entire lives without ever fully understanding the era we live in or why our lives unfolded the way they did.

In my article, [How to Recruit People and Find a Job](http://mp.weixin.qq.com/s?__biz=MzA5NDQ5NDQyOQ==\&mid=2649550407\&idx=1\&sn=918361a19346b5238d82cf3cd0e55d8f\&chksm=885585f5bf220ce37ad4d8d773cb157740b00255e92eda62afb28394287ddf60227a5a01df80\&scene=21\&utm_source=chatgpt.com#wechat_redirect), a friend left a comment saying that recruitment is fundamentally a process of mutual matching.

Your abilities, the other person’s needs, and whether the two sides can satisfy each other’s requirements all matter. Once there is a match, it becomes a question of compensation.

Truly valuable talent generally isn’t found through recruitment alone. Instead, both sides are attracted to each other. By continuously sharing valuable information, employers and candidates can find the right match.

Isn’t dating similar?

Rather than simply chasing attractive, accomplished women, perhaps the better approach is to attract one another by continuously sharing valuable information, allowing compatible men and women to find each other.

The truth is that there are many ways to interpret the world and many explanations for any given event. One reason I write articles is to hear interesting perspectives from my friends.

My wife, for example, initially agreed to go out to eat with me because she had read all my posts on WeChat Moments and was already favorably disposed toward me.

I had been sharing my life and accomplishments on my social feed. She also found reasons to chat with me, and eventually we naturally arranged to have a meal together.

At first, I thought it was because I was impressive. But after spending more time together, I realized that she was the impressive one.

## Why Are More People Single Today?

It seems that more and more people are single these days. Why aren’t people finding partners?

Everyone has their own honest answer.

Perhaps the internet has exposed us to a much bigger world, making us more selective. We may have developed such high standards that we no longer appreciate the ordinary people in our immediate social circles.

Perhaps the pressure of modern life is too great, and we are too busy earning money and surviving.

Perhaps we had relationships in university that ended painfully. We can’t let go of the past, can’t communicate honestly with new partners, and struggle to find someone who accepts us as we are.

Perhaps we’ve become increasingly unwilling to understand other people. We simply want to be liked by lots of people, while the people who like our posts and follow our updates are not necessarily people we like ourselves.

I once said that after making lots of friends, I discovered that the world outside was much like the world I knew when I had only a few hundred WeChat contacts.

As I came to understand myself better, I realized that the world was full of people just like me.

If I behave a certain way, there must be many other people who behave similarly. If I have a particular thought, there must be many others who have the same thought.

Every action I take in the physical world has a purpose. If something serves no purpose, I usually can’t be bothered to do it.

The same applies to the internet. Every time I browse a page, switch apps, swipe up or left, like a post, or leave a comment, there is a reason behind it.

My behavior follows an underlying logic. Even when I say something random or do something seemingly irrational on a whim, there are usually reasons behind it.

I have a friend whom I can chat with for a while and then make a surprisingly accurate guess about how many people are in his WeChat contacts. I can usually get within a hundred people of the actual number, whether he has two or three hundred contacts, five or six hundred, or one or two thousand.

After analyzing someone's social feed for a while, I can also form a rough picture of their personality.

If I ask a friend for help with something, can I predict whether they will help me? How do I judge whether they will?

There is so much to learn about the world. It contains many patterns, but it is also full of randomness.

The strategy of being brave and taking the initiative is so effective precisely because many things in the world are somewhat random. By acting boldly and proactively, we can discover patterns that make our strategies more effective, rather than simply acting blindly.
