**Question:** Is it viable and legal to create an institution in Guangzhou that helps Chinese children prepare for Silicon Valley — covering English, computer science, algorithms, and AI?

**Answer:**

This is actually one of the **best-timed** business ideas you could pursue in China right now. Let me break down what's legal, what's booming, and how to structure it.

---

### 🟢 The Core Opportunity: STEM / AI / Coding = Government-Favored

This is the critical distinction that makes your idea viable. The 双减 (Double Reduction) ban only targets **core academic school subjects** (English, math, Chinese) for K-9 students. Coding, AI, algorithms, and computer science are **explicitly exempt** — and the government is actually pushing for more of it.

AI-related courses from coding to programming are among the few extracurricular courses that are still being offered after the government implemented the Double Reduction policy in 2021. Offline STEM training centers and online coding courses provided by edtech giants have gained popularity among parents who want their kids to stay abreast with new technologies.

China's AI education market is projected to reach 160 billion yuan ($22 billion) by 2027 and nearly 180 billion yuan by 2030, according to domestic analytics firm Changjiang Securities.

China's Ministry of Education issued guidelines in 2025 converting artificial intelligence from an elective curiosity into compulsory infrastructure for every child in the country's school system, beginning at the age of six. Beijing alone now requires more than 1,400 primary and secondary schools to deliver a minimum of eight hours of AI instruction a year.

**This means the government is your tailwind, not your headwind.**

---

### 🟡 The English Problem — And How to Solve It

Standalone English tutoring for K-9 students remains banned. But there's a clean, legal workaround used by successful companies:

**Use English as the *medium of instruction* for tech subjects, not as the subject itself.**

- Teach Python, Scratch, algorithms *in English* → it's a CS class, not an English class
- Frame it as "Silicon Valley Tech English" (tech vocabulary, reading documentation, GitHub usage) → vocational/professional skill, not academic tutoring
- Position everything as "international tech career readiness" — this is a legitimate, unregulated category

Some schools creatively rebranded services as storytelling sessions, reading clubs, or drama and culture camps to avoid direct ties to the core school subjects targeted by regulators. Corporate English training, B2B solutions like business English workshops and workplace communication classes also became more popular.

Similarly, your framing matters: "Learning to read Stack Overflow, GitHub READMEs, and MIT OpenCourseWare in English" is tech training, not English tutoring.

### 📋 How to Structure It Legally in Guangzhou

**Recommended business structure: Two layers**

| Layer | What It Covers | License Needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tech Training Center** | CS, AI, algorithms, coding (Python, C++, Scratch), robotics | 办学许可证 (School License) from Guangzhou Education Bureau |
| **Study Abroad Consulting** | US college CS pathway planning, SAT/AP strategy, program selection | Study Abroad Consulting Registration from Education Dept. |

**Company registration in Guangzhou:**
Since 2021, registering a business in Guangzhou has become dramatically simpler. The entire process of opening a business in Guangzhou runs anywhere from one to three months. Guangzhou insists on a real, physical office address.

**Key requirements for a School License (办学许可证):**

- You need a School License if your business offers any structured educational services to the public in exchange for fees. Operating without one may result in license revocation and fines.
- Physical office + training space at registered address
- Qualified instructors (domestic; foreign instructors require proper work visas)
- The main person in charge must be a Chinese national residing in China
