Question: Should I focus on becoming a generalist in large models or a specialist, given my technical background, learning ability, and ambition to master AI model training and inference?

Answer:

Given your description, you’re in a very strong position: deep technical foundation, fast learning, prior experience with small models, and strong self-discipline. Choosing between being a generalist (“通才”) or specialist (“专才”) in large AI models depends on your goals, risk tolerance, and the value you want to create. Let’s break it down carefully.

---

### 1. **Specialist (专才)**

**Focus:** Master one core aspect of large AI models deeply—e.g., model architecture, training optimization, quantization, inference acceleration, or model evaluation.

**Pros:**

* **High technical leverage:** Your contributions can be recognized as unique, making you hard to replace.
* **Research opportunities:** Cutting-edge papers, novel algorithms, or efficient model techniques often come from specialists.
* **Monetary upside:** If you’re really top in one area, startups and research labs pay premiums.
* **Speed of mastery:** One domain allows faster iteration and deeper understanding.

**Cons:**

* **Risk of narrowness:** If the domain becomes less in demand or saturated, your advantage may shrink.
* **Limited holistic vision:** You may understand training deeply but miss bigger system-level or product-level integration.

---

### 2. **Generalist (通才)**

**Focus:** Understand the full pipeline of large AI models—data, training, inference, deployment, applications, optimization.

**Pros:**

* **Flexibility:** You can work on research, engineering, product, or deployment roles.
* **Strategic advantage:** Can connect research with applications, bridging gaps in industry.
* **Startup leverage:** Generalists can launch initiatives, coordinate teams, and spot opportunities.
* **Future-proofing:** You can pivot between roles as the AI landscape evolves.

**Cons:**

* **Slower technical mastery:** Knowledge is broader but shallower.
* **Harder to be recognized academically:** Papers or breakthroughs often come from specialists.

---

### 3. **Practical Hybrid Strategy for You**

Given your age (~30), strong foundational knowledge, and goal of mastering large models:

1. **Start as a specialist** in one high-leverage area:

   * Examples: efficient training on GPUs, quantized inference, low-parameter model adaptation, or multi-modal alignment.
   * This builds credibility, deep skills, and a portfolio of achievements.

2. **Gradually expand to generalist knowledge**:

   * Once your specialization is strong, broaden to related areas—deployment, distributed training, AI product design.
   * This allows you to be both deep and broad eventually.

3. **Leverage your unique advantage**:

   * Your prior experience with coding, systems, and small models accelerates both training and inference expertise.
   * Document experiments, blog insights, and build reproducible projects; these become evidence of mastery.

---

### 4. **Timing & Focus**

* **Immediate:** Specialist → fast iteration, reproducible experiments, strong technical credibility.
* **Medium-term (1–2 years):** Start bridging to generalist skills to increase market flexibility and strategic insight.
* **Long-term:** Become a “T-shaped” AI expert: deep core expertise + broad adjacent skills.

---

**Bottom line:** Right now, **specialist first** makes sense. It allows you to gain maximum leverage on your learning and career trajectory. Once you dominate one aspect of large model training/inference, the transition to generalist roles will be much easier and more valuable.
