Question: **What are Y Combinator's startup requirements in 2026?**

Answer:

YC does **not have a fixed checklist** like "need revenue / need users / need PhD". They mainly look for:

1. **Strong founders**
2. **A problem worth solving**
3. **Ability to build fast**
4. **Evidence people want it**
5. **Large potential market**

YC accepts applications from startups worldwide. You apply online; if selected, there is an interview. YC currently invests **$500,000 per company**. ([Y Combinator][1])

## 1. Founder requirements

YC likes:

* Technical founders who can build themselves
* People who have shipped products before
* Founders with unusual insight into a problem
* Very high execution speed

You do **not** need:

* Ivy League degree
* Previous startup experience
* Existing company
* Revenue

YC says many funded companies are only at the idea stage; they also fund companies with existing traction. ([Y Combinator][2])

Your background actually matches several YC signals:

* Built products before
* Engineering ability
* AI infrastructure experience
* Trained models
* Real users/community

Those are stronger than a polished business plan.

---

## 2. Startup stage

YC accepts:

### Idea stage

Example:

> "Develop an AI agent that automates enterprise software testing."

Need:

* Clear insight
* Why now?
* Why you?

### MVP stage (best position)

Example:

> "100 developers use our coding agent weekly, retention is 60%."

Much stronger.

### Revenue stage

Example:

> "$20k MRR growing 20% month over month."

Also accepted.

YC FAQ says around 40% of companies funded in each batch can be just ideas. ([Y Combinator][2])

---

## 3. What YC cares about in AI startups in 2026

The current AI wave makes YC especially interested in:

### AI agents

Examples:

* AI employee replacing workflows
* Autonomous research agents
* Coding agents
* Customer support agents
* Sales agents

### AI infrastructure

Examples:

* Model training tools
* Evaluation systems
* Agent memory
* Data pipelines
* Inference optimization

### Vertical AI

Examples:

* AI for lawyers
* AI for doctors
* AI for factories
* AI for finance

A generic "ChatGPT wrapper" is weak.

A better YC pitch:

> "We build AI agents that replace junior software engineers for internal enterprise tools."

---

## 4. Application questions you should prepare

Typical YC application asks:

### Company

"Describe what your company does in 50 characters."

Bad:

> "AI-powered next generation intelligent platform"

Good:

> "AI agents that maintain Kubernetes clusters"

---

### Problem

Why this problem?

Bad:

> "AI will change everything."

Good:

> "I spent 5 years operating production systems. Every week engineers waste 10 hours debugging Kubernetes failures."

---

### Progress

Show:

* Users
* Revenue
* GitHub stars
* Experiments
* Deployment
* Growth

Numbers matter.

---

### Why you?

The strongest answer:

> "I built X because I personally experienced Y."

---

## 5. What YC interview looks like

Usually:

* ~10 minutes
* Fast questions
* No slides

They test:

* Do you deeply understand users?
* Can you explain simply?
* Are you moving fast?

Example:

YC:

> Why will users choose you?

Weak:

> "Our AI is more advanced."

Strong:

> "Existing tools require 3 hours of setup. Ours connects GitHub and fixes issues automatically in 5 minutes."

---

## 6. Current application timing

YC runs multiple batches. For 2026 batches, applications are online and late applications may still be considered after deadlines, although YC recommends applying early. ([Y Combinator][1])

## For your situation (AI agent company)

A YC-style application could be:

**Company:**

> "AI employees for software engineering teams"

**Problem:**

> "Small engineering teams cannot hire enough developers."

**Product:**

> "Agents that code, test, deploy, and monitor production services."

**Why me:**

> "I have 10+ years building backend systems and have trained LLMs locally."

**Traction target before applying:**

* 20 real users
* 5 paying customers
* GitHub open-source adoption
* Demo video

The biggest YC mistake is spending 6 months preparing an application. Build first, apply second.
