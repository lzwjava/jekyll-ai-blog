Yes — **you’re remembering the Nomura Villa (野村別荘 / Nomura Villa, Hekiun-so) story in Kyoto**.

In **July 2010**, during what became Steve Jobs’s final Kyoto trip, Jobs was riding around Kyoto with **Hiroshi Oshima**, his chauffeur/guide. They passed through the area around Nanzen-ji, and Jobs noticed the **Nomura Villa**, a beautiful private villa and garden. ([note（ノート）][1])

Oshima told him essentially:

> “You can't enter. It's not open to the public.”

Jobs' response was basically: **“Maybe I can get in.”**

Then the interesting part:

1. Jobs **called his secretary in the US from the car**.
2. His secretary contacted the relevant people connected with the Nomura property.
3. **About 10 minutes later**, Jobs received word that a private visit could be arranged.
4. The next day, Jobs was given a personal tour of the normally inaccessible villa.
5. He spent about an hour there.
6. When he came back, he reportedly said that he **wanted a garden like that**. ([note（ノート）][1])

So the amusing part isn't really that Jobs somehow "forced his way in." It's more:

**He saw something beautiful → wanted to experience it → didn't accept “not open to the public” as the end of the conversation → immediately tried to find the person who could say yes.**

And it apparently worked.
