Question: You're asking whether the Internet can be viewed as an ecosystem where high-value creators (KOLs, mid-tier creators, developers, educators) produce code and content, and sustained influence comes from consistently delivering value over time.

Answer:

Yes, I think that's a useful mental model, with one important addition.

The Internet is not just an information network—it's a **market for attention, trust, and knowledge**.

A simplified flow looks like:

```text
Experience
    ↓
Practice
    ↓
Knowledge
    ↓
Content / Code / Products
    ↓
Distribution (Search, Social, Communities)
    ↓
Audience
    ↓
Feedback (comments, stars, forks, purchases, subscriptions)
    ↓
More practice
```

The creators at the center are not only KOLs. They include:

* researchers publishing papers
* open-source maintainers writing code
* founders sharing company-building lessons
* engineers blogging about debugging
* YouTubers explaining concepts
* newsletter writers
* short-video creators

They continuously convert **experience → reusable knowledge**.

The Internet provides the infrastructure that makes this scalable.

Another way to think about it is:

```text
Internet
├── Information layer
├── Social graph
├── Search/indexing
├── Recommendation algorithms
├── Communities
└── Economic layer
```

A creator succeeds when they repeatedly produce value that survives these layers.

Your examples fit well:

* Li Xiaolai — investing, learning methods, Bitcoin, writing.
* Y Combinator — startup knowledge through essays, videos, founders, and software.
* Luo Yonghao — speeches, entrepreneurship, livestream commerce.
* Dedao ("得到") — structured paid learning.
* WeChat Official Accounts — long-form articles.
* Personal blogs.
* GitHub repositories.
* YouTube.
* TikTok/Douyin.
* X.

One refinement I'd make is that **followers are not evidence of value by themselves**.

Long-lived influence usually means the creator consistently delivers one or more kinds of value:

* **Knowledge value** — "I learned something."
* **Practical value** — "I solved a problem."
* **Economic value** — "I made or saved money."
* **Entertainment value** — "I enjoyed this."
* **Emotional value** — "I felt understood or motivated."
* **Network value** — "I met useful people."

Creators who remain influential for many years often provide several of these simultaneously.

An interesting trend with AI is that the bottleneck is shifting.

Before:

```text
Knowledge → Human writes → Human reads
```

Now:

```text
Knowledge
    ↓
AI structures it
    ↓
Agents retrieve it
    ↓
Humans consume only what's relevant
```

So increasingly, creators are publishing not only for humans, but also for AI systems that retrieve, summarize, and execute on their knowledge. High-quality code, documentation, blogs, and discussions become training data or retrieval corpora for future agents.

In that sense, the Internet is evolving from a **human knowledge-sharing network** into a **human–AI knowledge ecosystem**, where durable, well-structured content has even greater leverage than before.
