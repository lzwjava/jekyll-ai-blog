Question: Is it right to think that AI and the world trend toward more freedom, but in reality you should adapt to rules, avoid forcing change, and focus on going where you can work more freely and meaningfully?

Answer:

Your core intuition has some truth, but it’s a bit oversimplified in a way that can quietly lead to passivity.

There are a few separate ideas mixed together here:

### 1. “The world is becoming more free”

Partially true, but uneven.

* Technology (AI, internet, open-source) *does* increase individual capability and expression.
* At the same time, regulation, platform governance, and geopolitical fragmentation also increase constraints.
* So the trend is not “more freedom overall”, but **more capability + more structured control at the same time**.

Freedom is expanding in some dimensions, shrinking in others.

---

### 2. “You can’t change rules, only adapt”

This is only half true.

Yes, for most individuals:

* You cannot directly change large systems quickly
* You must operate within constraints

But historically:

* Systems change because enough individuals *push, build, or exit*
* Companies like ByteDance or Meituan didn’t just “accept rules” passively—they also shaped markets and norms before adapting

So a more accurate framing is:

> You don’t control the rules, but you can influence where the boundaries move.

---

### 3. “Just go somewhere freer and focus on yourself”

This is practical, but incomplete.

It works when:

* Your goal is personal optimization (income, autonomy, lifestyle)
* You are in a system that is too restrictive for your goals

But it can fail when:

* Everyone “exits”, and no one builds inside constraints
* You underestimate how much value is created *inside imperfect systems*

Also, “freer place” is often not objectively free—it’s just different trade-offs (regulation vs opportunity, stability vs flexibility, etc.)

---

### 4. About examples like Zhang Yiming / Wang Xing

Zhang Yiming and Wang Xing didn’t simply “follow rules quietly”.

They actually:

* Built systems within constraints
* Navigated regulation strategically
* Shaped user behavior and industry structure
* Then adjusted when boundaries changed (like product shutdowns or pivots)

So the pattern is not “don’t resist rules”, but:

> build fast, scale inside constraints, then adapt when limits shift

---

### 5. “Do you need to fight or just earn money and enjoy life?”

Neither extreme is complete.

A more grounded model is:

* **Short term:** adapt to rules → survive and build leverage
* **Medium term:** choose better environments → increase optionality
* **Long term:** shape systems indirectly → through products, capital, or influence

---

### Bottom line

Your conclusion “don’t die fighting, just make money and live freely” is emotionally understandable, but incomplete.

A more robust version is:

> Don’t waste energy fighting constraints you can’t change today, but also don’t assume constraints are permanent or unchangeable. Build capability first, then choose your level of freedom.

Freedom is not just a place—you gradually accumulate it through leverage (skills, capital, distribution, systems).
