**Question:** How has U.S. import/export data trended in recent years, and what does it reflect about the economy?

**Answer:**

Here's a comprehensive look at U.S. trade data from 2024 through mid-2026, and what it signals economically:

---

### 📊 Overall Trade Balance

For full-year 2025, the U.S. goods and services trade deficit was $901.5 billion — essentially flat from $903.5 billion in 2024. Exports reached $3,432.3 billion (up ~$200B), while imports totaled $4,333.8 billion (also up ~$198B).

Despite sweeping tariff policies, the overall deficit barely moved — it was still the **third-highest on record** since 1960. The goods deficit alone widened 2% to a record **$1.24 trillion**, driven largely by surging imports of AI-related semiconductors and tech goods from Taiwan.

In 2026, the picture improved somewhat: the cumulative trade gap in the first half of 2026 narrowed to $371.2 billion, down from a record $560.5 billion in the same period the previous year, suggesting trade flows are gradually normalizing after the tariff-driven import front-loading of 2025.

---

### 🏭 Goods vs. Services: A Structural Split

The U.S. economy has a consistent two-track trade profile:

The goods deficit increased $25.5 billion (2.1%) to $1,240.9 billion in 2025, while the services surplus grew $27.6 billion (8.9%) to $339.5 billion. This reflects America's strength in financial services, software, IP licensing, and tourism — versus its reliance on foreign manufactured goods.

Capital goods exports — covering semiconductors, aircraft, and telecom equipment — rose 9.9% in 2025, and total exports set a record at $3.4 trillion, up 6.2%.

---

### 🌏 Key Trading Partners & Shifts

The deficit with the EU narrowed (to $219B from $236B) and the China deficit shrank sharply (to $202B from $296B) — but trade was redirected elsewhere. The gap with Vietnam jumped 44% to $178B, and Taiwan's deficit doubled to $147B.

In 2024, the top import sources were Mexico (15.2%), China, and Canada, with machinery, electrical equipment, and vehicles being the leading import categories.

---

### 📉 Tariff Policy & Its Effects

In calendar year 2025, the U.S. collected $264 billion in customs duties — up from $79 billion in 2024 — after the Trump administration imposed multiple rounds of new tariffs. However, the strategy had mixed results:

- Companies front-loaded imports in Q1 2025 ahead of tariffs, then slowed purchases — with October 2025 registering the lowest monthly deficit since 2009.
- In February 2026, the Supreme Court struck down tariffs imposed under IEEPA, requiring collected revenues plus interest to be returned to importers.
- The manufacturing sector continued losing jobs, with no evidence of meaningful reshoring underway.

---

### 💡 What It Reflects About the Economy

| Signal | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Persistent ~$900B deficit | Strong domestic consumer demand; U.S. absorbs global goods |
| Record goods deficit | Heavy AI/tech investment pulling in chips and hardware |
| Growing services surplus | U.S. competitive edge in finance, IP, and digital services |
| Trade diversion from China → Vietnam/Taiwan | Supply chain restructuring, not elimination of imports |
| Tariff revenue up, deficit unchanged | Structural trade imbalances not easily fixed by tariffs alone |

Real disposable income grew 1.6% in 2025, supporting consumer spending — though economists caution that attributing economic shifts solely to trade policy is complex, as macroeconomic data reflects many variables.
