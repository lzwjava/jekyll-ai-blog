Question: How do you think Hong Kong has changed in recent years, especially regarding economy, education, media, and startup environment?

Answer:

There *are* real changes in Hong Kong over the past 5–10 years, but some of the perceptions you mentioned mix accurate signals with exaggerations or incomplete interpretations. A clearer picture looks like this:

---

## 1. Economy: slowdown, but not “collapsed”

Hong Kong has definitely experienced **weaker growth compared to pre-2019**:

* The **real estate market has corrected** after years of strong growth.
* **Retail and tourism** were hit hard by COVID and are still recovering.
* The government has run **fiscal deficits for several years**, partly due to pandemic spending and slower revenue (especially land sales and stamp duties).

However:

* Hong Kong still has **large fiscal reserves** (tens of billions USD equivalent).
* It is not “out of money” or structurally bankrupt.
* The issue is more about **drawdown of reserves + slower revenue growth**, not exhaustion.

So “government became poor” is not accurate, but “financial pressure increased” is fair.

---

## 2. Education: budget pressure, but not extreme cuts

The claim that education spending was cut by ~50% is very likely **incorrect or misinterpreted**.

What is closer to reality:

* Overall government spending has been under pressure, so **growth of education budget may have slowed or been adjusted**
* Schools face **cost optimization**, especially due to demographic decline (fewer students)
* Some programs may feel reduced, but it is not a simple “halving of education funding”

Also, Hong Kong is currently facing:

* **Falling birth rates → shrinking student population**
* That naturally leads to **school consolidation pressure**

---

## 3. Media environment: more “local issues focus”

Platforms like HK01 and similar outlets tend to:

* Focus heavily on **crime, transport incidents, public disputes, and lifestyle news**
* This creates a perception of “no big news”

In reality:

* There is still significant coverage of economy, China integration, tech, etc.
* But **political + structural topics are more sensitive than before**, so coverage patterns have shifted

So your observation (“mostly transport / consumer council / small incidents”) reflects a **real shift in media tone**, but not absence of news.

---

## 4. Social atmosphere & National Security Law (NSL)

Since 2020, the NSL has changed the **boundary of public discourse**:

* People are generally **more cautious about public expression**
* Certain topics are less openly debated than before
* Online and offline speech tends to be more self-regulated

This does not mean “nothing can be discussed,” but:

* The **risk sensitivity is higher**
* So discussions often become more practical, less political

---

## 5. “Social behavior enforcement” (filming / complaints / public shaming)

What you described (people filming minor behaviors and posting online):

* This is not unique to Hong Kong
* It is also common in other dense cities (e.g., China mainland, Singapore, parts of US/EU)

But Hong Kong does have:

* High population density
* Strong “rule-based civic expectation culture”

So small public disputes sometimes get amplified online.

---

## 6. Startup / AI environment

This is actually the **most interesting shift**:

Hong Kong is not a pure Silicon Valley-style startup hub, but:

**Advantages:**

* Strong finance system (funding, banking, IPO access)
* Gateway between China and global markets
* Increasing support for **fintech and AI policy initiatives**

**Constraints:**

* Smaller local tech talent pool compared to Shenzhen / Beijing
* Regulatory sensitivity in certain domains
* Less “wild experimentation culture” than US startups

So for AI startups:

* Hong Kong is better as a **finance + internationalization base**
* Not necessarily the best place for deep technical R&D alone

---

## Overall summary

A more accurate framing is:

* ❌ Not “Hong Kong is collapsing”
* ❌ Not “everything is cut by 50%”
* ✔️ But: slower economy, tighter fiscal environment, structural adjustment
* ✔️ Media shifted toward local/practical topics
* ✔️ Public discourse is more cautious than before
* ✔️ Startup ecosystem is evolving toward finance + China-international bridge role
