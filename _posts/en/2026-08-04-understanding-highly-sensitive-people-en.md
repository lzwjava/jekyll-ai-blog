**Question: What kind of people are "highly sensitive people" (高敏感人 / Highly Sensitive Person, HSP)?**

**Answer:**

A **highly sensitive person (HSP)** is someone whose nervous system processes sensory, emotional, and social information more deeply than average. The concept was introduced by psychologist Elaine Aron in the 1990s. It is **not a mental disorder**; it is considered a personality trait related to deeper processing and stronger responsiveness to stimuli.

A common model is **DOES**:

### 1. D — Depth of processing (深度加工)

They tend to think deeply before acting.

Examples:

* Analyze many possible outcomes before making decisions.
* Notice subtle differences others ignore.
* Spend a lot of time reflecting on experiences.
* Ask "why" and "what does this mean?" frequently.

Example:

> Someone says a short "ok" in a message.
> A normal person may ignore it.
> A highly sensitive person may think: "Did I upset them? Is something wrong?"

---

### 2. O — Overstimulation (容易过载)

Because they process more information, they can become overwhelmed.

Triggers:

* Loud environments
* Crowded places
* Too many meetings
* Constant notifications
* Multitasking
* Lack of sleep

After a busy day, an HSP may need solitude to recover.

---

### 3. E — Emotional responsiveness and empathy (情绪反应强、共情强)

They often:

* Easily sense other people's moods.
* Feel other people's pain strongly.
* Become emotionally affected by movies, stories, animals, or injustice.
* Notice tension in a room.

Example:
A colleague says "I'm fine" but sounds different. An HSP may immediately notice.

---

### 4. S — Sensory sensitivity (感官敏感)

They may notice:

* Bright lights
* Strong smells
* Clothing texture
* Background noise
* Temperature changes

---

## Common strengths

Many HSP traits are valuable:

| Trait               | Advantage                           |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Deep processing     | Better analysis and problem solving |
| High empathy        | Better leadership and communication |
| Attention to detail | Good engineering, design, research  |
| Strong intuition    | Detect patterns early               |
| Creativity          | Rich imagination                    |

Many researchers, artists, engineers, writers, and entrepreneurs show some HSP characteristics.

---

## Common challenges

### 1. Decision fatigue

Because they consider many factors:

```
Choice A:
  benefits...
  risks...
  future impact...

Choice B:
  benefits...
  risks...
```

They may spend much longer deciding.

---

### 2. Emotional exhaustion

They may absorb other people's emotions:

```
Someone nearby:
    stressed
        ↓
HSP detects stress
        ↓
HSP internally experiences stress
```

---

### 3. Need for recovery time

A social event can be enjoyable but still exhausting.

Example:

```
Party:
  3 hours talking
        ↓
Brain processing:
  faces
  voices
  emotions
  conversations
        ↓
Need:
  quiet time
```

---

## HSP vs introvert

They overlap but are different:

|                    | Introvert      | HSP                          |
| ------------------ | -------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Main issue         | Social energy  | Sensory/emotional processing |
| Can enjoy parties? | Yes, sometimes | Yes, but may get overwhelmed |
| Alone time         | Recharge       | Reduce stimulation           |

A person can be:

* Introvert + HSP
* Extrovert + HSP
* Introvert + not HSP

---

## HSP and high performers

A lot of high-performing people have similar traits:

* They notice small system problems.
* They think about second-order effects.
* They spend time understanding fundamentals.
* They care deeply about quality.

The downside is that without boundaries, the same system can become:

```
high observation
      +
high processing
      +
high responsibility
      =
mental overload
```

The key skill is learning to control input:

* Reduce unnecessary noise.
* Protect deep-work time.
* Build recovery periods.
* Separate "notice something" from "must solve something".

A useful mental model:

> HSP is like having a high-resolution sensor.
> The advantage is seeing more detail; the challenge is filtering the noise.
