Question: Why do some people have difficulty reading?

Answer: There are several reasons why reading can be hard for people:

**Neurological & Learning Differences**
- **Dyslexia** — the most common reading disorder, affecting how the brain processes written language. Letters or words may appear jumbled or reversed.
- **ADHD** — difficulty sustaining attention makes it hard to follow lines of text.
- **Processing speed issues** — the brain takes longer to decode words, making reading slow and exhausting.

**Vision Problems**
- Uncorrected refractive errors (nearsightedness, astigmatism) blur text.
- Convergence insufficiency — the eyes struggle to work together, causing double vision or eye strain.

**Language & Literacy Gaps**
- Limited vocabulary makes comprehension slow even when decoding is fine.
- Reading in a second language adds cognitive load.
- Lack of early reading exposure leaves foundational skills underdeveloped.

**Environmental & Physical Factors**
- Poor lighting, low-contrast text, or small fonts increase strain.
- Fatigue and stress reduce the brain's ability to concentrate.
- Anxiety around reading (common in people with past struggles) creates a mental block.

**Cognitive Load**
- Working memory limitations make it hard to hold earlier parts of a sentence while finishing it.
- Reading is actually a *very complex* skill — it requires simultaneously decoding symbols, building meaning, and maintaining comprehension.
