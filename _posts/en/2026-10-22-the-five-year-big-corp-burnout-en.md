Question: Why do people say that after 5 years in a big corporation, a person becomes "废了" (ruined/burned out)?

Answer:

This is a widely discussed phenomenon in Chinese tech/work culture, especially about large companies (大厂) like Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, Baidu, etc. The core idea: the very things that make big corps comfortable slowly erode the capabilities that make you competitive outside of them. Here's why:

**1. Over-specialization / skill atrophy**
Big corps divide labor extremely finely. You own one tiny module, one button, one data pipeline. After 5 years you're world-class at that slice — but can't do the whole thing solo. Your breadth quietly disappears.

**2. Platform dependency illusion**
Your "achievements" ride on the company's brand, traffic, resources, and internal tools. Take all that away, and the skill that remains is often smaller than you think. Outside, the resume line says "Alibaba" but recruiters quickly find the underlying ability is narrow.

**3. Salary trap**
5 years of annual raises means your compensation expectations are calibrated to big-corp levels. Startups can't match it, smaller companies won't, and the big corp itself starts quietly replacing you with cheaper junior talent around age 30–35.

**4. Risk tolerance collapses**
Stable salary, good benefits, clear processes — you stop tolerating ambiguity. Joining an early-stage startup or starting something yourself feels genuinely terrifying, not exciting. The psychological muscle atrophies from disuse.

**5. Bureaucracy becomes your real skill**
You master internal politics, OKR alignment, cross-team negotiation, and getting things approved. These are real skills inside the company — they're nearly worthless outside it.

**6. Age discrimination runs on a short clock**
Chinese tech culture skews young (the "35-year-old curse" 35岁危机 is a separate but related concept). By the time you've spent 5+ years at one place, you're in the age window where being laid off becomes statistically likely — and re-hiring becomes harder.

**7. Disconnection from market reality**
Inside a big corp, you optimize for internal metrics. You stop tracking what customers actually need, what the competitive landscape looks like, or how to hustle for business from scratch. The outside world moves while you're insulated.

---

**The counterargument** worth noting: plenty of people spend 5+ years at big corps and *thrive* afterward — especially those who deliberately rotate roles, build cross-functional skills, maintain side projects, and network outside the company. "废了" isn't inevitable; it's a risk pattern for people who go on autopilot inside the comfort zone.

The saying is really a warning about **passive drift**, not a verdict on the companies themselves.
