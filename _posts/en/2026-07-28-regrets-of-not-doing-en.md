Question: **What do people most regret when they get old and look back on life?**

Answer:

Across many interviews, studies, and end-of-life reflections, the most common regrets are surprisingly not about money, failed businesses, or mistakes. They are usually about **things people did not do**.

The recurring themes:

## 1. Not having the courage to live their own life

A very common regret:

> "I lived the life others expected from me, not the life I wanted."

People spend decades optimizing for:

* parents' expectations
* social status
* stable jobs
* approval from others

Then later realize they rarely asked:

> "What would I choose if nobody judged me?"

This is probably the deepest regret.

---

## 2. Not taking more risks when they had the chance

Many older people regret:

* not starting the company
* not moving to another country
* not learning a difficult skill
* not asking someone out
* not making a bold career move

Not because every risk would have succeeded, but because they never discovered what they were capable of.

A failed attempt usually becomes a story.

A never-attempted dream becomes a question:

> "What if?"

---

## 3. Spending too much time working, too little time with people

A famous pattern among people near the end of life:

They rarely say:

> "I wish I had spent more nights at the office."

They say:

* "I wish I had been more present with my children."
* "I wish I called my parents more."
* "I wish I spent more time with friends."

The tragedy is that relationships have a time window.

A child is only 5 years old once.

A parent is only healthy for a limited number of years.

---

## 4. Not taking care of their body

People often underestimate compound effects.

At 30:

* sleep seems optional
* health seems permanent
* eating badly seems harmless

At 70:

* mobility becomes freedom
* health becomes wealth

Many regret:

> "I traded my health for things that did not matter."

---

## 5. Caring too much about other people's opinions

A lot of energy is wasted on:

* "What will people think?"
* "Will they laugh?"
* "Will I look stupid?"

Older people often realize:

Most people were too busy worrying about themselves to judge you.

---

## 6. Not becoming excellent at something

Many regret staying average because they avoided discomfort.

The pattern:

Age 20:

> "I have plenty of time."

Age 40:

> "I should have started earlier."

Age 60:

> "Why didn't I spend 10 years mastering something?"

Ten years of focused effort creates extraordinary results.

---

## 7. Not expressing love

People regret unsaid things:

* "I love you."
* "Thank you."
* "I'm sorry."
* "I forgive you."

Pride feels important at the moment.

Later it often feels meaningless.

---

For entrepreneurs and builders, there is another specific regret:

> "I had an idea, but I was afraid."

Many successful founders say their biggest advantage was not intelligence — it was willingness to look foolish for a while.

The difference between many people at 70:

Person A:

> "I tried many things. Some failed. Some worked. I have stories."

Person B:

> "I was always waiting for the right time."

The "right time" is often a trap.

A good life is usually not built by avoiding mistakes. It is built by **choosing which mistakes are worth making**.
