**Early Life & Origins**

Pieter Levels is originally from the Netherlands. A 2014 Wired profile described him as a 28-year-old Dutch programmer — which puts his approximate birth year around **1986**. He grew up in Amsterdam and is Dutch by nationality.

**Entrepreneur at Age 12**

His entrepreneurial instinct showed up very early. At 12 years old, he persuaded his father to sign a Worldpay contract with liability clauses worth up to $100 million. The business never materialized, but the ambition was clearly there from childhood.

**Early Interest in Computers & the Web**

He developed an early interest in computers and programming, which led him to create his first website at just 12 years old. This initial foray into web development sparked a passion that would shape his future.

**University Education**

Pieter Levels is an alumnus of RSM — the Rotterdam School of Management at Erasmus University Rotterdam. He studied Communication and Multimedia Design. After studying, he initially explored traditional career paths, but soon realized that the constraints of a corporate job were incompatible with his ambitions and lifestyle.

**First Money: YouTube & Music**

After university, he had a stint as a successful drum and bass DJ on YouTube. This PandaMix channel — a network of electronic music channels — became his first real income source, peaking around $8,000/month, then declining to $500–900/month as copyright claim disputes piled up.

**Self-Taught Developer**

Despite having a business/design education rather than a computer science degree, Pieter Levels is a self-taught developer. His development philosophy emphasizes simplicity and functionality over complex programming, and he has stated: "Programming is not my passion. Making stuff is my passion."

**The Nomad Phase**

After university and his YouTube years, Levels sold his possessions and began traveling the world. He has traveled to over 40 countries and 150 cities, embodying the digital nomad lifestyle. This nomadic experience directly became the raw material for his most successful products — Nomad List and Remote OK were both born from his own lived needs on the road.
