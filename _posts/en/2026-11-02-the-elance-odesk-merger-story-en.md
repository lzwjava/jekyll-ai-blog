**Question:** What is Upwork's founding story, why did the founders create it, and who are the founders?

---

**Answer:**

Upwork's story is actually the story of **two separate companies** — **Elance** (1998) and **oDesk** (2003) — that competed for a decade, then merged in 2013 as Elance-oDesk, and finally rebranded as **Upwork in 2015**.

---

## 🏢 Part 1: Elance (1998) — Digitizing the Talent Marketplace

**Founders:** Beerud Sheth, Srini Anumolu, and Sanjay Noronha

Elance was founded in 1998 by MIT graduate Beerud Sheth and Wall Street veteran Srini Anumolu in a two-bedroom apartment in Jersey City.

Beerud Sheth studied Computer Science at IIT Bombay, and it was along with his IIT classmates — Srini Anumolu and Sanjay Noronha — that he started Elance, an online freelance marketplace that eventually became Upwork.

**Why did they build it?** Elance was started out of a Harvard Business Review article published by professor Tom Malone in 1998–1999, titled *"The Dawn of the Elance Economy."* Elance was founded to digitize the RFP (request-for-proposal) process for hiring specialized talent — bringing the way companies hired specialist talent entirely online.

Beerud Sheth evangelized remote work long before it became a global reality, leading the company as its founding CEO and later as VP, raising $75M+ across 3 founding rounds and navigating the massive 2001 dot-com financial crisis.

## 🏢 Part 2: oDesk (2003) — Born From a Personal Problem

**Founders:** Odysseas Tsatalos and Stratis Karamanlakis

oDesk was founded in 2003 by two friends, Odysseas Tsatalos and Stratis Karamanlakis, who wanted to work together even though one of them was in the U.S. and the other was in Greece.

**Why did they build it?** The story is remarkably personal:

Odysseas Tsatalos had co-founded a San Jose company called Intacct (cloud accounting software). He wanted to hire his software-engineer friend Stratis Karamanlakis, who lived in Athens and didn't want to move to the U.S. But Intacct's CEO vetoed the idea, saying it would be too hard to oversee remote work. Tsatalos took that as a challenge, and started building a diary/tracking system to help remote workers document their progress for verification by supervisors. Eventually the CEO ran out of objections and hired Stratis — but by then, Tsatalos's software looked like a commercializable product. So he left Intacct to start oDesk, bringing Karamanlakis on as co-founder.

Tsatalos created a program that allowed screen-sharing with his high school friend in Greece, enabling his boss to monitor the work being produced by Stratis Karamanlakis. The program was enhanced with screen capture and time-tracking features — and they realized it might have broader appeal, so they started oDesk.

---

## 🤝 The Merger → Upwork (2013–2015)

The 2013 merger of Elance and oDesk formed a platform now handling over $4 billion in annual gross services volume and serving more than 30% of Fortune 500 firms. Their complementary approaches — Elance's RFP-style marketplace and oDesk's time-tracked transparency — set the stage for a consolidated industry leader.

The merged company was subsequently rebranded as Upwork in 2015, and Upwork went public in 2018.
