Short answer: **no, it's not true.** The page's central claim — that wearing glasses 1.50D below your full prescription reverses myopia — is contradicted by the clinical evidence.

## What the evidence says

- The key randomized trials (Chung 2002, Adler 2006, Vasudevan 2014) and two large systematic reviews all found undercorrection either **does nothing or slightly worsens** myopia progression. (PubMed, Cochrane Library) [cochranelibrary](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD014758.pub2/full)
- The **WSPOS 2023 consensus** and the **International Myopia Institute 2025** both state undercorrection "should no longer be advocated" and may increase progression. (WSPOS, PMC) [wspos](https://www.wspos.org/swdcore/uploads/WSPOS-Myopia-Consensus-Statement-2023-1.pdf)
- A 1.50D undercorrection is *larger* than what's been studied (0.50–0.75D), and a 2020 review found undercorrection above ~1.0D is especially likely to worsen myopia. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7356996/)

## The telling detail

Your own prescription table undercuts the claim. From Nov 2024 to Aug 2025, both eyes **worsened again** (left −2.50→−3.00, right −4.25→−5.00), rebounding back toward 2023 levels — exactly what the literature predicts for undercorrection. The page still claims "100 degrees reduction," but the latest data point contradicts that.

## What IS accurate

Near work genuinely contributes to myopia, and bifocal/multifocal lenses (with a near add over a *fully corrected* distance prescription) can modestly slow childhood progression. Those are real, but they don't support the "wear weaker glasses to reverse myopia" thesis — which relies on Todd Becker and Yin Wang, neither of whom are credentialed vision researchers and whose claims aren't accepted by the ophthalmology field.
