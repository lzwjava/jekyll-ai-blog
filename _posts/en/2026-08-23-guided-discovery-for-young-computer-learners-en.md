Question: What's an effective way to teach young children to use a computer?

Answer:

The approach you used is actually a well-established learning method — **guided discovery**. Here's why it works so well for young kids:

- **Low-stakes repetition** — scrolling YouTube Shorts is forgiving. There's no "wrong" answer, so a 5-year-old can try, fail, and try again without frustration.
- **Instant reward** — every successful scroll shows a new fun video, which is a tiny dopamine hit that reinforces the behavior.
- **Hand-over-hand start** — you guided her first, then stepped back. This is called *scaffolding* in child development: provide just enough support, then remove it as confidence grows.
- **Intrinsic motivation** — she *wanted* to see the next video, so the learning felt like play, not a lesson.

The key insight you stumbled onto: **let the child's curiosity drive it, and let them try again freely.** That beats any formal tutorial for a 5-year-old.

A few natural next steps when she's ready:

- Clicking links or buttons (slightly more precise than scrolling)
- Simple keyboard typing (her name, letters she knows)
- Using a trackpad vs. a mouse — kids often find trackpads more intuitive
