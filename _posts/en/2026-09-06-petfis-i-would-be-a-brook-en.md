**Question:** Introduction to "我愿是一条急流" (*I Would Be a Brook / I Would Be a Rapid Stream*) by Petőfi (Sándor Petőfi / 裴多菲)

**Answer:**

**About the Poet**

Sándor Petőfi (1823–1849) was born in Kiskőrös, Hungary. He disappeared on the battlefield and probably died on July 31, 1849, in Segesvár, Transylvania — at just 26 years old. He is considered Hungary's national poet. His reputation rests not only on the beauty of his lyric poems but also on his direct involvement in Hungary's revolutionary movement. His poem "National Song" was recited at public gatherings to rouse the Hungarian people to fight for their freedom from Austrian rule.

**About the Poem**

"我愿是一条急流" (*I Would Be a Brook / a Rapid Stream*) is one of Petőfi's celebrated love lyrics. It belongs to the tradition of his intimate, personal mode of writing — distinct from his overtly political poems. Central to the poem is the speaker's willingness to transform into any element of nature necessary to remain united with his beloved, reflecting Romantic ideals of love as both redemptive and all-consuming. Each stanza is built on a conditional "if," establishing a pattern of self-surrender; the speaker commits to unity with the beloved regardless of circumstance, even extremes of heaven or damnation.

Like other Romantic poets of his time, Petőfi became fascinated by the lives of rural people and idealized them in his writing. His verse was inspired by ancient forms of expression, with a melodic simplicity mirroring the ballads and folk songs of rural Hungary. This poem uses natural imagery — rivers, streams, forests, rocks — recalling the unspoiled Hungarian countryside, to create a plea for eternal love.

The poem's imagery cascades through paired elements: if she is a flower, he will be the tree; if she is flame, he will be the brook that flows beside her — nature itself becomes the language of devotion. The brook (急流, "rushing stream") symbolizes restless, passionate, unstoppable love.

**Reception in China**

Petőfi had a significant cult following in China at the beginning of the 20th century because of his poems about national freedom. Chinese progressive writers and intellectuals of the era turned to his work, and he had many translators in those years. Petőfi is a household name in China to this day, and "我愿是一条急流" remains one of the most beloved poems in Chinese schools and literary culture.

**Petőfi's Life and Love**

His romantic experiences were always all too brief. His first romance ended with his beloved's death just two months after their first meeting. He married Júlia Szendrey in 1846, but their happiness was short-lived — he died fighting for Hungarian independence only three years later, at 26. This poignant biography gives the poem's passionate longing for union a deeply moving resonance.
