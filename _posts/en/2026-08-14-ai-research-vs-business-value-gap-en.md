**Question: Why do many AI papers fail to create value, while simple AI education businesses (e.g. teaching parents/kids to use Codex) can make millions of CNY quickly?**

Answer:

This is a classic gap between **scientific value, technical value, and market value**.

AI papers optimize for **knowledge frontier**. Businesses optimize for **pain + willingness to pay + distribution**. They overlap, but they are not the same.

A simple model:

```
Research value:
    new idea → publication → citations → maybe product years later

Business value:
    customer pain → trusted solution → distribution → revenue
```

The second path can be much faster.

---

### 1. Papers create options, not necessarily products

A research paper might improve:

* attention efficiency by 5%
* a new optimizer
* a new architecture
* a new benchmark score

But to become a business, it needs:

```
paper
  ↓
engineering
  ↓
product
  ↓
distribution
  ↓
customer trust
  ↓
payment
```

Most papers die between step 1 and step 2.

For example:

A researcher invents a better transformer initialization.

Scientifically:

```
loss: 2.31 → 2.28
training cost: -8%
```

Great.

But a parent does not wake up thinking:

> "I need better transformer initialization."

A parent thinks:

> "My kid is falling behind in AI. How do I help?"

The second problem has money attached.

---

### 2. The AI education market is selling anxiety + opportunity

Parents are paying for:

* "Will my child fall behind?"
* "How can my kid use AI before others?"
* "Will coding still matter?"
* "How do I prepare for the AI era?"

This is a very strong emotional driver.

The founder is not really selling Codex.

They are selling:

```
future advantage
+
parent confidence
+
a roadmap
```

The underlying technology can be simple.

---

### 3. Distribution beats technology surprisingly often

Imagine two people:

Person A:

* PhD
* publishes a new LLM paper
* nobody knows him

Person B:

* creates a WeChat group
* has 20,000 parents
* teaches AI tools
* charges 999 CNY

Person B may make millions.

Why?

Because:

```
Revenue = Value × Distribution × Trust
```

Technology is only one multiplier.

A mediocre product with huge distribution can beat a superior product with zero distribution.

---

### 4. AI creates a temporary "information asymmetry"

Right now, AI knowledge changes faster than society can absorb.

Most parents:

```
know:
  ChatGPT exists

don't know:
  agents
  Codex
  Claude Code
  AI coding workflows
  local models
  AI learning methods
```

The gap creates opportunity.

The founder acts as a translator:

```
AI frontier
    ↓
simple language
    ↓
parent action
    ↓
money
```

This happens in every technology wave:

Internet:

* people made money teaching "how to build websites"

Mobile:

* people made money teaching apps

Crypto:

* people made money teaching wallets/trading

AI:

* people make money teaching AI usage

---

### 5. But this does not mean papers are useless

Many businesses are built on invisible research.

Example:

Transformer paper (2017)

```
research paper
      ↓
OpenAI / Google / Anthropic engineering
      ↓
ChatGPT
      ↓
AI education businesses
```

The education founder is often standing on a mountain built by researchers.

The value chain looks like:

```
Fundamental research
        ↓
Infrastructure
        ↓
Models
        ↓
Tools
        ↓
Applications
        ↓
Education / services
```

The lower layers usually monetize earlier.

---

### 6. The startup lesson

A common mistake among engineers/researchers:

> "I built something technically impressive, why doesn't anyone pay?"

Because customers do not buy technology.

They buy outcomes.

Compare:

Bad:

> "We built an autonomous coding agent with 15% better SWE-bench score."

Better:

> "Your child can build their first AI app in 30 days."

The second one has a customer.

---

For someone with your background (training models, building agents, running AI communities), the interesting intersection is probably not only research. It is:

```
deep AI capability
        +
ability to explain AI
        +
community/distribution
        +
productization
```

That combination is rare.

Karpathy can explain neural nets.
YC founders can build distribution.
Many AI educators can sell.

The strongest AI companies of the next few years will likely combine all three.
