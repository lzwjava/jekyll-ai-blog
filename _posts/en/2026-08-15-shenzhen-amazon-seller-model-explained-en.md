Question: How does the Shenzhen Amazon seller model work, and how do sellers register a company and register in Hong Kong?

Answer:

**Why Shenzhen dominates Amazon selling**

Shenzhen is China's biggest hub for Amazon third-party sellers — some estimates put it at up to a quarter of all Amazon third-party sellers since the city is home to numerous e-commerce industrial parks such as China South City, and provincial and municipal governments have actively promoted "cross-border e-commerce" (CBEC) because it counts as exports, which China's government wants to grow. This gave rise to the "made in China, sold on Amazon" model, where Shenzhen manufacturers and trading companies based in e-commerce industrial parks sell electronics and other goods directly to overseas consumers via Amazon.

**Why sellers use a Hong Kong company instead of a mainland entity**

Many Shenzhen-based sellers don't register their Amazon account under their mainland Chinese business — they set up a Hong Kong Limited Company instead, for several practical reasons:

1. **Regulatory scrutiny on mainland entities has increased.** As of October 2025, a new Chinese regulation (State Council Order No. 810) requires Amazon to report sellers' store cash flow, identity information, and account details to Chinese tax authorities. This caused a roughly 40% drop in approval rates for mainland Chinese entities, and Amazon also added a "triple penetrating" verification check on registered addresses, payment accounts, and billing addresses — meaning shell companies and mismatched addresses now frequently trigger account suspensions.
2. **Tax efficiency.** A Hong Kong company gives a territorial tax system, so sellers can legally pay 0% tax on profits earned outside Hong Kong (offshore profits), plus stronger IP protection and higher credibility for Amazon's KYC/KYB checks.
3. **Amazon compatibility & credibility.** Registering under a Hong Kong limited company is generally the most reliable path for a Professional Seller account — Amazon asks for a Certificate of Incorporation and a Business Registration Certificate to verify the company, which speeds up manual review and gives legal separation between personal and business finances.
4. **Banking and payouts.** Hong Kong sellers can receive Amazon payouts through a Hong Kong bank or a platform like Airwallex/Payoneer, converting and settling funds locally rather than dealing with a mainland bank account.

**How the Hong Kong company registration actually works**

- **Incorporation is fast.** Standard incorporation of a Hong Kong company usually takes 1–3 business days, though setting up the full compliance structure — company secretary, bank account, etc. — typically takes about 2 weeks.
- **What you need:** a company name, at least one director/shareholder (can be the same person, no residency requirement), a registered Hong Kong address, and a company secretary (mandatory under HK law — most sellers use a licensed formation agent for this).
- **Ongoing compliance:** annual costs include the business registration fee, company secretary fees, and a statutory audit. It's important to use a properly licensed provider — a TCSP (Trust and Company Service Provider) licensed agent — since Amazon's KYC/KYB checks scrutinize whether the company is a genuine, compliant entity.

**Registering the Amazon Seller Central account with the HK company**

Once the company exists, the Amazon Seller Central signup needs:
- **Business Registration Certificate (BRC)** and **Certificate of Incorporation (CI)** — both showing current validity, to verify the company is actively registered.
- **Government-issued photo ID** of the account's primary/legal representative.
- **A bank or credit card statement matching the company name** — a recent bank or credit card statement in the company's name, to show consistency between the business name and account details, plus often a utility bill in the company's name as address verification.
- **An internationally chargeable credit card** for seller subscription fees.
- **A bank account** to receive Amazon disbursements (directly in HK, or via a payments platform).

One nuance worth flagging: some Shenzhen operators register the HK company with a Hong Kong registered address but actually operate out of Shenzhen. This can complicate the utility-bill/address verification step — Amazon forums show sellers debating whether a Shenzhen utility bill is accepted when the bank has the company's correspondence address listed as Shenzhen even though the legal registration is in Hong Kong. Given Amazon's tightened address-verification checks (see the "triple penetrating" review mentioned above), keeping the registered address, bank correspondence address, and utility bills consistent is now more important than it used to be.

**A caveat on ownership**

If you're a US citizen (or a company 10%+ US-owned), this route isn't available — Amazon requires that non-US ownership be accurately declared, and if the company turns out to be 10% or more US-owned, Amazon will require you to restart registration as a US-based account instead.
