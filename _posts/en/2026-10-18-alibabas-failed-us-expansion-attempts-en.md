**Question:** Did Alibaba once try to expand into the US market but eventually fail and retreat? (Around the 2000s?)

**Answer:**

You're right that Alibaba has had multiple failed US expansion attempts, though the timeline is more accurately the **2010s** rather than the 2000s. Here are the major ones:

---

**1. "11 Main" — The B2C Marketplace Failure (2014–2015)**

This is likely the story you're thinking of. Following Alibaba's massive $25 billion IPO in September 2014, the company launched **11 Main** — a US consumer marketplace similar to Etsy, targeting small boutique sellers.

Alibaba envisioned 11 Main as a shop-window for small-time US merchants as an alternative to mass-consumer-facing Amazon and eBay. However, merchants grew disenchanted — furniture retailer Lishu Rodriguez quit the site after about a month, saying it "seemed amateur, almost."

After reportedly failing to gain traction in the market, Alibaba sold 11 Main to US firm OpenSky **just one year after launching it**, in June 2015, retaining only a minority stake.

---

**2. Alibaba.com B2B US Expansion — Scaled-Back Retreat (~2019–2022)**

Alibaba launched its B2B ecommerce site, Alibaba.com, in the US with the ambition of signing up more than **1 million local businesses** and competing globally with companies like Amazon.

The reality was much harsher: most subscribers cancelled after just a year on the site — which cost US$3,000 annually — partly because they struggled to compete with prices from sellers in other parts of the world. Meanwhile, more than half of Alibaba.com's team in New York quit within a year and many of their roles were not filled, in what was seen as a **quiet retreat from the US market**.

The company had to revise its growth target from over 1 million merchants down to just **2,000 merchants per year** — which one analyst called "a stunning admission of failure."

---

**3. Cloud Business Pullback (2015+)**

Alibaba moved into the US cloud market in 2015 with an eye toward grabbing market share from AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure, offering deep discounts to entice customers — but its initial efforts didn't live up to expectations, and it later put those expansion plans on hold.

---

**Summary:** Alibaba has repeatedly tried and failed to break into the US consumer and business markets. The core problems were a brand unknown to American consumers, intense competition from Amazon/eBay/AWS, and difficulty convincing US businesses that the platform offered real value.
