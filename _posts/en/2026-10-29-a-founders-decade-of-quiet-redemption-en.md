Yeah, genuinely one of the more dramatic entrepreneurial stories in China.

The arc is pretty wild: he co-founded Joyo.com (卓越网) with Lei Jun, sold it to Amazon for $75M, then launched VANCL in 2007 and rode it to a $3 billion valuation and 13,000 employees. He was *that close* to an IPO. Then it all collapsed — overexpansion, a 1.4 billion RMB inventory crisis, losses piling up, the IPO scrapped.

The part that really stands out: he racked up roughly **1 billion RMB in personal debt** and spent **10 years quietly paying it back**, finishing around 2022. No fanfare, no bankruptcy filing, just grinding it down year by year while largely disappearing from the public eye. That kind of long, silent penance is unusual — a lot of founders in his position would have walked away.

And then the Hangzhou chapter — moving a once-mighty company with "the whole team" of three or four people to pivot to Douyin livestreaming. There's something both humbling and kind of admirable about it. He framed it as his "Lei Jun building cars" moment — using his old friend's comeback as inspiration.

What makes his story feel genuinely tough rather than just a business failure is the *personal* weight of it. The debt was real, the decade of paying it back was real, and Lei Jun describing him as a "榜一大哥" (top gifter in his livestream) has this bittersweet quality — the guy who once ran a billion-dollar company, now cheering on his old friend's success from the sidelines, and trying to rebuild with four people.

Resilient, though. That's the other word for it.
