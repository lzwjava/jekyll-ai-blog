Question: Why did He Qinglian, author of *The Collapse of China* (*China: Crumbling But Not Collapsing*), go to the United States?

Answer:

He Qinglian went to the United States mainly because of **political pressure and concerns about personal safety**, not simply for academic or economic opportunities. ([He Qinglian][1])

Her background:

* She was a Chinese economist, writer, and journalist.
* She became famous in China after publishing The Pitfalls of Modernization (*现代化的陷阱*), which criticized corruption, inequality, and problems in China's reform process. ([He Qinglian][1])
* Later she wrote works criticizing media control and political structures in China. ([He Qinglian][1])

Around 2001, she left China. According to her own accounts and reports at the time, she believed she was under surveillance and feared possible arrest. She had originally planned overseas academic activity, but after increasing pressure she departed for the United States. ([ecoi.net][2])

The book you mention is probably:

China: Crumbling But Not Collapsing (*中国：溃而不崩*).

The core argument is not "China will suddenly disappear"; rather, she argues that China's system may continue to function politically while suffering long-term structural problems such as:

* demographic pressure,
* debt and economic distortions,
* corruption,
* weakening social trust,
* political rigidity.

([He Qinglian][1])

Why the US specifically?

1. **Freedom to publish and research**

   For writers who criticize governments, the US provides a large Chinese-language intellectual community, universities, think tanks, publishers, and media platforms.

2. **Personal safety**

   After leaving China, she could continue writing without the same level of domestic restrictions she experienced before leaving. ([ecoi.net][2])

3. **Access to global audiences**

   Her research became part of international China studies. She also participated in academic activities in the US. ([Minjian Dangan Guan][3])

A broader pattern: many Chinese intellectuals, scientists, entrepreneurs, and dissidents moved abroad after the 1980s–2000s because the US offered a combination of:

* universities,
* funding,
* publishing freedom,
* professional networks,
* immigrant pathways.

But their reasons varied greatly. Some moved for career opportunities; others, like He Qinglian, were more directly connected to political conflicts. ([ecoi.net][2])
