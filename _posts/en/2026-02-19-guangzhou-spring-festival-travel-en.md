Question: Where should I go and what should I see in Guangzhou during the Spring Festival week?

Answer:

Guangzhou transforms into a vibrant "Flower City" during the Spring Festival (Chinese New Year), offering a unique blend of traditional Cantonese customs, spectacular light shows, and festive markets. Since the holiday usually falls in late January or early February, the weather is generally mild, making it perfect for outdoor exploration.

Here are the top recommendations for where to go and what to see during Spring Festival week in Guangzhou:

### 1. Traditional Flower Markets (The Heart of Cantonese CNY)

No Spring Festival in Guangzhou is complete without visiting the **Flower Fairs (Huashi)**. It is a century-old tradition where locals buy flowers, kumquat trees, and decorations to symbolize prosperity.

* **Tianhe Flower Fair:** The largest and most modern fair, located near the CBD. It features massive floral sculptures and is great for photos.
* **Liwan Flower Fair (at Liwan Lake Park):** Offers a more traditional, "Old Guangzhou" vibe with folk arts and classic cantonese snacks nearby.
* **Haizhu Flower Fair:** Known for its long history and proximity to the Pearl River.
* *Tip:* These markets usually operate for 3 days leading up to New Year's Eve. They are crowded but essential for the atmosphere.

### 2. Spectacular Light Shows & Landmarks

* **Canton Tower & Zhujiang New Town:** The area around the Canton Tower and the skyscrapers of Zhujiang New Town usually feature elaborate lighting displays and drone shows throughout the festival week. Walking along the **Huacheng Square** offers the best view of the illuminated skyline.
* **Pearl River Night Cruise:** Taking a boat cruise on the Pearl River allows you to see the city's festive lights from the water. Many boats offer special New Year dinner packages.

### 3. Cultural & Historical Sites

* **Chen Clan Ancestral Hall (Chenjiaci):** This masterpiece of Lingnan architecture is often decorated with intricate paper-cuttings and clay figurines specific to the New Year. It houses the Guangdong Folk Art Museum.
* **Temple of the Six Banyan Trees (Liurong Temple):** A historic Buddhist temple where many locals go to pray for good fortune in the coming year. The atmosphere is solemn yet festive with incense and red ribbons.
* **Shamian Island:** A former concession area with European-style architecture. It is beautifully decorated with lanterns and flowers, offering a quieter, picturesque walk compared to the bustling city center.

### 4. Family Fun & Modern Attractions

* **Guangzhou Chimelong Tourist Resort:** If traveling with family, this is a top destination. It includes the Chimelong Paradise (theme park), Chimelong Safari Park, and Chimelong International Circus. They always have special Spring Festival parades and performances.
* **Yuexiu Park:** Home to the iconic Five Rams Statue (symbol of Guangzhou). During the festival, the park hosts large-scale lantern exhibitions and flower displays.

### 5. Culinary Experiences

* **Morning Tea (Yum Cha):** Visiting famous restaurants like **Guangzhou Restaurant**, **Panxi Restaurant**, or **Lianxiang Lou** for morning tea is a ritual. Expect long queues, so arrive early or book in advance. Look for special New Year dishes like turnip cakes and shrimp dumplings.
* **Beijing Road Pedestrian Street:** While shopping, you can try various street foods and visit the ancient road ruins visible under the glass pavement. The street is heavily decorated with lanterns.

### Practical Tips for Spring Festival Week

* **Crowds:** Expect significant crowds at all major attractions, especially from the 2nd day to the 7th day of the lunar new year.
* **Transport:** The metro is the most efficient way to travel, though some lines may be very packed. Note that some bus routes may change schedules during the holiday.
* **Red Packets (Hongbao):** If you interact with service staff or receive gifts, be prepared for the custom of exchanging red envelopes (though mostly given by elders to juniors or employers to employees).
* **Closures:** While major tourist sites and malls remain open, some small, family-owned restaurants and shops may close for a few days during the first three days of the New Year.
