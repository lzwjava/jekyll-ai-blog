Question: Is reading aloud an effective way to improve focus and comprehension?

Answer: Yes, absolutely. Reading aloud engages multiple cognitive channels simultaneously — your eyes see the text, your mouth produces the words, and your ears hear them. This multi-sensory loop makes it much harder for your mind to wander, which is why focus naturally improves.

A few reasons it works so well:

- **Pacing control** — speaking forces you to slow down to a natural rhythm, reducing the tendency to skim over things you didn't fully absorb.
- **Active processing** — your brain has to "perform" the sentence, not just passively scan it. This deepens encoding and retention.
- **Immediate feedback** — if something doesn't make sense, you notice it right away because it sounds wrong or awkward when spoken.
- **Reduced internal distraction** — the verbal channel occupies the part of your mind that usually wanders off into unrelated thoughts.

For AI-generated answers specifically, this is a great habit. AI responses tend to be dense with information, and reading them aloud forces you to process each sentence rather than glossing over paragraphs.

The tradeoff is speed — you read roughly 3–4x slower when speaking aloud. But if comprehension and retention matter more than throughput, the slowdown is worth it. A lighter version is **subvocalizing with intention** — mouthing the words quietly — which captures some of the same benefit without disturbing others.
