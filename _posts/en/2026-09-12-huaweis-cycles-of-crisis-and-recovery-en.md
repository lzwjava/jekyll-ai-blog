Question: Has Huawei actually experienced several waves (周期/cycles) of major technical challenges and comebacks throughout its history?

Answer: Yes, absolutely. Huawei's history is a textbook example of recurring technology crisis-and-recovery cycles. Here are the major ones:

**Wave 1 — R&D Chaos Cycle (Late 1990s)**
In the late 1990s, as Huawei's business scaled rapidly, its product line expanded significantly, causing protracted development cycles, compromised quality, and sluggish response speeds. R&D departments operated in isolation, neglecting user needs, and product development relied heavily on individual "hero" engineers rather than systematic processes. Huawei responded by adopting IBM's Integrated Product Development (IPD) framework. After five years of IPD implementation, the average product R&D cycle was shortened from 84 weeks to 54.5 weeks, and the product failure rate dropped from 17% to 1.3%.

**Wave 2 — First US Pressure Cycle (2018–2019)**
The US placed Huawei on the Entity List, and key partners began severing ties. With US companies barred from doing business with Huawei, Google could no longer license its Android operating system to Huawei. ARM also cut ties, threatening the Kirin chip architecture.

**Wave 3 — Semiconductor Blockade (2020–2022)**
This was the most severe wave. Tightened sanctions cut Huawei off from TSMC, the Taiwanese semiconductor foundry which manufactured Huawei's 5G chips. The few phones Huawei shipped during this period relied on remaining stockpiles of Kirin 9000 chips or Qualcomm Snapdragon processors purchased under limited licenses — chips restricted to 4G — meaning Huawei could not sell 5G phones even as the entire industry was transitioning to 5G as a baseline feature.

**Wave 4 — Domestic Reinvention & Recovery Cycle (2023–Present)**
Huawei refused to accept defeat. CEO Ren Zhengfei reportedly told employees that the sanctions would force Huawei to do what it should have done earlier: build independent capabilities in every critical technology domain. In 2023, TechInsights' teardown analysis confirmed that SMIC manufactured the Kirin processor inside the Mate 60 Pro — the device that catalyzed Huawei's return to the 5G smartphone market after years of restrictions.

So yes, the 周期 (cyclical pattern) is clear: **crisis → internal restructuring → technological breakthrough → renewed growth**, repeated multiple times across Huawei's ~37-year history. Each wave was more geopolitically intense than the last, and each time Huawei emerged with deeper vertical integration and self-reliance.
