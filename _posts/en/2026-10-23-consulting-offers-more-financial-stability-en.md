**Question:** Is software consulting a more financially stable choice compared to building products, given that many products eventually shut down?

**Answer:**

You're making a pretty sound observation, and honestly it's one that doesn't get enough credit in tech circles.

**Why consulting gets dismissed unfairly**

The startup/VC culture has created a narrative where "building a product" is noble and "doing consulting" is somehow settling. But that framing mostly serves people who want you to take on risk *for them* (investors, etc.). In reality:

- Consulting generates **predictable, immediate cash flow** — you invoice, you get paid.
- Products can take years to turn profitable, and most *do* eventually die — market shifts, competition, changing technology, founder burnout, or just bad timing.
- Even beloved products get sunset. Google kills products regularly. Startups with funding still close.

**The honest math**

| | Consulting | Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue timeline | Fast (weeks) | Slow (months/years) |
| Failure rate | Low (if you can sell) | High (~90% of startups fail) |
| Ceiling | Scales with headcount | Can scale infinitely (in theory) |
| Survival after 5 years | High | Low for most |

**The real trade-off**

Consulting doesn't scale *geometrically* — more revenue usually means more people. Products *can* scale without proportional costs. That's the actual appeal of products, not some moral superiority.

But "can scale" ≠ "will scale." Most don't. So your logic holds: **a consulting business that survives 10 years beats a product that raised $2M and shut down in year 3.**

**A middle path many miss**

Many strong tech businesses use consulting *to fund* product development — do client work to keep the lights on, use margin to build something proprietary on the side. 37signals (now Basecamp) did exactly this. ThoughtWorks built a consulting empire worth billions.

Bottom line: there's nothing wrong with consulting. It's a real business. The people who look down on it are usually either chasing VC funding or haven't made payroll themselves.
