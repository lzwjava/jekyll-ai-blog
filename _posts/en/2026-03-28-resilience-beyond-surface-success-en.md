Question: Is it true that the apparent success of others—like startups with big funding or people with high salaries—can be misleading, and that we should focus on building stability, independence, and resilience in our own careers rather than envying surface-level success?

Answer:

Yes, that perspective is very accurate. External markers of success—funding rounds, high salaries, flashy titles—often tell only part of the story. Many startups with rapid growth collapse under hidden pressures like debt, poor management, unrealistic expectations, or market volatility. Similarly, individuals with high incomes or prestigious positions may face personal stress, lack of autonomy, or unsustainable work-life balances.

Focusing on **stability and resilience** is crucial for long-term growth:

1. **Financial and career independence:** Build skills, networks, and resources that don’t rely solely on one employer, one client, or one funding source. This could include side projects, personal savings, or learning versatile technical and soft skills.

2. **Assess hidden costs:** Recognize that visible success can come with invisible trade-offs—stress, ethical compromises, overwork, or unstable structures. Understanding these helps you make more informed decisions about your own career.

3. **Sustainable startup growth:** For entrepreneurs, prioritize product-market fit, customer retention, operational resilience, and healthy team culture over chasing rapid funding or valuations. This strengthens the core, making the venture more resistant to shocks.

4. **Resilience mindset:** Consider “what if” scenarios—losing a job, funding drying up, market shifts. Build fallback options and cultivate adaptability so your career or startup can survive disruptions.

5. **Personal growth and independence:** Invest in your health, learning, and decision-making autonomy. These provide stability that external validation or income alone cannot.

In essence, true success is often less about speed or flashy metrics and more about building a strong, resilient foundation that can withstand challenges, grow sustainably, and maintain independence. Envying surface-level success can mislead you into chasing outcomes that aren’t durable.

It’s like comparing a glass tower and a solid brick house: the tower may look taller, but the brick house survives storms.
