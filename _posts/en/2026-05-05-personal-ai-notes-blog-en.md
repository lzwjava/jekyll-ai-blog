Question: What do you think about creating a personal AI-generated notes blog (like mine with 8000 answers), and what are the legal concerns and how others might perceive it?

Answer:

Your idea is directionally solid—but it’s important to separate *what it is* from *what it could become*, and to be realistic about both legal and social implications.

---

## 1. What your blog actually is (in essence)

At its core, your project is:

* A **personal knowledge base (PKM)**
* Generated via **AI-assisted thinking (prompt → answer → archive)**
* Published as a **public corpus of reasoning and notes**

That’s closer to:

* A “thinking log” or “intellectual trace”
* Not the same as a trained AI model
* Not equivalent to what companies like OpenAI or xAI do

So your comparison to “models trained on the internet” is **partially right but slightly off**:

* Big models → *train on raw data, compress into weights*
* You → *generate explicit outputs and publish them*

You’re not training a model—you’re **externalizing cognition**

That’s actually interesting.

---

## 2. Legal concerns (this is where you need to be careful)

### (1) Copyright / content ownership

Main risk:

* If your notes include:

  * Direct quotes
  * Paraphrased proprietary content
  * Summaries of paid materials (books, courses)

Then potential issues:

* **Copyright infringement**
* Especially if:

  * Content is recognizable
  * Not transformed enough
  * Used at scale (8000 notes is not small)

**Safe zone:**

* Your own reasoning
* Highly transformed explanations
* Original synthesis

**Risk zone:**

* “AI summarized X book/article” repeatedly
* Copy-like structure or phrasing

---

### (2) AI-generated content ownership

In most jurisdictions:

* Pure AI-generated content → **may not be fully copyrightable**
* But:

  * If *you curate, edit, structure* → stronger claim

Practically:

* You can publish it
* But enforcing ownership may be weak

---

### (3) Terms of service (LLMs)

Depending on tools you used:

* Some platforms restrict:

  * Bulk generation
  * Republishing at scale

Most modern tools allow it, but:

* Worth checking if you used APIs or platforms heavily

---

### (4) Data privacy / sensitive info

Avoid including:

* Private conversations
* Company/internal info (especially since you work with banks)
* Any identifiable personal data

This is **higher risk than copyright**, especially professionally.

---

## 3. How others will perceive it

This splits into **3 very different audiences**:

---

### (A) Builders / engineers

They’ll think:

* “Interesting dataset of thinking”
* “Could be useful if structured well”
* “But raw dumps are noisy”

What earns respect:

* Curation
* Depth
* Original insight

What reduces value:

* Mass AI outputs without filtering

---

### (B) General audience

They’ll likely think:

* “This is too much”
* “Hard to navigate”
* “Feels like AI spam”

8000 notes = **overwhelming without strong structure**

---

### (C) Serious thinkers / researchers

They’ll be more critical:

* “Is this original thinking or AI echo?”
* “Where is the signal vs noise?”
* “What is the author’s real contribution?”

They value:

* Compression (not expansion)
* Insight density

---

## 4. The real risk (not legal, but strategic)

The biggest issue is **not legality—it’s dilution**

If you publish everything:

* You become a **high-volume node**
* Not a **high-signal node**

In the AI era:

> People don’t value who produces the most
> They value who filters the best

---

## 5. How to evolve this into something powerful

Right now:

* You have **raw material**

To make it valuable:

### Step 1 — Layer structure

* Group by themes
* Build “learning paths”
* Connect ideas

### Step 2 — Add your voice

* What do *you* think?
* Where do you disagree with AI?

### Step 3 — Compress

Turn 8000 notes into:

* 100 essays
* 20 frameworks
* 5 core ideas

This is where value emerges.

## 6. Big picture

Your instinct is actually aligned with something deeper:

* You’re building a **personal cognitive dataset**
* Similar to:

  * Research logs
  * Lab notebooks
  * Thinking archives

But:

* Raw logs ≠ knowledge
* Curation = intelligence

* Strategically: high potential if you **shift from volume → insight**
