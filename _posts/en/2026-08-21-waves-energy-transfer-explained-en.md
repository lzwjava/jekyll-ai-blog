Question: What is a wave?

Answer: A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another through a medium (or in some cases, through a vacuum), without permanently displacing the matter itself. The particles in the medium oscillate around their equilibrium positions while the energy propagates forward.

**Key types of waves:**

- **Mechanical waves** — require a physical medium to travel (e.g., sound waves, water waves, seismic waves)
- **Electromagnetic waves** — require no medium and can travel through a vacuum (e.g., light, radio waves, X-rays)
- **Transverse waves** — particles oscillate perpendicular to the direction of propagation (e.g., light, waves on a string)
- **Longitudinal waves** — particles oscillate parallel to the direction of propagation (e.g., sound)

**Core properties of waves:**

| Property | Description |
|---|---|
| **Wavelength (λ)** | Distance between two consecutive crests or troughs |
| **Frequency (f)** | Number of oscillations per second (Hz) |
| **Amplitude** | Maximum displacement from equilibrium |
| **Speed (v)** | How fast the wave travels; v = fλ |
| **Period (T)** | Time for one complete oscillation; T = 1/f |

Waves also exhibit behaviors such as **reflection**, **refraction**, **diffraction**, and **interference** — phenomena that are fundamental to optics, acoustics, and quantum mechanics.
