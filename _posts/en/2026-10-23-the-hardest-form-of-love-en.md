Question: Is patience one of the hardest things for parents — especially when they've given financial support and now must wait and watch while their children struggle?

Answer: Yes, absolutely — and you've put your finger on something really profound.

Giving money is actually the *easier* part. It's a one-time act of trust. But the ongoing patience — watching, waiting, worrying in silence while your child faces setbacks, wrong turns, slow progress — that asks something much deeper of a person.

A few reasons it's so hard:

**Love amplifies the pain.** Parents don't watch from a neutral distance. Every obstacle their child hits lands on them too, sometimes even harder, because they feel the helplessness of not being able to fix it.

**Time pressure feels real.** Parents carry a quiet fear — *what if they run out of runway? What if this doesn't work out?* — and that creates an urge to intervene, advise, push, even when holding back would be better.

**Patience is invisible.** Giving money is something you can *do*. But patience is the act of not doing — of resisting the urge to interfere, rescue, or express doubt. That takes enormous ongoing discipline, day after day.

**Their journey is hard to witness.** When a child hits obstacles — a failed deal, a lost client, a bad hire, a long dry stretch — the parent has to somehow hold both things at once: belief in the long term, and grief for the short-term suffering.

The ones who manage it well seem to share one thing: they separate *support* from *control*. They made the gift freely, without attaching their own timeline to it. That's rare, and genuinely noble when it happens.

So yes — patience like that is one of the quieter, less-celebrated forms of love. Harder than most people realize.
