Question: What is the best approach to developing products and why is it more effective than overdesigning?

Answer:

Your observation reflects a core principle in modern product development: **start with yourself as the user, iterate with feedback, then scale**. The approach works in several stages:

1. **Build for Yourself First**

   * Creating a product that you personally love ensures authenticity.
   * You deeply understand the pain points and the value because you are experiencing it firsthand.
   * This helps avoid building features that are unnecessary or irrelevant.

2. **Early Feedback from a Small, Interested Group**

   * Invite a few people who are genuinely interested in the problem your product addresses.
   * Collect concrete feedback on usability, value, and features.
   * Focus on *learning* what resonates, rather than assuming what people want.

3. **Iterate and Improve**

   * Use feedback to refine the product, removing friction and enhancing the parts users love.
   * Small, incremental improvements are faster to implement and easier to validate than large overhauls.

4. **Gradually Expand the User Base**

   * Once a core group loves the product, it naturally spreads via word of mouth.
   * Scaling is smoother because the product has been validated and refined for real users.

**Why this method becomes faster over time:**

* Early iterations prevent wasted effort on features no one uses.
* Feedback loops accelerate as the product grows because you continuously learn what works.
* You avoid “analysis paralysis” and overdesign, which often stalls projects indefinitely.

**The problem with overdesign:**

* Teams often try to anticipate all user needs upfront.
* This can create a product that is bloated, confusing, or disconnected from actual user desires.
* Without real-world validation, the product may never evolve, and users never truly adopt it.

In short: **Start small, validate quickly, iterate, then scale.** It feels slow initially but compounds into rapid, meaningful growth as the product matures. This is the principle behind methods like **Lean Startup** and **customer-centric design**.

You could also view it as a *momentum curve*: the slower, careful start builds a foundation that accelerates the growth and adoption curve later.
