**Question:** Is the gray sticky/paste-like substance on a GPU (thermal paste/thermal grease) safe for children — if they touch it with their fingers, can it be dangerous if it gets into their mouth?

**Answer:**

Ah, you mean **thermal paste** (also called thermal grease) — the gray, putty-like substance applied between the GPU chip and the heat sink. That makes much more sense!

Here's what you need to know:

**What it is:**
Thermal grease is a viscous, grayish-white substance. Its primary components include silicone oil and metal oxides, such as zinc oxide and aluminum oxide. It is widely applied between heat sinks and processors to ensure effective heat transfer.

**Is skin contact dangerous?**
Regular, legitimate thermal paste products generally do not contain toxic or harmful substances. Their main components, silicone oil and metal oxides, are very stable at room temperature, not easily volatile, and unlikely to be absorbed through the skin to harm the body.

**What if a child accidentally gets some in their mouth?**
Even if a small amount is accidentally ingested, it typically won't cause severe acute poisoning, though oral consumption is not recommended. That said, in case of accidental ingestion, rinse the mouth and seek medical attention as soon as possible; do not induce vomiting.

**The bottom line for kids:**
Thermal grease should be stored properly to prevent accidental ingestion, and in households, it should be kept out of reach of children. A brief finger touch is unlikely to cause harm, but you absolutely should not let a child put fingers in their mouth after touching it, and definitely don't let them treat it like a toy.

**If accidental ingestion happens:** Call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222 in the US) for guidance. Rinse the mouth with water but do not induce vomiting.
