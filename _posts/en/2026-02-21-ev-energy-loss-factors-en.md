The **energy loss ratio** for an electric vehicle (EV) is typically expressed as **energy consumption per distance traveled**, such as kilowatt-hours per 100 kilometers (kWh/100 km). A consumption of **15 kWh per 100 km** is a reasonable and efficient figure for many modern EVs, but the actual value depends on several factors:

---

### **Typical Energy Consumption for EVs**

- **Efficient EVs (e.g., Tesla Model 3, Hyundai Ioniq 6):**
  **12–16 kWh/100 km**
  These models are optimized for aerodynamics, weight, and regenerative braking.

- **Average EVs (e.g., Nissan Leaf, Volkswagen ID.4):**
  **16–20 kWh/100 km**
  Slightly higher due to less aggressive efficiency optimizations.

- **Larger/Heavier EVs (e.g., Tesla Model X, Rivian R1T):**
  **20–25 kWh/100 km**
  Higher energy use due to weight, size, and sometimes less efficient aerodynamics.

---

### **Factors Affecting Energy Loss**

1. **Driving Conditions:**
   - **City driving** (frequent stops, regenerative braking) is more efficient than **highway driving** (higher speeds, wind resistance).
   - **Cold weather** can increase consumption by 20–30% due to battery inefficiency and heating needs.

2. **Vehicle Design:**
   - **Aerodynamics:** Streamlined shapes reduce drag.
   - **Weight:** Heavier vehicles require more energy.
   - **Tire resistance:** Low rolling resistance tires improve efficiency.

3. **Driver Behavior:**
   - Aggressive acceleration and braking increase energy use.
   - Moderate speeds (80–100 km/h) are optimal for efficiency.

4. **Battery and Powertrain Efficiency:**
   - Modern EVs convert ~80–90% of battery energy to wheel power, compared to ~20–30% for internal combustion engines.

---

### **Is 15 kWh/100 km Good?**

- **Yes, 15 kWh/100 km is excellent** and indicates a highly efficient EV, especially for smaller or well-optimized models.
- For comparison, the **Tesla Model 3** often achieves ~14–16 kWh/100 km in real-world conditions.
