**Question: How to get a US travel visa after renewing my passport, currently in Guangzhou?**

Answer:

Since you are in Guangzhou and have a new passport, you can apply for a **US B1/B2 visitor visa (tourism/business)** at the U.S. Consulate General Guangzhou. The process is:

### 1. Prepare documents

You need:

* New Chinese passport
* Old passport(s) (especially if they contain previous visas/travel history)
* Recent photo (US visa format)
* ID information
* Employment information
* Travel plan (rough plan is enough)
* Financial proof:

  * bank statements
  * salary records
  * tax records if available
* Evidence you will return to China:

  * job/business
  * family
  * property/mortgage
  * long-term commitments

The officer mainly evaluates:

* Why you go to the US
* Whether you have enough funds
* Whether you have strong reasons to return

### 2. Fill DS-160

Complete the online **DS-160 nonimmigrant visa application**. After submission, print the confirmation page with the barcode. ([Travel.state.gov][1])

Website:
[DS-160 Online Application (U.S. Department of State)](https://ceac.state.gov/genniv/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 3. Create visa appointment account

Register on the US visa scheduling system for China, pay the visa fee, and schedule an interview at Guangzhou. Interviews are generally required for B1/B2 applicants. ([Travel.state.gov][2])

The Guangzhou consulate handles visa services for applicants in southern China. ([Travel.state.gov][3])

### 4. Attend interview in Guangzhou

Typical questions:

**Q: Why do you want to visit the US?**

Example:

> "I want to travel to the US for tourism. I plan to visit California, national parks, and experience American culture."

**Q: What do you do?**

Be clear:

> "I am a software engineer focusing on AI and backend systems."

**Q: How long will you stay?**

Give a realistic answer:

> "Around 2-3 weeks."

**Q: Who pays for the trip?**

Answer truthfully.

### 5. After approval

They keep your passport temporarily and return it after visa issuance. Do not buy expensive flight tickets before approval. ([Travel.state.gov][3])
