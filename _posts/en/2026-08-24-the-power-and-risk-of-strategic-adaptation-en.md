Question: Is being highly adaptable across many paths (Hong Kong jobs, entrepreneurship, US grad school, AI Infra, GPU repair, freelancing) a strength or a weakness?

Answer:

I think your observation is accurate: **"善变化" is both your advantage and your risk.** The key is whether the changes are driven by a strategy or by uncertainty.

From your recent three weeks, the pattern is clear:

* Hong Kong work → exploring a higher-value market
* US master → increasing long-term options and immigration possibility
* Job applications → stable cash flow
* AI Infra → aligning with your strongest technical trend
* GPU repair → hardware business opportunity
* Small projects → immediate revenue
* One-person startup → leverage your engineering ability

This is not random. There is actually a common thread:

**You are searching for the highest leverage point between:**

1. your existing engineering ability,
2. AI wave,
3. hardware infrastructure,
4. geographic opportunities,
5. cash flow.

That is a good founder instinct.

But the weakness is:

A person who changes fast can spend all energy exploring and never compound.

Example:

* Spend 1 month researching GPU repair
* Spend 1 month applying HK jobs
* Spend 1 month preparing US applications
* Spend 1 month building an AI agent platform

Each individually makes sense, but the compound effect is low.

A better model:

```
             Long-term direction
                    |
              AI Infrastructure
                    |
        -------------------------
        |           |           |
      Job       Consulting    Startup
     income      income       equity

        |
   GPU knowledge
   (hardware edge)
```

Use side paths to strengthen the main path.

For example:

**AI Infra engineer path**

* Job: AI Infra / LLM Engineer
* Consulting: deploy LLM systems for companies
* Startup: agent platform
* Hardware: GPU repair / GPU cluster optimization

These are connected.

The danger is doing:

```
GPU repair shop
+
Hong Kong software job
+
US master
+
random outsourcing
+
AI course
```

because these become five separate businesses.

Your historical advantage is that you are a fast learner and builder. You trained GPT-2, run GPU workloads, build agents, have full-stack experience. Your problem is probably not capability; it is **focus allocation**.

A useful rule:

* 70% effort → one main bet (AI Infra)
* 20% → adjacent opportunities (consulting, GPU, Hong Kong)
* 10% → exploration

The next 6-12 months probably matter more than the last 10 years because AI infrastructure is still being built.

Your personality is closer to a founder/research engineer: high curiosity, high adaptation. The skill to add is not "be less flexible"; it is **learn when to stop exploring and exploit one direction deeply.**

A good founder changes direction quickly in year 0, but after finding a signal, they become extremely stubborn.
