**Question: What does "References" mean in a US master's program application?**

**Answer:**

In US master's applications, **References** usually means **people who can recommend you** — also called **letters of recommendation (LORs)** or **recommendation letters**.

The university wants to know:

> "Who can confirm that this applicant is capable of succeeding in our graduate program?"

Usually you provide **2–3 references**.

Common reference choices:

### 1. Academic references (most preferred for master's)

People who know your academic ability:

* Professor
* Research advisor
* Thesis supervisor
* Lecturer

Example:

```
Dr. John Smith
Professor, Department of Computer Science
ABC University
john.smith@abc.edu
Relationship: Undergraduate instructor
```

They may write:

> "Zhiwei demonstrated strong programming skills and independent learning ability..."

---

### 2. Professional references (very acceptable for CS/AI master's)

For applicants with industry experience:

* Engineering manager
* Technical lead
* CTO
* Senior colleague

Example:

```
Jane Wang
Engineering Manager
XYZ Technology
jane.wang@company.com
Relationship: Direct manager
```

They may write:

> "He designed distributed systems and led AI infrastructure projects..."

For someone with 10+ years engineering experience, professional references are often stronger than a professor who barely remembers you.

---

### 3. Research references (best for AI/research programs)

If you have:

* papers
* open-source projects
* ML research
* collaboration with researchers

A researcher can describe:

* your technical depth
* research ability
* independence

---

### What happens after you submit references?

Usually:

1. You enter the referee's name/email in the application portal.
2. University emails them a private recommendation link.
3. They upload a letter or answer questions.
4. You usually **cannot see the letter** (confidential recommendation).

Example workflow:

```
Your application
        |
        v
Add Reference:
  Name: Prof. Li
  Email: li@university.edu
        |
        v
University sends email
        |
        v
Professor uploads recommendation
```

---

For your situation (AI master's, 10+ years engineering, startup/LLM work), a strong combination would likely be:

1. Former engineering manager from a known company
2. Technical leader who worked with you
3. Professor/academic contact if available

A recommendation from someone who can say:

> "He trained GPT models, built production systems, and independently learns advanced AI research"

is usually more valuable than a generic professor letter.

For US CS/AI master's programs, references are one part of the application together with:

* Transcript
* Statement of Purpose (SOP)
* Resume/CV
* TOEFL/IELTS (if required)
* GRE (sometimes optional)
* Projects/research experience

References are basically your **professional/academic credibility proof**.
