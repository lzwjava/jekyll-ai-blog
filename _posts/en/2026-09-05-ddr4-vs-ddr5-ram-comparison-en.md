**Question:** How does DDR4 compare to DDR5 RAM?

**Answer:**

Here's a full breakdown across the key categories:

---

**Speed & Bandwidth**

DDR4 typically runs at 2133–3600 MHz, while DDR5 starts at 4800 MHz and scales well past 7200 MHz. A typical DDR5-5600 kit offers around 50% more bandwidth than DDR4-3600, translating to faster data transfers in gaming, content creation, and multitasking.

**Latency**

The early criticism of DDR5 — that higher CAS latency numbers negated the bandwidth gains — was accurate for first-generation kits running at DDR5-4800 with CL40. By 2026, mainstream gaming kits running DDR5-6400 at CL32 deliver both more bandwidth and comparable or better absolute latency than DDR4-3600 CL16.

**Architecture**

DDR5 doubled the number of independent channels per DIMM. Where DDR4 uses a single 64-bit channel per module, DDR5 splits this into two independent 32-bit subchannels, each with its own burst access logic — improving the memory controller's ability to interleave requests efficiently.

**Capacity**

DDR5 can hold up to 128 GB per stick, while DDR4 holds only 32 GB, making DDR5 great for heavy tasks.

**Power**

DDR5 uses less power than DDR4, running at 1.1V, which saves energy and keeps your computer cooler.

**Real-World Gaming Performance**

In most games, DDR5 provides a modest 5–15% FPS uplift over DDR4 at the same CPU, especially in CPU-bound scenarios — though at resolutions above 1080p, the gap narrows.

**Price (as of mid-2026)**

A 32 GB (2×16 GB) kit of DDR5-5600 runs around ~$370 (~$11.56/GB), while an equivalent DDR4-3600 kit costs ~$220 (~$6.87/GB) — roughly a 68% premium for DDR5.

**Platform Support**

AMD Ryzen 7000/8000/9000 (AM5) and Intel Arrow Lake (LGA1851) require DDR5 only, while Intel 12th–14th Gen (LGA1700) supports either DDR4 or DDR5 depending on the motherboard. DDR4 remains relevant only for upgrading existing LGA1700 or AM4 systems.

---

**Quick Decision Guide:**

| | DDR4 | DDR5 |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Up to ~3600 MHz | 4800–7200+ MHz |
| Latency | Lower CL (CL16) | Improved (CL32 at 6400) |
| Capacity/stick | Up to 32 GB | Up to 128 GB |
| Voltage | 1.2V | 1.1V |
| Price (32 GB kit) | ~$220 | ~$370 |
| New platforms | Legacy only | Required for AM5/LGA1851 |

For any new build in 2026, DDR5 is the obvious choice. DDR4 remains relevant only for existing system upgrades, not new builds.
