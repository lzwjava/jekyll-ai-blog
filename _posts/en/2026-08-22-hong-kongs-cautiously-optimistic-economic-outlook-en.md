**Question:** What will Hong Kong's economy look like in the coming years?

**Answer:**

Hong Kong's economic outlook for the next several years is cautiously optimistic, with solid growth tempered by external risks.

**2026 — Stronger than expected**

Hong Kong revised its full-year 2026 GDP growth forecast upward to 3.5–4.5%, after a stronger-than-expected first half. The government cited solid growth expected in the second half, driven by global AI-related electronics demand and rising visitor arrivals — the Tourism Board reported 26.71 million visitors in the first half of 2026, up 13% year-on-year.

**2027–2030 — Steady medium-term growth**

From 2027 to 2030, the economy is projected to grow at an average of around 3% per year in real terms, with underlying inflation averaging about 2%.

**Key growth drivers**

- Mainland China's continued strong performance is expected to remain a key driver, with Beijing implementing more proactive macro policies to expand domestic demand.
- Strong global demand for advanced electronics and AI-related products is expected to support goods exports, while services exports should remain firm — underpinned by inbound tourism, cross-boundary financial activity, and steady demand for business services.
- Hong Kong's status as an international financial center, its growing digital asset market, and its push to become a destination for family offices are seen as additional long-term advantages.

**Risks to watch**

Headwinds from geopolitical uncertainties and the US-China trade war remain, with some analysts forecasting more modest growth of around 2.4% in 2026 and 2.1% in 2027. Public debt is expected to stay below 15% of GDP, keeping short-term financial risk low.

Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, major central bank policy trajectories, trade protectionism, and risks around the rapid expansion of global AI investment all require close monitoring.

**In summary**, Hong Kong's economy looks resilient in the near term, with 2026 performing better than initially expected, and a stable 3% annual growth trend projected through 2030 — provided global trade tensions don't escalate significantly.
