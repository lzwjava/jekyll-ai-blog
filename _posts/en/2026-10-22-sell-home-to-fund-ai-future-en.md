## Answer

My recommendation: prepare to sell, but don't sell in a panic. Treat your house as a financial decision that should support your next 3–5 years, not as an asset you must hold at all costs.

Your situation is not simply about whether property prices will rise. You have three competing demands on your capital and time:

1. Family stability: mortgage payments, your wife, and your two daughters.
2. Education: completing your remaining exams, improving your academic credentials, and potentially pursuing a master's degree.
3. Career and entrepreneurship: continuing LLM research, model training, inference engineering, and building your AI consulting business.

You have already built a product with 30,000 users and generated meaningful revenue. Your strongest potential upside is your engineering ability and capacity to build products—not necessarily the appreciation of one residential property.

The critical question is whether keeping the house gives you enough financial stability to pursue that upside, or whether the mortgage is consuming the runway you need.

## 2. When I would sell versus keep

Keep the house temporarily if:

- You can comfortably cover the mortgage and family expenses from reliable income.
- You have enough savings to cover at least 6–12 months of essential expenses.
- Your family needs the home, and equivalent rental housing would not save much money.
- You have a credible route back to stable income without needing to liquidate the property.

Lean toward selling if:

- You are unemployed or your income is unreliable, and savings are shrinking every month.
- Mortgage payments materially reduce your ability to support your family.
- You need to borrow money just to maintain your current lifestyle.
- Selling and renting a cheaper home would substantially extend your financial runway.

Given your previously shared financial position and career transition, I would lean toward selling if the mortgage is genuinely threatening your family's financial stability. I would not hold the property solely because you hope its value will recover.

## 4. Education: don't let the degree consume your runway

I would separate your education into three stages.

## 01

Complete the remaining exams

Finish the outstanding subjects and practical requirements for your existing credential, subject to the program's current rules. This is likely the lowest-cost way to make measurable progress on your academic background.

## 02

Evaluate the associate/bachelor's pathway

Verify which credentials the program actually awards, whether credits transfer, and how long completion will take. Don't spend years on a qualification that does not unlock your intended next step.

## 03

Pursue a master's when the economics work

A master's could expand your options for AI/CS jobs and international study. But a program costing tens of thousands of dollars is a different financial commitment from taking a few exams. Compare total tuition, living expenses, visa and employment eligibility, and the probability of achieving your goal.

I would not sell your house simply to pay for an expensive master's program. I would consider selling to remove unsustainable debt and preserve flexibility, then choose a degree path based on its actual return.

## 5. Keep your core technical direction

You asked recently whether you should continue researching and training LLMs. I would keep that direction.

Your next stage can combine three things:

- Technical depth: pretraining, post-training, inference, GPU systems, and model optimization.
- Income: AI engineering, consulting, and agent development for customers with real problems.
- Credentials: exams and degree progress, pursued at a cost that your finances can support.

You do not need to choose between being an engineer, founder, and student. But you do need to avoid funding all three ambitions with a mortgage that you cannot comfortably service.

The order matters: stabilize the household, establish income, continue technical work, and progress through education without committing to an unaffordable program.
