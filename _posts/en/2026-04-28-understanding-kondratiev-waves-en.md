Question: What is the “Kangbo cycle” (康波周期), and how can it help us understand business, the world, and technology trends?

Answer:

The “康波周期” refers to the **Kondratiev wave** (also called the *Kondratiev cycle* or *K-wave*). It is a theory proposed by the Russian economist Nikolai Kondratiev in the 1920s.

---

## 1. Core Idea (Simple Understanding)

The theory says:

> The global economy moves in **long cycles of about 40–60 years**, driven mainly by **major technological breakthroughs**.

Each cycle typically has **4 phases**:

1. **Spring (Recovery / Growth)**

   * New technology emerges
   * Investment increases
   * Economy expands

2. **Summer (Boom / Prosperity)**

   * Technology widely adopted
   * Strong growth, inflation pressure

3. **Autumn (Peak / Bubble)**

   * Speculation (stocks, real estate bubbles)
   * Growth slows

4. **Winter (Recession / Reset)**

   * Crisis, debt collapse
   * Old industries decline
   * Prepares for next cycle

## 3. Why It Matters for Business

This framework helps explain why:

### (1) Some industries suddenly explode

* Example: Internet companies after 2000
* Driven by the **5th wave (digital revolution)**

### (2) Some industries slowly die

* Coal, traditional retail, legacy manufacturing
* They belong to **older cycles**

### (3) Timing matters more than effort

* Starting a company in the wrong phase = very hard
* Riding a wave = easier growth

## 5. How You Can Use It (Practical Thinking)

### Think in “waves,” not short-term noise

Instead of asking:

* “Is this company good?”

Ask:

* “Is this aligned with the next 20–30 year wave?”

---

### Choose direction, not just effort

For example:

* Working on:

  * AI / data → tailwind
  * Declining industries → headwind

Same effort, very different outcomes.

---

### Avoid bubbles (Autumn phase)

When:

* Everyone is hyped
* Easy money
* Crazy valuations

→ Often late stage of a cycle

---

## 6. Important Caveat (Critical Thinking)

The Kondratiev wave is **not a precise law**:

* Not all economists agree with it
* Timing is fuzzy
* Real world is influenced by:

  * Wars
  * Policy
  * Black swan events

So treat it as:

> A **mental model**, not a prediction tool.
