**Question: What other immigration/work routes should a 12-year software engineer consider if QMAS is available?**

**Answer:**

Yes. Given your profile — **~12 years software engineering + AI/LLM/ML experience + building products/company experience** — I would not treat QMAS as your only route.

My ranking would be:

| Route                                | My priority |                  Need job offer? | Why                                                             |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------: | -------------------------------: | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🇭🇰 **Hong Kong QMAS**              |       ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ |                           **No** | Best fit for entering HK independently                          |
| 🇭🇰 **HK GEP / ASMTP**              |       ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ |                          **Yes** | Very practical if you get an HK AI/software job                 |
| 🇭🇰 **HK TechTAS**                  |        ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | **Yes + eligible company quota** | Particularly interesting for your AI/R&D direction              |
| 🇸🇬 **Singapore EP**                |        ⭐⭐⭐⭐ |                          **Yes** | Strong tech market, straightforward employer-sponsored path     |
| 🇨🇦 **Canada Global Talent Stream** |        ⭐⭐⭐⭐ |                          **Yes** | Excellent if you find a Canadian tech employer                  |
| 🇸🇬 **Singapore Tech.Pass**         |          ⭐⭐ |                               No | Very high bar; probably not your first route                    |

### 1. Hong Kong QMAS — I would definitely pursue this

QMAS is particularly attractive because **you don't need to have an HK employer before applying**. It evaluates things such as age, education, language, work experience, income and business ownership. ([Immigration Department][1])

For someone with your experience, this is fundamentally different from a normal employment visa:

```text
QMAS
   ↓
enter HK
   ↓
look for AI/software job
   ↓
or establish business
   ↓
build HK career/business
```

The current QMAS General Points Test initial stay is normally **36 months**, followed by extensions subject to demonstrating settlement and contribution in HK. ([Immigration Department][2])

So if you can submit a strong QMAS application, **I would do it rather than waiting for an HK job first.**

---

### 2. HK GEP / ASMTP — probably your most practical backup

If you get an HK offer for:

* AI Engineer
* LLM Engineer
* Backend Engineer
* Senior Software Engineer
* ML Engineer
* AI Infrastructure Engineer

then the employment route becomes very attractive.

Hong Kong's GEP requires, among other things, a relevant job, remuneration comparable to the market, and appropriate education/technical/professional experience. ([HKEngage][3])

For a Mainland Chinese applicant, **ASMTP** is particularly relevant.

So I'd actually run these two tracks simultaneously:

```text
                 ┌── QMAS ──→ HK
You ─────────────┤
                 └── HK job ──→ ASMTP/GEP
```

Don't wait for QMAS before applying for HK jobs.

---

### 3. HK TechTAS — interesting specifically because you're moving toward AI

This one is more specialized.

TechTAS is for technology talent doing **R&D in Hong Kong**, including:

* artificial intelligence
* data analytics
* cybersecurity
* advanced communications
* fintech
* robotics
* IoT
* etc.

But there's an important catch: **the HK employer needs an approved TechTAS quota**, and the applicant normally needs a STEM degree from a sufficiently highly ranked university. ([Immigration Department][4])

So I wouldn't make TechTAS your primary plan, but if you get an offer from an HK AI company/research organization that already has the quota, **absolutely investigate it**.

---

### 4. Singapore Employment Pass — probably your best non-HK Asian option

Singapore is worth serious consideration for you.

The Employment Pass currently requires both:

```text
1. salary threshold
        +
2. COMPASS ≥ 40 points
```

The salary threshold rises with age. For example, the current non-financial-sector threshold ranges from S$5,600 at younger ages to S$10,700 at age 45+. ([Industrial Arbitration Court][5])

And COMPASS requires **40 points** unless an exemption applies. ([Ministry of Manpower Singapore][6])

For an experienced AI/software engineer, I'd target:

```text
Senior Software Engineer
Staff Engineer
AI Engineer
ML Engineer
LLM Engineer
AI Infrastructure Engineer
Backend/Distributed Systems Engineer
```

rather than generic "Software Developer" positions.

There is also a **5-year EP** route for experienced tech professionals in shortage occupations, but the salary requirement is considerably higher. ([Ministry of Manpower Singapore][7])

---

### 5. Canada — Global Talent Stream

Canada is another route I'd keep alive.

The **Global Talent Stream (GTS)** is employer-driven and designed for highly skilled foreign talent, particularly for Canadian innovative companies and in-demand highly skilled occupations. ([Canada][8])

The important distinction:

```text
QMAS:
you → immigration application → HK

GTS:
Canadian company → hires you → immigration/work authorization
```

So I'd treat Canada as a **job-search strategy**, not an independent immigration application like QMAS.

---

### 6. Singapore Tech.Pass — probably not your first shot

This sounds attractive:

> "Experienced tech founder/engineer can move to Singapore without being tied to one employer."

But the bar is much higher.

For example, the Tech.Pass salary benchmark is **S$22,500/month**, and there are additional technology/business criteria. ([Economic Development Board][9])

At your current stage, I would spend the effort on:

```text
QMAS
HK AI jobs
Singapore EP
Canada GTS
```

before spending much time on Tech.Pass.

## What I would actually do in your position

I would run **5 parallel funnels**, not choose one visa.

```text
                       ┌── HK QMAS
                       │

                       │
```

> Senior Java Developer

### My priority

**#1 QMAS → #2 HK AI job → #3 Singapore EP → #4 Canada GTS → #5 US routes**

The particularly nice thing is that **QMAS and job hunting aren't mutually exclusive**. QMAS gives you an independent HK entry path, while an HK employer gives you a second path. Hong Kong currently has several talent admission schemes with distinct routes and stay patterns. ([Immigration Department][10])

If your objective is **"get out of Mainland China and establish an international AI career/business base as quickly as realistically possible,"** I'd put **Hong Kong + Singapore** substantially ahead of trying to optimize around the US lottery system first.
