Question: **For QMAS, if my house in China is worth ~RMB 1.1M and I have ~RMB 800K mortgage, can I use it as proof of personal net worth?**

Answer: **Yes. The house can be used, and QMAS explicitly says to calculate real-estate net value after subtracting the outstanding mortgage.** ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][1])

Your rough calculation is:

```text
House market value       ¥1.10M
Outstanding mortgage    -¥0.80M
                         -------
Net property equity      ¥0.30M
```

So, **if the house is solely yours and the valuation is accepted, it contributes approximately RMB 300K to your personal net worth**, not RMB 1.1M.

The QMAS Guidance Notes specifically state that acceptable real-estate assets are:

> current market value of real estate **less the amount of outstanding mortgage**

and require:

* proof of ownership;
* a **valuation report issued within 2 years** by a qualified valuer;
* the latest outstanding mortgage amount, supported by a **mortgage repayment schedule from the financial institution**. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][1])

### The important part: there is no published fixed "you need HK$X" threshold

QMAS does **not** appear to say something like "you need HK$1M net worth." The actual requirement is that you demonstrate sufficient personal net worth to support **yourself and your dependants for the initial 12 months in Hong Kong**. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][2])

So if you have, for example:

```text
Property equity       ¥300K
Bank deposits          ¥500K
Investments            ¥200K
--------------------------------
Total net worth       ¥1.00M
```

you would have a much stronger financial package than showing the property alone.

And if you bring your spouse/children as dependants, the requirement applies to supporting **the whole family**, so I would want substantially more liquid cash rather than relying mainly on the ¥300K property equity. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][2])

### What I would submit

For the house:

```text
1. Property ownership / title documents
2. Recent professional valuation
3. Mortgage repayment schedule
4. Bank statements showing your liquid assets
5. Investment/brokerage statements, if applicable
6. Personal-debt statement, if applicable
```

The official guidance actually lists bank deposits, real estate, securities and privately owned businesses as acceptable asset categories. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][1])

**So your ¥1.1M house + ¥800K mortgage is not a problem by itself.** QMAS recognizes mortgaged property; you simply report the **net equity (~¥300K)**.
