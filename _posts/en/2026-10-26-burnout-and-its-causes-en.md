**Question:** What is burnout, and why do people experience it?

---

**Answer:**

## What Is Burnout?

Burnout is a state of **chronic, unresolved stress** that results in three core symptoms, first defined by psychologist Herbert Freudenberger in 1974 and later formalized by researcher Christina Maslach:

| Dimension | What It Feels Like |
|---|---|
| **Exhaustion** | Feeling emotionally and physically drained, with nothing left to give |
| **Cynicism / Depersonalization** | Becoming detached, negative, or indifferent toward work and people |
| **Reduced Efficacy** | Feeling ineffective, unaccomplished, like your efforts don't matter |

In 2019, the **WHO officially recognized burnout** in the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-11) as an occupational phenomenon — not a medical condition, but a significant workplace syndrome.

---

## Why Do People Feel Burnout?

Maslach identified **six root causes** of workplace burnout:

1. **Work Overload** — Too much to do, too little time; the demands exceed what a person can sustainably handle.
2. **Lack of Control** — No autonomy over decisions, schedule, or how work gets done.
3. **Insufficient Reward** — Not enough recognition, pay, or sense of satisfaction from the work.
4. **Poor Community** — Conflict with colleagues, feeling isolated, or a toxic team environment.
5. **Unfairness** — Favoritism, unequal treatment, or feeling like the system is rigged.
6. **Values Conflict** — Being asked to do things that clash with your personal ethics or beliefs.

### Personal Factors That Amplify Burnout

Beyond the workplace, certain personal tendencies make burnout more likely:

- **Perfectionism** — Impossible self-imposed standards create endless pressure.
- **Difficulty saying no** — Taking on more than is sustainable.
- **Neglecting recovery** — Skipping rest, hobbies, sleep, or social connection.
- **High sense of duty** — People who deeply care about their work often push too hard for too long.

---

## The Burnout Cycle

```
High demand + Low resources
        ↓
Chronic stress (no recovery)
        ↓
Exhaustion → Cynicism → Loss of purpose
        ↓
Burnout
```

It's important to distinguish burnout from ordinary tiredness: **regular fatigue goes away with rest; burnout doesn't.** It accumulates over months or years of sustained, unresolved stress.
