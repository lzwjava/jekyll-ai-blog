Several enterprise software products generate controversy similar to SAP ERP. Here are some notable examples:

**Oracle Database and Enterprise Software** - Known for aggressive licensing practices, complex pricing structures, and costly audits. Many organizations feel locked into Oracle's ecosystem and frustrated by licensing compliance requirements.

**Salesforce** - While popular, it draws criticism for becoming increasingly expensive over time, complex customization that can lead to "technical debt," and feature bloat that makes it overwhelming for smaller organizations.

**ServiceNow** - Criticized for high costs, slow implementation times, and a steep learning curve. Organizations often find themselves spending far more than initially budgeted.

**Workday** - The HR and financial management system faces complaints about rigid processes, difficult customization, and implementations that frequently go over budget and timeline.

**IBM Enterprise Software** (like Maximo, Cognos) - Often criticized for dated interfaces, complex licensing, and high maintenance costs relative to modern alternatives.

**Microsoft Dynamics** - Known for inconsistent quality across different modules, complex upgrades, and integration challenges despite being part of the Microsoft ecosystem.

**PeopleSoft** (Oracle) - Legacy system with a reputation for being difficult to maintain, expensive to upgrade, and challenging to find qualified talent for.

The common threads in these controversies typically include: high total cost of ownership, vendor lock-in, complex implementations that exceed time and budget estimates, steep learning curves, and licensing models that feel opaque or punitive. Like SAP, these are often mission-critical systems that become deeply embedded in organizations, making them difficult to replace despite frustrations.
