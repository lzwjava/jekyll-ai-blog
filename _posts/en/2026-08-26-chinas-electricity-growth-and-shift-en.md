**Question:** How has electricity consumption trended in China in recent years?

**Answer:**

China's electricity consumption has seen remarkable and accelerating growth over the past several years, with major shifts in both scale and energy mix.

**Overall Scale & Growth**

China's electricity demand has increased sevenfold from 2000 (1,347 TWh) to 2024 (10,066 TWh). The pace of growth has remained strong: demand grew by 6.9% in 2023 and 6.6% in 2024 (+623 TWh).

In 2025, China crossed a historic threshold, consuming 10.37 trillion kWh of electricity — the first time the nation has surpassed the 10 trillion mark.

**Who's Driving Demand?**

The secondary sector (industry/manufacturing) still dominates, accounting for 64% of total electricity consumption in 2025. Within this sector, high-tech and equipment manufacturing increased electricity use by 6.4%, while traditional energy-intensive industries grew just 1.8%. The service sector is electrifying even faster, with tertiary industry power consumption jumping 8.2% from 2024, driven largely by new infrastructure like EV charging stations and 5G base stations.

**Per Capita Consumption**

Total electricity consumption per capita rose from 7,401 kWh in 2025 to 7,483 kWh in 2026, with clean energy generation per person reaching 3,182 kWh.

**Energy Mix Transformation**

Coal's share of generation has fallen from 70% in 2015 to 58% in 2024, even as absolute coal generation hit a new record. In 2025, coal output actually decreased for the first time since 2015, and fossil fuels' share dropped to 58%, down from ~62% in 2024 — a structural shift that may signal approaching peak fossil fuel use.

On the renewables side, wind and solar generation tripled over the past five years, from 629 TWh in 2019 to 1,826 TWh in 2024, meeting over half of the increase in electricity demand in 2024. By the end of 2024, installed wind and solar capacity reached 1,406 GW — already exceeding China's 2030 target of 1,200 GW, six years ahead of schedule.
