Question: What is the difference between scoring around 70 on average versus 90+ in exams like China's self-study higher education exams (self-taught examination), and how do high achievers reach those levels compared to Gaokao high scorers or competitive programming ratings?

Answer:

Your observation is accurate: the gap between an average of around **70** (solid pass level in self-study exams, where 60 is the passing threshold) and **90+** is significant, but it is rarely due to "magic" or innate genius. High scores in academic exams—whether self-study higher education exams (自考), Gaokao, or even competitive settings like Codeforces—are typically the result of **consistent application of effective principles**: deliberate practice, targeting weaknesses, doing far more exercises, building deep familiarity with knowledge points, and maintaining disciplined habits.

### In Self-Study Higher Education Exams (自考)

- Passing (60+) is achievable with basic understanding and some targeted review (e.g., brushing past papers, memorizing key points for short-answer questions).
- Your scores (mostly 60-80 range, with some higher like 88 in Java) reflect good foundational knowledge plus your 10 years of software engineering experience, which helps in practical, programming-related courses.
- Achieving a consistent **90+ average** (often needed for a degree certificate in some cases or strong applications abroad) requires much more:
  - Extremely thorough mastery of the textbook and question patterns.
  - Heavy emphasis on **past exam papers** (真题): many high scorers brush 10–20+ years of papers, memorizing common short-answer/essay structures and spotting recurring themes.
  - Precision in answers: clear structure (point 1, point 2...), neat handwriting, and filling every question (even partially correct answers get partial credit).
  - More time invested per subject: slower, patient analysis of weak areas, repeated practice until concepts become automatic.
  - Discipline: steady daily study, no cramming reliance.

From experiences shared online, 90+ scorers often treat it like a system—focus on volume of correct practice, error analysis, and volume of review rather than raw talent.

### Comparison to Gaokao High Scorers (e.g., 650+ out of 750)

The gap between 500–550 (decent) and 650+ (top-tier, often for elite universities) shows similar patterns:

- High scorers (650+) tolerate slow, careful problem-solving, identify weak points early, do **massive amounts of exercises** (刷题), and become extremely familiar with every knowledge point.
- They use deliberate practice: analyze mistakes deeply, redo wrong questions, build speed and accuracy through repetition.
- Many describe it as effort + method + persistence rather than genius. Top performers often have strong habits (consistent review, notes, textbook mastery) and endure long hours without shortcuts.

### Parallel to Codeforces Ratings (e.g., 1400 vs 2100)

- A 1400-rated coder solves basic-to-intermediate problems reliably.
- A 2100-rated (expert/master) solves much harder problems quickly and accurately, spots patterns others miss, and handles edge cases effortlessly.
- The gap comes from **thousands more hours** of targeted practice, upsolving contests, studying advanced algorithms, and learning from failures—not magic talent.

### Key Takeaway

There is **no magic or genius** required for high achievement in these areas. High performers follow core principles more rigorously and consistently:

- Detail-oriented work.
- Targeting weaknesses systematically.
- Doing far more deliberate exercises than others.
- Building deep familiarity through repetition.
- Patience to figure things out slowly and correctly at first, then efficiently.

Your software engineering background already gives you a strong edge in practical courses—many self-study students lack that real-world context. With more focused exercises (especially past papers for the remaining written and practical exams), deeper weakness-fixing, and consistent routines, closing the gap to higher averages (80+ or even 90+) is realistic and comes down to **applying those principles more intensely**. Many people reach high levels simply by doing what others know they should do—but actually execute relentlessly.
