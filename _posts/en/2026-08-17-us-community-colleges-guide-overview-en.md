Question: **What community colleges are available in the U.S.?**

Answer: There are **hundreds of community colleges across the U.S.** They are generally 2-year colleges offering Associate degrees (AA/AS/AAS), certificates, and transfer pathways to 4-year universities. The classic route is:

```text
Community College (2 years)
        ↓
Transfer
        ↓
University (2 years)
        ↓
Bachelor's degree
```

This is the U.S. **2+2 model**, and community colleges generally have lower tuition and more flexible admissions than 4-year universities. ([EducationUSA][1])

### Some particularly interesting ones

**California** has a huge community-college system. The California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office maintains the official statewide list. ([California Community Colleges][2])

California alone has a very large network of community colleges; for example, the Los Angeles Community College District has **nine colleges**. ([Los Angeles Community College District][3])

### For an international student

This is important: **community college is not only for Americans.**

For example, Santa Monica College explicitly accepts international F-1 students, and Los Angeles City College has an F-1 international-student pathway for Associate degrees and certificates. ([Santa Monica College][4])

For someone interested in **CS/AI**, I'd especially look at:

```text
De Anza
   ↓
transfer → UC / CSU / other universities

Santa Monica College
   ↓
transfer → UC / UCLA / USC / etc.

Los Angeles City College
   ↓
transfer → 4-year university
```

The key is **not simply choosing the cheapest community college**. You want to choose one whose courses have articulation agreements with the university you eventually want. EducationUSA explicitly recommends planning the transfer target early. ([EducationUSA][5])
