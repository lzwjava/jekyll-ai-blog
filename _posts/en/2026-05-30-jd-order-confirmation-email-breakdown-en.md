## The Legal Disclaimer (the bulk of the email)

This is standard Chinese e-commerce contract law boilerplate. Key points:

1. **Product listings are not offers, they're invitations to offer** — what's shown on the site is just a solicitation. Your placing an order is the actual "offer."

2. **The contract is only formed when goods physically leave the warehouse** — not when you click "buy," not when you pay. The trigger is the item being dispatched from the warehouse.

3. **Partial shipment = partial contract** — if you ordered multiple items and only some ship, you only have a legal contract for what actually shipped. The rest becomes a contract only when it ships.

4. **Exception for digital goods** (e-books, music, phone top-ups): contract forms immediately upon payment.

5. **Consumer exception**: if you're buying as a regular consumer for personal use, the contract is considered formed once payment succeeds.
