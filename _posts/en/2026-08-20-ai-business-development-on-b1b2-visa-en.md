Question: Can I use a U.S. tourist visa for 2 months to visit, negotiate AI projects, find clients, then return to China and execute the work remotely?

Answer:

Yes — **this is a much better idea than using a tourist visa to physically work in the U.S.** Your described model is closer to a legitimate business-development trip:

```
China company / freelancer
        |
        |
   B1/B2 visit (2 months)
        |
        |
Meet US companies
Discuss AI projects
Negotiate contracts
Build relationships
        |
        |
Return to China
        |
        |
Develop AI solutions remotely
Deliver from China
```

This is much more aligned with what B-1 business visitor activities allow: business meetings, consultations, conferences, and negotiating contracts. ([Travel.state.gov][1])

The important boundary:

Allowed:

✅ Meet potential clients
✅ Discuss AI consulting needs
✅ Give presentations
✅ Negotiate contracts
✅ Explore partnerships
✅ Attend AI conferences/events

Not allowed:

❌ Sit in a U.S. company office and work as their engineer
❌ Provide on-site labor for a U.S. employer
❌ Receive salary for work performed in the U.S.

Visitor status does not authorize employment in the U.S. ([Travel.state.gov][2])
