**Question:** Why was VIPKID restricted/banned in China, and can parents use proxy tools (VPNs) to attend classes?

**Answer:**

**Why VIPKID got restricted**

The root cause is China's **"Double Reduction" (双减) policy**, enacted in July 2021. China issued rules barring curriculum-based tutoring for profit, aiming to ease financial pressures that have contributed to low birth rates, but leaving private education firms facing significant business impact.

Under China's tough new law on after-school classes, tutoring services are banned from using online teachers living outside the country to teach 6 to 15-year-olds any core curriculum subjects, including English.

One of the policy's provisions states that tutoring companies cannot employ foreign tutors to teach students based in China. VIPKID — whose entire model was exactly that — was directly hit. VIPKid said it would stop selling classes taught by foreign-based tutors to students in China with immediate effect to comply with sweeping new rules.

**What VIPKID did afterward**

The company could no longer hire teachers from the United States and Canada to instruct Chinese kids online. Within a few months, though, the company had pivoted to a wider global market. It now calls itself VIPKid Global. Its international business for students outside China was not affected.

**The "VIPKID Global" grey-area situation (as of 2026)**

Despite the ban, there is a workaround that some families in China have been using — not through proxy/VPN tools per se, but through a structural loophole: classes are attended via the VIPKID Global overseas platform account registered with a Hong Kong "+852" area code, and class hours are deducted from an account registered with a Chinese mainland mobile phone number. Essentially: domestic marketing to acquire customers → overseas entity collecting payment → overseas accounts delivering classes.

However, for one-on-one overseas foreign teacher courses targeting domestic primary and secondary school students, the problem is not whether it exceeds a limit, but that it is **prohibited for sale**. The 2021 双减 policy explicitly prohibits it, and documents issued by 12 departments including the General Office of the Ministry of Education in December 2022 require strict investigation of online academic training carried out by overseas online platforms for domestic primary and secondary school students.

**On using VPNs/proxies**

There is no official stance from VIPKID encouraging parents to use VPNs, and VIPKID itself would not publicly recommend this. More importantly, using unauthorized VPNs in China is legally risky — Chinese law prohibits VPNs not authorized by the state. In practice, some families do use VPN-like tools to access blocked services, but doing so to access a service that is **explicitly banned by education regulation** compounds the legal exposure significantly.

In short: VIPKID is not just "geo-blocked" like a streaming site — it is **legally prohibited** for mainland Chinese K-12 students. Using a VPN to work around the ban doesn't change the legal status of the underlying activity.
