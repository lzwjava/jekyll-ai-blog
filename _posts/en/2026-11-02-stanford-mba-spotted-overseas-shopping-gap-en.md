**Question:** Why did Xiaohongshu (RED) CEO Mao Wenchao return to China from Stanford to start a business?

**Answer:**

The CEO in question is **Mao Wenchao (毛文超 / Charlwin Mao)**, co-founder and CEO of Xiaohongshu. Here's his journey:

**His Background**

Mao Wenchao graduated with a degree in Computer Science and Engineering (Mechatronics) and went on to work at Bain & Company. While working at a private equity firm, Mao was sent to Stanford University to study for an MBA. A 2016 interview described his Stanford studies as taking place from 2011 to 2013.

**Why He Came Back to China**

In December 2013, Mao, an "overseas returnee" from Stanford, noticed that Chinese tourists were fond of shopping abroad, and launched a simple social app to help travelers share their finds.

Specifically, the insight came from **personal experience**: Mao's parents went to America to travel in 2012, and he tried to search for overseas shopping guides for them online — only to find that while there were many travel guides, overseas shopping guides were almost completely absent.

He also saw the macro trend: China had become rich enough that Chinese people were spending more and more on travel. By 2012, overseas consumption had exceeded $100 billion.

**Why He Quit His Job**

Mao Wenchao willingly paid a compensation of RMB 1 million and resigned, then founded Xiaohongshu in June 2013 in Shanghai — turning it into a platform for sharing outbound shopping guides, which was quite innovative in China at that time.

**The Spark from Stanford**

According to co-founder Mao Wenchao, the name Xiaohongshu was also inspired by his career at Bain & Company and education at Stanford Graduate School of Business — both institutions feature red as their main color.

In short, he returned because he **spotted a real gap**: millions of Chinese consumers going abroad with no good resource for overseas shopping, and he had both the business insight (from Bain) and the entrepreneurial culture (from Stanford) to act on it.
