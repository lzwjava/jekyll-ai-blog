**Question:** How does Zhang Yiming (ByteDance founder) interview people — what aspects does he look for?

**Answer:**

Zhang Yiming is known for taking hiring extremely seriously as a founder. In the first 10 years of ByteDance, he personally interviewed nearly 2,000 young candidates, viewing talent acquisition as one of his core CEO responsibilities. His hiring approach was direct — rather than hiring editors, ByteDance hired engineers and data scientists who studied user behavior at scale. His overall philosophy was blunt: he said "The company that wants to achieve the most, you need the best talent," and pushed the idea of "unlimited salary for unlimited talent."

**The 5 Traits He Looks For**

From interviewing thousands of candidates, Zhang identified five common traits among outstanding talent:

1. **Curiosity** — Proactive learning of new technologies and skills. Zhang himself is described as a lifelong information-absorber and sees intellectual restlessness as a core indicator.

2. **Optimism Toward Uncertainty** — Willingness to experiment despite unknown outcomes. This is especially important in a fast-moving tech environment where the right answer isn't always clear.

3. **Disdain for Mediocrity** — Setting high personal standards and avoiding complacency. Zhang famously used the Weibo signature: "Mediocrity has gravity, you need escape velocity" — reflecting his constant drive to avoid complacency and strive for excellence.

4. **Humility and Delayed Gratification** — Focusing on responsibility rather than entitlement. He values people who invest in the long game rather than seeking quick rewards.

5. **Judgment on What Matters** — Making long-term career and project choices beyond short-term gains.

**His Broader Hiring Philosophy**

Zhang believes talent quality determines the effectiveness of resource allocation and ultimately the company's output — and that a CEO should act as an excellent HR, balancing capital, opportunities, and talent. He also argued that adding more processes as a company scales is a trap — the better path is increasing the density of high-quality talent, allowing simple principles rather than exhaustive procedures.

Zhang adapted Steve Jobs' famous phrase to reflect his own values: "Stay hungry, Stay young" — maintaining hunger and a youthful mindset. He extended this to hiring: he believes the most important thing for a company is to stay young, and this matters more than becoming a mature, established organization.
