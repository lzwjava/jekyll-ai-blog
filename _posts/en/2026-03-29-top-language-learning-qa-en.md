Question: What apps or platforms are similar to Quora that are good for learning languages and offer one‑click or easy full‑site language switching, including both UI and content?

Answer:
Here are platforms like Quora that are useful for language learning and that either support multiple languages or help you practice languages through community Q&A, discussions, or user‑generated content:

### 🌍 1. Reddit

* **What it is:** A massive collection of communities (subreddits) on almost any topic.
* **Why it’s good for language learning:** There are dedicated language learning subreddits (e.g., r/languagelearning, r/French, r/ChineseLanguage) where you can ask questions, share resources, and get explanations from native speakers and learners.
* **Language support:** Not a full UI language switch like Quora, but communities are language‑specific and you can create language practice threads.

### 📘 2. Stack Exchange (including language communities)

* **What it is:** A network of question‑and‑answer sites focused on expert knowledge.
* **Why it’s good:** It includes specific language Q&A communities such as:

  * **Language Learners Stack Exchange (Beta)** – general language learning questions
  * **Japanese Language** – detailed grammar and usage
  * **German, French, Spanish Language** sites
* **Language support:** The UI stays in your browser language, but the communities are clearly focused on particular languages.

### 🧠 3. HiNative

* **What it is:** A Q&A app built specifically for asking native speakers questions about language and culture.
* **Why it’s good:** You can ask questions like “How do you say this?” or “Is this natural?” and get answers from native speakers. Great for pronunciation, nuance, slang, and usage.
* **Language support:** Choose target languages and switch between them easily.

### 🌐 4. Tandem

* **What it is:** A language exchange app where you chat with native speakers around the world.
* **Why it’s good:** You get real conversational practice rather than just Q&A.
* **Language support:** You can learn or practice dozens of languages; the UI and chat languages adapt to your choices.

### 🤖 5. Duolingo Community / Discussions

* **What it is:** While Duolingo is primarily a structured language course, it also has *discussions* and *clubs*.
* **Why it’s good:** You get explanations and tips from other learners and occasionally moderators.
* **Language support:** UI offers many target languages and explanations often in your native language.

### 📖 6. LingQ

* **What it is:** A platform for reading and listening practice with user‑generated content.
* **Why it’s good:** You import texts, get translations, and see vocabulary in context. Strong “learn by example” approach.
* **Language support:** Wide range of languages; content is tagged/translatable.

### 🎧 7. HelloTalk

* **What it is:** Another language‑exchange social network.
* **Why it’s good:** Focused on texting, voice messages, and correcting each other’s language.
* **Language support:** Supports dozens of language pairs.

### 🗣️ 8. Speaky

* **What it is:** Language exchange community platform.
* **Why it’s good:** Real time chats with global users.
* **Language support:** Many languages; you set learning and native languages.

### 🧪 9. StackOverflow in Other Languages

* **What it is:** Localized StackExchange sites (e.g., StackOverflow en español, StackOverflow на русском).
* **Why it’s good:** If you’re learning a language, you get technical Q&A in that language. Not specifically for language learning, but great immersion.

### 📚 10. Quora Itself (Localized Versions)

* Quora has UI and some content in multiple languages (e.g., Spanish, Portuguese, Hindi).
* Often the locale version gives you community answers in your target language.

---

**Tips for learning languages using platforms like these:**

* **Set your target language in UI where available:** Some apps let you switch the interface itself, which boosts immersion.
* **Follow language‑specific groups or topics:** That ensures relevant content.
* **Engage regularly by asking and answering:** Producing language helps more than just reading.
