Question: Why is Costco so successful?

Answer:

Costco's success stems from its unique membership-based warehouse club model, which prioritizes delivering exceptional value to customers while generating stable, high-margin revenue from membership fees.

### Key Factors Contributing to Costco's Success

- **Membership Model**: Customers pay an annual fee (e.g., $65 basic or higher for executive in recent years) for access, creating recurring revenue that funds low merchandise prices. This fosters loyalty, with renewal rates consistently over 90% in the US and Canada.

- **Low Markup Pricing**: Costco caps markups at 14-15% (far below typical retail 25-50%), enabling unbeatable prices on bulk items. High sales volume compensates for thin margins.

- **Limited Selection and Efficiency**: Offering ~3,700-4,000 SKUs (vs. 30,000+ in supermarkets) simplifies operations, strengthens supplier negotiations, and creates a "treasure hunt" shopping experience with rotating high-value items.

- **Kirkland Signature Private Label**: This in-house brand accounts for ~30% of sales, offering high-quality products 15-20% cheaper than name brands, building trust and margins.

- **Employee Focus**: High wages, benefits, and promote-from-within culture result in low turnover (~10% overall, 6% for tenured staff), leading to better service and efficiency.

- **Minimal Advertising**: Relies on word-of-mouth and member loyalty instead of big ad budgets, keeping costs low.

- **Strong Customer Experience**: Iconic loss leaders like the $1.50 hot dog combo (unchanged since 1985), generous returns, and ancillary services (gas, pharmacy) drive frequent visits and loyalty.

- **Adaptation and Expansion**: In 2025, Costco reported fiscal year net sales of ~$270 billion (up 8.1%), with e-commerce growth ~15%, expanding "big and bulky" items, and leaning into Kirkland to mitigate tariffs. Global warehouse count neared 900+, with ongoing international growth.

These elements create a virtuous cycle: low prices attract members, high volume and fees ensure profitability, and loyalty sustains growth even in tough economies.
