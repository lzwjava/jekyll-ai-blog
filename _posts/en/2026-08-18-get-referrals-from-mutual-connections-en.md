Question: In job interviews at big corps or startups, if we have mutual connections with the interviewer, should we ask those friends to put in a good word for us?

Answer: Yes, absolutely — this is called a **warm referral** or **internal referral**, and it's one of the most effective ways to boost your chances. Here's how it works and how to do it well:

**Why it works**
- Hiring managers trust people they know. A voucher from a mutual connection carries far more weight than a cold application.
- At big corps (Google, Meta, etc.), an internal referral can get your resume *past the ATS filter* entirely and land it directly on a recruiter's desk.
- At startups, where culture fit matters enormously, a trusted person saying "I know this person, they're solid" can be decisive.

**How to do it right**
- **Ask your mutual friend specifically**, not vaguely. Don't just say "put in a good word" — give them 2–3 talking points: your relevant skills, a past win, why you're excited about this role.
- **Make it easy for them** — draft a short message they can copy/paste or adapt. People are more likely to help if it requires minimal effort.
- **Timing matters** — ideally before your resume is submitted or right after, not after you've already had a bad first round.
- **Don't over-rely on it** — the referral gets you in the door, but you still need to perform. Think of it as a boost, not a guarantee.

**One caution**
Make sure your mutual friend actually knows you well enough to speak genuinely. A lukewarm or uncertain endorsement can backfire if the interviewer probes deeper.
