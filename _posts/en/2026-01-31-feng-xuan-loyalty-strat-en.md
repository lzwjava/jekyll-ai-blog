## Introduction to "Feng Xuan, Retainer of Lord Mengchang" (冯谖客孟尝君)

### Historical Context

This narrative comes from the *Zhanguoce* (战国策, *Strategies of the Warring States*), a collection of historical anecdotes and political stratagems compiled during China's Warring States period (475-221 BCE). The text chronicles the tumultuous era when seven major states vied for supremacy before the Qin unification.

The story is set in the state of Qi during the 4th century BCE, centering on one of the period's most famous aristocrats, Lord Mengchang (孟尝君, Meng Changjun), whose given name was Tian Wen (田文). He served as prime minister of Qi and was renowned as one of the "Four Lords" of the Warring States—aristocratic patrons who maintained thousands of retainers (*ke* 客 or *menke* 门客) of varied talents and social backgrounds.

### The Retainer System

The *menke* system was a defining feature of Warring States politics. Powerful lords competed to attract talented individuals—strategists, warriors, scholars, artisans—offering them patronage in exchange for service. These retainers formed private armies and think tanks, enhancing their patron's political influence. Lord Mengchang reportedly maintained over 3,000 retainers, famous for accepting men regardless of their background or apparent usefulness.

### The Protagonist: Feng Xuan

Feng Xuan (冯谖, also written as Feng Huan 冯驩) arrives at Mengchang's estate as an apparently unremarkable figure who makes increasingly demanding requests for better treatment. What initially appears as presumptuous ingratitude gradually reveals itself as the behavior of an extraordinary strategist testing his patron's character and demonstrating his own worth through seemingly eccentric actions.

### Literary Significance

This text is celebrated for several reasons:

**Narrative artistry**: The story unfolds through a series of three missions Feng Xuan undertakes for Mengchang, each revealing deeper layers of political wisdom. The structure builds suspense as Feng's apparently reckless or inexplicable actions later prove to be brilliant foresight.

**Character development**: Feng Xuan emerges as an archetype of the hidden sage—the unassuming figure whose true worth exceeds surface appearances. The text explores themes of loyalty, strategic thinking, and the complex relationship between patron and client.

**Political philosophy**: The narrative illustrates key Warring States concepts: the importance of reputation (*ming* 名), the strategic value of benevolence, and the need for long-term thinking in statecraft. Feng's famous metaphor of "the three burrows of the cunning rabbit" (狡兔三窟) has become a proverbial expression for maintaining multiple escape routes or backup plans.

**Prose style**: The text exemplifies classical Chinese narrative at its finest—economical yet vivid, using dialogue and action to reveal character, with minimal authorial commentary.

### Themes

The story explores the tension between immediate benefit and long-term security, the wisdom of investing in intangible assets like popular goodwill, and the value of advisors who dare to act independently for their patron's ultimate benefit. It questions conventional wisdom about gratitude, loyalty, and the true nature of effective service.

### Cultural Legacy

"Feng Xuan, Retainer of Lord Mengchang" has profoundly influenced Chinese political culture and literature. The phrase "three burrows of the cunning rabbit" remains current in modern Chinese, and Feng Xuan has become a cultural archetype of the wise strategist whose unconventional methods serve deeper purposes. The text is standard reading in Chinese literary education and continues to offer insights into loyalty, strategy, and the art of securing one's position in an uncertain world.
