**Question:** How can I reduce the habit of comparing myself to others — in grades, social status, achievements, and similar areas?

---

**Answer:**

Social comparison is deeply wired into us (psychologist Leon Festinger called it "social comparison theory" in 1954), but it's a habit you can deliberately weaken. Here's how:

**1. Shift your reference point inward**
Compare yourself only to *your past self*. Ask: "Am I better than I was last month?" rather than "Am I better than them?" Progress over performance.

**2. Recognize comparison is almost always unfair**
You're seeing someone else's highlight reel versus your full behind-the-scenes. Their grade, title, or follower count tells you nothing about their anxiety, sacrifice, or luck involved.

**3. Audit your information diet**
Social media is a comparison machine by design. Reducing exposure — unfollowing, muting, or setting time limits — directly reduces the trigger volume.

**4. Reframe envy as information, not judgement**
When you feel envious, ask: *"What does this tell me about what I actually value?"* Envy points to desire, not inadequacy. Use it as a compass, not a verdict.

**5. Practise "compersion" (shared joy)**
Actively celebrate others' wins as if they were yours. It trains your brain to see other people's success as non-threatening — even inspiring.

**6. Define your own metrics**
If you haven't decided what *success* looks like for *you*, you'll automatically borrow society's metrics (GPA, salary, followers). Write down 3–5 things you personally care about. Judge yourself only by those.

**7. Gratitude anchoring**
A brief daily gratitude practice is well-studied for reducing upward comparison. It reorients attention toward what you have rather than what others have more of.

**8. Limit zero-sum thinking**
Grades on a curve feel zero-sum; most of life isn't. Someone else's achievement rarely takes anything from you.

---

The core shift: move from **"Where do I rank?"** → **"Where am I going?"**
