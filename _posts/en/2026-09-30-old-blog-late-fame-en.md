**Question: Why did I only discover a website like kexue.fm around 2024, when the author became more popular?**

**Answer:**

Actually, 科学空间 (kexue.fm) is much older than its recent popularity. It was created around **2009**, originally as a personal science blog, and later evolved into a place focusing on original mathematical and AI-related technical writing. ([Kexue][1]) The domain `kexue.fm` itself was added in 2017; before that it used `spaces.ac.cn`. ([Kexue][2])

The reason many people (including AI engineers) discovered it only around 2023–2024 is mainly a **distribution change**, not that the website suddenly appeared.

### 1. Before LLM era, the audience was small

Before ChatGPT/LLM became mainstream, articles about:

* Transformer mathematics
* attention mechanisms
* optimization theory
* diffusion models
* probability/statistics
* mathematical derivations

were read mostly by a small group:

* NLP researchers
* graduate students
* serious ML engineers

A blog explaining formulas deeply has a very narrow natural audience.

Most software engineers in China before 2023 were learning from:

* tutorials
* courses
* GitHub
* Stack Overflow
* engineering blogs

not mathematical AI blogs.

---

### 2. LLM explosion created a huge new audience

After ChatGPT (2022), suddenly millions of engineers wanted to understand:

* "Why does Transformer work?"
* "What is attention really doing?"
* "How does scaling law work?"
* "Why does SGD train giant models?"

A person searching these questions would naturally find articles from kexue.fm.

For example, the website now has many AI topics around:

* Attention
* Transformer
* language models
* optimization
* diffusion
* MoE

which match the interests of the 2024 AI wave. ([Kexue][3])

---

### 3. Search engines have a long-term compounding effect

A blog written continuously for 10+ years has a huge advantage.

Imagine:

2009:

* 10 readers/day

2015:

* 500 readers/day

2020:

* 5,000 readers/day

2024:

* AI boom brings 50,000 new searches/day

The old content becomes an "AI knowledge graph" indexed by Google/Baidu.

This is similar to how:

* Karpathy's old lectures became popular after ChatGPT
* Andrej Karpathy's old GitHub repos gained attention later
* Unix/Linux papers became famous decades later

The value was created earlier; the market arrived later.

---

### 4. The author's style fits the LLM era

Su Jianlin's writing style is unusual:

* starts from first principles
* derives equations
* avoids too much academic ceremony
* connects theory with implementation

For example, instead of:

> "Attention mechanism was proposed in paper X"

he often explains:

> Given matrices Q, K, V, why does softmax(QKᵀ)V work mathematically?

This matches the current generation of AI engineers who want to go deeper than just calling APIs.

---

### 5. Personal blogs have a "quiet accumulation" advantage

A traditional media company needs:

* editors
* funding
* traffic

A technical personal blog needs only:

* one curious person
* 10 years of writing

The author accumulated a knowledge asset.

This is very similar to:

* Andrej Karpathy writing educational materials
* 王垠 writing deep technical essays
* Paul Graham writing essays

The audience often comes later.

---

There is also an interesting lesson for your own `lzwjava.com`:

A technical blog is usually **not a news product**. It is closer to planting trees.

A 2026 article about:

* agent architecture
* GPU debugging
* LLM inference
* training small models
* AI consulting methodology

may have only 100 readers today, but if AI continues growing, the search demand in 2030 may be much larger.

kexue.fm is basically a 15-year example of this compounding effect. ([Kexue][1])
