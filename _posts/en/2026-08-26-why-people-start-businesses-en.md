Question: Why do people start businesses or startups?

Answer:

People start businesses for a mix of personal, financial, and societal reasons:

**Financial Motivation**
- To build wealth beyond what a salary can offer — ownership creates equity and long-term value
- To capture the full reward of their own effort rather than working for someone else's profit

**Autonomy & Control**
- Freedom to set their own schedule, culture, and direction
- Escaping organizational bureaucracy and office politics
- Making decisions without needing approval from above

**Problem-Solving Drive**
- Spotting a gap in the market that frustrates them personally ("scratch your own itch")
- Believing they can do something better than what currently exists
- Genuine passion for fixing a real problem for real people

**Impact & Legacy**
- Creating jobs and contributing to the economy
- Building something that outlasts them
- Shaping an industry or changing how people live

**Identity & Purpose**
- Entrepreneurship is deeply tied to identity for many — being a *builder* feels more meaningful than being an employee
- The challenge itself is intrinsically motivating for ambitious people

**Opportunism**
- Timing: a new technology, regulation change, or market shift opens a window
- Access to capital (venture funding, crowdfunding) has never been easier
- Low startup costs in software mean the barrier to try is minimal

**Social Proof & Culture**
- Startup culture (especially in tech hubs) glamorizes founding a company
- Role models like Jobs, Musk, or Zuckerberg make it aspirational

The honest truth is that most founders are driven by *several* of these at once — rarely just one.
