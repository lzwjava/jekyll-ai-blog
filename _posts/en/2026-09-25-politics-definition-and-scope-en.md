Question: What is politics?

Answer: Politics is the activity and process through which people make collective decisions, exercise power, and govern communities — from small groups to entire nations. It encompasses:

- **Power and authority** — who gets to make binding decisions for a group, and how that authority is established or contested
- **Governance** — the systems, institutions, and rules (governments, laws, constitutions) through which societies are organized and managed
- **Conflict and cooperation** — the negotiation of competing interests, values, and resources among individuals and groups
- **Policy-making** — deciding how public resources are allocated and which problems society should address
- **Representation** — how the voices and interests of citizens are expressed and channeled into decision-making

The word comes from the Greek *polis* (city-state), reflecting its ancient roots in Aristotle's idea that humans are inherently "political animals" who naturally form organized communities.

Politics exists at every level — local, national, and international — and spans formal institutions (parliaments, courts) as well as informal dynamics (social movements, public opinion, media influence).
