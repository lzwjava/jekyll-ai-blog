**Question:** For a public company in China, how is finance/accounting handled, and does bookkeeping (记账) need to be especially careful?

**Answer:**

Yes — absolutely. Public companies in China face a significantly stricter financial compliance regime than private ones. Here's a breakdown of what's required:

---

### 📚 Accounting Standards (记账标准)

Chinese Accounting Standards for Business Enterprises (ASBEs) are **mandatory** for all listed Chinese enterprises. Companies listed on both mainland and Hong Kong exchanges may adopt ASBEs for the preparation of financial reports, and as of 2026 many dual-listed companies are consolidating toward ASBEs as a unified standard.

---

### 🗂️ Bookkeeping Rules — What's Strictly Forbidden

Article 217 of the Company Law **prohibits** a company from keeping accounting books outside its statutory books, and also prohibits holding company funds in an account opened in any individual's name. This means **no "shadow books" (小账/两套账)** — a very common compliance trap.

Article 216 also requires the company to give its accounting service provider true and complete vouchers, ledgers, reports, and other accounting materials, and prohibits refusing, concealing, or misreporting them.

---

### 📊 Periodic Reporting Requirements (定期报告)

According to the Measures for the Administration of Information Disclosure by Listed Companies, once a company is listed, it must publish its **quarterly, interim, and annual** accounts within prescribed timeframes.

Specifically:

- Within **two months** of the conclusion of the first half of each fiscal year, a listed company must submit a report including financial and accounting reports, business situation, major litigation, and share changes.
- Within **four months** of the last day of each fiscal year, the annual report must include the company's general circumstances, financial and accounting reports, shareholding details of directors/supervisors/managers, and shares/bond details.

---

### 🔍 Audit Requirements

Every company in China must prepare annual financial statements and have them audited. Only a CPA firm licensed by the provincial finance department may perform that audit, and reports must carry the firm's seal together with the signatures of two China-registered certified public accountants.

Publicly traded companies face even more rigorous periodic reporting obligations mandated by the CSRC.

---

### ⚠️ Regulator Enforcement (监管力度)

The CSRC is watching closely. As of April 30, 2026, the CSRC found that while most listed companies correctly implemented accounting standards, some made errors — particularly in revenue recognition, financial instruments, long-term equity investment, business combinations, asset impairment, and R&D expenditures.

The CSRC has stated it will further refine its oversight framework for financial information disclosure and enhance regulatory effectiveness.

---

### 🌱 New: Sustainability Reporting (2025–2026)

All three Chinese securities exchanges now require constituent companies of major indices (SSE 180, STAR 50, Shenzhen 100, ChiNext) and dual-listed companies to prepare and publicly disclose a **corporate sustainability report** covering calendar year 2025 by April 30, 2026.

---

### Summary

| Requirement | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Accounting standard | ASBEs (mandatory for listed companies) |
| Bookkeeping | Statutory books only, no dual/shadow books |
| Audit | Annual, by licensed CPA firm |
| Reporting cadence | Quarterly + Semi-annual + Annual |
| Regulator | CSRC (securities) + MoF (accounting) |
| Penalties for non-compliance | Rectification orders, fines, license revocation |

Short answer: **Yes, 记账 needs to be meticulous.** The CSRC actively reviews disclosures and flags companies with errors, so having a qualified CFO, internal compliance team, and a reputable audit firm is non-negotiable for a listed entity.
