Question: Should social media mindset be “I am already popular, I don’t care about followers, I just express myself” like some independent bloggers, and does caring too much about others’ opinions make you less confident and more insecure?

Answer:

There are **two different psychological states** mixed in your idea, and separating them makes this much clearer:

### 1. Healthy independence ≠ “I am already popular”

A strong creator mindset is not really:

> “I am popular, I have high reputation”

That can quietly turn into **ego protection** or self-delusion.

A more stable version is:

> “I express ideas I believe are valuable. Some people will like it, some won’t, and that’s fine.”

This is closer to how independent thinkers operate—what you observed in some long-form bloggers is usually:

* low dependence on approval loops
* high tolerance for disagreement
* focus on internal curiosity, not metrics

That *looks like* “I don’t care about followers,” but internally it is more like **selective attention**, not arrogance.

---

### 2. Caring too much about opinions can reduce clarity (true)

Yes, you are right on this part:

If someone constantly thinks:

* “Did people unfollow me?”
* “Do they approve this post?”
* “Is this embarrassing?”

Then the mind shifts from **expression → performance anxiety**.

That usually leads to:

* less originality
* safer, more boring content
* emotional dependence on feedback loops
* reduced consistency

So in that sense, excessive social validation dependence can weaken confidence and output quality.

---

### 3. But “not caring at all” is also not optimal

A common mistake is swinging to the opposite extreme:

> “I don’t care about anyone’s opinion at all.”

That can lead to:

* ignoring useful feedback
* repeating low-quality ideas without refinement
* missing audience signals (what actually works)

High-level creators usually do something more balanced:

> They **don’t depend on approval emotionally**, but they **do observe feedback strategically**.

---

### 4. A better mindset model (practical)

Think of it like this:

#### Inner layer (identity)

* “I am exploring and expressing ideas.”
* “My worth is not decided by likes or followers.”

#### Outer layer (strategy)

* “I will observe what resonates.”
* “I will improve based on signal, not emotion.”

This creates:

* confidence without arrogance
* openness without insecurity
* consistency without obsession

---

### 5. About examples like independent bloggers

People like the one you mentioned often succeed not because they “don’t care about others,” but because:

* they are **highly internally driven**
* they can tolerate being misunderstood
* they publish ideas without waiting for permission
* they don’t optimize emotionally for short-term reactions

That’s different from “I am popular, I don’t care.”
It’s closer to: **“I am anchored internally.”**

---

### 6. Key correction to your conclusion

You said:

> “too care about others' opinions will make one less confident and more insecure, right?”

More accurate version is:

* **Too much dependence on approval → insecurity increases**
* **But zero awareness of audience → content quality drops**

So the goal is not “not caring,” but **de-linking self-worth from feedback while still learning from feedback.**
