**Question: Is Federer an example of a better mindset because tennis is about winning only slightly more than losing?**

**Answer:**

Yes. Roger Federer is a very good example of this mindset.

In tennis, even the greatest players lose many points and many matches. A famous idea in tennis is:

> A player can be a champion while winning only slightly more than half of the important moments.

For example, if a player wins:

- 52% of points → they can dominate a match
- 54% of points → they can become one of the greatest players ever

The difference between winning and losing is often very small.

Federer's career mindset was not "I must never make mistakes." It was closer to:

> "I will play the next point well."

A tennis match contains thousands of emotional resets:

```
win point → forget → next point
lose point → forget → next point
```

If a player becomes too attached:

```
miss shot
↓
anger
↓
tension
↓
worse movement
↓
more mistakes
```

they lose control.

Great athletes develop the ability to **care deeply but recover quickly**.

Federer was known for:
- staying calm after errors
- accepting that losing is part of the game
- focusing on process rather than the scoreboard
- enjoying competition instead of treating every mistake as a disaster

This is similar to startup or engineering life:

```
experiment → failure → learn → adjust → next experiment
```

A founder who thinks:

> "Every failed attempt means I am a failure"

will suffer too much and stop experimenting.

A founder with a Federer-like mindset thinks:

> "This attempt failed. What information did reality give me?"

The goal is not to become emotionless. Federer still cared enormously. The difference is that he did not let one point control his mind.

A useful formula:

```
High standards + low ego attachment = long-term excellence
```

This is why some extremely successful people appear relaxed. Their calmness does not mean they care less; it means they understand that the game is made of many small wins and losses.

I can also compare Federer’s mindset with Nadal and Djokovic, because their mental styles are very different.
