Question: **Who are the major Shenzhen cross-border e-commerce merchants/sellers on Amazon and TikTok Shop, and what kind of businesses are they?**

Answer:

Yes — **Shenzhen is one of the main “China → global e-commerce” operating clusters**, especially for Amazon. But there are two different generations of companies:

1. **Old-school marketplace sellers** — Amazon/eBay/AliExpress, SKU-heavy, supply-chain driven.
2. **New global brands / TikTok-native operators** — brand + content + creator + overseas warehouse.

The companies I would actually put on your radar are:

### 1. The absolute heavyweight: Anker

Anker Innovations is probably the most important Shenzhen example.

This isn't really a "seller" anymore. It's a **global consumer-electronics company whose initial growth engine was Amazon**.

Its brands include:

* Anker
* soundcore
* eufy
* Nebula
* Anker SOLIX
* AnkerWork
* eufyMake

In 2024, Amazon reportedly contributed **RMB 13.147B** of Anker's RMB 24.71B revenue — roughly **53%**. ([100ec][1])

That's the Shenzhen playbook at its highest level:

```text
Shenzhen supply chain
        ↓
Amazon listing
        ↓
Product-market fit
        ↓
Brand
        ↓
DTC
        ↓
TikTok / creators
        ↓
Offline retail
        ↓
Global consumer brand
```

### 2. Aukey

AUKEY is another very Amazon-native Shenzhen company.

Historically:

```text
OEM/ODM
  ↓
Amazon seller
  ↓
AUKEY brand
  ↓
global consumer-electronics company
```

Industry rankings put Aukey among Shenzhen's largest Amazon sellers; one 2025 industry ranking reports **RMB 10.71B 2024 revenue**. ([Weibo][2])

Typical categories:

* chargers
* power banks
* cables
* hubs
* computer accessories
* consumer electronics

---

### 3. Sailvan Times / 赛维时代

Sailvan Times is particularly interesting if you're looking for a **large-scale merchant/operator rather than a single product brand**.

Their own description is basically:

> technology-driven export cross-border brand e-commerce

They operate through Amazon, Walmart, eBay, Wish and their own vertical sites. ([SailVan][3])

Their stack looks more like:

```text
supplier network
      ↓
data/product selection
      ↓
product development
      ↓
multiple brands
      ↓
Amazon + Walmart + eBay + DTC
      ↓
overseas warehouse
```

Their strength is **many brands × many SKUs × centralized data/supply chain**.

---

### 4. Tomtop / 通拓

Tomtop is one of the older Shenzhen cross-border players.

Think of it as the **pre-TikTok-generation Shenzhen merchant**:

```text
China factory ecosystem
       ↓
select products
       ↓
Amazon/eBay/AliExpress
       ↓
own websites
       ↓
overseas customers
```

It is a useful company to study because it represents the original Shenzhen cross-border model rather than today's brand-first model. Shenzhen industry reports still list Tongtuo among the major cross-border operators. ([100ec][1])

---

### 5. UGREEN / 绿联

UGREEN is another very important one.

Unlike a generic Amazon merchant, UGREEN has become a **real hardware brand**:

* USB-C accessories
* chargers
* NAS
* hubs
* cables
* power products
* storage
* networking

It is basically another version of the **"Shenzhen manufacturing → Amazon → global brand"** transition. Shenzhen's cross-border industry report specifically identifies UGREEN as a major brand-export company. ([100ec][1])

---

### 6. TORRAS / 图拉斯

TORRAS is especially interesting because it shows how a **single category can become a huge global business**.

Phone cases + screen protectors → Amazon → global brand.

The 2025 Shenzhen cross-border report says TORRAS had accumulated **100M+ global users** and sells into 100+ countries. ([Caifuhao][4])

This is a very different strategy from Tomtop:

```text
Tomtop:
10,000 products × lots of categories

TORRAS:
one category × extreme product/brand depth
```

## And TikTok Shop is changing the Shenzhen merchant archetype

This is where I think the distinction becomes important.

The **top TikTok Shop Shenzhen operators are not necessarily the same companies as the old Amazon giants**.

A good example is **同舟互联 / First Class Interconnected**.

They explicitly report operating:

* Amazon
* TikTok Shop
* Shopee
* Lazada
* MercadoLibre

and focus heavily on Southeast Asia, North America and Australia. Their reported 2025 cross-border GMV was **RMB 100M+**, with 15–20K daily orders from Thailand as of June 2026. ([头等舱互联科技（深圳）有限公司][5])

That's much closer to the new model:

```text
TikTok content
      ↓
creator / affiliate
      ↓
viral SKU
      ↓
TikTok Shop
      ↓
local warehouse
      ↓
replenishment
      ↓
new markets
```

rather than:

```text
Amazon keyword
    ↓
SEO
    ↓
PPC
    ↓
ranking
    ↓
FBA
```

## The Shenzhen ecosystem is actually more interesting than the sellers

If your purpose is **finding people/companies to sell AI, agents, SaaS, automation, or infrastructure to**, I would not only target the merchant brands.

There are roughly **four layers**:

```text
                    Shenzhen CBEC
                         │
        ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
        │                │                │
     BRANDS          MERCHANTS        SERVICE STACK
        │                │                │
 Anker/Ugreen       Sailvan/Tomtop    ERP
 TORRAS             Aukey             logistics
 PatPat             TikTok sellers   payment
        │                │                │
        └────────────────┴────────────────┘
                         │
                    SUPPLY CHAIN
                         │
              factories / sourcing
              warehouses / fulfillment
```

And Shenzhen has a huge software/service ecosystem around them: **店小秘, 易仓, 领星, 店匠, 17TRACK, 易达云, PingPong, 连连国际**, etc. A 2025 Shenzhen industry inventory identified **97 cross-border e-commerce-related companies** across merchants and infrastructure. ([100ec][1])

### If I were mapping the market for you

I'd divide the targets into:

**Tier A — giant brands**

* Anker
* UGREEN
* TORRAS
* AUKEY
* PatPat

**Tier B — giant merchant operators**

* Sailvan
* Tomtop
* Youkeshu
* Globalegrow

**Tier C — TikTok-native / multi-platform operators**

* newer Shenzhen sellers
* SEA-focused operators
* creator-affiliate organizations
* live-commerce teams

**Tier D — infrastructure**

* ERP
* sourcing
* logistics
* overseas warehouse
* payment
* tax
* advertising/agency

For an **AI/agent company**, I'd actually prioritize **Tier B + C + the service layer**. Their workflows are much more repetitive and agentizable: product research, competitor monitoring, listing generation, creative generation, customer service, ad optimization, creator outreach, inventory forecasting, procurement, and cross-market localization.
