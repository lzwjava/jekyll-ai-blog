Question: Is it true that high learning ability and deep knowledge create a "moat" that increases a person's value and attractiveness, and can we judge someone's quality by their long-term learning progress?

Answer: You have identified a core principle of modern economics and social dynamics: **Cognitive Scarcity**. When a skill takes years of disciplined practice to master—whether it is hitting an IELTS 6.5+ or becoming an expert in Generative AI—it creates a "moat" that is difficult for others to cross quickly.

### 1. The Power of the "Learning Moat"
In the business world, a "moat" is a competitive advantage that protects a company from rivals. For individuals, your "moat" is the **compounded knowledge** you have built over time.
* **Irreplaceability:** As you noted, big corporations have money, but money cannot buy "time spent learning." Even with billions of dollars, a bank cannot instantly "buy" the neural intuition a lead researcher at OpenAI has developed over a decade.
* **Signaling Value:** Achieving something difficult, like a high IELTS score or a technical certification, serves as a "signal" to society. It tells others you possess discipline, persistence, and high cognitive overhead.

### 2. Knowledge as an Attraction Magnet
High-value individuals (like top AI researchers or professors) attract "good people" because they offer **mentorship and proximity to excellence**. 
* In the IT/AI fields, the rate of change is so fast that being near someone with deep, foundational knowledge is a shortcut for others to learn. 
* This creates a "flywheel effect": Expert knowledge attracts talented peers, which leads to better projects, which further increases the value of that knowledge.

### 3. Judging Progress vs. Achievement
You asked if we can judge someone by their "progress." This is often a more accurate metric than their current status. 
* **The Velocity of Learning:** A person who has learned a vast amount in the last three years is often more valuable than someone who learned one thing ten years ago and stopped. 
* **Consistency:** Asking "What have you learned lately?" reveals a person's **growth mindset**. In a world where AI and technology shift every six months, the ability to learn *how* to learn is the ultimate currency.

### 4. Why Money Can't Always Compete
You mentioned that big banks or corps struggle to train in-house models despite having money. This is because training frontier models (like those from Anthropic or OpenAI) requires more than just GPUs; it requires:
1.  **Rare Talent:** People who have spent years failing and iterating.
2.  **Culture:** An environment that allows for high-risk experimentation, which "safe" big corporations often lack.
3.  **Data Moats:** Proprietary ways of cleaning and utilizing data that take years to refine.



In summary, you are correct. Learning is slow precisely because it is valuable. If it were fast, everyone would do it, and the value would drop to zero. Your "slow" progress is actually the process of building a wall that others cannot easily climb.
