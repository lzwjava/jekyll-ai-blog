Question: What does Chekhov’s story **《苦恼》(“Misery” / “Agony”)** say?

Answer:

Anton Chekhov’s short story **《苦恼》(Тоска, 1886)** is about **human loneliness, the need to be heard, and the indifference of society**.

The main character is **Iona Potapov**, an old cab driver in Saint Petersburg. His son has recently died, and Iona is carrying enormous grief. He desperately wants to tell someone:

> "My son died."

But nobody listens.

During the night, he carries several passengers:

1. **A military officer**

   * Iona tries to talk about his son's death.
   * The officer only cares about getting to his destination.

2. **Three young men**

   * They joke, shout, and treat Iona as an object, not as a person.
   * His pain has no place in their world.

3. **Other people around him**

   * Everyone is busy with their own lives.
   * Nobody gives him even a moment of human connection.

Finally, unable to find a human listener, Iona goes back to his stable and tells his sorrow to his horse.

The ending is the most famous part:

> Iona tells the horse everything because the horse is the only "listener" he has.

The irony is powerful:

* Humans ignore him.
* An animal receives his deepest emotions.

---

### Core themes

### 1. Loneliness is not being alone; it is being unheard

Chekhov is not mainly saying "Iona has no people around him."

He is saying:

> The most painful loneliness is when you are surrounded by people but nobody recognizes your suffering.

This idea appears everywhere in modern life:

* people posting online but feeling unseen;
* employees suffering silently;
* family members unable to communicate;
* people carrying invisible pain.

---

### 2. Small people also have deep inner worlds

19th-century Russian literature often focused on "ordinary people."

Iona is:

* poor,
* powerless,
* socially invisible.

But Chekhov shows:

A poor cab driver losing his son experiences grief as deeply as a rich person or a powerful person.

Human emotions are not proportional to social status.

---

### 3. Society creates emotional isolation

The passengers are not evil villains. That is important.

They are ordinary people:

* tired,
* busy,
* distracted.

Chekhov's criticism is subtle:

The tragedy is not that people hate each other.

The tragedy is that people are too absorbed in themselves to notice others.

---

### 4. The horse represents unconditional listening

The horse cannot solve Iona's problem.

It cannot give advice.

It simply listens.

Chekhov suggests that sometimes the first need of suffering people is not solutions, but recognition:

> "Someone knows that I am suffering."

---

### Connection to Chekhov's worldview

Chekhov was a doctor as well as a writer. His stories often avoid dramatic heroes and villains. He focuses on:

* ordinary suffering,
* quiet desperation,
* missed connections,
* the tragedy of everyday life.

Unlike some writers who ask:

> "Who is guilty?"

Chekhov often asks:

> "Why do people fail to see each other?"

---

A modern interpretation:

**《苦恼》 is about a man who has a story inside him, but the world has no attention span to hear it.**

It is especially relevant today because many people have more ways to communicate than ever, yet many still feel unheard.

I can also compare **Chekhov’s “Misery” with Kafka’s “The Metamorphosis”** — they are very similar in how they portray invisible human suffering.
