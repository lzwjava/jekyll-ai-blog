**Question:** How can I adopt Yin Wang's (yinwang.org) "care less" attitude toward social judgment and conventional success?

**Answer:**

Yin Wang (王垠) is a Chinese programmer and blogger known for his fiercely independent thinking and contempt for conventional social pressures. His philosophy of "caring less" runs throughout his writing and can be distilled into a few core ideas:

**1. Reject external scorekeeping**

Yin Wang argues that mainstream Chinese society's definition of "success" is a trap. If you care about it and strive for it, you become controlled by others — essentially their slave. Anyone who worries about others' opinions and chases conventional success metrics is, in his view, subordinate. His antidote: define success entirely on your own terms. *"Whether I am successful is for me to decide."*

**2. Recognize who has the right to judge you**

He points out that most people have been scored their whole lives — by parents, teachers, rankings, and competitions — yet they never stopped to ask whether those evaluators actually had the standing to judge them. Developing this critical distance is the first step to caring less.

**3. Stop performing for admirers**

He is repulsed by flattery and sycophancy. He doesn't care about making people submit through money or status — what matters to him is whether someone genuinely respects him from within, not what they perform on the surface. Once you stop valuing shallow approval, the pull of it weakens.

**4. Live in the present rather than documenting it**

He argues that the real loss in compulsive photography isn't others' perception of you, but the loss of present-moment experience itself. The moment you reach for the camera, the truest, most real information — the clarity of water, the sound of engines, the feeling of wildness — is missed by your eyes, ears, and body. Caring less means actually being *here* rather than performing your life for an audience.

**5. Cultivate genuine aesthetic independence**

He describes the truly elegant and independent person as one who doesn't submit to trends because they're popular, doesn't fear being seen as "old-fashioned" for being different, and is guided by their own taste — absorbing whatever is genuinely beautiful from the world.

**6. Simplify the mundane to free your mind**

Yin Wang practices what he preaches at a practical level too. He buys many identical socks so he never has to sort them. He doesn't fold T-shirts — just tosses them in a bin. The insight is that while the bin looks messy inside, the room still looks orderly overall. Eliminating petty decisions frees mental energy for what actually matters to you.

**The core shift:** Yin Wang's "care less" isn't apathy — it's a reorientation of *what you care about*. You stop caring about rankings, flattery, and others' definitions of success, so you can care deeply about the things you've chosen: your work, your taste, your genuine relationships.
