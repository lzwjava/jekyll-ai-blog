**Question:** Is the story of Xu Wei (徐伟), who researched a disease himself, synthesized his own medicine, and helped extend his child's life through his own efforts, true?

**Answer:**

Yes, this is a real and remarkable story that received major international attention. Here's a full summary:

**Background — The Diagnosis**

Xu Wei was a small business owner from Kunming, Yunnan province in southwest China, selling electrical sockets online. His one-year-old son Haoyang was diagnosed with Menkes Disease, a rare genetic disorder that typically results in death within just a few years.

**The Disease**

Haoyang has Menkes syndrome, a rare genetic copper-deficiency disorder that affects just one in 50,000 to 250,000 people. The disorder affects cellular copper transport and is associated with seizures, stunted growth, failure to thrive, and intellectual disability. Infants diagnosed with it usually have a life expectancy not exceeding three years.

**What Xu Wei Did**

With only a high school education, Xu Wei taught himself biomedicine from scratch. He quit his e-commerce job and poured nearly 300,000 RMB into the experiment — mostly from family savings, some credit-card spending, and public donations.

Determined to save his son, he turned his father's gym into a DIY laboratory. He enrolled in public courses to study pharmacology and read American research papers using translation software. He found that creating the copper histidine treatment involves mixing water with copper chloride dihydrate, L-histidine, and sodium hydroxide.

He first tested the mixture on rabbits, then injected the treatment into his own body before giving it to his son.

**The Result — Life Extended**

Xu gives Haoyang a daily dose of homemade medicine, which gives the child some of the copper his body is missing. A few blood test results returned to normal within two weeks of beginning treatment.

**Gene Therapy — The Next Step**

VectorBuilder formed a partnership with Lantu to develop AAV-based gene therapy candidates for Menkes. After receiving official approval, the drug LTGT06 was administered to Haoyang shortly after his third birthday. Xu Wei stated: "I knew that copper histidine can only slow down Menkes, and gene therapy is the only hope for reversing the disease. We are now beginning to notice improvements."

**Current Status**

Although the child is not fully cured, he has lived beyond the age of six — far surpassing the typical 3-year life expectancy for Menkes patients.

Xu accepts that the copper treatment can "only slow down the disease." Health authorities said they would not intervene as long as he only makes the treatment for home use.
