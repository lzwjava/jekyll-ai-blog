**Question:** Is it normal in the USA for people in their 30s or 40s to change industries or go back to university?

**Answer:**

Yes, it is very normal — and increasingly common — in the United States.

**Changing Job Industries**

A growing number of Americans in their 30s and 40s are changing careers, reflecting a shift away from traditional, linear career paths toward more flexible and purpose-driven work. According to U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics longitudinal surveys, Americans held an average of 2.9 jobs between ages 35 and 44, highlighting continued mobility during prime working years.

Workforce analyses estimate the average age of a career switch is about 39, placing many pivots in the late 30s and early 40s, as workers seek higher pay, flexibility, or better work-life balance.

Professionals over 45 who make the move report an 82% success rate, and about 66% of workers have changed or seriously considered changing career fields in the last year alone. In fact, those aged 45–54 saw average wage jumps of 7.4% after changing fields, and about 91% of career changers eventually enjoyed higher pay.

Common reasons for switching include burnout, layoffs, automation anxiety, and the desire for more meaningful work. Data shows that workers in their 40s and 50s often experience stronger wage growth and greater job satisfaction after making a switch.

**Going Back to University**

Returning to school as an adult is also widely accepted and well-supported in the US. Over a quarter of U.S. adults between the ages of 25 and 64 — representing 41.7 million people — intend to enroll in some type of higher education program or training within the next two years, according to a 2026 report from the Council for Adult and Experiential Learning.

More than 40 million Americans have some college credit but no degree, and more than one million students re-enrolled during the 2023–24 academic year — the most the National Student Clearinghouse has ever recorded, a 7% increase over the prior year.

Students who are over 25 make up 47% of the new and returning student population on many of today's college campuses, so adults in their 30s and 40s are far from alone. Online programs, night classes, and part-time options make it practical even for working adults with families.

**In short:** The US cultural attitude strongly supports reinvention at any age. There is no social stigma — if anything, it is admired.
