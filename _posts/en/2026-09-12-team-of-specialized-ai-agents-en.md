An **agent swarm** is a group of AI agents that work together—often in parallel—to solve a problem, rather than relying on one AI agent to do everything.

Think of it like a **team of specialized AI workers**:

* One agent might research information.
* Another analyzes the findings.
* Another writes code or performs calculations.
* Another checks the work for errors.
* A coordinator agent decides who should do what and combines the results.

The key idea is **coordination between multiple semi-autonomous agents**. They may communicate with each other, divide tasks dynamically, critique one another, or explore several possible solutions simultaneously.

For example, if you asked an agent swarm to **research whether a startup idea is viable**, it could operate roughly like:

**Market agent** → researches competitors
**Customer agent** → identifies customer needs
**Finance agent** → estimates economics
**Technical agent** → evaluates feasibility
**Critic agent** → challenges assumptions
↓
**Coordinator** → synthesizes everything into a recommendation

### Agent swarm vs. a single AI agent

A single agent generally follows one workflow sequentially. A swarm can have multiple agents pursuing different approaches at the same time and sharing what they discover.

That can make swarms useful for **complex research, software engineering, simulations, planning, and optimization**. But more agents don't automatically mean better results: swarms can be slower and more expensive, duplicate work, propagate incorrect information, or get stuck coordinating with each other.

In AI, **"agent swarm" isn't one precisely defined architecture**. It's a broad term covering multi-agent systems inspired partly by distributed teams and swarm intelligence.
