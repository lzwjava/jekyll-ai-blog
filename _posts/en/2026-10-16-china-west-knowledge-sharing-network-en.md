My recommendation is to evolve Fun Live from a paid live-streaming product into a **global knowledge-sharing community connecting Chinese and Western developers, engineers, researchers, and founders.**

## 1. Keep the original DNA, expand the mission

Your original Fun Live concept was already interesting: experts shared knowledge, audiences paid to attend, and speakers earned money for their work. You even experimented with referral-based revenue sharing. [Zhiwei Li](https://lzwjava.com/lieyun-en?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The new version could preserve that economic model while adding three capabilities:

- **Global distribution:** Every expert can reach audiences across language boundaries.
- **Persistent knowledge:** A live session becomes an article, video, transcript, and searchable technical resource.
- **Long-term relationships:** Participants can meet, collaborate on open source, find clients, and build products together.

Consider how the product could work.

A Chinese AI engineer gives a live presentation in Mandarin. AI generates English subtitles and a translated transcript. An American engineer asks a question in English, which the speaker reads in Chinese. They continue discussing the implementation.

Afterward, the recording and technical notes become available to the global community.

A week later, they collaborate on GitHub.

That is a more valuable outcome than simply watching a livestream.

Importantly, **you don't need to own every piece of content or build every collaboration tool.** Let contributors retain ownership of their work, integrate existing developer tools, and focus on connecting people.

## 3. How Fun Live could differentiate itself

I would not position it as another Coursera.

Coursera focuses on structured learning and courses. GeekTime has established a model for expert-led technical education. GitHub provides the infrastructure for software collaboration.

Fun Live could focus on a different intersection:

| Dimension | Fun Live Global |
| --- | --- |
| Primary audience | Chinese and international technical practitioners |
| Core experience | Live knowledge exchange and ongoing collaboration |
| Language | Chinese and English, with AI-assisted translation |
| Content | Real engineering experience, research, and startup lessons |
| Community | Relationships between people across countries |
| Practical outcomes | Knowledge, open-source contributions, partnerships, and business opportunities |
| Initial revenue | Consulting, sponsored sessions, and paid workshops |

The distinction is that the community isn't just somewhere to learn from experts. It's somewhere to **meet experts and build things with them**.

## 4. Your first 10 speakers matter more than your first 10,000 users

Start with people you can personally reach.

For example:

- Three Chinese AI engineers with interesting projects.
- Two international engineers working on open source.
- Two practitioners from Hong Kong or Singapore.
- Two founders building AI products.
- One researcher working on LLM training or inference.

Host a series called *Builders Across Borders*.

Each session should address a real question, such as how to build efficient AI inference systems, ship products with coding agents, or acquire international software customers.

Invite speakers to contribute their expertise, not confidential information from their employers.

Publish each session in Chinese and English, then ask participants to invite one person who would add value to the next discussion.

This tests whether the community can grow through meaningful relationships rather than advertising alone.

## 5. The business model

I'd keep consulting as the initial revenue engine.

Your existing engineering and AI capabilities give you a way to serve companies while the community develops.

For example:

1. An international company attends an AI engineering session.
2. Its CTO discovers your technical work.
3. The CTO contacts you about an AI automation project.
4. You deliver the project yourself or assemble a suitable team.
5. You use the revenue to fund better content, events, and community operations.

Later, you can add paid workshops, expert courses, sponsorships, and project-matching services.

You don't need to wait until Fun Live has a massive audience to make money.

## 6. My strongest recommendation

**Don't rebuild the old Fun Live product first. Rebuild the network first.**

Your old product helped people attend live knowledge sessions. The new product should help people discover one another, exchange knowledge, and develop lasting relationships.

Start with your existing website, content, social channels, and community. Use AI agents to automate translation and content distribution. Host recurring sessions. Find out which people participate repeatedly and what they want to build together.

Build dedicated software only when you understand the repeated problems that existing tools cannot solve adequately.

Your advantage is not just AI translation. It's the combination of your previous startup experience, technical credibility, existing audience, and ability to build software yourself.

The vision can be ambitious without making the first step expensive.
