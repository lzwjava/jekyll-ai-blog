Question: **If I set up a Hong Kong company, can I use a Mainland Exit-Entry Permit with a business endorsement to go to Hong Kong, find clients, and work there? If not, how do I get the right to actually live and work in Hong Kong?**

Answer: **Your distinction is exactly right: a Hong Kong company gives you a business vehicle, but your Mainland travel endorsement and your Hong Kong immigration status are separate.**

For a Mainland Chinese resident, I would **not** structure the plan as:

```text
HK company
   ↓
Business endorsement (商务签注)
   ↓
Go to HK
   ↓
Actually work there
```

A business endorsement is for **visiting/business activities**, not a general work/residence permission. Hong Kong Immigration explicitly says people who come as visitors are not permitted to take employment, paid or unpaid, or establish/join a business. ([Immigration Department][1])

### The route that matches your idea

If your real goal is:

> **I establish my own HK software/AI consulting company, develop clients there, and actually live/work in Hong Kong.**

Then the relevant immigration concept is **“Investment as Entrepreneur”**.

But there is a major catch for you:

**The standard Investment as Entrepreneur scheme under the GEP explicitly excludes Chinese residents of Mainland China.** ([Immigration Department][2])

So you cannot simply say:

```text
I'm a Mainland Chinese citizen
        +
I own a HK company
        ↓
apply GEP entrepreneur visa
```

That specific route is not available to Mainland Chinese residents.

### What about getting hired by your own company?

This is where the **Admission Scheme for Mainland Talents and Professionals (ASMTP)** becomes relevant.

Conceptually:

```text
You
 │
 │ employment
 ▼
Hong Kong company
 │
 │ sponsor
 ▼
Hong Kong Immigration
 │
 ▼
employment entry permission
 │
 ▼
live + work in HK
```

The professional-employment route is separate from merely owning the company. Hong Kong Immigration currently maintains a dedicated guide for employment as professionals, including ASMTP. ([Immigration Department][3])

But you should **not assume that incorporating your own tiny company automatically makes it eligible to sponsor you**. Immigration will look at the employment/company circumstances and supporting evidence.

---

## So for you, I would think about it as two phases

### Phase 1 — Business endorsement

You could use the Mainland system's business travel arrangement to:

```text
Guangzhou/Shenzhen
      ↓
Hong Kong
      ↓
meet potential clients
meet partners
attend meetings
negotiate contracts
research market
      ↓
return to Mainland
```

That is fundamentally **business visiting**, not relocating your workplace to HK.

### Phase 2 — Actual relocation/work

Once you have something like:

```text
HK Ltd
  │
  ├── real clients
  ├── contracts
  ├── revenue
  ├── business activity
  └── genuine position for you
          ↓
   immigration application
          ↓
   employment/residence permission
```

you pursue an immigration route that actually authorizes employment/residence.

---

## The interesting part: your HK company can still be very useful

You don't necessarily need the company **because the company itself gives you immigration status**.

You can use it to build the evidence that makes your eventual Hong Kong operation credible:

```text
2026
│
├── Incorporate HK Ltd
│
├── Build website / portfolio
│
├── Shenzhen/Guangzhou
│
├── Visit HK for legitimate business meetings
│
├── Find HK clients
│
├── Sign consulting contracts
│
├── Generate revenue
│
└── Build actual HK business operation
          │
          ▼
   immigration application
          │
          ▼
   move to HK
```

That's a much more realistic strategy than:

> “I'll register a HK company and therefore I can live in Hong Kong.”

---

### And there is a second important consequence

If you eventually obtain an eligible Hong Kong employment/residence status, **your family situation becomes much more interesting**.

Hong Kong's current rules allow dependants of people admitted for certain employment categories and for investment as entrepreneurs to apply for residence, subject to the applicable requirements. ([Immigration Department][4])

So the long-term structure can potentially become:

```text
                  You
                   │
          HK employment status
                   │
          ┌────────┴────────┐
          │                 │
       HK Ltd             HK residence
                            │
                    dependant applications
                            │
                    spouse + children
```

That is a fundamentally different situation from repeatedly entering HK on business visits.

**Bottom line:** yes, your idea of **using business visits first to validate the HK market** makes sense. But don't treat the business endorsement as a backdoor work visa. For actually living and working there as a Mainland Chinese resident, you need an appropriate Hong Kong employment/residence route; and the normal GEP entrepreneur route specifically excludes Mainland Chinese residents. ([Immigration Department][2])

For your particular plan, I'd investigate **ASMTP + your own HK company** much more seriously than “business endorsement → just stay and work.”
