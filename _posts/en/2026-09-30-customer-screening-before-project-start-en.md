**Question: Should companies do customer background checks before starting work, similar to employee background checks?**

**Answer:**

Yes, for many B2B projects, doing a lightweight customer qualification/background check can save significant time and reduce risk. The idea is similar to hiring: before investing a lot of engineering effort, you verify that the relationship is likely to work.

Large companies usually do this in different forms:

* **Employee side:** background checks → identity, employment history, legal checks, references.
* **Customer side:** customer qualification → business legitimacy, budget, requirements, decision process, expectations.

A 30-minute discovery call is a common practice. The goal is not to "judge" the customer, but to reduce uncertainty.

A useful checklist:

### 1. Business background

* Who are they?
* What company/product/team?
* Why do they need this now?
* Who is the decision maker?
* Have they done similar projects before?

### 2. Requirement matching

* What problem are they solving?
* What does success look like?
* What is explicitly out of scope?
* Do they understand the complexity?

### 3. Timeline and deadline reality

Ask:

* "Why this deadline?"
* "What happens if we miss it?"
* "Who depends on this delivery?"

Many failed projects happen because the deadline is not technically realistic.

### 4. Working style compatibility

Check:

* Communication frequency
* How they make decisions
* Whether they give feedback quickly
* Whether they respect engineering process

A technically easy project can become painful with a bad collaboration pattern.

### 5. Commercial risk

Check:

* Budget range
* Payment terms
* Procurement process
* Contract requirements

A common mistake is spending weeks designing solutions before discovering the customer cannot approve payment.

A simple equation:

```
Project Risk = Technical Risk × Customer Risk × Communication Risk
```

Even if technical risk is low, high customer risk can make the whole project fail.

For consulting/AI agent projects especially, customer screening is valuable because many customers have unclear expectations like:

> "Build an AI agent that replaces our whole workflow in two weeks."

A 30–60 minute qualification call can reveal whether they need:

* a prototype,
* a production system,
* a research experiment,
* or just consulting.

The best companies usually do not "background check customers" in the same formal way as employees; they use **customer discovery, qualification, references, and commercial checks** before committing large resources.
