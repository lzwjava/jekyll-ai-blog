**Question:** How has China's loan issuance volume (贷款发放量) trended in recent years?

**Answer:**

China's new RMB loan issuance has gone through three distinct phases over the past five years — a surge, a peak, and a structural slowdown.

**Annual New RMB Loans (PBOC data):****Key highlights by year:**

**2021:** New yuan loans totaled ¥19.95 trillion, setting a record at the time, up ¥315 billion year-on-year as the economy rebounded from COVID.

**2022:** The real economy received ¥20.91 trillion in new yuan loans, up ¥974.6 billion year-on-year.

**2023:** Lending surged further to approximately ¥22.75 trillion, the historical peak, as post-COVID reopening unleashed pent-up credit demand, particularly in the corporate sector.

**2024:** New RMB loans totaled ¥18.09 trillion for the full year — a significant pullback, reflecting weak property market demand and soft consumer confidence.

**2025 (full year):** China's new bank loans totalled ¥16.27 trillion in 2025, the lowest since 2018, highlighting weak borrowing needs as a prolonged property downturn and tepid demand suppressed business and household appetite for credit.

**2026 (YTD):** The slowdown continues. New loans totalled ¥10.38 trillion in the first seven months of 2026, down from ¥12.87 trillion year-on-year. July even saw a record monthly contraction.

**Why the decline?** Several structural headwinds are at work: the real estate market downturn has gutted mortgage demand; local governments are constrained by debt overhangs; and businesses and households remain cautious. Loan growth rate reached 11.0% in 2023, slightly declined from 11.1% the previous year, but by 2025 outstanding loan growth had slowed to around 6–7% — near multi-decade lows. Outstanding loan growth also slowed to 6.5% in early 2026, the least since at least 1998.
