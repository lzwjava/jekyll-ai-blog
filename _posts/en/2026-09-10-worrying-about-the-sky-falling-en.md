Question: What does **杞人忧天 (qǐ rén yōu tiān)** mean?

Answer: **杞人忧天** literally means **“a person from Qi worrying that the sky might fall.”**

It comes from an ancient Chinese story: a man from the state of **Qi (杞)** was constantly afraid that the sky would collapse and crush him. His fear was unnecessary because there was no real evidence that it would happen.

So today, **杞人忧天** means:

> **To worry excessively about something unlikely or imaginary.**
> **To have unnecessary anxiety about hypothetical problems.**

Examples:

* 他总担心 AI 会把自己的工作抢走，真是杞人忧天。
  → *He is always worrying that AI will take his job; he's worrying about something unnecessarily.*

* 你现在就担心十年后的事情，有点杞人忧天了。
  → *You're already worrying about something ten years from now—that's a bit excessive.*

### Nuance

It's usually **slightly dismissive**: the speaker thinks the worry is **unnecessary or unrealistic**.

But it doesn't simply mean **“worrying”**. The key idea is:

**worry → about a hypothetical/remote danger → without sufficient reason**

A useful English equivalent is **“worrying about something that may never happen”** or **“borrowing trouble.”**
