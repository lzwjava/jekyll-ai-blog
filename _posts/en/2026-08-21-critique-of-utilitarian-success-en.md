**Question:** How does Yin Wang (王垠) on yinwang.org criticize 功利之心 (the utilitarian/mercenary mindset), and what is the value of the article at lzwjava.com/success-en?

**Answer:**

The article translated and curated at lzwjava.com/success-en is Yin Wang's essay *"On So-Called Success"*, which is a direct, sustained critique of 功利之心 — the mercenary mindset that chases external validation, status, and money as definitions of achievement.

Here are the core threads of his critique:

**1. "Success" as social slavery**

Yin Wang argues that the "success" recognized by the Chinese public is a trap — if you chase it, you become a slave to others' standards and validation. Those who crave external approval and conform to mainstream definitions of success are, in his view, nothing but slaves.

**2. The grading machine**

He traces the root of 功利之心 to a lifetime of being graded — by parents, teachers, exams, and rankings — which conditions people to never evaluate the world from their own perspective or question the authority of those who judge them. After school, this continues through bosses, colleagues, public opinion, and partners. Chinese "success" is just another scorecard, and the graded are always inferior to the graders.

**3. Self-defined standards over external metrics**

Yin Wang states plainly: "I define my own success." He does what fulfills him and attracts those who share his values, explicitly ignoring mainstream tastes, which he says often signal mediocrity.

**4. Money and fame without integrity are worthless**

He describes refusing multiple offers where people waved money at him and asked him to lend his name to their platforms. He also criticizes "successful" executives, senior engineers, and even Ivy League professors who succumb to money by endorsing vulgar or manipulative figures — once respectable in his eyes, their choices destroyed that image. To the uncritical, they're "successful"; to him, they're trash.

**5. Real value vs. bubble companies**

He challenges the reader to study the balance sheets and business models of many celebrated internet companies, arguing most are bubbles and toxins that, when burst, harm the economy and everyone in it. Many famous companies just churn out garbage information.

**6. Words carry their own weight**

His counter-thesis to 功利之心 is that genuine ideas create lasting influence without needing status or money. He says many young engineers, researchers, and professors grew up reading his work, and his words resonate directly with their hearts — this is where *true* value lies, not in mainstream recognition.
