**Question: When did X/Twitter begin to support automatic translation to the target language, such as setting content language to English so that all other languages like Chinese are automatically translated to English without needing to click one by one?**

**Answer:**

Twitter (now X) first experimented with and began rolling out **automatic tweet translation** (where tweets in other languages are translated by default without manual clicking) in **August 2020**.

It started as a limited test in Brazil, where English tweets were automatically translated to Portuguese for some users on iOS and Android, based on their account's preferred/display language. Users could see the translation immediately on their homepage/feed, with options to view the original or toggle back. This was an expansion of the existing "Translate Tweet" button feature (powered initially by Bing, later Google), which required manual clicks.

The test aimed to make conversations easier to follow across languages, and reports indicated plans to expand it if successful. While early translation efforts existed (like manual translation added around 2013–2015 via Bing), true **automatic/default translation** without user action per tweet began with this 2020 test.

Over time, the feature appears to have become more widely available on X, especially with improvements in machine translation. Recent user reports (around 2024–2025) mention Grok AI integration for translations and ways to disable auto-translation per language in settings (under Accessibility, display, and languages). If your account's display/content language is set to English, non-English posts (like Chinese) now often appear auto-translated in the feed for convenience, without needing to tap each one individually.

This matches the convenient behavior you described. Note that full global rollout details aren't pinpointed to one exact date beyond the initial 2020 test, but it has evolved into a standard experience on the platform today.
