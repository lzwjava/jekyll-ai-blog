Question: Would Wang Xing (Meituan founder) regret returning to China from the US to start a company?

Answer:

Probably **no**. From the available evidence, Wang Xing's own view is that returning to China was the correct decision. He left his PhD program in computer engineering at the University of Delaware around 2003 and returned to China to pursue entrepreneurship. ([Wikipedia][1])

The interesting part is that the decision was not simply "US vs China"; it was about **where the opportunity was at that time**.

Around 2003–2010:

* The US had:

  * stronger universities
  * better research environment
  * mature venture capital
  * companies like Google, Facebook, Amazon

* China had:

  * explosive Internet growth
  * huge unsolved consumer markets
  * fewer competitors
  * opportunities to localize US Internet models

Wang Xing saw the second opportunity. His early projects copied ideas like Friendster/Facebook, but adapted them for China. His first attempts failed, including early social-network projects, but eventually he built Xiaonei (校内网), Fanfou (饭否), and later Meituan. ([How They Began][2])

The counterfactual:

### If Wang Xing stayed in the US

Possible path:

```
University of Delaware PhD
        ↓
research engineer / professor
        ↓
Silicon Valley startup employee/founder
```

He might have had a good career, but becoming one of China's largest Internet entrepreneurs would have been much less likely.

### By returning to China

He gained:

```
China 2003 Internet wave
        +
large consumer market
        +
local understanding
        +
execution speed
        ↓
Meituan
```

Meituan eventually became a major Chinese technology company, and Wang Xing remains its founder and CEO. ([Meituan][3])

However, there are things he might regret:

1. **Personal difficulty**

The road was extremely painful:

* several failed startups
* financing problems
* competition
* regulatory pressure later

Many entrepreneurs would not survive that period.

2. **US advantages**

He gave up:

* US academic network
* Silicon Valley ecosystem
* possible immigration path
* access to frontier research

For someone whose goal was AI research, the US might have been a better choice.

3. **Country risk**

Building a huge company in any country means accepting local risks. Meituan, Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, etc. all experienced periods of regulatory uncertainty.

But from a startup expected-value perspective:

```
2003 China entrepreneur opportunity:

Probability of failure: very high
Probability of huge success: low

Expected value:
   huge upside × small probability
   > safe PhD path
```

Wang Xing essentially made a high-variance founder bet.

A useful comparison:

* Andrew Ng stayed in the US → research + AI companies.
* Elon Musk moved toward Silicon Valley → startups.
* Wang Xing returned to China → consumer Internet empire.

Different environments reward different strategies.

For today's AI era, the calculation is different:

```
2003:
China consumer Internet > US

2026:
AI frontier research:
US advantage

AI application/startups:
China + US both have opportunities
```

So Wang Xing probably does not regret it. His decision was rational given the information available in 2003. The mistake would have been staying somewhere only because it was prestigious, while ignoring where the largest opportunity was. ([zhihu.com][4])
