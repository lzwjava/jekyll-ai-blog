Question: Is using clock alarms instead of timers for cooking a good idea, and how can I improve this workflow?

Answer:

## Your Idea: Using Alarms Instead of Timers

This is actually a **reasonable workaround**, and you're not alone — many people find the iOS Timer experience annoying for specific reasons. Let's break it down.

---

## Why You Prefer Alarms Over Timers (Valid Reasons)

- The iOS timer **lives in Control Center and the Lock Screen persistently**, showing a countdown that feels intrusive and cluttered
- You can't easily run multiple timers with labels in older iOS versions (Apple only added multiple timers in iOS 17)
- Alarms are **fire-and-forget** — set it, forget it, get notified
- Alarms feel less "in your face" during the countdown

---

## Problems With Your Current Approach

- **100 alarms** is cluttered and hard to manage
- Easy to accidentally enable/disable the wrong one
- No label context — you forget what each alarm was for
- Alarms don't auto-delete after firing, so they pile up forever

---

## How to Improve It

### ✅ Option 1: Use iOS 17+ Multiple Timers (If You Haven't)

If you're on **iOS 17 or later**, Apple now supports **multiple named timers** natively in the Clock app. You can:

- Set a "Porridge" timer for 1 hour
- It won't interfere with other timers
- Less persistent/annoying UI than before

### ✅ Option 2: Clean Up Your 100 Alarms

- Group alarms by time intervals: 15 min, 30 min, 45 min, 1 hr, 2 hr
- **Label each one clearly** (e.g., "1 Hour Cooking")
- Keep only ~10–15 reusable alarms and toggle them on/off as needed

### ✅ Option 3: Use Siri for One-Shot Alarms
>
> *"Hey Siri, set an alarm for 1 hour from now"*

Siri creates a **temporary alarm** that you can delete after. Fast, clean, no clutter.

### ✅ Option 4: Use a Third-Party Timer App

Apps like **Timerlist**, **Multi Timer**, or **Due** offer:

- Multiple named timers
- Recurring timer templates (e.g., "Porridge = 1 hour")
- Persistent reminders that repeat until dismissed
- Much cleaner UX than iOS stock timer

### ✅ Option 5: Use Shortcuts Automation

Create an **iOS Shortcut** that:

1. Asks "How long?" or offers preset durations
2. Silently sets a single alarm X minutes from now
3. Auto-labels it "Cooking Timer"

This gives you alarm behavior without the clutter.

---

## Recommendation Summary

| Method | Convenience | Clutter | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your current 100 alarms | Medium | Very High | Not ideal |
| iOS 17 Multiple Timers | High | Low | Most users |
| Siri alarm | Very High | None | Quick, one-off |
| Third-party app | High | None | Power users |
| Shortcuts | High | None | Tech-savvy users |

**Best quick fix:** Use **Siri** to set a one-time alarm by voice — zero clutter, no managing 100 alarms, instant.
