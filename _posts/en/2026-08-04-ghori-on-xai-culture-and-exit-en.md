Question: What other details did Ghori share about xAI/Grok that I haven't covered yet?

Answer: Pulled the full StartupHub writeup + Grokipedia bio for more specifics.

**Direct quotes on autonomy/culture**
His actual words on day one: "My first day, they just gave me a laptop and a badge. And I was like, okay, now what? I don't even have a team, I have not been told what to do." On what he likes best: "No one tells me no. If I have a good idea, I can usually go and implement it that same day and show it to Elon or whoever and get an answer."

**"$2.5M per commit" line**
Ghori jokingly quantified his team's recent contribution: "We did the math right now, we're at I think about $2.5 million per commit to the main repo. And I did five today." — his way of making the "every hour = massive value" culture concrete.

**First-principles take on software limits**
Many perceived limitations in software, especially latency and speed, are dismissed as "not true" — he says a lot of the existing industry stack has "a lot of stupid stuff," and stripping it out typically nets a 2x–8x speedup on almost anything. The actual bottleneck to AGI, in his framing, isn't software complexity but the physical constraints of energy and hardware availability.

**The Tesla-compute rationale, stated as a real quote**
He frames the challenge as: "We want 1 million human emulators. We need 1 million computers. How do we do that?" and says the Tesla car computer is "actually much more capital efficient" than cloud VMs or buying Nvidia chips directly.

**Why he got fired**
Per Grokipedia: he was Member of Technical Staff at xAI from March 2025 to January 2026, and his tenure ended after he disclosed proprietary details about the confidential "Macro Hard" project on the Relentless podcast — the human-emulator/digital-employee plan using idle Tesla compute — and was reportedly terminated as a result. He confirmed the exit himself: on January 19, 2026 he posted on X, "I have left xAI. Nothing but love to my former team and coworkers!"

**Background that isn't xAI-specific but explains why he was hired**
- He'd been building startups since 2019 — lead engineer at jeeny.ai at 18 (an AI ed-tech B2B SaaS, assembled a 20-engineer team), founded Swiftink.io (AI transcription/translation, 10K+ users) in Oct 2022, then Invisibility in Nov 2023, then Khan Space Industries (asteroid mining) in Aug 2024.
- Long-term he's aiming at Von Neumann self-replicating probes for seeding life on other planets — a goal he estimates is about 3% complete. He got a 2025 O'Shaughnessy Ventures fellows grant for this and for cutting the time/cost of integrating robots into workflows.
- Open-source under github.com/djmango: pyddle, obsidian-transcription (200+ GitHub stars), a Rust celestial-sphere simulator (Starfinder) used for star-tracker work, VEX robotics code (Korvex), and a Neuralink compression-challenge entry (smallbrain).
- Colorful aside: he designed and built a liquid-fueled rocket engine in his backyard, and during a firing test he caught fire.

Notably absent from anything he said: no model architecture, no parameter counts, no training-loop specifics for Grok itself — the "leak" was org/strategy/infra, not weights or code.
