**Question:** How is China's startup trend expected to look in the coming years?

**Answer:**

The short answer: a **narrow, selective recovery** — not a return to the 2018 boom. The era of mass startup formation is likely over; what's emerging is something structurally different.

Here's what the evidence shows for 2025–2026 and beyond:

**Signs of stabilization, not a full comeback**

China's VC market demonstrated tentative stabilization throughout 2025 and into early 2026, yet this recovery has diverged significantly from global trends. While the US and Europe experienced a robust VC rebound, China's recovery remained constrained by persistent IPO bottlenecks, geopolitical tensions, and evolving regulatory frameworks.

**The AI exception — a narrow bright spot**

The year 2025 began with DeepSeek releasing its R1 model, which surprised the global tech community with its exceptional efficiency — creating a powerful global reference point that materially strengthened confidence in China's deep-tech capabilities among investors and the media. That shift in perception helped propel a broader wave of Chinese AI companies, including MiniMax, Moonshot, and Manus AI.

In 2025, China nearly reached parity with Europe in total PE/VC exit count and surpassed Europe — while almost matching the US — in the number of deep-tech exits. This was driven by a sharp increase in both IPO activity (up 70% year-on-year) and M&A transactions (up 36% year-on-year), led by the landmark CATL IPO ($134B).

**But structural problems remain deeply embedded**

The capital raised for Chinese VC funds is less than a quarter of what it was last year, which was itself at its lowest level in nearly a decade. VC deal activity involving foreign investors has fallen sharply, with only 112 deals totaling $2.1 billion involving overseas investors.

The recovery remains uneven, with fundraising demand still about 2.5 times LP supply, and the top 10 fund managers accounting for about 80% of fundraising in 2025.

**State capital is replacing private VC**

Government-linked investors in China went from fewer than 10 AI deals per year before 2018 to more than 140 deals in 2025 — roughly a 15x increase in participation. Beijing has also instructed key AI startups like Moonshot AI and StepFun not to accept US capital without explicit government clearance. This means the startup ecosystem is becoming more state-directed and less entrepreneurially free — the opposite of what drove the 2015–2018 boom.

**The new model: "Accelerator State" deeptech**

Startups that invest in long-term value, technological ingenuity, and sustainable business models are best-positioned to stand out. The firm focus on deeptech reflects a strategic shift from business model innovation to core technology breakthroughs. Sectors favored include AI, semiconductors, robotics, and new energy — all state-strategic priorities.

---

**The bottom line:** China's startup count is unlikely to recover to anywhere near its 2018 peak. The new normal looks like fewer but larger bets, concentrated in deeptech and AI, heavily state-backed, with foreign capital still cautious. For broad consumer internet or "app economy" startups — the kind that defined the 2012–2018 boom — the conditions that made those possible (loose regulation, foreign VC, entrepreneurial freedom) have not returned.
