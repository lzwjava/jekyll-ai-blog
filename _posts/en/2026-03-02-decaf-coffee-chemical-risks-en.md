The article expresses strong concerns about decaffeinated (decaf) coffee. The author explains that he avoids caffeinated drinks and also refuses to drink decaf coffee. He references an article from The Conversation (<https://theconversation.com/how-is-decaf-coffee-made-and-is-it-really-caffeine-free-215546>) and describes the two main decaffeination methods, focusing especially on the solvent-based approach.

Key points from the post:

- There are two primary ways to remove caffeine, but the solvent method (using organic solvents) is the most common because it is cheaper.
- The solvent method has two variants: **direct** and **indirect**.

**Direct solvent method (described in detail):**

- Coffee beans are first steamed.
- Then they are soaked in dichloromethane (methylene chloride) or ethyl acetate to dissolve the caffeine.
- After soaking, the beans are steamed again to remove the residual solvent.

The author questions whether this second steaming step can truly eliminate all traces of the solvent. He doubts it, arguing that:

- These are volatile compounds, but they have been soaked into the beans for a long time.
- It's uncertain whether the solvents fully evaporate, whether they bind chemically with components in the coffee bean, or whether they become trapped inside the bean structure.
