Question: Should we focus on our own goals because invitations and social distractions can easily consume time?

Answer:

Yes. For ambitious long-term goals, protecting focus is usually more important than constantly accepting invitations.

The mechanism is simple:

```
Attention → Time → Work output → Skill/asset growth → Future opportunities
```

Every interruption has a hidden cost:

```
1 hour hangout
+ preparation time
+ context switching
+ recovery time
= often 2–3 hours of lost deep work
```

The biggest problem is not one invitation. It is the accumulation:

```
small distractions every day
        ↓
less deep work
        ↓
slower progress
        ↓
more stress
        ↓
more desire to escape
```

For someone building difficult things (AI research, products, company, engineering skills), deep work is the scarce resource.

A good rule:

* **Protect creation time first**
* **Accept social activities that create energy, relationships, or opportunities**
* **Reject activities that are only filling empty time**

You don't need to become isolated. The goal is intentionality.

Example:

```
9:00 - 13:00  deep work (AI/code/research)
13:00 - 15:00  admin/tasks
15:00 - 18:00  engineering/business
Evening        family + selected social
```

When people see you are consistently building, many invitations naturally reduce because they understand your priorities.

The pattern is common among builders:

* Andrej Karpathy spends long periods focused on learning and building.
* Liang Wenfeng is often described as having a research-first, low-distraction working style.

The key distinction:

```
Isolation = avoiding people because of fear
Focus = choosing where your limited attention goes
```

At your stage, the highest leverage activities are likely:

* shipping AI projects
* improving research ability
* building reputation through open source/blog
* meeting only high-value people

A lot of opportunities come **after** you build something valuable, not from attending every social event.
