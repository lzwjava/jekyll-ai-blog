Question: What are common vegetables in China?

Answer:

China is one of the world's largest producers and consumers of vegetables, with fresh vegetable consumption averaging around 110 kg per person annually. Vegetables play a central role in Chinese cuisine, often stir-fried, steamed, boiled in soups, or used in hotpots. Common ones include a mix of leafy greens, root vegetables, and others widely grown and eaten across regions.

Here are some of the most common vegetables in China:

- **Chinese cabbage (Napa cabbage or dàbái cài)**: A staple leafy vegetable, especially in northern China; used in soups, stir-fries, hotpots, and kimchi-like pickles.
- **Bok choy (xiǎobái cài or pak choi)**: One of the most popular greens; crisp and mild, commonly stir-fried or added to soups.
- **Tomatoes (fānqié)**: Widely used in dishes like stir-fried tomatoes with eggs; China is the top global producer.
- **Potatoes (tǔdòu)**: China produces the most potatoes worldwide; popular in stir-fries, especially spicy versions in Sichuan.
- **Cucumbers (huángguā)**: Eaten fresh, pickled, or stir-fried; a everyday vegetable.
- **Chinese broccoli (gài lán)**: Bitter and earthy; often stir-fried with garlic or oyster sauce.
- **Eggplant (qiézi)**: Long, thin varieties common; used in garlic sauce dishes or braised.
- **Lotus root (ǒu)**: Crunchy slices added to stir-fries or soups.
- **Bitter melon (kǔguā)**: Bumpy green gourd with bitter flavor; stir-fried, especially in southern China.
- **Long beans (yardlong beans or dòujiǎo)**: Long green beans; stir-fried or dry-fried.
- **Bean sprouts (dòuyá)**: Mung bean sprouts; added to stir-fries and soups.
- **Water spinach (kōng xīn cài or ong choy)**: Hollow-stemmed green; quick stir-fry favorite in southern regions.

Other frequently mentioned ones include cauliflower, carrots, radishes (daikon), winter melon, bamboo shoots, and various mushrooms. Regional variations exist—leafy greens dominate in the south, while root vegetables are more common in the north.
