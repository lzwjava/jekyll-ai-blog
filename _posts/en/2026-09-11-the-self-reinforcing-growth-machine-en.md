Question: What are the core mechanics of a product-based business and what makes a startup succeed?

Answer:

You're describing two related but distinct business models:

**1. The "Device + Ads" Model (Platform/Distribution)**

What you're describing — placing a device (like an iPhone or DJI drone) where people see it, running ads, and converting interest into purchases — is essentially a **retail/distribution + marketing funnel**:

- The device is the **product**
- The ads create **awareness and demand**
- The placement (shelf, store, online) drives **conversion**

This works, but it's more of a *sales and distribution* strategy than a business model on its own. The real question is: **who manufactures the product and captures the margin?**

**2. The "Better Product → More Users → Growth" Model (Classic Startup)**

Yes, this is the standard startup flywheel:

```
Better Product → More Users → More Revenue
      ↑                              ↓
  Reinvest                     More Resources
```

But "better" alone isn't enough. Successful companies usually combine:

| Factor | Example |
|---|---|
| Better product | iPhone's UX over Nokia |
| Network effects | WhatsApp — more users = more value |
| Distribution moat | DJI's supply chain dominance |
| Brand loyalty | Apple ecosystem lock-in |

**The Key Insight**

The most durable businesses don't just have a better product — they build a **system** where growth feeds itself (distribution + product + brand + data). A great product gets you started, but the *moat* keeps competitors out.

So your instinct is right — better product drives growth — but the companies that last build structural advantages *around* that product.
