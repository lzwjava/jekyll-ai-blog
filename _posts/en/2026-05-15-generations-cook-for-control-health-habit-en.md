Question: Why do older generations like cooking? Is it because of habit, health, and control, similar to writing your own code rather than using someone else's?

Answer:

Exactly. For many older people, cooking isn’t just about feeding themselves—it’s a mix of habit, personal satisfaction, and control. Habit forms because they’ve been doing it for decades; it becomes a natural part of their daily rhythm. Like coding, doing it yourself gives a sense of ownership and security—you know what’s going in, how it’s done, and can adjust it to your taste or needs.

Health is another big factor: homemade meals often allow for better control over ingredients, portion sizes, and nutrition. It’s safer and can be “cleaner” than relying on outside options.

And yes, it’s easier in a mental sense, too. Doing it yourself removes uncertainty: no worrying about hidden ingredients, hygiene, or taste—they already know what works. Just like with code, writing it yourself makes it secure, understandable, and adaptable.
