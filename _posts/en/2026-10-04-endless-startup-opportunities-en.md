Question: Is startup opportunity basically unlimited because humans will always want better things, even in 2060 or 2100?

Answer:

Yes, this is a core idea behind entrepreneurship: **human desires and problems are not a finite list**. Even if technology becomes extremely advanced, there will likely still be endless opportunities because civilization keeps creating new needs.

A useful way to see it:

```
Technology improves
        ↓
New capabilities appear
        ↓
People imagine new possibilities
        ↓
New problems and desires emerge
        ↓
New companies are created
```

For example:

* 1990s: people wanted easier communication → email, web companies
* 2000s: people wanted mobile internet → smartphones, apps
* 2010s: people wanted on-demand services → Uber, Airbnb, food delivery
* 2020s: people want AI coworkers → AI agents, AI software factories
* 2060s: people may want things we cannot even clearly imagine today

Even if AI can produce software cheaply, humans may still want:

* better education
* better healthcare
* longer and healthier lives
* better entertainment
* new forms of social connection
* space exploration
* personalized AI companions
* better cities
* new industries created by technologies we haven't invented yet

This connects strongly with YC's philosophy: **"Make something people want."** The important part is not the specific technology; it is finding a real human desire and creating a better solution.

Paul Graham often argues that startups work because founders notice something that is possible but not yet done. The world is full of "obvious in hindsight" improvements.

However, "unlimited opportunity" does not mean every startup succeeds. The bottleneck is usually:

* finding a painful enough problem
* building something 10x better
* distribution (getting users)
* execution speed
* surviving competition

A future with powerful AI may actually increase startup opportunities because the cost of creating products decreases. A small team or even one person may be able to build what previously required a large company.

The deeper principle is:

> Civilization is an optimization process. There is almost always a better way to do something.

As long as humans have curiosity, preferences, competition, and imagination, there will be things to improve.

A founder mindset is less "I need to discover a once-in-history opportunity" and more:

> "Find something people already want, then make it dramatically better."
