Question: How have Hong Kong’s real estate market and economy performed in recent years?

Answer: **Hong Kong has gone through a pretty sharp boom → correction → early recovery cycle.** As of September 2026, the economy is recovering, while residential property has already stabilized/rebounded from its 2021–24 downturn.

### 1. The property story

The important timeline is:

**2021 peak → 2022–24 correction → 2025 stabilization → 2026 recovery**

Hong Kong private-home prices peaked around **September 2021**. By December 2025, the official private-home price index was **298.6 versus the 2021 peak of 398.1** — roughly **25% below the peak**. ([LegCo][1])

But 2025 was a major turning point:

* Private-home transactions reached **62,832**, up **18.3% YoY**.
* Rents reached a **record high** in December 2025.
* Prices began rising again after a very long decline. ([LegCo][1])
* Reuters reported that prices had risen for eight consecutive months through January 2026. ([Reuters][2])
* By mid-2026, the recovery had continued for more than a year, although July 2026 prices finally slipped slightly after a 13-month winning streak. ([RTHK News][3])

So **Hong Kong real estate isn't in the same situation as mainland China's property market**. Mainland China is still experiencing broad property weakness, whereas Hong Kong residential property has been recovering.

### 2. But commercial real estate is different

This distinction is important.

**Residential:** recovering.

**Office/retail:** much weaker.

Hong Kong still has substantial structural pressure on offices and retail because of changing work patterns, vacancies, and weaker traditional demand. The IMF specifically says commercial real estate remains a risk even though residential prices have broadly stabilized. ([IMF][4])

Interestingly, there are signs of improvement in Central: JLL reported that Grade-A Central office rents had their strongest first-half growth in **15 years** in 1H 2026. ([JLL][5])

So I wouldn't treat "Hong Kong property" as one homogeneous market.

## 3. The economy: much better than the property headlines suggest

Hong Kong's economy was hit by several shocks:

**COVID + China slowdown + US-China tensions + high interest rates + property correction**

GDP:

| Year              | Real GDP growth |
| ----------------- | --------------: |
| 2021              |           +6.5% |
| 2022              |       **-3.7%** |
| 2023              |           +3.2% |
| 2024              |           +2.6% |
| 2025              |       **+3.5%** |
| 2026 IMF forecast |           +2.4% |

The IMF says Hong Kong's **real GDP finally exceeded its pre-pandemic peak in 2025**, helped by technology-related exports, improving private demand and financial-market activity. ([IMF][4])

That's a pretty important point:

> **Hong Kong isn't economically collapsing. It's recovering, but growth is not spectacular.**

And 2026 has actually produced some strong quarters; Hong Kong recorded **5.9% YoY quarterly growth** in one of the latest reported quarters, its strongest in almost five years. ([South China Morning Post][6])

## 4. The deeper structural change

This is probably the most interesting part for you.

The old Hong Kong model was roughly:

**China trade + property + finance + logistics + professional services**

The emerging model is increasingly:

**Finance + China/ASEAN capital flows + technology + asset management + RMB + digital assets + advanced services**

The IMF describes Hong Kong as a financial center and a **"super-connector" between mainland China and the rest of the world**. Technology-related exports and financial-market activity have become important parts of the recovery. ([IMF][4])

The government's new five-year plan is also explicitly trying to diversify toward technology, finance, Northern Metropolis development, digital assets, green finance, etc. ([Reuters][7])

---

## 5. One major weakness: government finances

Hong Kong historically had an unusually strong fiscal position.

But the combination of:

* COVID spending
* falling land-sale revenue
* falling property prices
* weaker stamp-duty revenue
* large infrastructure spending

has produced persistent deficits.

The IMF estimates the consolidated fiscal deficit at about **4.9% of GDP in 2025–26**, with fiscal reserves around **20% of GDP**. ([IMF][4])

That's not an immediate solvency crisis, but it represents a meaningful change from Hong Kong's old reputation for enormous fiscal surpluses.

## 6. What I think is the key picture

I'd visualize the last few years like this:

```text
                    2021
                     ▲
                     │  Property peak
                     │
                     │
                     ▼
2022 ─────── 2023 ─────── 2024
  recession     recovery     property correction
                                │
                                ▼
                              2025
                                │
                     economy + property
                       start recovering
                                │
                                ▼
                              2026
                     ┌──────────────────┐
                     │ economy recovering│
                     │ residential ↑     │
                     │ rents ↑           │
                     │ offices mixed      │
                     │ risks remain       │
                     └──────────────────┘
```

The particularly interesting fact is that **Hong Kong housing became considerably more affordable relative to its own 2021 peak**, while rents remained strong. The official Legislative Council research describes a remarkable 29-month period in which **prices were falling while rents were rising**, from April 2023 through August 2025. ([LegCo][1])

So if you're thinking about Hong Kong not merely as a tourist destination but as a place to **live, work, build a company, or buy property**, the 2026 Hong Kong is quite different from the 2018–21 Hong Kong.

The economy is **not booming**, but the combination of **financial infrastructure + rule-based institutions + recovering housing + mainland connectivity + technology/AI development** makes the current environment quite interesting.
