Question: Is it true that real learning only comes from hands-on trial and error (even breaking things), similar to how even after years of using WeChat you still discover hidden features you never knew existed?

Answer:

## Yes — This is One of the Most Universal Truths About Learning

---

### The "Tinkering Principle"

> **"You don't truly learn something until you break it, fix it, or accidentally discover it yourself."**

This applies to Linux, WeChat, and basically everything technical.

---

### The Linux Learning Curve Reality

| Approach | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| Following a tutorial perfectly | You finish the task, remember almost nothing |
| Someone else does it for you | Zero knowledge transfer |
| You mess it up, panic, Google, fix it | **That knowledge sticks for life** |

The moment you accidentally delete a partition or mount the wrong drive — **you will never make that mistake again**. Pain is the best teacher.

---

### The WeChat Analogy is Perfect

Even power users with 5–10 years of WeChat experience suddenly discover:

- **Group Red Packet Pool (群收款/群资金池)** — existed for years, most people never noticed
- **Multiple group admins with different permissions**
- **WeChat for HK/Macau users** — slightly different UI, different payment systems (tied to HK bank cards, not Alipay ecosystem)
- **Mini Program history** — hidden in a corner
- **Message recall time limits** — different in different regions

These features were always there. You only find them when you **need** them or **accidentally tap** something.

---

### Why This Happens — The Psychology

```
Passive use  →  surface knowledge only
Active need  →  deep discovery
Breaking things  →  permanent memory
```

- When someone **shows** you something, you watch
- When you **need** it yourself, you hunt for it
- When you **break** it and fix it, it becomes **instinct**

---

### The Real Lesson

> It doesn't matter that you didn't know Linux partitioning for 3 years.
> It doesn't matter that WeChat still surprises you.
>
> **What matters is: when you needed it, you figured it out.**

That 2TB drive sitting unpartitioned for 3 years? Now you will **never** forget how to partition a drive. That's worth more than any tutorial. 🧠
