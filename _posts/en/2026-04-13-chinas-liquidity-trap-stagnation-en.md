Understanding China’s economy through the lens of **liquidity depletion** (or "liquidity traps") provides a clear picture of why traditional stimulus is losing its punch. In 2026, the challenge isn't a lack of money in the system, but rather a lack of **velocity**—money is being "depleted" from the productive economy and trapped in unproductive cycles.

Here is how to use this concept to analyze the current market:

### 1. The "Two-Speed" Liquidity Divergence

Liquidity in China is currently bifurcated. While the People’s Bank of China (PBoC) is maintaining a "moderately loose" policy with frequent RRR cuts, that liquidity is not reaching the broader market:

* **The Trap:** Banks are flushed with cash, but they are hesitant to lend to the private sector due to high risk.
* **The Depletion:** Instead of circulating, liquidity is being "absorbed" by state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and used to refinance the massive debt of local governments rather than funding new growth.

### 2. Household Savings vs. Market Liquidity

One of the most significant signs of liquidity depletion in the consumer market is the record-high household savings (exceeding **$20 trillion** as of early 2026).

* **The Indicator:** When households refuse to spend despite low interest rates, liquidity is effectively "depleted" from the retail and service sectors.
* **Market Meaning:** This suggests a crisis of confidence. Even if the government "prints" money, the market remains stagnant because the money stays in bank deposits rather than flowing into equities or consumption.

### 3. The Property Sector "Sponge"

For decades, real estate was the primary engine for liquidity circulation in China. Now, it acts as a liquidity sponge:

* **Deleveraging:** As developers like those in the 2021-2025 crisis continue to restructure, any new capital injected into the sector is immediately used to pay off old debts (deleveraging) rather than starting new projects.
* **Wealth Effect:** Depleted property values mean the "middle class" feels poorer, leading to a further withdrawal of liquidity from the stock market (A-shares).

### 4. Capital Efficiency and "Zombie" Companies

To understand the current market, look at the **incremental capital output ratio (ICOR)**—how much investment is needed to produce one unit of GDP growth.

* **Observation:** China’s capital efficiency has plummeted. This means liquidity is being "depleted" by "zombie" companies—struggling firms that stay alive only through constant credit injections.
* **Investment Strategy:** In this environment, the market favors "New Quality Productive Forces" (tech, green energy) because these are the only sectors where liquidity is still generating actual returns rather than just covering debt.
