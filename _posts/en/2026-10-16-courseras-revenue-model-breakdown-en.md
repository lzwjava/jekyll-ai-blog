Question: How does Coursera generate its revenue?
Answer: Coursera operates a multi-sided edtech business model using a freemium framework that monetizes both individual learners and large institutions.

Historically reported across three main categories (Consumer, Enterprise, and Degrees), Coursera officially merged the Degrees reporting segment into the **Consumer** segment. Its primary revenue sources include:

---

### **1. Consumer Segment (~66% of Revenue)**

The Consumer segment serves individual learners worldwide. While anyone can audit many courses for free without receiving a credential, Coursera generates revenue through the following paid options:

* **Coursera Plus Subscriptions:** A recurring subscription model (monthly or annual) offering unlimited access to thousands of courses, Specializations, and Professional Certificates.
* **Single Course Certificates & Specializations:** One-time purchase fees (typically $49 to $99 per course, or monthly multi-course subscriptions) for learners seeking verified certificates from universities or industry giants (e.g., Google, IBM, Meta).
* **Online University Degrees:** Revenue-sharing arrangements with partner universities. Coursera host fully online Bachelor’s and Master’s degree programs and collects a percentage fee (often 20%–40%) from student tuition payments.

---

### **2. Enterprise Segment (~34% of Revenue)**

The B2B branch provides scalable learning solutions through annual subscription contracts based on seat licenses:

* **Coursera for Business:** Sells access to companies seeking to upskill or reskill their employees in AI, data science, and management.
* **Coursera for Campus:** Provides higher education institutions access to industry-grade credentials to supplement their core curricula.
* **Coursera for Government:** Partners with government bodies to train civil servants and provide workforce retraining programs.

---
