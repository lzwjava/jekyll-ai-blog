Question: Is it a good strategy to consume a massive amount of content or experiences, filter the best, and share them with others, similar to what Zhiwei Li does, and does this resemble what content creators, senior engineers, or CEOs do?

Answer:

Yes, this is a highly effective strategy, and it aligns with how top performers in many fields operate. Conceptually, it’s about **maximizing learning and curation**:

1. **Volume + Filtering = Expertise**

   * Consuming 10,000 products, articles, or books gives you a wide exposure to ideas, trends, and approaches.
   * Filtering the top 1–10% helps you identify what truly matters. This builds **pattern recognition**, a key trait of experts and leaders.

2. **Sharing = Influence + Impact**

   * By sharing the distilled insights (100 best products, 100 best articles), you save others time and establish yourself as a **trusted information processor**.
   * This is exactly what effective content creators do: they read broadly, test ideas, and share the distilled wisdom.

3. **Parallel in Professional Roles**

   * **Staff software engineers**: Evaluate hundreds of tools or solutions, then recommend the best to the team.
   * **CEOs or executives**: Hire broadly, experiment with ideas, and retain the best people or initiatives. This is similar to your “hire 1,000, keep 100” analogy.

4. **Cognitive Efficiency**

   * The strategy leverages the brain’s ability to detect patterns across a large dataset. Your value grows not from the raw volume of consumption, but from the **curation and interpretation**.
   * You act as a **human filter**, which is increasingly rare in the age of information overload.

5. **Risks and Considerations**

   * Without careful filtering criteria, it’s easy to get lost in quantity over quality.
   * Sharing too broadly without context can overwhelm others; focus on actionable or high-impact recommendations.

In short, you are essentially positioning yourself as an **expert curator of experience and knowledge**, which is a strategy used by influential engineers, creators, and leaders. The formula of “consume a lot, filter the best, share selectively” is a **proven multiplier of both personal knowledge and social impact**.

You can think of yourself as an **information processor and amplifier**: the more high-quality input you process, the more value you can deliver to others.
