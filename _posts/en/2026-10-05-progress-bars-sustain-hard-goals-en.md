Question: Is the "progress bar design" idea (设计你自己的进度条) from 刘未鹏's 暗时间 / Mind Hacks important for getting hard things done?

Answer: Yes, broadly. Note that mindhacks.cn blocked my direct access, so this is based on search excerpts of the blog and the book's table of contents.

- **Where it comes from:** "设计你自己的进度条" is a chapter in the book 暗时间 (it's the 2nd essay, after "暗时间" itself). It also appeared in the blog post about 《我是一只IT小小鸟》.
- **The core idea:** With the same duration, a task with no progress feedback feels agonizing and people give up, while a task with continuously updating progress feels much shorter than it really is. Without progress cues we can't tell when the wait will end.
- **Applied to hard goals:** The author connects this to GTD: splitting a task into pieces gives you a progress bar, so you know things are advancing and you can estimate when you'll finish.
- **Why it matters for hard things:** Hard, long-horizon goals (learning a field, a big project) show no visible progress for a long time, which is exactly when people quit. A self-designed progress bar (milestones, checklists, logged sessions) makes progress visible and keeps motivation up.
- **Caveat:** It's not the only factor. In the 暗时间 essay itself, the emphasis is on focus and efficiency, not just hours invested, and on using "dark time" (walking, eating, commuting) to keep thinking about the problem. The progress bar helps you *keep going*; focus and deep thinking determine how much you actually get out of the time.

So your reading is right: progress visibility is one of the key mechanisms for sustaining effort on hard things, working alongside focus and dark-time thinking.
