Question: Is it true that Hong Kong people do not accept the “35-year-old career cutoff” culture, and that people pushed back against mainland companies bringing this habit to Hong Kong?

Answer:

Partly true, but the reality is more nuanced.

The **“35岁危机” (age 35 career cutoff)** is much more associated with mainland China’s labor market, especially in some internet/tech companies and public-sector recruitment. Many mainland workers complain that after 35, companies prefer younger candidates because they are perceived as cheaper, easier to manage, and more willing to work long hours. ([South China Morning Post][1])

Hong Kong workplace culture is generally different:

* **Experience is valued more.** In finance, engineering, consulting, professional services, management, and many SME roles, a 35–50 year old worker is not considered "old"; often they are considered more valuable because of accumulated expertise.
* **Job hopping and career changes are more normal.** Hong Kong has a more mature labor market, with many people changing industries or moving into management roles in their 30s and 40s.
* **Overt age limits can attract criticism.** Hong Kong has public discussion around age discrimination. While age is not currently one of the protected characteristics under Hong Kong's main anti-discrimination laws, the Equal Opportunities Commission has studied age discrimination and called for age-friendly employment practices. ([EOC Hong Kong][2])

The Xiaomi/HK recruitment controversy you mention appears to be one example: a video claimed a mainland phone brand's Hong Kong recruitment had a "35-year-old limit", causing online criticism. Xiaomi later denied that it had such a policy and said its Hong Kong recruitment did not have an age restriction. ([Jiemian][3])

The deeper cultural difference:

**Mainland tech culture (especially during the 2010s):**

```
young = cheaper
young = more overtime
young = more flexible
35+ = expensive / management problem
```

**Hong Kong professional culture:**

```
skills + reputation + network + experience
        ↓
more senior = often more valuable
```

But don't idealize Hong Kong too much. Hong Kong is still a competitive capitalist city. There are layoffs, salary pressure, and some employers absolutely prefer younger workers, especially for junior administrative/service jobs. Age discrimination exists there too. ([EOC Hong Kong][2])

For a senior software/AI engineer, however, Hong Kong is generally closer to Singapore/Western markets than mainland internet companies. A 35–45-year-old engineer with strong technical ability, English, and business skills can still be attractive.

Given your profile (AI engineering, LLM infra, consulting mindset), Hong Kong's market would likely evaluate you more on:

```
Can you solve expensive problems?
Can you communicate with clients?
Can you deliver AI systems?
Do you have credibility?
```

rather than simply:

```
How old are you?
```

That difference is one reason some mainland senior engineers consider Hong Kong, Singapore, or overseas markets.
