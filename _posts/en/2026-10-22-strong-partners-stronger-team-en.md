**Question:** Do two assertive/dominant people in a relationship inevitably fight with each other?

**Answer:**

Not necessarily — but it takes more intentional work. Here's a nuanced breakdown:

**Why conflict is more likely:**
- Both want control over decisions, space, and outcomes
- Neither naturally "yields" — so small disagreements can escalate faster
- Egos and pride can make it harder to back down or apologize
- Power struggles can become a recurring pattern

**Why it can actually work well:**
- Two strong people tend to *respect* each other more — neither tolerates being talked down to, and both appreciate directness
- Neither person is secretly resentful of being dominated (a common problem when one partner is much more passive)
- They often share ambition, energy, and drive — which can be deeply compatible
- Both can handle conflict without falling apart, so fights don't have to be devastating

**The real key — what makes or breaks it:**

| Works | Doesn't Work |
|---|---|
| Assertive in the *world*, collaborative at *home* | Need to "win" every argument |
| Different domains of authority (e.g., one handles finances, one handles social life) | Compete in the same areas constantly |
| High mutual respect | See each other as rivals |
| Can separate ego from the relationship's wellbeing | Confuse "being right" with "loving well" |

**Bottom line:** Two assertive people don't automatically fight — they fight *badly* when neither has learned to distinguish between *strength* and *stubbornness*. The best assertive couples treat their relationship as a team, not a contest.
