The message is describing a home treatment idea for **athlete’s foot (脚气, tinea pedis)** using **hydrogen peroxide (双氧水)**:

> “Using hydrogen peroxide for disinfection. Soaking feet from time to time is really troublesome. In the future, I’ll change it to putting a layer of hydrogen peroxide on the shoes, wearing them and walking for an hour, then washing my feet. For the athlete’s foot.”

The person’s idea is:

1. Use hydrogen peroxide to kill fungi/bacteria.
2. Instead of soaking feet regularly:

   * Put hydrogen peroxide inside shoes.
   * Wear the shoes for about an hour.
   * Wash feet afterward.

However, medically this is **not a very effective or recommended way** to treat athlete’s foot:

* **Athlete’s foot is mainly caused by dermatophyte fungi**, not just bacteria.
* Hydrogen peroxide can kill some microorganisms on surfaces, but:

  * It does not penetrate well into infected skin layers.
  * It can irritate skin and slow healing if used repeatedly.
  * Putting it inside shoes and then wearing them may expose skin to irritating chemicals.

More effective approaches:

### For feet

* Use antifungal creams:

  * terbinafine (特比萘芬)
  * clotrimazole (克霉唑)
  * miconazole (咪康唑)

Typical course:

* Terbinafine: often ~1 week (depending on product)
* Other azoles: usually 2–4 weeks

### For shoes

The fungus can live in shoes, so shoe hygiene matters:

* Dry shoes completely.
* Rotate shoes (avoid wearing the same pair every day).
* Use antifungal shoe powder/spray.
* Wash socks with hot water if possible.

### Simple routine

A low-effort routine:

```
Morning:
  wear clean dry socks

After work/exercise:
  wash feet
  dry between toes carefully
  apply antifungal cream if needed

Shoes:
  alternate pairs
  keep dry
```

The key is: **kill the fungus in the skin, not only disinfect the shoe.** Hydrogen peroxide may make shoes feel “clean,” but it usually won’t solve chronic athlete’s foot.
