**Question:** What does “欲买桂花，终不似，少年游” mean?

**Answer:**

It means roughly:

> **“I may want to buy some osmanthus flowers, but in the end, it is no longer like traveling in my youth.”**

The line comes from **人间词话**? More precisely, the famous phrase is from **鹧鸪天·桂花** by **刘过**:

> **欲买桂花同载酒，终不似，少年游。**

Literally:

* **欲买桂花** — I want to buy some osmanthus flowers
* **同载酒** — and carry wine with them
* **终不似** — but ultimately, it is not the same as
* **少年游** — traveling / wandering in one's youth

### The deeper meaning

This isn't really about flowers or wine.

The poet remembers his **youthful travels**, when life felt open, exciting, and full of possibilities. Now he tries to recreate that experience—buy the same flowers, bring wine—but realizes:

**You can reproduce the scene, but you cannot reproduce the person you were.**

That's why **“终不似”** is so powerful.

It isn't:

> “I can't travel anymore.”

It's closer to:

> **“I can return to the same place, drink the same wine, see the same flowers—but I can no longer be the young person who experienced them.”**

There is a very particular kind of sadness here: **time changes the meaning of things.**

A useful English rendering would be:

> **I might buy osmanthus and carry wine with me,
> but it will never again be like those journeys of my youth.**

And perhaps the most beautiful interpretation is:

**You don't miss the place. You miss the self who once existed in that place.**

That's why this line hits so hard as people get older.
