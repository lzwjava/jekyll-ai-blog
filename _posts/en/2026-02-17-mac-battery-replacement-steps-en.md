Question: How do I replace the battery in my Mac now that its health has degraded to 70%?

Answer: Reaching 70% capacity is usually the "service threshold" where you'll notice significant shutdowns or short runtimes. Replacing a Mac battery ranges from a simple DIY project to a complex surgical procedure, depending on which model you own.

---

### 1. Identify Your Model

Before buying parts, you must know your exact model (e.g., *MacBook Air 13-inch, M1, 2020*).

* Click the **Apple Menu ()** > **About This Mac**.
* Note the model name and year.

### 2. Choose Your Replacement Path

There are three main ways to handle a battery swap:

#### **Option A: Apple Official Service (Recommended)**

If you want a guarantee and a genuine battery, this is the safest route.

* **Cost:** Usually between **$129 – $249** depending on the model.
* **Benefit:** They often replace the entire "top case" (keyboard and trackpad area) along with the battery, effectively giving you a fresh-feeling machine.

#### **Option B: Self-Service Repair (Apple Official)**

For newer Macs (M1 chips and later), Apple offers a **Self Service Repair** program where they sell you the official parts and rent you the professional tools.

#### **Option C: Third-Party DIY (iFixit)**

For older or out-of-warranty Macs, sites like **iFixit** sell kits that include the battery, specialized screwdrivers, and adhesive remover.

* **Warning:** Most Mac batteries since 2012 are **glued** into the chassis. Removing them requires a chemical solvent and extreme caution to avoid puncturing the lithium cells.

---

### 3. General DIY Steps (If you choose Option C)

1. **Safety First:** Discharge your battery below 25% to reduce the risk of fire if the battery is accidentally punctured.
2. **Open the Case:** Use a **Pentalobe screwdriver** to remove the bottom screws.
3. **Disconnect the Battery:** This is the most important step to prevent electrical shorts.
4. **Dissolve the Adhesive:** Apply a specialized adhesive remover under the battery cells.
5. **Lift and Replace:** Gently pry up the old cells, clean the residue, and press the new battery into place.
6. **Calibrate:** After installation, charge the new battery to 100%, keep charging for 2 hours, then let it drain until the Mac shuts down before charging it back to full.
