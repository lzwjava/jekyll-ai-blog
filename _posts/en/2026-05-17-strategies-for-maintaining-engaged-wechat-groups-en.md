**Question:** After running Fun Live WeChat groups for many years with 10+ groups (totaling ~1,500+ members), how can I continue running them effectively to share my job-search direction, AI interests, and technical journey?

**Answer:**

You have a rare and valuable asset: 10+ active WeChat groups with a combined ~1,500 highly relevant members — iOS/Android engineers, ML practitioners, startup founders, and AI enthusiasts. That's a warm, pre-qualified audience. Here's how to run it well for the long term.

Your groups are not homogeneous. Tailor accordingly:

| Group | Audience | Best Content |

## 2. Content Strategy for Sharing Your Direction

**What to share regularly:**

- **Personal AI milestones** — "This month I consumed 500M tokens via OpenRouter. Here's what I learned." These are concrete, credible, and unique to you.
- **Job search updates** — Frame as a journey, not a broadcast. "I'm looking for an AI-centric role. Here's why and what I've built." This invites conversation.
- **Micro-insights from your blog** — You have ~400 posts. Distill one post into 3–5 lines for WeChat, then link the full post.
- **Questions to the group** — "Anyone working on LLM agents at a bank? Curious how they handle compliance." This turns a broadcast into a discussion.
- **Tool tips** — Share a quick tip about `ww`, `iclaw`, or `zz` weekly. Engineers love concrete tools.

---

## 3. Your Cross-Posting System Is Smart — Refine It

You already repost from one group to the other 9 — this is efficient. To improve it:

- **Write one "master message"** that works for all groups, but occasionally write a **group-specific follow-up** (e.g., a harder technical question just for Fun Live 2–10).
- **Pin important messages** (like your job-search link) in each group so new members see it.
- Research on WeChat communities shows that individuals are more likely to engage in discussions related to topics they previously participated in — so consistent themes build momentum over time. Stick to 2–3 recurring themes (AI tools, job market, personal growth) rather than random topics.

---

## 4. Long-Term Sustainability

The biggest risk with 10 groups is **decay** — people mute groups that go quiet or spammy.

- **Cadence matters:** 2–4 messages per week across groups is sustainable. Daily is too much; monthly is too little.
- Prepare a content plan ahead of time so conversation doesn't go quiet after the first burst of activity. A simple weekly theme helps: Monday = AI tool of the week, Thursday = career/job reflection, weekend = casual question.
- **Welcome new value-givers.** When someone in a group shares something great, amplify it. This keeps others contributing and reduces the "one person broadcasting" fatigue.
- Use polls, quizzes, or other interactive formats to spark conversation — these signal to the group (and members) that the community is alive and interesting.

## 5. Positioning Your Job Search Naturally

Since your [job-search post](https://lzwjava.github.io/job-search-en) targets people *outside* your current employer, you can share it naturally in groups by framing it as:

> "I wrote up my current direction — AI engineer roles, LLM systems, agents. If anyone knows teams hiring in this space or wants to connect me, I'd appreciate it."

This is honest, specific, and invites referrals without feeling like a cold broadcast. Your credentials in the post (top 6% Copilot usage, nanoGPT training, 1.5B tokens) are strong signals that give people confidence to refer you.
