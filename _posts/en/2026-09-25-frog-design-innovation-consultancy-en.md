## Question: How does frog design work, and what kind of company is frog?

**Answer:**

Yes — you mean **frog**, the famous design/innovation consultancy originally founded by Hartmut Esslinger. It is **not a product company like Apple**. Think of it more like a combination of:

> **IDEO + McKinsey/BCG strategy + product studio + engineering agency**

Today, frog is part of **Capgemini Invent**. It has grown to **2,000+ people across 70+ studios/offices**, including designers, technologists, data scientists, consultants, and strategists. ([frog, part of Capgemini Invent][1])

### 1. The origin is actually fascinating

frog started in **1969**, when Hartmut Esslinger founded *esslinger design* in Germany.

The key idea was unusual for the time:

**Don't just make technology functional. Make people emotionally want it.**

They became known for industrial design, working with WEGA and Sony. Then Steve Jobs hired Esslinger, and frog moved to California.

One famous result was the **Apple IIc** design language in 1984. ([frog, part of Capgemini Invent][2])

So historically:

```text
1969
  ↓
industrial design
  ↓
Sony / Apple
  ↓
product design
  ↓
UX / software
  ↓
strategy + business
  ↓
technology + engineering
  ↓
today: full innovation consultancy
```

### 2. What does frog actually sell?

This is the important part.

A company like Apple sells:

```text
iPhone → $999
```

A company like frog sells:

```text
"Help us figure out what our next product/business should be."

        ↓

research
        ↓
strategy
        ↓
concept
        ↓
industrial / UX design
        ↓
prototype
        ↓
software / hardware engineering
        ↓
launch
```

So the **unit of business is a consulting/innovation engagement**.

For example, imagine a bank tells frog:

> "We need to completely rethink banking for Gen Z."

frog might assemble:

```text
                 Client
                   │
        ┌──────────┴──────────┐
        │                     │
   Strategy              Research
        │                     │
        └──────────┬──────────┘
                   ↓
              Product team
          ┌────────┼────────┐
          ↓        ↓        ↓
        UX       Brand     Tech
          │        │        │
          └────────┼────────┘
                   ↓
               Prototype
                   ↓
              Production
```

That's why their current service catalog includes things like **growth strategy, innovation strategy, venture building, digital commerce, product/service design, technology, and engineering**. ([frog, part of Capgemini Invent][3])

### 3. The interesting part: they're deliberately multidisciplinary

This is probably the part you'll find most interesting as an engineer.

A frog project isn't:

```text
Designer → throws Figma over wall → Engineer
```

The intended model is more like:

```text
             PROJECT
                │
     ┌──────────┼──────────┐
     ↓          ↓          ↓
  Strategy    Design      Tech
     │          │          │
 business     UX/UI      software
 model        research   hardware
     │          │          │
     └──────────┼──────────┘
                ↓
             Prototype
                ↓
             Product
```

frog explicitly describes teams containing combinations of **interaction design, industrial design, visual design, design research, design technology, mechanical engineering, software engineering, QA, architecture, business strategy and program management**. ([frog, part of Capgemini Invent][4])

And their engineering roles can cover the entire lifecycle:

```text
inception
   ↓
POC
   ↓
prototype
   ↓
build
   ↓
test
   ↓
launch
   ↓
operate at scale
```

([frog, part of Capgemini Invent][5])

So "design firm" is actually a somewhat misleading mental model today.

It's closer to a **temporary product company that gets hired by another company**.

### 4. How does the business model work?

At a high level:

```text
              Enterprise
                  │
                  │ $$
                  ↓
             ┌─────────┐
             │   frog  │
             └────┬────┘
                  │
       ┌──────────┼──────────┐
       ↓          ↓          ↓
   Strategy    Design     Technology
       │          │          │
       └──────────┼──────────┘
                  ↓
          Client transformation
```

The commercial engine is therefore largely:

**sell expertise + teams + outcomes to large enterprises.**

A senior person doesn't just "manage designers." They're also expected to **win projects, pitch clients, define scope, negotiate fees, maintain executive relationships, and grow the practice**. frog's current job descriptions make this explicit. ([frog, part of Capgemini Invent][6])

For example, a strategy director may be responsible for generating **£2M in sales** while also leading strategy projects and hiring/mentoring the team. ([frog, part of Capgemini Invent][6])

That tells you something important:

```text
frog employee
     │
     ├── craft
     ├── delivery
     ├── client relationship
     └── business development
```

It's not an academic design studio.

It's a **professional-services business**.

### 5. Why did frog become famous?

Because they operated unusually close to the frontier of technology.

The Apple relationship is the canonical example.

Jobs wasn't merely asking:

> "Can you make this box prettier?"

The collaboration was about creating an entirely different **design language for personal computers**.

That became a frog signature:

> **design isn't decoration; design can determine what the product/business actually is.**

That philosophy eventually expanded from:

```text
physical object
```

to:

```text
object
 ↓
interface
 ↓
service
 ↓
brand
 ↓
business model
 ↓
entire company experience
```

frog says its work now spans products, services, experiences, business models, brands, and communications. ([Capgemini][7])

### 6. And now there's a huge corporate layer

This is the part that's easy to miss if you only know "frog = cool design studio."

In **2021**, frog became part of **Capgemini Invent**. The integration brought together frog with organizations including Fahrenheit 212, Idean, June21 and Capgemini Invent teams. ([frog, part of Capgemini Invent][8])

So today:

```text
                 Capgemini
                     │
              Capgemini Invent
                     │
                    frog
                     │
       ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
       ↓             ↓             ↓
   Strategy        Design         Tech
       │             │             │
       └─────────────┼─────────────┘
                     ↓
              Enterprise clients
```

This gives frog something a small design agency doesn't have:

**massive enterprise distribution and implementation capability.**

That is arguably the strategic reason the model works at large scale.

```text
                  frog
                   │
       ┌───────────┴───────────┐
       │                       │

       │                       │
   strategy                 design

                   ↓
             "Can we build it?"

             actual product

             client business
```

So **frog is essentially a high-end external R&D/product/innovation organization for enterprises**.

That's why its history goes from **Sony → Apple → UX → software → strategy → venture building → AI/data/technology** rather naturally. ([frog, part of Capgemini Invent][2])

And if you're asking because you're thinking **"how could an AI-native company recreate frog's business model, but with 10–100× more leverage?"** — that's a much more interesting question. The answer is basically **frog's multidisciplinary project team → agentic product team**, with humans concentrated on taste, architecture, client trust, and high-level decisions.
