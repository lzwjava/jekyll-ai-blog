**Question:** Why has a wholesale food supplier to restaurants in the USA seen business decline in recent years — is it because the bad economy, high costs, and restaurants cutting back on ingredients?

**Answer:**

Yes, your analysis is directionally correct. Here's the full causal chain:

**1. Restaurant industry contraction**

Restaurants are the end customer. If fewer restaurants exist or they serve fewer covers, wholesale demand drops. Post-2021, many small/mid restaurants closed permanently — they never recovered from COVID disruption. Your friend's customer base literally shrank.

**2. Food inflation → restaurants order less or substitute**

Input costs (ingredients) went up 20-30% from 2021-2024. Restaurants responded by:

- Shrinking menus (fewer SKUs = fewer wholesale line items)
- Switching to cheaper substitutes (your friend may not carry those)
- Buying from Costco/Restaurant Depot directly to cut out the middleman

**3. Consumer pullback → restaurants serve less**

High inflation hurt American consumers' disposable income. People eat out less, especially at mid-tier restaurants. Less foot traffic = restaurants order less from wholesalers. This is the indirect chain you described — exactly right.

**4. Tariffs 2025 (new factor)**

Trump's 2025 tariffs hit food imports hard. If your friend sources from China, Mexico, or Canada, their cost of goods went up significantly. They either eat the margin or pass it on — both hurt competitiveness.

**5. Competition from large distributors**

Sysco, US Foods, Gordon Food Service dominate. Small wholesale operators get squeezed on price and logistics. In a lean environment, restaurants consolidate vendors to negotiate better deals — small guys lose accounts.

**Summary chain:**

```
High inflation → consumers eat out less
→ restaurants cut orders / close
→ wholesale volumes drop
+ tariffs raise cost of imported goods
+ large distributors undercut small wholesalers
= small food wholesale business suffers badly
```

Your friend is caught in a structural squeeze, not just a temporary cycle. Unless they niche down (e.g., specialty Asian ingredients that Sysco doesn't carry well), it's hard to compete on volume/price.
