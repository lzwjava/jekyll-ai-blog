Question: How would a Chinese immigrant / U.S. green card holder / software engineer in America look at China in recent years?

Answer:

A Chinese software engineer who moved to the U.S. and got a green card usually has a **more balanced and personal view** than either extreme:

* "China is declining completely"
* "China is the future and America is finished"

Their view is often something like:

## 1. "China became much stronger technically, but the environment changed"

Many Chinese engineers who left after 2015–2020 saw:

**Positive changes:**

* world-class infrastructure
* strong manufacturing ecosystem
* huge consumer internet market
* fast adoption of AI, EVs, robotics
* extremely efficient execution

For example, Chinese AI companies and engineers have become serious global competitors. The rise of companies like DeepSeek showed that China can produce frontier AI talent and systems despite restrictions. ([Brookings][1])

A U.S.-based engineer may think:

> "China is no longer the copycat country I knew 15 years ago. It can build world-class products."

---

## 2. But they also see economic pressure

Many immigrants notice:

* real estate problems
* slower job growth
* intense competition among young engineers
* lower startup funding compared with the 2010s
* uncertainty about long-term opportunities

A software engineer who left China during the golden internet era (Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance expansion) may feel:

> "The opportunity window in China was much bigger before."

---

## 3. They compare work culture

A Chinese engineer in Silicon Valley might compare:

### China

Advantages:

* faster execution
* huge user base
* easier to launch consumer products
* strong engineering culture

Disadvantages:

* long hours
* intense competition
* frequent company restructuring
* less separation between work and life

### USA

Advantages:

* higher engineer compensation
* stronger research ecosystem
* more global opportunities
* more respect for individual career paths

Disadvantages:

* slower processes
* expensive housing
* healthcare complexity
* immigration uncertainty

Many immigrants choose the U.S. because they value the ecosystem around innovation, not because they hate China.

---

## 4. They often become more pragmatic about politics

After living abroad, many Chinese immigrants separate:

* Chinese people
* Chinese culture
* Chinese technology
* Chinese government policies

A person may say:

> "I love Chinese food, language, family culture, and engineering talent. But I prefer living under the U.S. legal and social system."

Chinese Americans overall have diverse views; surveys show many hold positive views of both their heritage and the U.S., though opinions about China vary significantly by generation and length of time in America. ([Pew Research Center][2])

---

## 5. Software engineers especially think about opportunity

A senior AI/software engineer might compare:

### China:

```
+ huge market
+ fast product iteration
+ manufacturing advantage
+ many talented engineers

- fewer global companies
- geopolitical constraints
- lower upside for some careers
```

### USA:

```
+ frontier AI labs
+ global capital
+ higher salaries
+ stronger university/research ecosystem

- visa problems
- expensive life
- layoffs
```

The U.S. technology ecosystem has historically benefited heavily from skilled Asian immigration; research estimates that Asian migrants, including Chinese migrants, contributed substantially to U.S. growth in software developers and scientists/engineers. ([American Economic Association][3])

---

## 6. A common immigrant conclusion

After 5–10 years in America, many Chinese engineers arrive at something like:

> "China is very impressive and I respect what it built. But my personal future, family, career, and values fit better in America."

Or:

> "America is better for research and global careers. China is better for certain businesses and daily convenience."

They usually stop seeing it as a simple "China vs USA" competition.

They think:

```
China = execution + manufacturing + huge market

USA = capital + frontier research + global network
```

For an AI engineer specifically, the decision is often less about which country is "better" and more about:

**Where can my skills compound faster over the next 10–20 years?**
