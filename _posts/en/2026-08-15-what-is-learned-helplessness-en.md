Question: What is 习得性无助?

Answer: It's the Chinese term for **learned helplessness** — the psychological concept from Martin Seligman's 1967 experiments with dogs.

Core finding: when a subject (animal or human) is repeatedly exposed to negative outcomes it cannot control or escape, it eventually stops trying to escape or improve the situation — even when control later becomes available. The failure isn't from lack of ability; it's a learned belief that effort doesn't matter.

Seligman's original setup: dogs given inescapable shocks later failed to jump a low barrier to escape shocks they *could* avoid, while dogs never exposed to inescapable shocks jumped immediately. The "helplessness" transferred across contexts.

In humans it shows up as: giving up early after repeated failure, attributing bad outcomes to stable/global causes ("I'm just bad at this") rather than fixable ones, and reduced initiative even when the environment changes for the better.

Relevant framing if you're thinking about it in a work/founder context: it's distinguished from realistic assessment by the *overgeneralization* — treating one uncontrollable domain as evidence that all effort is futile everywhere.
