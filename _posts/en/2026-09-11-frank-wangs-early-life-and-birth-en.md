Question: **When was Frank Wang born, and what was his early life like?**

Answer: **Frank Wang (Wang Tao, 汪滔) was born in 1980 in Hangzhou, Zhejiang.** His exact birth date is not reliably public. HKUST itself confirms the 1980 birth year and that he grew up in Hangzhou. ([HKUST School of Engineering][1])

### His childhood is actually pretty interesting

His father was an **engineer**, while his mother was a **teacher who later became a small-business owner**. His parents eventually went into business in Shenzhen. ([Forbes][2])

The important thing is that **he was not a stereotypical top student**.

Forbes describes his school grades as mediocre. Instead, he spent a huge amount of his free time reading about **model aircraft and flying machines**. He was apparently much more intellectually absorbed by that than by schoolwork. ([Forbes][2])

A particularly important childhood event:

```text
elementary school
       ↓
reads a comic/story about helicopters
       ↓
becomes fascinated by flying machines
       ↓
reads model-aircraft books
       ↓
dreams of building a flying machine
```

He later described a dream of building a small aircraft that could **fly alongside him and transmit images**. That is remarkably close to what DJI eventually built. ([Forbes][2])

### At 16, he got his first RC helicopter

His parents bought him a remote-controlled helicopter after he got good marks on an exam.

And then:

```text
RC helicopter
      ↓
crashes
      ↓
needs replacement parts
      ↓
waits months for parts from Hong Kong
      ↓
becomes even more interested in understanding
how the machine actually works
```

That story is significant because his interest wasn't just **"I like drones."**

It became:

> **Why can't this thing fly properly, and how do I make it work?**

That's basically the seed of DJI's eventual core technology: **flight control**. ([Forbes][2])

### Then his education took a strange path

He initially entered **East China Normal University** in Shanghai in 2001. Sources differ somewhat on how they describe his original major, but he left during his third year in 2003 and applied to universities abroad/Hong Kong. ([Wikipedia][3])

He reportedly applied to elite US schools including **MIT and Stanford**, but wasn't admitted. Instead, **HKUST accepted him** for Electronic and Computer Engineering. ([Forbes][2])

And that turned out to be enormously important.

At HKUST:

```text
childhood obsession
       ↓
RC helicopters
       ↓
electronics
       ↓
robotics
       ↓
autonomous flight
       ↓
flight-control thesis
       ↓
DJI
```

HKUST says Wang's final thesis in 2006 involved developing a **flight-control system for unmanned platforms**, and he founded DJI based on that system. ([UST Alumni][4])

He also joined the HKUST team for **ABU Robocon in 2005**, where the team won third prize among teams from across Asia. ([UST Alumni][4])

### The really interesting pattern

Frank Wang's story isn't really:

> "A brilliant student decided to become an entrepreneur."

It's more like:

```text
school:
    mediocre grades

but:

    unusually strong obsession
             ↓
    model aircraft
             ↓
    RC helicopter
             ↓
    robotics
             ↓
    autonomous control
             ↓
    one very hard engineering problem
             ↓
    years of iteration
             ↓
    DJI
```

That's probably the most interesting part of his early life.

He appears to have had **one extremely persistent technical interest from childhood through university**, rather than suddenly discovering entrepreneurship at 25.

And HKUST's 2026 profile explicitly describes him as having been **passionate about flying devices since childhood**, with the university providing the electronics, robotics, experimentation, and mentorship needed to turn that obsession into autonomous flying machines. ([HKUST School of Engineering][1])
