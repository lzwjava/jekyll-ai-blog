Question: What does "buyout" mean?

Answer:

A **buyout** refers to the purchase of a controlling interest (or all) of a company, asset, or someone's share in a business. The goal is typically to gain full ownership or decision-making control.

There are several common types:

- **Leveraged Buyout (LBO):** A company is acquired using a significant amount of borrowed money (debt), with the acquired company's assets often used as collateral. Private equity firms frequently use this method.

- **Management Buyout (MBO):** The existing management team purchases the company (or a division of it) from its current owners, taking over ownership themselves.

- **Employee Buyout (EBO):** Employees collectively purchase the company, often to prevent a closure or restructuring.

- **Partner Buyout:** One business partner buys out the other partner's share, becoming the sole (or majority) owner.

- **Buyout in employment:** An employer pays an employee a lump sum to terminate a contract early — sometimes called a "buyout package" or severance.

**In short:** A buyout = paying to take over ownership or eliminate someone's stake in a business or agreement.

**Example:**
> A private equity firm conducts a **leveraged buyout** of a retail chain, borrowing heavily to purchase 100% of its shares and take it private.
