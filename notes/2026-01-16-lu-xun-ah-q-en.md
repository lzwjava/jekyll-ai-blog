Question: Provide an introduction to "The True Story of Ah Q" (《阿Q正传》).

Answer:

**《阿Q正传》** (The True Story of Ah Q) is a famous novella written by **Lu Xun** (鲁迅), first published in 1921–1922 in installments in the Beijing newspaper 《晨报副刊》. It is widely regarded as one of the most important works of modern Chinese literature and the pinnacle of Lu Xun's satirical fiction.

### Core Information

- **Author**: Lu Xun (real name Zhou Shuren 周树人), 1881–1936
- **Publication**: December 4, 1921 – February 12, 1922 (serialized)
  First collected edition: 1923 in 《呐喊》 (Call to Arms)
- **Length**: About 28,000 Chinese characters (medium-length novella)
- **Setting**: Fictional Weizhuang village (未庄), late Qing Dynasty (around 1900–1911, including the Xinhai Revolution period)
- **Language**: Vernacular Chinese (白话文) with some classical elements and strong Shaoxing dialect flavor

### Main Content Summary

The story follows **Ah Q** (阿Q), a poor, homeless, illiterate day laborer in a rural village. He has no real name, no family, no fixed residence, and lives at the bottom of society.

Ah Q has several famous characteristics:

- **"Spiritual victory method" (精神胜利法)** — the most iconic concept
  Whenever he is humiliated, beaten, or fails, he always finds a way to mentally declare himself the winner through self-deception and reinterpretation of reality.
- Extremely strong sense of superiority toward those weaker than him, while being extremely submissive to the strong
- Lusts after women but is cowardly and incompetent
- Imitates revolutionary slogans during the 1911 Revolution without understanding them at all
- Finally gets falsely accused of robbery, sentenced to death, and executed — still trying to save face even on the execution ground

### Core Themes & Significance

1. **Critique of national character (国民性批判)**
   Lu Xun uses Ah Q to expose what he saw as deep-rooted weaknesses in Chinese national character at that time:
   - Self-deception
   - Self-comforting through fantasy
   - Fear of the strong + bullying the weak
   - Ignorance + blind arrogance
   - Lack of real revolutionary consciousness

2. **Tragicomedy of the lumpenproletariat**
   Ah Q is both pitiful and hateful, ridiculous and tragic — a very complex literary image.

3. **Satire of fake revolution**
   The description of how the 1911 Revolution was received in the countryside is extremely ironic: the revolution changed almost nothing for ordinary peasants.

### Most Famous Lines / Concepts

- "精神胜利法" (spiritual victory method) → became a common phrase in Chinese
- "我总觉得我以前仿佛见过她" (used when Ah Q wants to justify harassing a woman)
- "我先前比你阔得多啦！你算什么东西！" (Ah Q's typical comeback after being beaten)
- Final scene: Ah Q wants to sing an opera aria on the way to execution but forgets the words → "二十年后又是一条好汉" becomes his last self-consolation

### Literary Status

- Considered **the most important single work** in Lu Xun's fiction
- One of the representative works of modern Chinese literature
- Frequently appears in Chinese textbooks (middle school & university)
- Has been translated into many languages (English title usually "The True Story of Ah Q" or "The Real Story of Ah-Q")
- The image of "Ah Q" has become a cultural symbol in Chinese-speaking world, similar to how "Don Quixote" or "Catch-22" became archetypes in their cultures.

In short:
《阿Q正传》 uses extremely sharp satire and black humor to portray a tragic yet ridiculous "everyman" at the bottom of Chinese society, while profoundly exposing the spiritual diseases of an era and a nation.
