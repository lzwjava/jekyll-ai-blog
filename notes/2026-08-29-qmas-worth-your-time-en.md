**Question: How much time/effort does QMAS take, and is it worth applying?**

**Answer: Yes — for you, I think QMAS is worth serious consideration.** The key point is that the current QMAS is much simpler than the old “calculate 100/225 points” system.

### 1. The actual effort

For the **General Points Test**, Hong Kong changed QMAS in November 2024 to a questionnaire with **12 objective criteria across 6 areas**:

* age
* academic qualifications
* language
* work experience
* annual income
* business ownership

You only need to meet the **current passing threshold**, and the government says applications meeting more criteria are generally viewed more favorably. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][1])

For someone with a reasonably organized engineering career, I'd estimate:

| Task                                        |           Your effort |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------: |
| Read rules + self-assessment                |             1–2 hours |
| Collect degree/education docs               |             1–3 hours |
| Collect employment/work-experience evidence |             2–5 hours |
| Income/business evidence                    |             1–3 hours |
| Language evidence                           |  0–1 hour if existing |
| Passport/photos/family documents            |             1–3 hours |
| Fill application                            |             1–2 hours |
| **Total**                                   | **~1–2 focused days** |

If your documents are messy, maybe **3–7 days**. This isn't a months-long immigration project.

The official application accepts documents such as proof of net worth, academic qualifications, work experience, company information, income, language proficiency and achievements. ([GovHK][2])

### 2. Your likely QMAS profile

The interesting part is that your profile naturally maps onto QMAS:

* **Age ≤50** → yes
* **5+ years graduate/specialist work experience** → likely yes
* **English proficiency** → likely yes
* **Professional/technical experience** → strong
* **IT/AI background** → directly relevant to Hong Kong's talent needs
* **Business ownership / entrepreneurship** → potentially useful
* **International experience / clients** → potentially useful

But there is one thing I would check **before spending much effort**: your **academic qualification**.

The baseline requirement normally expects a first degree from a recognized university, although Hong Kong explicitly says exceptional technical qualifications, professional ability, experience and achievements can be considered in special circumstances. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][3])

So your education history is the biggest thing I'd investigate carefully rather than assume you're automatically eligible.

### 3. The really important catch

QMAS is **not simply "get 6 criteria → automatic visa."**

The process is:

```text
Prerequisites
     ↓
General Points Test
     ↓
Meet passing threshold
     ↓
Selection exercise
     ↓
Assessment Panel / Immigration Department
     ↓
Approval
```

Hong Kong explicitly says that **meeting the threshold does not guarantee approval**. Applications satisfying more criteria are generally more favorable, and selection considers things such as Hong Kong's manpower needs, your credentials and socioeconomic needs. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][3])

That's why I would optimize for **maximum criteria**, not merely the minimum.

### 4. Why I think it's unusually valuable for you

The biggest benefit isn't merely "a Hong Kong visa."

QMAS **doesn't require you to have a Hong Kong job offer before applying**. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][3])

That creates a very useful option:

```text
QMAS
  ↓
Hong Kong residence
  ↓
AI consulting / employment / startup
  ↓
Hong Kong company + international clients
```

And you're not locked into one employer.

The government describes QMAS specifically as a route for highly skilled/talented people who haven't necessarily secured employment in Hong Kong. ([HK Engage][4])

For comparison, a normal employment visa is much more tied to the employer/job.

### 5. Family makes it more attractive

QMAS can also cover your **spouse and unmarried dependent children under 18**, assuming the financial/support requirements are met. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][3])

That's a major difference between:

> "I can work in Hong Kong"

and

> "My family has a potential Hong Kong residence route."

### 6. One subtle but important point

QMAS currently has **no annual quota** under the enhanced GPT. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][5])

And Hong Kong's recent numbers show that this is a real, actively used program: **7,101 QMAS cases passed the selection exercise in 2025**, including **1,864 in Information Technology and Telecommunications**. In the first half of 2026, there were another **1,940 successful cases**, including **538 in IT/Telecommunications**. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][6])

So this isn't some obscure immigration route that nobody actually uses.

### My recommendation

I would **not spend money on an immigration agent yet**.

Spend **2–4 hours doing a serious QMAS self-assessment first**. If you clearly pass the threshold and can document your work/financial/business history, then prepare the application yourself.

The application effort is tiny compared with the potential upside:

```text
~2–7 days preparation
        ↓
Potential Hong Kong residence
        ↓
No employer sponsorship required
        ↓
Can bring family
        ↓
Can build/work/consult/business in HK
```

For your situation, I'd rate it **high ROI**.

The next useful step is actually to **calculate your current 12 QMAS criteria one by one** from your real background and see whether you're at 6, 7, 8, 9+ rather than just guessing.
