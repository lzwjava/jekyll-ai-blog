## Question: Why do big corporations exit some markets? Do they ever re-enter those markets?

## Answer

Big companies exit markets for the same reason investors sell stocks: **the expected future return is lower than the cost and opportunity cost**.

A market exit is usually not "we failed"; it is often a capital allocation decision.

A simple model:

```
Expected Value = Future Profit × Probability of Success - Cost - Opportunity Cost
```

If this becomes negative, even a huge company may leave.

---

## 1. Reasons big corporations exit markets

### 1. Market no longer fits their strategy

Example:

IBM sold its PC business to Lenovo Group in 2005.

Why?

IBM calculated:

```
PC hardware:
- low margin
- intense competition
- commodity product

Enterprise software/services:
- higher margin
- stronger customer relationships
- better fit
```

IBM did not disappear from computing; it moved resources to areas where it had more advantage.

---

### 2. Economics become bad

A company can have billions of dollars of revenue and still exit.

Example:

Uber Technologies exited some markets because:

* local competitors had stronger networks
* regulation was unfavorable
* subsidies required too much money

A market can be large but still unattractive.

---

### 3. Local competition is too strong

Foreign companies often underestimate local ecosystems.

Examples:

* Google exited China search in 2010.
* Amazon China marketplace operations ended in 2019.

Reasons included:

* strong local competitors
* different consumer behavior
* regulatory environment

Local players had advantages:

* distribution
* payment systems
* government relationships
* user habits

---

### 4. Regulatory or political risk

Companies may leave because the risk-adjusted return changes.

Example:

Google reduced operations in some countries because of regulatory conflicts.

The calculation becomes:

```
Profit potential: +$5B
Political/legal risk: -$8B
Decision: leave
```

---

### 5. Management attention is limited

This is underestimated.

A company has finite:

* engineers
* executives
* capital
* sales teams

A CEO may ask:

> "If we invest $1B here, can we create more value than investing it elsewhere?"

Example:

Microsoft exited many consumer businesses:

* Windows Phone
* Zune
* some gaming experiments

Then concentrated on:

* Azure
* enterprise software
* AI

---

# Do companies come back after exiting?

Yes. It happens often.

The pattern:

```
Enter market
      ↓
Conditions become bad
      ↓
Exit
      ↓
Technology / regulation / competition changes
      ↓
Re-enter
```

---

## Example 1: Microsoft returns to mobile

Microsoft failed with Windows Phone.

But later it returned through:

* Android apps
* Surface devices
* mobile cloud services
* AI assistants

Not the same strategy.

They changed the battlefield.

---

## Example 2: Apple returns to markets

Apple exited many areas historically.

Example:

Apple stopped making:

* printers
* servers
* networking products

But later entered adjacent markets:

* music (iPod)
* phones (iPhone)
* watches (Apple Watch)

The lesson:

> Companies often abandon a product, not necessarily a market.

---

## Example 3: Tesla entering markets after others leave

Many companies abandoned electric cars because economics were poor.

Tesla entered when:

* battery costs dropped
* software became important
* charging infrastructure improved

The market conditions changed.

---

## Example 4: Nvidia's return to markets

Nvidia was originally a graphics company.

It entered:

* gaming GPUs
* professional visualization
* AI compute

The AI market transformed GPUs into data-center accelerators.

Same hardware, completely different market.

---

# A famous pattern: "exit too early" vs "exit correctly"

Sometimes companies leave and regret it.

Example:

Netflix and physical media.

Netflix correctly abandoned DVD because streaming was coming.

But many companies abandoned physical retail too aggressively and lost customer relationships.

The question is not:

> "Is this market bad?"

The better question:

> "Can we build a unique advantage here?"

---

# Why startups usually enter while big companies exit

This is a common cycle:

```
Old market:
Large company dominates
        ↓
Margins decline
        ↓
Large company exits
        ↓
Startup enters with new technology
        ↓
New market created
        ↓
Large company returns through acquisition
```

Examples:

* Tesla → EV market
* OpenAI → AI assistants
* Nvidia → AI infrastructure

Large companies often prefer buying winners instead of fighting every early battle.

---

# For AI today, watch this pattern

Many companies are leaving some AI areas because they became commodities:

Low value:

```
generic chatbot wrapper
simple RAG app
basic AI content generation
```

Higher value:

```
AI infrastructure
chips
training systems
agents
robotics
enterprise automation
```

This is why companies like Nvidia, Google, Microsoft, Amazon are spending heavily on infrastructure while many small AI app companies struggle.

The strategic question is:

```
Where will the bottleneck be in 5 years?
```

Companies exit yesterday's bottlenecks and invest in tomorrow's.
