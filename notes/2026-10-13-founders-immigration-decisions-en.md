Question: Do successful startup founders with 100+ employees or 100M RMB annual revenue usually want to immigrate to the US? Why leave if they already have meaningful businesses and good lives in China?

Answer:

Your intuition is often correct: **many successful founders do not necessarily want to immigrate permanently**, because they already have something valuable where they are.

A founder with:

- 100+ employees
- 100M RMB+ annual revenue
- strong customers and reputation
- a capable executive team

has already built a "small ecosystem". Their daily life is very different from a normal employee.

They may have:

- meaningful work (building products, creating jobs, solving problems)
- social status and reputation
- financial freedom
- influence over their environment
- a team that executes their vision

At that stage, moving to another country can actually mean giving up some advantages:

- losing local networks
- rebuilding reputation
- learning a new market
- becoming a newcomer again

However, some founders still move or expand to the US for reasons like:

### 1. Access to bigger markets

For AI, software, biotech, finance, etc., the US market can be strategically important.

Example:

- Keep engineering/team in China
- Open a US company
- Get US customers and investors

This is not "escaping"; it is global expansion.

### 2. Children education / family reasons

Some founders choose:

- children study abroad
- family gets another residency option
- more global exposure

The founder may still spend most time operating the company.

### 3. Capital and networks

Silicon Valley has:

- venture capital
- researchers
- large tech companies
- startup ecosystem

For certain industries, being physically close helps.

### 4. Lifestyle preference

Some people simply prefer:

- less competition
- more privacy
- different culture
- different environment

But this is personal.

A common pattern among wealthy entrepreneurs is not:

> "I succeeded in China, then I leave China."

It is more:

> "I built a strong base, then I create options globally."

For example, many founders maintain:

- company headquarters in their original market
- overseas subsidiaries
- multiple residences

A founder's biggest asset is usually not the country they live in; it is the **company, network, reputation, and ability to create value**.

So yes, for many successful entrepreneurs, staying and continuing to build can be more meaningful than moving somewhere else just for a visa. Immigration becomes a tool, not necessarily the goal.
