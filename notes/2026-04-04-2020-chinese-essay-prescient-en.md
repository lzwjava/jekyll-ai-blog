Question: If I wrote this Chinese essay in March 2020 (influenced by He Qinglian's work and internet sources), was the analysis good and were the predictions accurate about China's future economy?

Answer:

## Overall Assessment: Remarkably Prescient, With Some Caveats

Your 2020 essay was, frankly, ahead of its time. Many of its core structural diagnoses have been validated by real-world events between 2021–2025. However, some predictions were overstated or didn't fully account for the Chinese government's resilience and intervention capacity. Let's go through it carefully.

---

## ✅ What You Got Right (Strongly Validated)

**1. Real Estate Crisis and Developer Collapses**

This is your strongest prediction. China's property crisis began unfolding after 2020, with the collapse of overleveraged developers — most dramatically Evergrande in 2021 with over $300 billion in liabilities. Housing starts dropped by more than 60% from pre-pandemic levels by 2023, one of the largest housing busts globally in decades. You predicted exactly this: developers going bankrupt, unfinished apartments ("烂尾楼"), and buyers losing both their money and their homes. The "mortgage boycott" (断贷潮) of 2022 was a real phenomenon that matched your prediction.

**2. Local Government Revenue Collapse**

You correctly predicted that government land sale revenue would dry up. In 2022, land sale revenue dropped to 6.7 trillion yuan, a 23% decrease from 2021, and in 2023, land sale revenue fell 27% in the first quarter. Local governments across China have been in acute fiscal distress, exactly as you described.

**3. Weak Consumer Confidence and Demand Stagnation**

Despite early signs of possible stabilization, economic uncertainty, weak consumer confidence, and surging household debt — now over 60% of GDP, more than double its 2010 level — continue to weigh on the housing market. Your point about ordinary people having no money left after mortgage payments suppressing consumption has proven largely correct.

**4. Infrastructure Overcapacity**

Real estate, including both residential and commercial real estate, accounted for 25% of China's economy in 2021, rising to 31% if infrastructure is included — far exceeding the United States and rivaling Spain and Ireland at the peak of their construction booms. The IMF also noted that this came on top of two decades of rapid buildup, validating your "巨大的建筑工地" (giant construction site) metaphor.

**5. Deflationary Spiral Risk**

CPI growth would likely still be negative if not for surging gold prices and if falling real estate prices were properly measured. Structural factors have shown little improvement, including impaired consumer confidence and wealth effects of the property crisis. This matches your vicious cycle argument precisely.

**6. Young People Retreating / Unemployment**

Your prediction that young unemployed people would retreat to rural areas and live frugally has been validated by the well-documented "躺平" (lying flat) and "润" (emigrate) phenomena that became cultural touchstones post-2021.

---

## ⚠️ Where You Overstated or Oversimplified

**1. Banking System Collapse**

You predicted mass bank failures and government money-printing causing hyperinflation. This has NOT happened to the degree you described. China's real estate market does not have the genetic predisposition for a subprime mortgage crisis like that in the U.S. China has always required significant down payments and has never allowed zero down payment purchases. The Chinese government has maintained tighter financial controls than you assumed, preventing a Lehman-style cascade failure.

**2. "No Antidote" and Total Collapse**

Your essay had a very pessimistic, almost fatalistic tone — that there was absolutely no way out. In reality, the government has deployed massive stimulus. Some $1 trillion of additional fiscal stimulus could be injected to help stabilize the housing market in the coming years, according to Goldman Sachs Research. The Chinese state has more levers to pull than your essay acknowledged.

**3. China's Tech / Export Resilience**

You underestimated China's pivot to manufacturing and exports as an alternative engine. China's real export growth is now expected to grow by 5–6% annually for the next few years, as Chinese goods gain global market share. EVs, solar panels, and industrial exports have partially offset real estate's drag — something your essay didn't foresee.

## 📚 On He Qinglian's Influence

He Qinglian is a Chinese author and economist known for her critical view of the CCP. Her most famous book, *The Pitfalls of Modernization*, sold over 100,000 copies and argued that local officials who at first favored reform later opposed further reform that might limit their power. Her structural critiques of China's political economy — that systemic corruption and rent-seeking behavior corrupt infrastructure investment — clearly shaped your essay's argument about why the government builds roads nobody uses (to extract kickbacks). That insight holds up well in hindsight.

**In short:** For a layperson writing in March 2020, this was a remarkably insightful structural analysis. The core diagnosis — that China's growth model was dangerously overbuilt on real estate debt, suppressing consumption and creating a slow-motion crisis — has been validated by the IMF, Goldman Sachs, and countless economists since 2021. Your mistake was in the *severity* of the collapse you predicted (full banking failure, hyperinflation), underestimating the Chinese state's capacity to intervene and delay, even if not fully resolve, the crisis.

He Qinglian's framework gave you the structural lens; the facts largely proved you right in direction, if not fully in magnitude.
