Question: What's the difference between the F/M/J visa group and the H/L/O/P/Q visa group, and why do they have different typical timelines (~1.5 vs ~2 months)?

Answer:

**F/M/J — non-petition, status-based (student/exchange)**
- **F-1**: academic study (university, college)
- **M-1**: vocational/non-academic technical training
- **J-1**: exchange visitor — scholars, interns, trainees, au pairs, medical residents
- No USCIS petition needed. Sponsor (school/program) issues **I-20** (F/M) or **DS-2019** (J), you pay the SEVIS I-901 fee, fill DS-160, then get a consular interview.
- Total processing takes 6 to 12 weeks for most applicants in 2026. Typical range is seven to eight weeks, though some wait a month or less — matches your "1.5 months" estimate.
- **Big 2026 change**: DHS published a final rule on July 17, 2026 eliminating Duration of Status (D/S) for F-1 and J-1, replacing it with a fixed admission period, effective September 15, 2026. This is expected to increase I-539 extension filings and processing delays, currently running about 3-4 months for those extensions. If you're renewing/extending status after Sept 15, budget more than 1.5 months.

**H/L/O/P/Q — petition-based work visas**
- **H-1B**: specialty occupation (lottery-capped)
- **L-1**: intracompany transfer
- **O-1**: extraordinary ability
- **P**: athletes/entertainers
- **Q**: cultural exchange
- These require an **employer-filed I-129 petition** to USCIS first, then (if you're abroad) a consular interview.
- Standard USCIS adjudication: H-1B processing times range 2–6 months for regular processing in 2026. Most L-1 petitions take 6.5 months at USCIS as of 2026 without premium processing.
- **Premium processing** (I-907, $2,965) cuts USCIS decision to 15 business days for Form I-129 filings including H-1B and L-1.
- Your "2 months" estimate only holds if you use premium processing *and* skip/streamline the consular step. Realistically: the planning timeline is USCIS processing time plus consular appointment wait time plus visa issuance time, and for a beneficiary in India even with premium processing the total from filing to first day of work is 3.5–5 months, because consular appointments are typically 6–8 weeks out and can't be scheduled until USCIS approves the petition.

**TL;DR gap between your two numbers**: F/M/J's "1.5 months" is realistic for a first-time entry with no complications. H/L/O/P/Q's "2 months" is only realistic with premium processing *and* if you're already in the US changing status — if you need a consular stamp abroad, add another 6-8+ weeks on top.
