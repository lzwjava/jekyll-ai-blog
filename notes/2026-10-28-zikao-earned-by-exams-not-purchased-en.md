**Question:** Do poor / 老实 (simple/honest) families in China pursue self-study degrees (自考), and can you basically pay a fee to obtain one?

---

**Answer:**

You're touching on China's **自学考试 (Zìxué Kǎoshì / Zikao)** — the Self-Taught Higher Education Examination system. Here's the full picture:

**Who uses it — yes, often working-class / 老实 families**

Zikao (高等教育自学考试) has been held annually since 1981 and is administered by the National Education Examinations Authority under the Ministry of Education. It has no admission threshold and no conflicts with work schedules — applicants face no restrictions based on education level, age, or household registration. This makes it a natural fit for people from lower-income or rural backgrounds who missed the Gaokao path, or for working adults who couldn't afford full-time university. So yes, the stereotype of 老实/hardworking ordinary families taking this route is quite accurate culturally.

**Can you just "pay a fee" and get the degree? — No, not legitimately**

The fees are very low: registration per subject is roughly 40–60 RMB (varies by province), and each course's textbook and exam fee runs about 70 RMB. But there is no entrance exam to enroll — the difficulty lies in actually passing the coursework exams, since every subject in a given specialty must be passed before a graduation certificate is issued.

Most Zikao candidates are working adults with limited study time and self-discipline, which makes the pass rates genuinely challenging.

So the degree is **not purchased** — it is earned through a series of national standardized exams. However:

- **Private tutoring agencies (辅导机构)** do charge fees to help students prep for each subject — so you can spend money to make the process easier.
- **Illegal diploma mills** do exist in China that sell fake credentials — but that's a criminal offense, completely separate from the official Zikao system.
- Some commercial programs allow up to 30% of credits to come from attendance and assignments rather than final exams alone — making it somewhat more accessible if you enroll through a supported learning center.

**Bottom line**

| Aspect | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| Who uses it | Working class, rural families, adults who missed college |
| Entry barrier | None — open to all citizens |
| Cost | Very low (few hundred RMB total) |
| Can you just pay & get a degree? | **No** — must pass all subject exams |
| Is the degree legitimate? | Yes, government-issued, but carries lower prestige than Gaokao-entry degrees |

The "老实" framing fits — it's a route for people who play by the rules, study on their own, and grind through the exams the hard way rather than shortcuts.
