Question: Which operating system can be freely modified and redistributed by any person or organization as long as they comply with the GNU General Public License?

Answer:

### Why Linux (D) is Correct and UNIX (A) is Incorrect

The main distinction lies in **open-source licensing (GPL)** versus **proprietary commercial copyright**:

* **Linux and the GPL:**
* Linux is an **open-source** operating system kernel.
* It is released under the **GNU General Public License (GPL)**, which explicitly grants anyone the right to inspect, **modify, and redistribute** the source code.
* A key requirement of the GPL is "copyleft": any modified version created and distributed must also be released under the GPL, keeping the software free and open for everyone.

* **Why UNIX (A) is Incorrect:**
* UNIX is a **proprietary, commercial** operating system originally created at AT&T Bell Labs.
* Traditional UNIX variants (e.g., IBM AIX, HP-UX, Oracle Solaris) are closed-source and protected by strict commercial licenses and copyrights. You cannot freely view, modify, or redistribute UNIX source code.
* While Linux is "Unix-like" in design and command structure, it was written completely independently by Linus Torvalds and global contributors, making it free from UNIX's restricted commercial license.

### Analysis of Other Options

* **B. NetWare:** A proprietary, closed-source network operating system developed by Novell.
* **C. Windows:** A commercial, closed-source operating system owned by Microsoft. Source code modifications and redistribution are strictly prohibited by law.
