**Question:** What does "王孙满观师" (*Wáng Sūn Mǎn Guān Shī* — Wang Sun Man Observes the Army) mean and say?

**Answer:**

"王孙满观师" (full title: **《王孙满观秦师》**) is a short but celebrated prose piece from the *Guoyu* (《国语·周语》), written by Zuoqiu Ming during the Spring and Autumn period. Here is what it says:

---

**The Scene**

In the 24th year of King Xiang of Zhou (628 BCE), the Qin army was marching to launch a surprise attack on the state of Zheng, and passed through the northern gate of the Zhou royal capital. The soldiers on the left and right of each chariot merely removed their helmets and bowed toward the royal palace as a gesture of respect for the Son of Heaven — but then leaped back onto their chariots in a jumpy, undisciplined manner. Three hundred chariots passed by in this fashion. Wang Sun Man observed them carefully.

---

**Wang Sun Man's Prophecy**

Wang Sun Man spoke to the king, saying: "The Qin army will surely suffer a punishing defeat." The king asked why. Wang Sun Man replied: "The army is arrogant and reckless. Recklessness leads to poor strategy; arrogance leads to disrespect of ritual. Without ritual there is laxness, and without strategy one falls into traps. An army that enters dangerous terrain while being lax — how can it not be defeated? If the Qin army goes unpunished, then the Way itself is abandoned."

---

**The Fulfillment**

And so it came to pass: when Qin's plan to surprise-attack Zheng failed (a Zheng merchant named Xian Gao had intercepted and warned Zheng), the Qin army turned back. On the retreat, Jin forces ambushed them at the Xiao Mountains, and the three Qin commanders — Bing, Shu, and Shi (i.e., Bai Yi Bing, Xi Qi Shu, and Meng Ming Shi) — were all captured.

---

**Core Message**

The central teaching of the text is captured in one line:

> **"师轻而骄，轻则寡谋，骄则无礼；无礼则脱，寡谋自陷。"**
> *(An army that is reckless is lacking in strategy; an army that is arrogant disregards propriety. Disregarding propriety brings laxness; lacking strategy brings self-destruction.)*

This piece became a classic example in *Guoyu* of predicting the outcome of a battle from fine behavioral details — illustrating the timeless military principle that **"an arrogant army is destined to lose" (骄兵必败)**.

This story is closely linked to the Battle of Xiao (崤之战) discussed previously — Wang Sun Man was watching the very army that would later be annihilated.
