**Question:** Do CEOs face extreme pressure that has driven some to suicide?

**Answer:**

Yes, this is a documented and serious issue. C-suite executives face uniquely intense pressures, and several high-profile cases have highlighted the mental health crisis in corporate leadership.

**Notable Cases**

- In Switzerland alone, two top executives died by suicide in close succession: Carsten Schloter, CEO of Swisscom, and Pierre Wauthier, CFO of Zurich Insurance Group.
- Years later, Martin Senn, former CEO of Zurich Insurance Group, also took his own life shortly after leaving the company.
- Adrian Kohler, CEO of herbal sweets firm Ricola, died by suicide in 2011 after admitting responsibility for what turned out to be relatively minor accounting irregularities — and he left a note explicitly tying his death to the immense pressure of his working life.
- In 2022, the CFO of Bed Bath & Beyond fell from the 18th floor of a Manhattan skyscraper in what was ruled a suicide.
- Charles Chiang, CEO of computer hardware maker MSI, died after falling from the seventh floor of MSI's headquarters in New Taipei, Taiwan.

**Why CEOs Are Vulnerable**

While it seems counterintuitive — since top executives tend to have better mental health than those on lower rungs — pressure on C-level executives is undeniably mounting.

Key stressors include:
- Heavy workloads, frenetic schedules, and extensive overseas travel that have obliterated work-life balance, compounded by shareholder and board pressure.
- Average CEO tenure has shortened dramatically, adding instability and urgency to already high-stakes roles.
- Despite being surrounded by staff, CEOs often feel deeply isolated due to their position and the high-stakes decisions they must make alone.
- Research on entrepreneurs found that nearly 49% of those surveyed reported having a mental health condition.

**The Broader Problem**

The same compassion and push for destigmatizing mental health that employers champion among regular employees must be extended to the C-suite, where it may be even more necessary. Many executives still believe that depression or anxiety signals weakness, which prevents them from seeking help.
