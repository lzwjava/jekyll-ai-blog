Question: Are big corps rushing to flatten management layers to run leaner?

Answer: Yes, this is happening right now and it's called "The Great Flattening." Real data points:

- **Coinbase**: Brian Armstrong is testing one-person teams that cover engineering, design, and product simultaneously, with the goal of concentrating around AI-native talent managing fleets of agents rather than people managing people.Brian Armstrong tells employees organisational changes will flatten management layers and concentrate on AI-native talent, using AI to experiment with reduced pod sizes and testing one-person teams that balance engineering, design and product management

- **Amazon**: Andy Jassy mandated raising the individual-contributor-to-manager ratio to remove layers and speed decisions.

- **Base rate**: Gartner predicts 20% of organizations will use AI to flatten their organizational structure through 2026, eliminating more than half of current middle management positions. Manager headcount at public companies already fell 6.1% between 2022 and 2025, with Meta, Amazon, Google, Intel, and Estee Lauder all flattening hierarchies.

- **Employee-side confirmation**: around 41% of employees say their companies trimmed management layers last year, per Korn Ferry.

Why it's real and not just PR: middle management's core job — status aggregation, reporting, scheduling, task routing — is exactly what an LLM orchestrator does well. AI is automating the administrative side of management such as reporting and scheduling, and companies are cutting layers to save cost, but the human core of coaching, judgment, and team leadership is proving hard to replace. So it's not "no management," it's compressing the coordination layer and pushing the surviving humans into orchestrator/coach roles over bigger fleets of agents + fewer direct reports.

Risk flagged by skeptics: eliminating middle managers now may be dismantling the pipeline that produces future senior leaders, and 37% of employees who lost managers in the Korn Ferry research reported feeling directionless — so it's a real bet, not a free lunch.
