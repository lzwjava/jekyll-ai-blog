**Question: Do people regret moving to Singapore for work or immigration from mainland China, India, etc.?**

Answer:

Yes, some people regret it, but many do not. The pattern is very similar to other high-income immigrant destinations: **Singapore is excellent for career and safety, but not always easy for belonging, family, and long-term identity.**

For mainland Chinese and Indian professionals, common experiences are:

## Why people are happy they moved

### 1. Higher stability and rule-based society

Many immigrants appreciate:

* low crime
* clean environment
* efficient government
* good schools
* healthcare
* predictable business environment

Some Indian professionals who moved to Singapore describe it as a long-term home because of public services, safety, and governance quality. ([CNA][1])

### 2. Career opportunities

Singapore is attractive for:

* finance
* banking
* AI/software
* biotech
* logistics
* regional headquarters

For skilled workers, Singapore can be a career accelerator because many global companies use it as their Asia hub.

### 3. Better environment for children

Many Chinese families especially like:

* English education
* international exposure
* less exam pressure than mainland China
* multicultural environment

---

## Why some people regret it

### 1. They underestimate cost of living

Singapore salaries are high, but:

* housing is extremely expensive
* private condos are costly
* raising children is expensive
* cars are almost impossible for many middle-class families

A person earning SGD 8k/month may feel comfortable alone but much less comfortable supporting a family.

---

### 2. They feel they are always "foreigners"

This is probably the biggest emotional issue.

Even after years:

* PR does not always feel like citizenship
* locals may see newcomers as outsiders
* immigrants may miss their original culture/community

Research on Chinese and Indian migrants shows many struggle with belonging and integration despite successful careers. ([Taylor & Francis Online][2])

---

### 3. Work pressure and uncertainty

For Employment Pass holders:

* losing a job can create immigration pressure
* career competition is high
* companies may prefer locals/PRs for some roles

Some foreign professionals reported anxiety about job security and being treated as outsiders during periods of tighter foreign worker policies. ([CNA][3])

---

### 4. Missing family

Especially for immigrants from China and India:

* parents remain in home country
* children grow up away from grandparents
* returning home becomes complicated

Many regrets are not "Singapore is bad"; they are:

> "I achieved more money, but I lost connection with my original family."

---

## Mainland Chinese immigrants: typical pattern

More satisfied:

* young engineers
* entrepreneurs
* finance professionals
* people who value order and English education

More regret:

* people expecting easy citizenship
* people expecting Singapore to feel like "another Chinese city"
* people with parents/family responsibilities in China

Singapore is culturally Chinese-majority, but socially it is a very different country.

---

## Indian immigrants: typical pattern

More satisfied:

* IT engineers
* managers
* finance professionals
* people with global mindset

Challenges:

* housing/rental issues
* accent/cultural adjustment
* competition perception

Singapore has had public debates around foreign professionals, especially Indian professionals in some sectors. ([NUS Faculty of Arts & Social Sciences][4])

---

## Compared with Hong Kong (since you are also considering HK)

Roughly:

|                              | Singapore      | Hong Kong                |
| ---------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------ |
| Government stability         | Higher         | Lower                    |
| English environment          | Better         | Good                     |
| AI/tech jobs                 | Strong         | Stronger finance         |
| Housing                      | Very expensive | Extremely expensive      |
| Chinese cultural familiarity | Higher         | Much higher              |
| Immigration path             | Hard but clear | Possible through schemes |
| Family life                  | Better         | More stressful           |
| Entrepreneurship             | Friendly       | Also strong              |

For a Chinese AI/software entrepreneur, Singapore is often attractive because it is globally connected and English-speaking. Hong Kong is attractive because of proximity to mainland China and finance/business networks.

My observation: **people usually regret Singapore when they expected a "better China". People usually like Singapore when they expected a "small global country with high standards".** ([CNA][1])

For someone with your profile (AI engineer, entrepreneur, Chinese family, considering HK/Singapore/US), the key question is not "is Singapore good?" but:

**Do you want a stable global base for your family, or do you want a bigger market with more upside?**

Singapore is better as a stable base; China/HK/US may offer more aggressive upside.
