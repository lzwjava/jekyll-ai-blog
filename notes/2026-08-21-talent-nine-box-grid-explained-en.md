Question: Explain the **talent nine-box grid**.

Answer:

The **talent nine-box grid（人才九宫格）** is a management framework used by companies to evaluate employees across two dimensions:

1. **Current performance（当前绩效）**
2. **Future potential（未来潜力）**

It creates a 3×3 matrix:

```
                 Potential（潜力）

                 Low        Medium        High

Performance
High          Expert       High Performer   Star

Medium        Solid        Core Employee    High Potential

Low           Problem      Underdeveloped   Hidden Talent
              Employee     Employee        / Risky Talent
```

Different companies use slightly different names, but the core idea is the same.

---

## Axis 1: Performance（绩效）

This asks:

> "How well does this person deliver today?"

Examples:

### High performance

* Consistently achieves goals.
* Delivers quality work.
* Reliable.

Example:

A senior backend engineer who:

* ships features on time,
* fixes production issues,
* writes maintainable code.

---

### Medium performance

* Does acceptable work.
* Meets expectations.
* Still has room to improve.

---

### Low performance

* Misses goals.
* Needs frequent help.
* Has execution problems.

---

## Axis 2: Potential（潜力）

This asks:

> "How much bigger can this person become?"

Potential includes:

* Learning speed.
* Ability to handle complexity.
* Leadership ability.
* Adaptability.
* Creativity.
* Strategic thinking.

A person can have low current performance but high potential.

Example:

A new graduate:

```
Current:
    weak production experience

Potential:
    learns extremely fast
    understands systems deeply
```

---

## The nine categories

### 1. High Performance + High Potential

Usually called:

* Star
* Future leader
* Top talent

Example:

An engineer who:

* solves hard problems,
* mentors others,
* learns quickly,
* can lead future projects.

Companies try hard to retain these people.

---

### 2. High Performance + Medium Potential

The reliable expert.

Example:

A database engineer with 15 years experience:

* excellent execution,
* deep domain knowledge,
* but less interested in management or new areas.

Very valuable.

---

### 3. High Performance + Low Potential

The specialist.

Example:

A person who is extremely good at a specific process:

* stable,
* productive,
* but unlikely to expand scope.

Still important.

---

### 4. Medium Performance + High Potential

The "future star".

Example:

A young AI researcher:

* understands papers quickly,
* has strong ideas,
* but lacks engineering experience.

The company invests here.

---

### 5. Medium Performance + Medium Potential

The core employee.

Most employees are here.

They:

* do their jobs,
* grow steadily,
* provide stability.

---

### 6. Low Performance + High Potential

The "wild horse" / high-risk talent.

Example:

A brilliant engineer:

```
Pros:
+ invents new algorithms
+ learns extremely fast

Cons:
- poor communication
- inconsistent delivery
- lacks experience
```

The question is:

> "Can we help this person become reliable?"

---

### 7. Low Performance + Low Potential

Usually a poor fit.

Companies may:

* retrain,
* move roles,
* or eventually exit.

---

## Why companies use it

The mistake many companies make:

They only reward today's performance.

But:

```
Current performance = value today

Potential = value tomorrow
```

A 25-year-old engineer and a 50-year-old engineer may have the same performance today, but different growth trajectories.

The grid helps answer:

* Who should get promotion?
* Who needs training?
* Who should receive important projects?
* Who are future leaders?

---

## In startup / AI company context

For frontier AI teams, the grid often looks different.

A traditional company may prefer:

```
High performance + predictable behavior
```

because it values execution.

A research startup may tolerate:

```
High potential + high risk
```

because breakthrough ideas often come from unusual people.

Example:

Early-stage researcher:

```
Today:
    50% success rate

Potential:
    could create a new model architecture
```

A mature company may reject this person.

A research-driven company may invest heavily.

---

A simple formula:

```
Talent value ≈
    Current Performance
    ×
    Future Potential
    ×
    Ability to work with others
```

The nine-box grid is basically a way for managers to estimate this value.
