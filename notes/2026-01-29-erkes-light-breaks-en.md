Answer: **"The Lights of Erke" (爱尔克的灯光)** is a poignant and reflective prose piece written by the celebrated Chinese author **Ba Jin** in 1941. It is a cornerstone of modern Chinese literature, blending personal nostalgia with a sharp critique of the suffocating nature of traditional feudal families.

### 1. The Core Symbolism

The essay centers on the contrast between three different types of "lights":

* **The Physical Lamp:** The actual light in the room where the author stays, serving as a catalyst for his memories.
* **The Light of Erke:** Inspired by a Western legend (the story of Hero and Leander), this represents a **tragic, lost love** and a hope that was extinguished by fate or societal barriers.
* **The Lights of the Ancestral Home:** These represent the **decaying feudal system**. Ba Jin views these lights as gloomy and oppressive, symbolizing a "grave" for the youth and dreams of those trapped within the old family structure.

---

### 2. Themes and Message

* **Critique of the Feudal Family:** Much like his famous novel *Family (家)*, this essay reflects Ba Jin’s resentment toward the rigid traditional household that suppresses individuality and ruins young lives (specifically referencing the tragic fate of his sister).
* **The Search for a New Path:** The author concludes that one must leave the "old home" to find a meaningful life. He famously asserts that the true light is not found within the ancestral walls, but out in the world where one struggles and lives authentically.
* **Nostalgia vs. Reality:** While there is a sense of longing for his childhood, it is quickly overshadowed by the realization that the past is a "shrine" of dead things that must be abandoned to move toward the future.

### 3. Historical Context

Written during the **War of Resistance against Japanese Aggression**, the essay reflects the broader Chinese intellectual movement of the time: the urge to break away from the "old" (feudalism/tradition) to save the "new" (the nation/the individual).
