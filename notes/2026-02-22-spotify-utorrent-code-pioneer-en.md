Ludvig Strigeus (born January 15, 1981), widely known by his online alias "ludde," is a renowned Swedish programmer and software engineer. He is celebrated for creating the ultra-lightweight BitTorrent client **µTorrent** and for his pivotal role as a key developer behind the music streaming giant **Spotify** . A graduate of Chalmers University of Technology with a master's degree in computer science, Strigeus has made significant contributions to both open-source and commercial software .

His journey began with a passion for reverse-engineering and game development. He was a core contributor to **ScummVM**, an interpreter that allows classic adventure games to run on modern platforms, and he co-founded **OpenTTD**, a popular open-source remake of the transport simulation classic *Transport Tycoon* . In 2005, he achieved a different kind of recognition by winning PuzzleCrack, a competitive puzzle hunt that combined problem-solving with hacking skills .

Strigeus's most famous creation, **µTorrent**, was launched in 2005. Its groundbreaking design—a single executable file under 300 KB with a rich feature set—revolutionized the BitTorrent landscape by offering a powerful yet incredibly efficient client for file sharing . The software's success caught the attention of the music startup Spotify. In a strategic move, **Spotify acquired µTorrent in 2006**, not primarily for the technology, but to bring Strigeus onto their team . Shortly after, the µTorrent client itself was sold to BitTorrent, Inc., while Strigeus joined Spotify as a foundational engineer, where he helped build the platform's core technology .

His immense talent has earned him widespread recognition. Strigeus has been awarded the **John Ericsson Medal** (2006), the **Tenzingpriset** (2011), and the prestigious **Polhem Prize** (2020) for his excellence in software development . He was also appointed an **honorary doctorate** by his alma mater, Chalmers University of Technology, in 2015 . In 2023, he was elected as a fellow of the Royal Swedish Academy of Engineering Sciences . Despite using a wheelchair due to a rare muscular disease, Ludvig Strigeus continues to work as a software engineer, and his early shareholding in Spotify reportedly made him a billionaire, cementing his legacy as one of Sweden's most influential and successful programmers .
