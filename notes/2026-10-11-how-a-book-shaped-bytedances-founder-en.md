**Question:** Did ByteDance founder Zhang Yiming mention *The Road Less Traveled* a lot?

**Answer:**

Yes — and this is a well-documented connection. Zhang Yiming (张一鸣) has explicitly mentioned and recommended ***The Road Less Traveled*** (*少有人走的路* in Chinese), and the book's influence on his thinking is significant.

**He's listed it as one of his top book recommendations.** Zhang Yiming's personal reading list includes *The Road Less Traveled* alongside *Ikigai* (活法), *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*, and *Fundamentals of Biology*.

**The "Delayed Gratification" connection is the most famous link.** In a 2016 interview with *Caijing* magazine, Zhang Yiming said he is fundamentally conservative — and that the essence of his conservatism is his belief in "delayed gratification" (延迟满足感). While this concept became widely associated with Zhang Yiming in China, it was not his original idea — he first encountered the theory about ten years earlier, drawn directly from *The Road Less Traveled*.

**He mentioned it as part of his reading.** In another interview, Zhang Yiming confirmed he had read *The Road Less Traveled* that year, along with a few other books — showing it was an active part of his intellectual life, not just a passing reference.

**His Douban reading history backs it up.** Zhang Yiming joined Douban (China's book/film social network) as early as 2005 under the ID "onebird," and his reading history spans internet history, programming, economics, corporate management, and personal development books — with *The Road Less Traveled* being a key title in the personal growth category.

In short, the most direct impact the book had on Zhang Yiming was Peck's idea of **delaying gratification as a discipline** — which became a cornerstone of his personal philosophy and the way he built ByteDance.
