Question: Provide an introduction to the essay 《君子之交》 by Zhu Ziqing (朱自清).

Answer:

《君子之交》 (The Friendship of Gentlemen / A Gentleman's Friendship) is a short, classic prose essay written by Zhu Ziqing (朱自清, 1898–1948), one of the most important modern Chinese writers and essayists of the May Fourth era.

### Key Facts

- **Author**: Zhu Ziqing (pen name: 佩弦 Péi Xián)
- **Written**: 1930s (most sources point to around 1933–1935)
- **Genre**: lyrical prose (散文 sǎnwén), reflective essay
- **Length**: Very short (~600–800 characters), often only 2–3 pages in print
- **Period**: Republican era modern Chinese literature

### Main Content & Core Idea

The essay is built around the famous Confucian saying:

> 君子之交淡如水
> The friendship of superior men is plain / insipid like water.

Zhu Ziqing uses this ancient phrase as the central thesis and then develops a gentle, introspective meditation on the nature of true friendship.

Main points he conveys:

- Genuine friendship does **not** need intense passion, frequent contact, grand gestures, or constant excitement.
- True friends can go long periods without meeting or even writing, yet the bond remains deep and unchanged.
- This kind of relationship is quiet, pure, steady, and long-lasting — like plain water that has no strong taste but is essential and trustworthy.
- In contrast, superficial or利益-based relationships are often intense at first but fade quickly (he implicitly contrasts them with “小人之交甘若醴” — the friendship of petty men is sweet like sweet wine).

### Writing Style & Tone

- Extremely concise and restrained
- Simple, clear, elegant vernacular Chinese (白话文)
- Poetic yet conversational
- Calm, introspective, slightly melancholic tone
- Almost no ornamentation — Zhu Ziqing deliberately uses very plain language to mirror the theme of “淡” (plainness / lightness)

### Cultural & Educational Significance

《君子之交》 is one of the most frequently anthologized modern Chinese essays in middle-school and high-school textbooks across Chinese-speaking regions (Mainland China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, etc.).
It is often taught together with Zhu Ziqing’s other famous pieces such as:

- 《背影》 (Father’s Back Shadow)
- 《荷塘月色》 (Moonlight over the Lotus Pond)
- 《春》 (Spring)

Many Chinese readers remember the sentence “君子之交淡如水” from this essay even if they have forgotten most other details — it has become one of the most commonly quoted modern interpretations of traditional friendship ideals.

### Famous Opening & Closing Lines

Opening (very famous):
> 朋友我将告诉你一件最平常也最不平常的事，就是我对于“君子之交淡如水”这句话的体验。

Closing part (often quoted):
> ……真正的君子之交是淡而持久的，淡得几乎近于无味，但却可以终生不渝。

In short, 《君子之交》 is Zhu Ziqing’s quiet but profound modern reflection on how real friendship can be undemonstrative, unspectacular, and yet extremely durable — a message that continues to resonate deeply in Chinese culture today.
