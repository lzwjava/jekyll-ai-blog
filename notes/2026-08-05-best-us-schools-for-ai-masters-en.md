**Question: Which lesser-known US schools are good for a CS/AI master's?**

If you mean **less famous than MIT, Stanford, CMU, Berkeley**, but still strong enough for **AI engineering/research and US job opportunities**, there are many good options. For your background (11 years engineering, LLM infra, GPT training, but no traditional CS degree), I would look for schools with:

* strong CS/AI faculty
* practical MS programs
* reasonable admission chances
* STEM OPT eligibility
* location near tech companies

Some underrated choices:

| School                               | Why interesting for AI/CS                                         | Reputation                                         |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Northeastern University              | Very strong co-op system, good AI/software industry connection    | Less research-famous but excellent career pipeline |
| University of California, Irvine     | Strong ML, systems, NLP; near LA tech ecosystem                   | Often underrated internationally                   |
| University of California, Santa Cruz | Good AI, robotics, computer vision                                | Smaller but strong research                        |
| Arizona State University             | Huge CS department, many AI courses, easier admission             | Not elite but very practical                       |
| University of Utah                   | Excellent graphics, AI, systems                                   | Strong research, less famous abroad                |
| Stony Brook University               | Very strong CS, algorithms, AI                                    | Public-school hidden gem                           |
| Virginia Tech                        | Strong systems, AI, engineering culture                           | Very respected in US                               |
| University of Colorado Boulder       | ML, robotics, distributed systems                                 | Good industry connection                           |
| Rochester Institute of Technology    | Applied CS, software engineering, good for international students | Less academic prestige, practical                  |
| Illinois Institute of Technology     | CS master's, Chicago job market                                   | Easier admission                                   |

For **AI infrastructure / LLM engineering**, I would particularly check:

### Tier 1 hidden gems

### More realistic admission targets

* Arizona State University

For your profile specifically:

* **CMU MSCS** → extremely ambitious, because they care about academic CS background.
* A good strategy could be:

  * 2–3 ambitious: CMU, UIUC, Georgia Tech
  * 4–6 strong but realistic: Stony Brook, Utah, UCI, Virginia Tech, Northeastern
  * 2–3 safer: ASU, RIT, IIT Chicago

Your advantage:

* real production engineering experience
* trained GPT models
* LLM infra projects
* open-source work

Your weakness:

* no bachelor's degree from a traditional university (this matters for many US MS programs).

Some schools may be more flexible if you show a strong portfolio.
