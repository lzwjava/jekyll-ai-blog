The important distinction is that a prominent industry position does not automatically establish that a particular immigration program is safe, legally compliant, or likely to succeed.

If you're considering her company for U.S. EW-3/EB-3 immigration, I would check the following before paying a substantial fee:

1. The actual U.S. employer: Verify that the job is genuine, the employer exists, and it has a real hiring need.
2. The U.S. immigration attorney: Obtain the attorney's full name and verify their license with the relevant state bar.
3. The contract: Check refund conditions, what happens if the employer withdraws, and which payments are refundable.
4. The immigration process: Understand the PERM labor certification, I-140 petition, visa availability, and consular processing. No agency can guarantee government approval or a particular timeline.
5. Independent client references: Speak with clients who completed the same program, ideally without the company selecting every person you contact.

Official resources: [USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/permanent-workers?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and the [U.S. Department of Labor's Foreign Labor Certification program](https://flag.dol.gov/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).
