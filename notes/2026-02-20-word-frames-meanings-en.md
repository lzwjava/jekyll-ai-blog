The word **"frames"** can have different meanings depending on the context. Here are some common uses:

### 1. **Physical Frames**

- **Picture Frames:** A structure (usually made of wood, metal, or plastic) that holds and displays a picture, photo, or artwork.
- **Glasses Frames:** The part of eyeglasses that holds the lenses and fits on the face.

### 2. **Technology & Computing**

- **Video Frames:** In video and animation, a frame is a single still image in a sequence that makes up the moving picture. For example, a video at 30 frames per second (fps) displays 30 images each second.
- **Web Frames:** In web development, frames (or iframes) are used to embed one HTML document inside another, allowing multiple web pages to be displayed in a single browser window.

### 3. **Programming & Data Structures**

- **Stack Frames:** In programming, a stack frame (or call frame) is a data structure that stores information about a function call, such as local variables, return addresses, and parameters.
- **Data Frames:** In data science (e.g., using Python's Pandas library), a DataFrame is a 2-dimensional labeled data structure with columns that can be of different types.

### 4. **Linguistics & Communication**

- **Framing:** In communication, framing refers to how information is presented to influence perception or interpretation (e.g., "framing an argument").

### 5. **Sports & Games**

- **Bowling Frames:** In bowling, a frame is a turn in which a player rolls the ball to knock down pins.
- **Pool/Snooker Frames:** In cue sports, a frame is a single game or round.
