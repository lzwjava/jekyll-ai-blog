Question: What is the "Liang Wenfeng's Four-Hour Investor Meeting Full Transcript" article about?

Answer:

This article is a **collection of notes/transcripts from a reported ~4-hour fundraising meeting between Liang Wenfeng and investors**, where he explained the philosophy, strategy, and future direction of DeepSeek. The article was published by Elsewhere on July 22, 2026. It is not an official DeepSeek document; it is a media reconstruction based on collected materials. ([Elsewhere News][1])

The core idea:

> DeepSeek is not trying to become "another OpenAI product company". It is optimizing for the probability of achieving AGI.

The main themes:

---

## 1. "AGI is the only main line"

Liang argues that many things are distractions:

* consumer apps
* enterprise vertical AI
* video generation
* 3D generation
* world models

His view:

> If you reach a higher level of intelligence, lower-level applications become easier.

So instead of building many products, DeepSeek focuses on the intelligence engine itself. ([Elsewhere News][1])

This is similar to the early Google philosophy:

* build the best search engine first
* applications come later

---

## 2. Coding Agent is the current priority

One interesting point:

He reportedly says the most important current application is **general-purpose Coding Agent**.

Why?

Because coding is:

* measurable
* automatically evaluated
* connected to real-world tasks
* close to autonomous labor

This aligns with the current frontier:

```
LLM
 ↓
reasoning
 ↓
coding agent
 ↓
software engineering automation
 ↓
AI worker
```

He considers finance/medical agents lower priority because they are vertical wrappers around intelligence. ([Reddit][2])

---

## 3. Open source is a strategy, not charity

This is probably the most discussed part.

His argument:

Closed source:

```
higher price
       ↓
more revenue
       ↓
but fewer users
       ↓
slower ecosystem growth
```

Open source:

```
more users
       ↓
more developers
       ↓
more feedback
       ↓
stronger ecosystem
       ↓
higher AGI probability
```

He views open source as a way to increase DeepSeek's chance of winning the AGI race. ([STARPATH GLOBAL][3])

---

## 4. Low cost is an engineering advantage

Another important point.

He says low price is not a marketing decision.

It comes from:

* architecture efficiency
* training efficiency
* reducing compute requirements

The reasoning:

A company that needs:

```
10x compute
```

has a disadvantage versus one that can achieve the same intelligence with:

```
1x compute
```

because compute is the bottleneck.

This is basically the DeepSeek story:

```
limited GPU resources
        ↓
need algorithmic efficiency
        ↓
better architecture
        ↓
lower cost
```

---

## 5. Team culture: research organization, not normal startup

A very unusual point:

He reportedly says DeepSeek tries to maintain:

* bottom-up exploration
* researcher freedom
* limited hierarchy
* not excessive overtime

The organization model:

```
50%:
important company direction

50%:
researcher-driven exploration
```

This resembles:

* Google early days
* Bell Labs
* OpenAI early research culture

rather than:

* traditional startup KPI management

([Reddit][2])

---

## 6. "Not genius, ordinary people doing extraordinary things"

One philosophical theme:

He rejects the "AI genius founder" narrative.

The preferred story:

```
not:
a group of geniuses created something amazing

but:
a group of ordinary people,
focused on a hard problem,
created something extraordinary
```

This is actually a strong cultural message because it makes the organization scalable.

---

## My interpretation

The deepest idea is not open source or pricing.

It is this:

> DeepSeek is positioning itself like a research lab that happens to be a company.

The strategy:

```
Goal:
        AGI

Constraint:
        limited resources

Strategy:
        extreme focus

Methods:
        efficient models
        open ecosystem
        low cost
        attract researchers

Revenue:
        secondary
```

This is closer to:

* Bell Labs
* early Google
* early OpenAI

than:

* SaaS startup
* consumer internet company
