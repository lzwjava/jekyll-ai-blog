Question: Can a 6.3 GB GGUF model (Qwen3.8-27B-UD-IQ1_M) run on a Tesla M40 with only 4 GB of system RAM?

Answer: Yes, it should run, with some caveats.

**VRAM (the main constraint)**
- The M40 has 12 GB or 24 GB of VRAM, depending on the version. The 6.3 GB file plus the KV cache and compute buffers fits in either one.
- On a 12 GB card, keep the context modest (4k to 8k) so the KV cache doesn't eat the remaining space.
- Offload all layers to the GPU with `-ngl 99`.

**4 GB system RAM**
- This is tight but workable if the whole model sits on the GPU. The OS and CUDA context will use roughly 1 to 2 GB.
- Loading may be slow if the file is memory-mapped. If it stalls or gets killed, add swap space or try `--no-mmap`.
- Don't offload layers to the CPU. With 4 GB of RAM, that would be very slow or crash.

**Software and GPU limits**
- The M40 is Maxwell (compute capability 5.2). Use a llama.cpp build with CUDA 12.x, because CUDA 13 dropped support for this architecture.
- Compile with `-DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=52`.
- The card has no tensor cores and weak FP16 performance, so expect slow generation, perhaps a few to ~10 tokens/sec for a 27B model. IQ-type quants are also somewhat slower than K-quants on old GPUs.

**Hardware**
- The M40 is passive-cooled, so it needs a fan or blower.
- It uses an 8-pin EPS (CPU-style) power connector, not PCIe.
- Your motherboard needs "Above 4G Decoding" enabled.

**Quality**
- IQ1_M is a very aggressive 1-bit quantization. A 27B model at this level will run, but output quality may be noticeably degraded. If your VRAM allows (24 GB), a Q4 quant would give much better results.
- I couldn't verify the "Qwen3.8" model name, so I can't comment on that specific release.

Example command:
```
./llama-cli -m Qwen3.8-27B-UD-IQ1_M.gguf -ngl 99 -c 4096 --no-mmap
```
