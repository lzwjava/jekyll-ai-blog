**Question:** What does CPT mean in the context of a US master's degree or program?

---

**Answer:**

**CPT = Curricular Practical Training**

CPT is a type of academics-related work/training authorization for **F-1 visa holders** (international students) to gain practical experience in their field of study. It can be used **during** the degree program — it is **not** available after graduation.

---

### Key Points

**What it is:**
The purpose of CPT is academic, not just for employment. The internship or practicum must be considered an **integral part of the student's degree program**.

**Eligibility:**
CPT is defined as employment that is directly related to the student's major area of study. The student must be enrolled in a course that requires a work experience (such as co-op, internship, or practicum), or be enrolled in a degree program that requires one.

**One-year rule:**
In general, students must have been enrolled full-time for at least one full academic year (fall + spring) before applying — though an exception exists for graduate programs that require practical training immediately from the start.

**Part-time vs. Full-time:**
Part-time CPT (≤20 hrs/week) is **not** deducted from a student's OPT (Optional Practical Training) eligibility upon graduation.

**Critical OPT impact:**
If a student accumulates **12 months or more of full-time CPT** at the same degree level, they become **ineligible for OPT** at that degree level — this is a major trade-off to watch out for.

**Employer-specific:**
CPT authorization is employer-specific and valid only for the current employer. If the student changes jobs, they must apply for new CPT authorization.

---

### CPT vs. OPT (Quick Comparison)

| | CPT | OPT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **When** | During the degree | After (or before) graduation |
| **Requirement** | Must be part of curriculum | More flexible |
| **Approval** | School's international office (DSO) | USCIS |
| **Risk** | 12+ months full-time → loses OPT | — |
