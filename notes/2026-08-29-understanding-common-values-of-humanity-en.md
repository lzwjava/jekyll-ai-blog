Question: **What is “全人类共同价值” (common values of all humanity), and how should I understand this text?**

Answer:

This is a **Chinese Communist Party / Chinese government political concept**. The official formulation is:

> **和平、发展、公平、正义、民主、自由**
> peace, development, fairness, justice, democracy, and freedom.

The important thing is to separate **the words themselves** from **the political framework in which China uses them**.

### 1. What does it mean literally?

The six values are fairly intuitive:

| Chinese | English     | Basic idea                                  |
| ------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 和平      | Peace       | Avoid war and conflict                      |
| 发展      | Development | Improve people's material/social conditions |
| 公平      | Fairness    | Reduce arbitrary or unjust treatment        |
| 正义      | Justice     | People and states should be treated justly  |
| 民主      | Democracy   | People should participate in governance     |
| 自由      | Freedom     | People should have meaningful freedom       |

So at the abstract level, there is nothing particularly strange about the list. Many societies would say these are desirable human values.

### 2. Where is the political part?

The interesting part is **“全人类共同价值” vs. “普世价值.”**

The Chinese government deliberately distinguishes its concept from what it calls Western “universal values.”

The underlying argument is roughly:

```text
Western universalism:
    There are universal principles
            ↓
    these principles can be used
    as standards for different countries

Chinese formulation:
    Humanity shares some broad aspirations
            ↓
    but civilizations / countries
    have different histories and systems
            ↓
    therefore don't impose one country's
    political model on everyone
```

So **“共同”** is doing important work.

It does not mean:

> Every country must have exactly the same political institutions.

It is closer to:

> Different civilizations can disagree about political systems while still finding common ground around broad human goals.

### 3. The word “democracy” is especially important

This is where the concept becomes politically interesting.

China does **not** necessarily use “democracy” to mean exactly the same institutional package that liberal democracies usually mean by it.

For example, in a Western liberal-democratic framework, democracy is often strongly associated with:

```text
competitive elections
+
political pluralism
+
separation of powers
+
independent judiciary
+
freedom of expression
+
civil liberties
```

The Chinese political system uses a different conception, emphasizing things such as:

```text
people's participation
+
representation
+
consultation
+
policy responsiveness
+
political stability
+
development outcomes
```

Therefore, when you encounter **“民主”** in Chinese political-study material, don't automatically substitute your entire Western political definition into it.

### 4. Why does the textbook emphasize “not Western universal values”?

Because this is part of a larger foreign-policy argument:

```text
不同文明
    ↓
不同历史、文化、制度、发展阶段
    ↓
不应该强迫所有国家采用同一种制度
    ↓
寻找最大公约数
    ↓
和平共处 + 合作
    ↓
人类命运共同体
```

That's also why phrases like **“和而不同”**, **“共商共建共享”**, and **“人类命运共同体”** appear around the same material.

### 5. Historically, where did the phrase come from?

The text you pasted is describing a concept promoted by **Xi Jinping**.

The six-value formulation was explicitly put forward by Xi at the **UN General Assembly in September 2015**, and was subsequently incorporated into Chinese diplomatic and political discourse.

So if you're studying this for an exam, the key memorization structure is:

```text
全人类共同价值
        │
        ├── 和平
        ├── 发展
        ├── 公平
        ├── 正义
        ├── 民主
        └── 自由
```

and:

```text
全人类共同价值
        ↓
价值基础
        ↓
构建人类命运共同体
```

### 6. One subtle correction to your pasted summary

I would **not** interpret it as:

> “China's values are completely different from Western values.”

That's too strong.

There is substantial overlap: **peace, freedom, democracy, justice, and fairness** are also central concepts in Western political philosophy.

The real disagreement is more about:

**who defines the values, how they are interpreted, how they are institutionalized, and whether one country's interpretation should become a universal political standard.**

That's a much more useful way to understand the concept.

For your notes, I'd compress the whole thing to:

> **全人类共同价值 = 和平、发展、公平、正义、民主、自由。**
> It is a Chinese diplomatic/political framework arguing that humanity can share broad values while different civilizations retain different histories, cultures, and political systems. It is presented as the value foundation for **构建人类命运共同体**.
