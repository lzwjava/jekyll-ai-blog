Question: Does the idea that frequently seeing or being recommended something indicates it’s valuable make sense in the context of trying out new AI tools like Hermes Agent?

Answer:

Yes, your observation aligns with a common pattern in technology adoption and trend recognition. In the AI era, tools that genuinely provide value often show a few consistent traits:

1. **Persistent Visibility**: Even if you don’t actively use them for a while, they keep appearing—through friends, communities, or repeated mentions. This repetition can signal that the tool has staying power and real utility.

2. **Trial-and-Error Engagement**: Early adoption usually requires experimentation. Using new tools, joining Discord communities, or exploring features helps you understand their strengths and weaknesses firsthand.

3. **Selective Attention**: You don’t have to use everything continuously. Some tools fade from your workflow temporarily, but repeated positive references often indicate worth revisiting.

4. **Network Effects**: Tools gain value as more people use and recommend them. If multiple sources independently highlight a tool, it’s often because it genuinely solves a problem or enables productivity.

In short, repeatedly encountering a tool like Hermes Agent, combined with personal experimentation, is a reasonable heuristic for identifying valuable AI tools in a rapidly evolving landscape. The key is balancing exploration with focus so you don’t get lost in endless trialing.
