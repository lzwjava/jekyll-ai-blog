Question: I see a very clear future where every company and individual has their own large model that is updated monthly, communicates with external APIs, exchanges information, and provides value. Answer:

Yes, your vision is quite plausible. Here's a breakdown of the main points:

1. **Individual & Company LLMs**

   * In the near future, having personal or enterprise-specific large models (LLMs) that are continuously updated and can interface with APIs is realistic.
   * These models could act as personal assistants, knowledge bases, or automation agents. The key trend is “modular LLM ecosystems” where models exchange information safely, like you described.

2. **Cost Trends**

   * Training costs for large models are dropping steadily due to:

     * **Algorithmic improvements** (sparse models, LoRA, QLoRA, etc.)
     * **More efficient hardware**
     * **Better software stack and optimization**
   * If DeepSeek v2 cost around $5M (USD) for training today, future iterations could indeed drop to a fraction—maybe $500k—thanks to these improvements.

3. **Hardware Options**

   * **Domestic Chinese chips:** Companies like Biren, Cambricon, and Hygon are developing AI-focused accelerators. Performance may approach NVIDIA’s high-end chips in a few years, especially for inference and fine-tuning rather than full-scale pretraining.
   * **AMD GPUs:** With MI300 and next-gen RDNA architectures, AMD is becoming more competitive, particularly for mixed workloads. They are less dominant than NVIDIA in AI training but still viable.
   * **ASIC / custom chips:**

     * Custom AI ASICs (like Google TPU, Cerebras, or Graphcore) drastically reduce per-token training cost.
     * In China, several startups and state-backed companies are pushing ASICs for inference-heavy workloads, which aligns perfectly with your vision of monthly-updated models rather than constant full-scale retraining.

4. **Economic Implications**

   * Once costs drop to a fraction of current levels, it will be feasible for small companies and even individuals to maintain specialized models.
   * API-based knowledge exchange between models could create a decentralized “AI economy,” where value is exchanged efficiently between models without manual intervention.
