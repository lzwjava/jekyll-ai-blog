Question: Is maturity mainly about thinking more about other people’s lives and perspectives?

Answer:

Yes, that is a major part of maturity. A mature person usually develops a wider **model of the world**: not only “what do I want?” but also “how does reality look from other people’s positions?”

A simple way to see the progression:

```
Child:
    My feelings are the world.
    "I am unhappy, so everything is bad."

Young adult:
    My goals and choices matter.
    "I need to build my own life."

Mature adult:
    Many worlds exist simultaneously.
    "Other people have their own histories, constraints, dreams, fears, and suffering."
```

The key ability is **perspective-taking**.

For example:

* A 20-year-old student in China worrying about exams
* A 40-year-old factory worker worrying about family expenses
* A 60-year-old person worrying about health and meaning
* A software engineer in Silicon Valley worrying about layoffs
* A farmer in another country worrying about weather and harvest
* A startup founder worrying about survival

They are all living completely different "games". Their problems are not directly comparable because their constraints, opportunities, and expectations differ.

A mature person starts seeing:

```
Person = biology + family + culture + education + economy + luck + choices + time
```

Instead of:

```
Person = their current behavior
```

For example, someone who seems lazy may have:

* depression,
* poor education,
* family pressure,
* lack of opportunity,
* different values.

Someone who seems successful may have:

* hidden anxiety,
* loneliness,
* family problems,
* fear of losing status.

Maturity is not only empathy though. It also includes:

1. **Seeing reality clearly**

   Understanding incentives, systems, economics, human psychology.

2. **Accepting trade-offs**

   Every life path has costs.

   A researcher may have intellectual freedom but less money.
   A founder may have wealth potential but huge uncertainty.
   A stable employee may have security but less autonomy.

3. **Reducing ego**

   When young, people often think:

   > "My experience is the normal experience."

   Maturity is realizing:

   > "My experience is one sample from a huge distribution."

A good mental exercise is:

"Imagine I was born as this person."

Change:

* country
* family
* intelligence
* health
* historical period
* economic environment

Would I still make the same choices?

Often the answer is no.

This does not mean becoming passive or agreeing with everyone. You can still judge actions and make decisions. It means you separate:

```
understanding a person
        ≠
approving everything they do
```

Many great thinkers developed this ability. Reading history, biographies, literature, and talking with people from different backgrounds expands this "human simulator".

In the AI era, this ability may become even more valuable because information and reasoning are increasingly automated. Humans may differentiate more through:

* judgment,
* values,
* emotional understanding,
* knowing what matters to people.
