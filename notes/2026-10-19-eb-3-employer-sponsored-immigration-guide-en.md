**Question:** What is the US EB-3 Employer-Sponsored Immigration route and how does it work?

---

**Answer:**

## What Is EB-3?

The EB-3 (Employment-Based Third Preference) is a U.S. immigrant visa category that provides a path to permanent residence for workers sponsored by a U.S. employer. It is the broadest employer-sponsored Green Card category, covering three subcategories: **Skilled Workers**, **Professionals**, and **Other Workers**. It requires employer sponsorship, a specific job offer, and PERM labor certification in every case. There is no self-petition option.

---

## Three Subcategories

| Subcategory | Requirement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Skilled Workers** | 2+ years training/experience | Electricians, chefs, IT techs |
| **Professionals** | U.S. bachelor's degree | Accountants, engineers, nurses |
| **Other Workers** | < 2 years training | Laborers, housekeepers, aides |

All three subcategories require employer sponsorship and PERM labor certification. The employer must demonstrate that no qualified U.S. worker is available for the position before sponsoring a foreign worker.

## Full Migration Route (Step by Step)

### Stage 1 — Prevailing Wage Determination (PWD)
The employer submits a prevailing wage request to the DOL's National Prevailing Wage Center. The DOL issues a determination establishing the minimum wage the employer must offer. PWD processing currently takes approximately **6 to 10 months**.

### Stage 2 — Mandatory Recruitment (PERM Labor Market Test)
With the PWD in hand, the employer conducts mandatory recruitment steps to test the U.S. labor market, including a job order with the State Workforce Agency for a minimum of 30 days, two print advertisements in a Sunday newspaper, a 30-day internal job posting, and (for professional positions) at least three additional recruitment steps. This phase typically takes **2 to 3 months**.

### Stage 3 — PERM Filing (ETA Form 9089)
If no qualified U.S. worker is identified, the employer files the PERM application with the DOL. The DOL reviews it and, if everything is in order, certifies the PERM. Adjudication takes approximately **6 to 12 months** after filing. The DOL may select applications for audit, which adds time.

### Stage 4 — I-140 Petition (Filed by Employer with USCIS)
After PERM is certified, the employer files **Form I-140** (Immigrant Petition for Alien Workers) with USCIS, including the certified PERM, evidence the beneficiary meets job requirements, and proof of the employer's ability to pay. Premium Processing is available for a **45-business-day** decision.

### Stage 5 — Wait for Priority Date (Visa Bulletin)
Once the I-140 is filed, the beneficiary receives a **priority date** — the date the PERM was filed with DOL. This determines their place in line for a visa number. You cannot file I-485 or complete consular processing until your priority date becomes current as shown in the monthly visa bulletin.

### Stage 6 — I-485 (Adjustment of Status) or Consular Processing
After I-140 approval and when the priority date is current:
- **If inside the U.S.:** File Form I-485 (Adjustment of Status). If filing concurrently with I-140, the beneficiary can apply for EAD (work permit) and Advance Parole while waiting.
- **If outside the U.S.:** Complete consular processing at a U.S. Embassy or consulate, then enter the U.S. and the Green Card is mailed.

## Key Warnings

- **Priority date backlogs can be severe.** Applicants from India and China face multi-year waits. Other Workers from these countries may wait **10 years or more**.
- **You are tied to the sponsoring employer** until your I-485 has been pending for 180 days, at which point AC21 portability applies and allows a job change to a same or similar role.
- **The employer cannot pass PERM costs to the employee.** The employer is legally required to pay all PERM-related costs including recruitment advertising and attorney fees.
