**Question:** Do Chinese and Indian people regret going to the USA for study or work?

**Answer:**

The answer is nuanced — it depends heavily on *when* they went and *what's happening now*. Regret has grown significantly in recent years, driven mostly by geopolitics and policy changes rather than the American experience itself.

---

**🇨🇳 Chinese Students & Workers**

- **Declining enrollment signals growing hesitation.** Chinese student enrollment in the US has fallen sharply — from a record 373,000 in 2019-20 to 277,000 in 2023-24.

- **Visa crackdowns are creating fear.** In May 2025, Secretary of State Rubio announced the revocation of visas for some Chinese students, including those in "critical fields" or with CCP ties, sending students scrambling to figure out their futures.

- **Mental health and isolation are real issues.** One Chinese returnee noted that fewer and fewer Chinese students remained around him over the years: "Some compromise everything just for a work visa, and many have mental health issues while studying abroad — they're not happy."

- **Returning home isn't easy either.** Some Chinese companies have become suspicious of returnees, with one major executive saying the company would "never use returnees because there could be spies among them" — a comment later criticized by state media.

- **Split sentiment.** A 2024 survey of nearly 1,300 Chinese students found them roughly equally split between intending to stay in the US and planning to return to China — yet most still expressed a desire to work in the US after graduation.

---

**🇮🇳 Indian Students & Workers**

- **Dominant but increasingly anxious.** Over 70% of H-1B visas issued in 2024 went to workers born in India, making them the single largest group — but also the most exposed to policy changes.

- **The $100,000 H-1B fee was a gut punch.** Stricter compliance requirements and a proposed $100,000 sponsorship fee have made employers more cautious about hiring foreign workers, while outbound migration to the US has slowed significantly — from 21,200 professionals moving to the US in 2025 to just 9,100 in early 2026.

- **Students are reconsidering before they even leave.** Stories like Paridhi Upadhaya — who was already packing her bags for a CS scholarship in the US before her family scrapped the plan after Trump's H-1B crackdown — are becoming common.

- **Those already there live in fear.** A 2025 poll of 1,584 Indian H-1B holders found that 1 in 6 had been served deportation notices within the 60-day grace period after a layoff, often in as little as two weeks. Nearly half said they would return to India if forced to leave.

- **Debt traps are a major regret driver.** Many Indian families take education loans of 50–70 lakh rupees (roughly $60,000–$85,000) to fund US education. Stricter work visa policies mean fewer high-paying jobs to repay that debt, creating serious financial anxiety.

---

**Bottom Line**

Both groups historically saw the US as a golden opportunity. That perception hasn't disappeared, but under Trump-era immigration policies (2025–present), regret and uncertainty have surged — especially around job security, visa unpredictability, and feeling politically unwelcome. Many who are already there stay out of sunk-cost logic; many newer prospects are actively choosing Europe, Canada, or staying home.
