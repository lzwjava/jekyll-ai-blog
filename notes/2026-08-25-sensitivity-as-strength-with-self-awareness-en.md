Question: How does Naval Ravikant view highly sensitive people?

Answer:

Naval does not talk much specifically about **"highly sensitive people" (HSP)** as a psychological category. His comments are usually about **anxiety, self-awareness, emotional regulation, and sensitivity to reality** rather than HSP traits. ([The Blog of Author Tim Ferriss][1])

His likely view would be something like:

### 1. Sensitivity itself is not a weakness

Naval values people who can perceive subtle things. A person who is sensitive can have advantages:

* notice patterns earlier
* understand people better
* detect dishonesty
* have deeper curiosity
* create better art/products/ideas

He often talks about "specific knowledge" — unique combinations of interests, experiences, and observations that others cannot easily copy. A sensitive person may accumulate unusual observations that become an advantage.

But sensitivity becomes harmful when the mind cannot separate:

```
input → interpretation → emotional reaction → action
```

A highly sensitive person may receive more signals, but the skill is controlling the downstream reaction.

---

### 2. Naval would probably say: don't be controlled by your emotions

One of his recurring ideas is **self-awareness**:

> "The people that I want to spend time around ... they're highly self-aware. They're not running on autopilot." ([PodClips][2])

For example:

Sensitive person:

```
Someone criticizes me
        ↓
I feel attacked
        ↓
I defend myself
        ↓
I suffer
```

Naval-style approach:

```
Someone criticizes me
        ↓
Notice the emotion
        ↓
Ask: "Is this useful information?"
        ↓
Keep useful part, discard ego reaction
```

The goal is not becoming insensitive. It is becoming **less reactive**.

---

### 3. Anxiety is wasted energy

Naval has a famous idea:

> "Imagine how effective you would be if you weren't anxious all the time." ([Podcasts - Your Podcast Transcripts][3])

A sensitive person often has a powerful simulation engine:

```
brain:
"What if this happens?"
"What if they think this?"
"What if I fail?"
```

This same ability can be useful for engineering, research, entrepreneurship:

```
simulate futures → find risks → prepare
```

But harmful if it becomes:

```
simulate futures → endless fear → no action
```

---

### 4. He prefers calm, self-aware people

Naval says he prefers people who:

* are highly self-aware
* don't get angry easily
* don't take themselves too seriously
* are not trapped by their past stories ([The Blog of Author Tim Ferriss][1])

So he would probably distinguish:

**High sensitivity + low self-awareness**

```
"I feel something strongly, therefore it must be true."
```

→ difficult person

**High sensitivity + high self-awareness**

```
"I notice many things. Let me investigate before reacting."
```

→ valuable person

---

### 5. For engineers/researchers, sensitivity can become an advantage

Many strong technical people have traits similar to HSP:

* dislike shallow explanations
* notice inconsistencies
* obsess over details
* spend years exploring one question

The danger:

```
high standards
      ↓
perfectionism
      ↓
fear of releasing
      ↓
no output
```

Naval's philosophy would push toward:

```
observe deeply
+
act quickly
+
detach from ego
```

---

My interpretation: Naval would not tell a sensitive person to "be tougher". He would probably say:

> Keep the sensitivity. Remove the suffering.

Turn:

```
sensitivity → awareness → creativity → leverage
```

instead of:

```
sensitivity → anxiety → rumination → paralysis
```
