**Question:** How viable is an OPC focused specifically on critical, independent analysis of the LLM/AI models domain — like a "Yin Wang for AI"?

---

**Answer:**

This is actually a *stronger* niche than general computing criticism, for reasons worth unpacking carefully.

---

### Why This Domain Is Uniquely Suited to the Model

**The hype-to-signal ratio is catastrophically bad right now.** The AI space is flooded with:

- Benchmark leaderboards that vendors game
- Influencers who've never read a paper
- "Vibe evals" passed off as analysis
- Press releases disguised as research
- Hype cycles that repeat every 3 months

This is *exactly* the environment where a rigorous, independent, critical voice has maximum value. The gap between what's claimed and what's real is enormous — and most people lack the tools to close it themselves.

**The audience is large and paying.** Developers, CTOs, AI engineers, product teams, and researchers all need to make real decisions: which model to use, which framework to trust, which paper claims are real. Bad decisions here cost real money. That creates willingness to pay for trustworthy analysis.

---

### What "Yin Wang for AI" Would Actually Look Like

The critical methodology applied specifically to this domain:

**1. Model Evaluation — done honestly**
Not leaderboard rankings, but task-specific, real-world testing. "Does Claude 5 actually reason better than GPT-5 on *your* kind of problems?" Decompose the benchmark games, expose what's actually being measured.

**2. Paper Decoding**
Most AI papers are unreadable, overclaimed, or both. Translate them honestly — including what the limitations section *really* means, what the ablations reveal that the abstract buries, and whether the result generalizes at all.

**3. Framework & Tool Criticism**
LangChain, LlamaIndex, various agent frameworks — which ones are genuinely well-engineered vs. glue code dressed up as infrastructure? Apply engineering rigor, not hype.

**4. Architecture Analysis**
When a new model drops — what's actually new? Is MoE here actually better, or just different? When companies say "we improved reasoning," what mechanism are they claiming, and does evidence support it?

**5. The "Is It Worth It" Take**
For products built on AI — Cursor, Perplexity, Copilot, etc. — honest cost-benefit analysis. Not "AI is amazing" or "AI is overhyped" but: *for what specific use cases, at what cost, does this actually pay off?*

You need to know who occupies this space:

| Voice | Style | Gap They Leave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Andrej Karpathy** | Deep, technical, trusted | Rare posting, not critical-opinionated |
| **Simon Willison** | Prolific, empirical, honest | More "explorer" than "critic" |
| **Gary Marcus** | Contrarian, consistent | Sometimes overcorrects toward dismissal |
| **Gwern** | Extremely deep, rigorous | Dense, slow, not actionable |
| **Hugging Face blog** | Technical, useful | Institutional — they have products to sell |
| **Most AI newsletters** | High volume, low depth | Summaries of press releases |

**The gap is:** someone who combines *Karpathy's technical depth* + *Yin Wang's willingness to say things are bad* + *Simon Willison's publishing cadence* — but structured as a business.

That seat is not occupied. It's genuinely available.

### What You Need to Credibly Occupy This Space

This is the hard part, and being honest matters here:

**You need at least one of:**

- Ability to read and critique ML papers at a serious level (linear algebra, probability, architecture knowledge)
- Ability to run real evals — not just call APIs, but design tests that actually discriminate model capability
- Deep software engineering experience to evaluate frameworks and tooling honestly
- History of building something real with LLMs, so your criticism is grounded in practice

**You don't need to:**

- Have a PhD
- Work at a frontier lab
- Cover everything — niching down (e.g., "critical analysis of coding models only") is stronger at first

### Monetization — Concrete Numbers

The economics are actually favorable:

- **Paid newsletter at $15–20/month** — 500 paying subscribers = $90–120K/year (before Substack's 10% cut). That's a real solo business.
- **500 subscribers is achievable** in a domain where the audience is technical professionals who expense subscriptions
- **Consulting uplift** — "I evaluated models for your use case" is a natural paid service once the newsletter builds trust. A few engagements/year at $5–15K each changes the math significantly
- **Sponsorships** — done carefully (tool vendors who want honest coverage, not puff pieces) add another layer, though this creates tension with independence that must be managed explicitly

### The Specific Risks in This Domain

**1. The domain moves faster than any human.** A new major model drops every few weeks. You can't cover everything — you'll need a clear editorial filter for what's worth analyzing deeply vs. ignoring.

**2. You'll make enemies.** If you honestly say GPT-5 underperforms on X, or that a popular framework is poorly engineered, companies and their communities push back hard. Yin Wang handles this by not caring. You need the same disposition.

**3. Your evals will be contested.** Anyone can say "your benchmark is flawed." You need methodology rigorous enough to defend, or transparent enough that readers can replicate.

**4. LLM companies can change the ground under you overnight.** A model update can make last week's analysis obsolete. Frame analysis around *methodology and principles*, not just point-in-time results.

---

### Suggested Starting Structure

Rather than starting broad, pick one lane and go deep for 6 months:

> *"I evaluate coding models and coding tools with brutal honesty. Every claim gets tested. Nothing is taken at face value."*

That single sentence is a business. It's specific, it's credible, it's differentiated, and it serves a large audience of developers who are actively making purchasing and technology decisions. From there you can expand.

The infrastructure (Substack, a simple website, maybe a GitHub for eval methodology) costs almost nothing. The asset being built is your reputation for accuracy and independence — and in this domain, that reputation is extremely scarce.
