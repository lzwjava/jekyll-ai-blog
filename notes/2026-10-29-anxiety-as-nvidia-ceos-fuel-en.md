**Question:** Is Jensen Huang constantly worrying?

**Answer:**

Yes — and he's said so himself, openly. Jensen Huang has admitted he works seven days a week in a "state of anxiety" over the fear that Nvidia could go bankrupt. He wakes up at 4 a.m. to read emails and says he reads several thousand emails a day — "It's exhausting," he said. "Always in a state of anxiety."

His biographer, **Stephen Witt**, who wrote *The Thinking Machine* (published January 2025), shed more light on this mindset: Witt says Huang "is almost totally driven by negative emotions" — anxiety is his "gasoline," the thing that makes him go. Witt also noted that Huang "becomes very uncomfortable and nervous when things are going well."

Huang himself has explained the psychology behind it: "I have a greater drive from not wanting to fail than the drive of wanting to succeed." He also says his resilience comes from having "low expectations," always preparing for potential failure, and strategizing how to avoid an implosion — but you just can't let anxiety prevent you from taking calculated risks.

This isn't false modesty either. He recounted a 1990s near-collapse where Nvidia was rescued by Sega converting a payment into an investment, and that early brush with failure clearly left a permanent mark on how he runs the company.

So yes — constant worry is essentially his management philosophy, and it seems to be working: Nvidia is now valued at over $3.3 trillion, and Huang ranks among the wealthiest people in the world.
