Question: What are the pros and cons of working, studying, or living in the USA (from a China-based AI founder/engineer perspective)?

Answer:

**Pros**

- **Frontier compute access** — Full-stack Nvidia (Blackwell, GB200/300) without export-control workarounds. You're on MI300X now; in the US you'd have unrestricted access to whatever's newest, not whatever AMD/domestic chip supply allows this quarter.
- **Capital density** — VC checks for AI-native companies are bigger and faster in SF than anywhere else. If "AI company" is the product, this is the market that prices it highest.
- **Talent gravity** — Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepMind, Meta FAIR are here. Casual conversations at meetups/conferences carry more signal than reading their papers 3 months later.
- **English-language distribution** — If you want a global (not just China) user base or open-source following, US presence gives you native access to HN, Twitter/X, GitHub trending — the channels that actually move AI adoption.
- **Legal/IP infrastructure** — Patents, corporate structuring (Delaware C-corp), and enforceability matter more once you're dealing with US enterprise customers or fundraising from US LPs.

**Cons — and this is the sharp one right now**

- **H-1B is functionally broken for you in 2026.** A $100K supplemental fee for petitions filed on workers abroad, wage-weighted lottery selection that deprioritizes anyone not already at senior/high-salary levels, and a 38.5% drop in FY2027 registrations as skilled workers route around the US entirely (Canada, UK, UAE). A US judge ruled the $100,000 fee unenforceable on June 8, but the government is appealing, and the policy churn itself has become the deterrent — years of shifting rules have pushed workers to consider Canada, the UK, and the Gulf instead. Employers are told to plan as if the fee remains in effect through at least September 2026.
- **You don't fit the new selection criteria as a founder-track applicant.** The system now explicitly favors high-salaried specialized talent over entry-level or campus-style hires — if you're not walking in with a big-tech offer letter at senior comp, the lottery odds get worse, not better.
- **O-1 is the realistic path, not H-1B.** Given your track record (Fun Live at scale, model training, shipped CLI agents), O-1A ("extraordinary ability") is a stronger fit than gambling on an H-1B lottery. No cap, no lottery, employer or self-petition via agent. Worth a real look before writing off the US route.
- **China-side leverage you'd give up**: faster iteration speed on regulatory/market access, domestic distribution (WeChat, existing user base from Fun Live), cheaper GPU/labor costs, and — bluntly — an AI company serving the China market is easier to run *from* China than remotely.
- **Export-control whiplash cuts both ways.** You watched Anthropic's own Fable/Mythos models get suspended and restored under Commerce Dept export rules in June–July 2026. If you're moving between US and China with a training/consulting business, you're now a direct target of exactly that kind of policy volatility — in both directions.
- **Tax and cost of living** — US federal + state (esp. CA) taxes on a profitable company are materially higher than what you're likely paying now; SF/Bay Area COL will eat into whatever margin you gain from being closer to capital.

**Bottom line for someone in your position**: the "study then H-1B then stay" pipeline that used to be the default is largely dead in 2026 — the fee and lottery redesign specifically punish that path. If you want US presence, O-1A + Delaware C-corp + raising from US VCs while keeping training/compute ops flexible (not necessarily onshore) is the more realistic 2026 play than trying to relocate as an employee. Given you already run a profitable company with real revenue and a trained model to point to, you have more O-1 ammunition than most.
