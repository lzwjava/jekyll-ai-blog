Question: Can B1/B2 visa holders (Chinese citizens or others) live in the USA for 10 years by entering every half year?

Answer:

No. A B1/B2 visa does **not** allow someone to live in the USA for 10 years. It allows **temporary visits**. ([U.S. Department of State - Travel][1])

The confusion comes from two different concepts:

1. **Visa validity**

* Many B1/B2 visas (including many Chinese citizen visas) can be valid for **10 years** and allow multiple entries. ([CBP Help Center][2])
* This means: "you can ask to enter the USA during those 10 years."
* It does **not** mean: "you can stay 10 years."

2. **Length of each stay**

* Each entry is decided by the CBP officer at the border.
* Many B1/B2 visitors are admitted for up to about **6 months**. ([CBP Help Center][3])
* Example:

```
2026 Jan: enter USA
2026 Jul: leave USA

2027 Jan: enter USA again
2027 Jul: leave USA
```

Technically, repeated visits are possible.

But if someone does:

```
USA 6 months
leave 1 month
USA 6 months
leave 1 month
repeat for years
```

CBP may decide:

> "This person is not really visiting; this person is trying to live in the USA."

They can deny entry. CBP specifically looks for whether the visitor maintains a residence abroad and whether the travel purpose is genuinely temporary. ([CBP Help Center][3])

A typical acceptable pattern:

```
Home country:
  lives, works, family, taxes

USA:
  2-3 month vacation
  visit relatives
  tourism
```

Risky pattern:

```
USA:
  10 months/year

Home country:
  almost no life
```

That starts looking like unofficial residence.

Also:

* B1/B2 holders cannot work in the USA. ([U.S. Department of State - Travel][1])
* Cannot use it as a substitute for a green card or immigrant status. ([U.S. Department of State - Travel][1])

For Chinese citizens specifically:

* A common B1/B2 arrangement is a **10-year multiple-entry visa**, but Chinese citizens with 10-year B1/B2 visas also have EVUS requirements before travel. ([CBP Help Center][2])

So the practical answer:

**Yes:** A person may visit the USA many times over 10 years.
**No:** They cannot simply "live in America for 10 years by leaving every 6 months." Frequent long stays can lead to questioning or refusal of entry.
