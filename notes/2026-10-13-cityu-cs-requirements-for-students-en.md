**Question:** What are City University of Hong Kong's (CityU) requirements for mainland China students applying to Computer Science (Bachelor's or Master's)? And can a China self-study exam (自考) associate degree be used for a 2-year top-up Bachelor's pathway?

---

**Answer:**

Here's a full breakdown for your situation. I'll be direct about where it gets tricky.

---

## 🎓 MSc Computer Science (Master's)

**Academic Requirement:**
To be eligible for admission, you must have a recognised bachelor's degree in a computing discipline such as Computer Science, Information Technology, Computer Engineering, Information Systems, or equivalent — or a related discipline with applicable working experience in IT.

> ⚠️ **Your situation:** An associate degree (自考 or otherwise) does **not** qualify for the MSc. You need a **full bachelor's degree** first. This path is not yet open to you.

**English Requirement (for MSc):**
You need either a TOEFL iBT score of 79 (for tests taken prior to 21 January 2026) or 4 (for tests from 21 January 2026 onwards), OR an overall IELTS band score of 6.0 (Academic), OR a score of 450 in the mainland's CET-6.

---

## 🎓 BSc Computer Science (Bachelor's)

**Academic Requirement:**
To be considered for admission, you must satisfy the General Entrance Requirements or have obtained an Associate Degree or Higher Diploma in engineering or a related discipline, or an equivalent qualification.

For mainland China students, CityU has two undergraduate admission routes:

- **Gaokao route:** Students taking the current year Chinese Mainland Gaokao must attain a score above the first-tier cut-off line (一本线) and an English score above 110 out of 150.
- **Non-Gaokao route:** For those with other qualifications (GCE A-levels, IB, sub-degrees, transfer from other universities, etc.)

**English Requirement (for UG):**
An IELTS overall band score of 6.5 or above (Academic Module) is required, or TOEFL iBT 4.5+ (for tests taken from 21 January 2026 onwards) / 79+ (for earlier tests).

## ⚠️ The Critical Question: Is Your 自考 Associate Degree Recognized?

This is where it gets complicated — here's an honest assessment:

| Factor | Reality |
|---|---|
| **自考 (National Self-Study Exam)** | NOT explicitly listed as an accepted qualification on CityU's mainland non-Gaokao page |
| **HK "2+2" top-up pathway** | This refers to HK's own local Associate Degree system, transferring to Year 3 of a HK university — not a mainland 自考 degree |
| **Advanced Standing II (Year 3 entry)** | Available for local students only — mainland China students are **not eligible** for this direct Year 3 entry |
| **Advanced Standing I** | Students admitted with Advanced Standing I (non-senior year) must complete a minimum of 91 credit units for graduation — you'd still need ~3 years at CityU, not 2 |
| **Degree completion** | Your 自考 is still **in progress** (5 written + 2 practical exams pending), so you can't apply yet regardless |

Students admitted with Advanced Standing will not be entitled to credit transfer on the basis of qualifications attained prior to admission. Instead, students may be granted exemption from specific courses based on prior study — but credit units are not earned for exempted courses.

## 🗺️ Realistic Pathways for You

Given where you are (self-study 自考, in progress, Computer Application), here are your actual options ranked by feasibility:

1. **Finish the 自考 first** — you have 5 written + 2 practical exams left; complete it as soon as possible
2. **Contact CityU admissions directly** to ask if a completed 自考 associate degree qualifies under "equivalent qualifications" — this is genuinely ambiguous and only they can confirm
3. **Consider a Hong Kong Associate Degree (HK AD)** — programs at institutions affiliated with HKU, CUHK, or Lingnan. A HK AD is explicitly recognized for Year 3 entry (for local students) and Advanced Standing I (for all)
4. **Full mainland bachelor's (4-year)** — A formal full-time bachelor's in CS from a mainland university is the cleanest path to either CityU's BSc Year 1 Advanced Standing I, or eventually the MSc
