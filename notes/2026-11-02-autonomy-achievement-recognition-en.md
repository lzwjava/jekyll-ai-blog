## 2. I think the analysis overemphasizes injustice

There is a subtle danger in this narrative.

It interprets almost every difficult experience as an instance of the same underlying injustice.

- Companies reject you because of credentials.
- Your father corrects you because he doesn't understand you.
- The housing market creates financial pressure.
- Visa systems restrict your opportunities.
- Society doesn't recognize your abilities.

Some of these interpretations may be accurate.

But I would be careful about turning them into a universal explanation.

For example:

Your startup failure wasn't primarily an injustice. It was a business execution failure.

You already recognize this in your writing: hiring too quickly, losing focus, experimenting without sufficient discipline.

That experience is valuable precisely because you were responsible for some of the outcome.

Likewise, not getting into YC doesn't necessarily mean the system failed to recognize your genius. It means the application wasn't successful.

The distinction matters because:

- Injustice encourages you to demand a different system.
- Personal responsibility encourages you to build a better strategy.

You need both perspectives.

But the second one gives you more control.

Consider two versions of yourself.

Version A:

I don't care about society's expectations. I'll define my own success. I'll build what I believe is important.

Version B:

I want to become internationally recognized. I want people to acknowledge my technical abilities. I want to demonstrate that dropping out wasn't a mistake.

Both are understandable.

You might spend another ten years trying to prove something to people who aren't particularly important to your future.

Build things because they deserve to exist, not because their existence proves something about you.

If you build an excellent LLM training system, an open-source project, a successful company, or a useful developer community, recognition might follow.

But recognition should be a consequence, not the objective function.
