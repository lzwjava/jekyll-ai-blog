**Question:** What does Liu Weipeng say about "dark time" or productive guide in his Chinese book?

**Answer:** 

Liu Weipeng (刘未鹏), in his Chinese book **《暗时间》** (Dark Time / An Shi Jian, also translated as Hidden Time or Dark Time: Thinking Changes Life), introduces and elaborates on the core concept of **"暗时间" (dark time)**.

"Dark time" refers to the periods of time in daily life that are not directly used for focused, visible work or "bright time" (明时间), but can still be utilized for thinking, learning, and mental growth. These are the seemingly idle or unstructured moments—such as walking, commuting, waiting in line, washing dishes, taking a shower, eating alone, or other routine activities—when your mind is free but often wasted on autopilot or low-value distractions.

Key points Liu Weipeng makes about dark time:

- He argues that true lifespan or life quality should be measured not just by calendar years, but by the effective **thinking time** accumulated. "Dark time" is essentially **mental/unused thinking time** that compounds into personal growth if deliberately harnessed.
- The gap between people (especially in cognitive ability, expertise, and productivity) often comes less from the obvious working hours and more from how individuals exploit these hidden fragments of time for reflection, problem-solving, reading mentally, or building mental models.
- To make dark time productive, one must train the habit of **deliberate thinking** during these periods instead of letting the mind wander aimlessly or default to entertainment. This turns "wasted" time into invisible but powerful investment in self-improvement.
- He connects this to broader productivity and learning principles: minimizing mental context switches (like process switches in computing), improving focus and learning density, designing personal "progress bars" for motivation, effective memory techniques, and applying insights from psychology, cognitive science, and neuroscience to optimize thinking habits.

The book is not a step-by-step "productive guide" in the typical self-help sense but a collection of essays (originally from his blog and articles) offering a mindset framework. It guides readers on **how to think better, learn more efficiently, and reclaim fragmented time** to change life through superior mental habits. Later editions (e.g., 2nd edition) expand on reflective training, better mental models, and efficient learning methods.

In essence, Liu Weipeng views dark time as a hidden multiplier for productivity: masters of it "steal" extra years of growth by thinking deeply in moments others ignore.
