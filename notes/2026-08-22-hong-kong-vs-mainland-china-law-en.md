**Question:** What are the key legal differences between Hong Kong and mainland China?

**Answer:**

Hong Kong operates under the **"One Country, Two Systems"** framework, which gives it a separate legal system from mainland China — though this autonomy has been narrowing significantly since 2020.

---

### Areas Where Hong Kong Law Still Differs

**1. Legal System Foundation**
Hong Kong uses a **common law** system inherited from British rule, with independent courts and the right of final appeal. Mainland China uses a **civil law / socialist legal** system where the Communist Party plays a direct role in judicial decisions.

**2. Trade & Customs**
Hong Kong is a separate customs union, sets its own monetary and fiscal policies, retains an open capital account (unlike mainland China), and maintains an open trade regime with negligible tariff or non-tariff barriers.

**3. Financial Regulation**
The Hong Kong Monetary Authority sets monetary policy autonomously from the People's Bank of China, and Hong Kong's financial regulators continue to act independently.

**4. Internet Access**
Key differences remain in internet access — Hong Kong does not enforce the "Great Firewall," meaning Google, Facebook, and other platforms blocked in mainland China are accessible.

**5. Freedom of Religion**
Freedom of religion remains a key difference between Hong Kong and mainland China.

---

### Areas Where the Gap Has Narrowed

**6. National Security Law (2020)**
In 2020, Beijing imposed a sweeping national security law on Hong Kong. In the years since, authorities have arrested dozens of pro-democracy activists, lawmakers, and journalists; curbed voting rights; and limited freedoms of press and speech.

**7. Article 23 (2024)**
In March 2024, Hong Kong lawmakers unanimously passed another sweeping security law, known as Article 23, which broadens the scope and definition of political crimes, targets "external interference" and theft of state secrets, and prohibits foreign political organizations from conducting political activities in Hong Kong.

**8. Mandatory Reporting**
Like their counterparts in mainland China, Hong Kong residents are now legally compelled to inform authorities when they suspect someone of committing an infraction. The two laws now operate in tandem, giving the PRC a comprehensive legal architecture to govern national security in Hong Kong.

---

### Summary Table

| Area | Hong Kong | Mainland China |
|---|---|---|
| Legal tradition | Common law | Civil/Socialist law |
| Internet | Open | Firewall restricted |
| Trade | Free port, separate customs | Controlled, tariffs |
| Currency/Monetary | Autonomous (HKD) | PBoC controlled (CNY) |
| National security | NSL + Article 23 | Similar laws |
| Political dissent | Increasingly restricted | Tightly restricted |

The gap between the two systems continues to shrink, particularly on national security and political freedom matters.
