## 1. Why I think this is a real opportunity

There is an interesting structural change happening in the AI industry.

Historically, doing serious AI research required:

- Large research teams.
- Expensive GPU clusters.
- Significant institutional funding.
- Years of infrastructure development.

Now, an individual engineer can:

- Train small models from scratch.
- Fine-tune larger open models.
- Experiment on rented H100/H200/MI300X.
- Build inference engines and evaluation pipelines.
- Use coding agents to accelerate implementation.
- Publish research, code, models and benchmarks globally.

The cost of conducting useful experiments has fallen dramatically.

But the important opportunity isn't necessarily training another foundation model.

It's becoming an independent research-and-engineering organization that can solve problems other companies cannot easily solve themselves.

## 5. What I would change about the current Truth Lab

The website has a good foundation. But I would make three improvements.

A. Make the specialization more obvious.

Currently, it covers training, inference, hardware and consulting. That's intellectually coherent, but customers might not immediately understand what to hire you for.

I would lead with:

> Independent AI Research & Engineering\
> We train models, optimize inference, build AI infrastructure, and solve difficult engineering problems. Every claim backed by experiments.

B. Publish deeper technical research.

Don't just publish that you trained a 760M GPT-2.

Publish:

- Training throughput and GPU utilization.
- Scaling behavior.
- Loss curves and dataset composition.
- Memory consumption.
- Cost per billion tokens.
- Comparison against alternative implementations.

Build a reputation for reliable engineering data.

C. Build one flagship product.

For example, an open-source inference benchmarking and optimization toolkit.

It could eventually become a commercial product.

A lab becomes much more valuable when its research produces something other people repeatedly use.

## 7. One important warning

Don't confuse having a research lab with doing research that matters.

For example:

- Training GPT-2 124M is a useful learning experiment.
- Training GPT-2 760M is a more substantial engineering exercise.
- But neither automatically establishes a competitive research advantage.

The next step is to find problems where you can make a measurable contribution.

I would rather see you spend three months improving inference throughput by 30% for a real workload than spend three months training another model without a meaningful research question.

Your advantage isn't that you own 52 GB of VRAM.

It's that you can understand the entire stack, from data and model training to CUDA, inference, hardware and production software.

That's a valuable combination.
