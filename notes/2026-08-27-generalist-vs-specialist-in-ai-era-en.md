Question: In the AI era, is it better to be a generalist or specialist — and does curiosity-driven, broad learning actually lead to growth?

Answer:

Your English learning analogy is sharp. Diverse inputs — novels, news, podcasts, random articles — all reinforce the same underlying system. The learning looks scattered but it compounds. The same dynamic applies to tech curiosity across hardware, software, MIS, and adjacent fields.

**On the generalist vs. specialist debate in the AI era:**

The honest answer is that the dichotomy is becoming less meaningful. What's emerging is a third model — sometimes called a **"T-shaped"** or **"π-shaped"** person:

- **Vertical bar** = depth in one or two areas (your anchor)
- **Horizontal bar** = broad curiosity and ability to connect ideas across domains

AI is rapidly commoditizing narrow specialist knowledge. If your only value is knowing one tool or one system deeply, AI can replicate much of that. But **AI struggles to connect dots across domains, navigate ambiguity, and apply judgment in novel contexts** — which is exactly what broad curious learners develop over years.

**Why your pattern of learning is actually an advantage:**

- Cross-domain curiosity builds **mental models**, not just facts
- Exposure to hardware *and* software *and* MIS creates **systems thinking** — you understand how pieces interact, which is rare
- Generalists often become the people who **translate between specialists** — an increasingly valuable role as teams grow more technical

**The risk to watch:**

Breadth without any anchor can leave you feeling like you know a little about everything but excel at nothing. The goal is to let curiosity roam freely, but *notice* where your energy concentrates naturally — that's often where your real depth wants to form.

**For you specifically:**

Someone curious across hardware, software, and MIS in the AI era is well-positioned — not despite the breadth, but because of it. The people who will thrive are those who can ask *"how does this connect to that?"* — and you're already wired that way.

So yes — keep going. A year of curious, broad engagement compounds more than it looks like it does from the inside.
