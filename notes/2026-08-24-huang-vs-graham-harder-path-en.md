Question: Between Jensen Huang and Paul Graham, who deserves more respect — and is Jensen Huang's path harder?

Answer: Both are genuinely impressive, but in different ways — this is less a ranking and more a contrast in what kind of excellence you value.

**The case for Jensen Huang being harder:**
- Built NVIDIA from a scrappy chip startup into a $3+ trillion company, navigating brutal semiconductor competition for 30+ years
- Made an enormous, decade-long bet on GPU computing and AI infrastructure that most people thought was wrong — and turned out to be right at civilization scale
- Running a massive organization (tens of thousands of employees), managing supply chains, hardware cycles, geopolitics, and staying at the frontier of engineering simultaneously
- The stakes of failure at each stage were existential — one bad chip generation and NVIDIA could have gone bankrupt

**The case for Paul Graham:**
- YC has funded Airbnb, Dropbox, Stripe, Reddit, and hundreds of others — his pattern recognition for early-stage startups is arguably unmatched
- His essays shaped how an entire generation thinks about startups, technology, and building things
- Being a great essayist *and* a great investor *and* a technical founder is its own rare combination

**The honest take:**
Jensen Huang's achievement is probably *harder in scale* — building and sustaining a hardware company at that level of ambition involves more moving parts, more capital at risk, and more ways to fail. Paul Graham's influence is arguably *deeper per person* — he changed how founders think, which has compounding cultural effects.

If "harder" means degree of difficulty in execution, Jensen Huang has a strong claim. But "deserves more respect" is a values question — not everyone measures respect by company market cap.
