**Question:** How do people in China earn their first million (万元/百万)?

---

**Answer:**

China's path to wealth has its own distinct flavors — shaped by three decades of rapid economic reform, a unique property market, state-linked industries, and explosive tech growth. Here's how Chinese millionaires typically made their money:

---

### 🏦 Who Are China's Millionaires? (By Source of Wealth)

According to the Hurun Report and related studies, more than half (55%) of Chinese millionaires started their own businesses. Of the rest, 10% made their fortunes from the stock market, 15% from real estate, and 20% are highly paid executives.

Among the super-rich (100M+ RMB), some 80% own their own firms, 15% made their money from real estate, and another 5% made their fortune in stocks.

---

### 🌊 The Historic "Waves" of Chinese Wealth

China's millionaires tend to trace back to one of several historical economic waves:

**Wave 1 — Reform Era Profiteers (1980s)**
In the 1980s, upstart "profiteers" made their fortunes by purchasing low-priced goods and reselling them at a higher value. Early market liberalization created huge arbitrage opportunities for the bold.

**Wave 2 — Manufacturing (1990s–2000s)**
Other waves include those who were successful in manufacturing — the "labor faction" — who built wealth supplying China's booming factory economy. Among Chinese billionaires, around 20% made their wealth from the manufacturing sector — far above the global average of 8.5%.

**Wave 3 — Real Estate Boom (1998–2020s)**
Local governments and real estate companies created a system together to reap profits. The wealth redistribution from this super real estate bubble created an unprecedented amount of wealth. Today, nearly half of Chinese billionaires are in the real estate business. On Hurun China Rich Lists, more than one-quarter of the 1,000 wealthiest Chinese got rich from real estate.

**Wave 4 — Internet & Tech (2000s–2020s)**
For decades, wealth in China was dominated by real estate, but the new elite increasingly comes from innovative and productive sectors such as technology, digital finance, and advanced manufacturing. The rise of e-commerce platforms and digital payment systems created entirely new industries within just a decade. Jack Ma (Alibaba), Pony Ma (Tencent), and Huang Zheng (Pinduoduo) are the most famous examples.

---

### 🛤️ The 4 Main Paths for Ordinary Chinese Today

| Path | How | Typical Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| **Entrepreneur** | Build a business (manufacturing, e-commerce, services) | 5–15 years |
| **Real Estate** | Buy property early in tier-1/2 cities, ride price appreciation | 10–20 years |
| **Stock Market** | Invest in A-shares, funds, or IPOs | Variable (high risk) |
| **Corporate Climber** | Rise to senior executive at SOEs or major private firms | 15–25 years |

---

### ⚠️ The China-Specific Context

China's Gini coefficient hovers around 0.46–0.47, and wealth Gini exceeds 0.7. Roughly 300 million Chinese live on under ¥1,000 a month (~$140), a demographic that struggles to benefit from the country's rapid urbanization and technological advances.

This matters because wealth-building in China is heavily geography-dependent. Areas with greater technological innovation, commerce, and real estate development host most of the country's major fortunes — Guangdong (298,000 millionaires), Beijing (296,500), Shanghai (263,000), and Zhejiang (213,000).

Most importantly, just 2% of China's billionaires gained their wealth through inheritance — the vast majority are self-made, driven by massive growth in technology, consumer retail, and real estate sectors over the past few decades.

---

### 🔑 Key Takeaway

Unlike the West where the "Saver-Investor" path is very accessible, China's first generation of millionaires overwhelmingly built wealth through **entrepreneurship + real estate**. The new generation is increasingly doing it through **tech startups, e-commerce, and advanced manufacturing** — particularly in EVs, AI, and green energy. The window from the old real estate wave is largely closed; the new opportunity lies in the tech and innovation economy.
