Question: What is ADD, and what does “dong dong zheng” refer to?

Answer:

**ADD** usually means **Attention Deficit Disorder**. Today it is generally considered a presentation of **ADHD (Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder)**, specifically the **predominantly inattentive presentation**.

The core idea:

* Brain has difficulty regulating **attention, working memory, and task switching**
* Not necessarily "lack of attention" — often **attention is hard to control**
* People may have:

  * trouble starting boring tasks
  * procrastination despite knowing consequences
  * forgetting appointments/things
  * losing track during conversations
  * difficulty organizing long projects
  * jumping between ideas
  * hyperfocus on interesting topics

A common misconception:

> ADHD people cannot focus.

Actually many can focus extremely deeply on things they find interesting (sometimes called **hyperfocus**). The issue is **attention regulation**, not attention ability.

A simplified neuroscience view:

* The prefrontal cortex controls:

  * planning
  * inhibition
  * prioritization
  * working memory
* ADHD is associated with differences in dopamine/norepinephrine signaling in these networks.
* The brain has a harder time assigning "importance signals" to low-reward tasks.

A simple model:

```
Normal brain:

Task importance
      |
      v
Prefrontal cortex
      |
      v
Start task -> maintain effort -> finish


ADHD brain:

Task importance
      |
      v
"Reward signal too weak"
      |
      v
Need stronger stimulation:
  deadline
  novelty
  urgency
  passion
      |
      v
Can suddenly enter hyperfocus
```

For **“dong dong zheng” (东东症?)**:

This phrase is not a standard medical term. It might be:

1. **“多动症” (duō dòng zhèng)**

   * The common Chinese term people use for ADHD.
   * Literally means "hyperactivity disorder."
   * Many people casually say "多动症" when they mean ADHD.

2. A typo/transliteration of something else.

If you mean **“dong dong zheng = 多动症”**, then:

* ADD ≈ ADHD inattentive type
* 多动症 usually refers broadly to ADHD, especially the hyperactive/impulsive side.

ADHD has three common presentations:

| Type                  | Main symptoms                       |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Inattentive (old ADD) | distracted, forgetful, disorganized |
| Hyperactive/impulsive | restless, interrupts, acts quickly  |
| Combined              | both                                |

Many high-performing engineers, researchers, founders, and creatives describe ADHD-like traits because some traits can be advantageous:

Positive tendencies:

* curiosity
* rapid idea generation
* intense interest-driven learning
* willingness to explore new areas

Challenges:

* finishing boring work
* maintaining routines
* administrative tasks
* long-term consistency

A diagnosis requires professional assessment; being energetic, creative, or distracted sometimes does not automatically mean ADHD.
