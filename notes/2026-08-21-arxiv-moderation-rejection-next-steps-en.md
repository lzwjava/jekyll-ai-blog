**Question:** How should I respond to an arXiv moderation rejection, and what are my options going forward?

**Answer:**

Getting rejected by arXiv moderation is frustrating but manageable. Here are your options:

**Option 1: Submit to a Peer-Reviewed Journal First**
The email itself hints at the clearest path forward. If your paper gets accepted in a conventional journal, you can appeal with a DOI or journal link. Good venues depending on your field:

- IEEE, ACM, Springer, Elsevier journals
- Field-specific journals (PLOS ONE, Nature Communications, etc.)

**Option 2: File an Appeal Directly**
If you believe the rejection was a mistake (e.g., wrong category, formatting issues):

1. Go to <https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/appeals.html>
2. Write a clear, professional appeal explaining why your work fits arXiv's scope
3. Include any endorsements from established researchers in your field

**Option 3: Get an Endorsement**
arXiv sometimes rejects submissions from authors without a track record. Ask a colleague who already has arXiv papers to **endorse you** for your subject category.

**Option 4: Resubmit to the Correct Category**
Sometimes moderation rejects papers placed in the wrong subject area. Double-check that your submission matches the right category (cs.AI, math.ST, etc.).

**Option 5: Post Elsewhere in the Meantime**

- **Zenodo** (zenodo.org) — free, gives you a DOI
- **SSRN** — popular for economics/social sciences
- **ResearchGate** — for visibility
- **OSF Preprints** — open science framework

**Practical Tips for Your Appeal Letter:**

- Be respectful and concise
- Clearly state your paper's contribution and why it's appropriate for arXiv
- Reference similar accepted papers in your target category
- Do *not* argue emotionally — focus on the academic merit
