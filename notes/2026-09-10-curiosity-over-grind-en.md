Question: Do top AI researchers like Andrej Karpathy tend to have a relaxed, curiosity-driven lifestyle with hobbies, rather than grinding — and does that mindset make learning easier?

Answer:

Yes, and it's one of the most underrated insights about how elite technical people actually operate.

**They follow genuine curiosity, not discipline**

Andrej Karpathy builds neural nets from scratch *for fun* on weekends, does his "Zero to Hero" series because he genuinely enjoys teaching, and explored Bitcoin/blockchain as a hobby project. Su Jianliang (the Kimi/Moonshot guy, and rope author) similarly tinkers in ways that feel more like play than work. These aren't "side hustles" in the hustle-culture sense — they're just curious people following interesting threads.

**The relaxed state is actually optimal for learning**

This isn't accidental. There's solid neuroscience behind it:

- **Low cortisol = better memory consolidation.** Stress actively impairs the hippocampus.
- **Diffuse thinking mode** (relaxed, wandering) is when the brain makes unexpected connections — the "shower thought" effect, but sustained.
- **Intrinsic motivation** produces deeper encoding than external pressure. You remember what you *wanted* to learn.

**What this looks like in practice for them**

- Karpathy: slow, deliberate re-implementations (micrograd, makemore) — not rushing to ship, just understanding deeply
- Tinkering with Arduino, astronomy, embeddings etc. keeps the brain in a *playful problem-solving* state that transfers back to serious work
- They write and teach publicly not for clout, but because explaining forces clarity

**The trap most people fall into**

Grinding feels productive but often produces shallow, anxious learning. The relaxed experts look "inefficient" but are actually compounding curiosity at a sustainable rate over decades.

> The pattern: **follow what's genuinely interesting → go deep → don't force it → repeat for 10+ years**

That's basically the whole secret.
