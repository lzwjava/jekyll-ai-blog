Question: For ordinary Chinese citizens, isn't the more practical “road problem” simply how to live a good life?

Answer: **Yes.** At the individual level, you can translate the abstract “道路问题” into a much more concrete question:

> **What kind of society and life should I choose so that I and my family can live well, safely, freely, and with dignity?**

That naturally includes:

* **Economic security** — a good job, income, savings, housing, and the ability to become wealthier.
* **Freedom** — being able to choose your work, relationships, residence, beliefs, lifestyle, and opinions within the law.
* **Safety** — physical security, predictable laws, protection of property, and protection from arbitrary abuse of power.
* **Family** — being able to support your spouse, children, and parents and give your children good opportunities.
* **Personal development** — education, skills, meaningful work, and the freedom to pursue what you're good at.
* **Dignity and autonomy** — having meaningful control over your own life rather than merely being an instrument of some larger organization.
* **A functioning society** — infrastructure, healthcare, education, courts, public order, etc., that make individual life easier rather than harder.

And **“taking care of yourself” is not selfish in the negative sense**. At the individual level, it is the foundation:

```text
Take care of yourself
        ↓
Become capable / productive
        ↓
Earn money + build assets
        ↓
Take care of family
        ↓
Give children opportunities
        ↓
Contribute to society
```

The interesting philosophical distinction is that **the CCP's “道路问题” and an individual's “道路问题” are asking questions at different levels**.

```text
国家层面:
“What institutional/developmental road should China take?”

                  ↓

个人层面:
“What kind of life should I live?”

                  ↓

Practical questions:
How do I earn?
How do I stay safe?
How do I preserve freedom?
How do I raise my children?
How do I build wealth?
How do I maintain dignity?
```

These two levels **can overlap, but they are not identical**.

A government may define “national success” using things like GDP, technological capability, national power, stability, or geopolitical strength. An individual may define success much more simply:

> **“My family is safe. I have a good job. I have enough money. My children have opportunities. I can make important decisions about my own life. I can live with dignity.”**

That's a perfectly coherent way to think about society.

In fact, I would put the individual version of the “road problem” even more fundamentally:

> **Don't start with “Which ideology is correct?” Start with “What outcomes do I actually value, and which institutions reliably produce those outcomes?”**

Then you can evaluate political and economic systems **instrumentally**, rather than treating the system itself as the ultimate objective.

For example:

```text
Freedom ────────┐
Safety ─────────┤
Wealth ─────────┤
Family stability ├──→ What institutions produce these?
Opportunity ────┤
Dignity ────────┘
```

That is a very different starting point from asking *“Which road is politically correct?”*

And there is an important second-order question: **what happens when the interests of the individual, family, society, and state conflict?** That is where political philosophy becomes much more interesting.
