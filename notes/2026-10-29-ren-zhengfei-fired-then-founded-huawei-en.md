**Question:** How did Huawei's founder Ren Zhengfei start Huawei — and is it true he was unemployed and got fired before that?

**Answer:**

Yes — your instinct is essentially right. Ren Zhengfei's path to founding Huawei went through two major setbacks before he ever got started.

**1. Military career → forced out**

Before Huawei, Ren Zhengfei served in the PLA's engineering corps. He rose to become the deputy director of a small construction research institute, reaching the equivalent rank of deputy regimental chief. He was demobilized in 1983 when the government disbanded the entire engineering corps. So he didn't quit — the whole unit was dissolved and everyone was pushed out.

**2. State-owned company → got fired**

After the military, Ren worked in the logistics service base of the Shenzhen South Sea Oil Corporation. This is where the "fired" part you're thinking of comes in. In his own words from a CNBC interview: "I couldn't really get what the market economy was about, so I ended up making a mistake while working for a state-owned company which got me let go. Where was I supposed to go from there? I had no idea."

A Huawei statement adds more detail: he was a deputy manager in a small state-owned enterprise when someone offered to sell them TVs — they paid but never received the TVs in return. Because he hadn't done a good job there, he wrote a letter of guarantee but was refused further employment.

**3. Founded Huawei out of desperation**

In 1987, the 43-year-old former military engineer founded Huawei with the equivalent of $5,000. This wasn't strategic minimalism — it was all Ren and his co-investors could scrape together. The company started as a reseller of telephone equipment imported from Hong Kong, but later started developing its own technology.

So to directly answer: yes, he was effectively forced out of the military, then got fired from a state-owned company after a business mishap, and started Huawei at 43 with almost nothing. He's said he had suicidal thoughts in those dark early days.
