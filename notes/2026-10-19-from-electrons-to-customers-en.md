## 3. AI changes how much one person can explore

Previously, studying hardware, systems software, AI, product design, and business deeply enough to build across them could require a large team.

AI lowers the cost of learning, prototyping, and implementation. It can help you:

- Read unfamiliar codebases and technical papers.
- Write Verilog and simulate simple digital circuits.
- Implement a small compiler, operating-system component, or neural network.
- Understand GPU kernels and profile inference workloads.
- Prototype hardware-control software and agent workflows.
- Research markets, analyze customer feedback, and build products faster.

But AI does not eliminate the need for judgment. Hardware still needs measurement; generated code still needs testing; and customer demand still needs validation.

A good loop is:

Learn the principle → build the smallest implementation → measure it → inspect failure cases → explain what you learned → use it in a real product.

That is how you turn AI into a force multiplier for your own engineering ability.

## 4. A practical learning strategy for you

I would use three parallel tracks rather than studying every subject sequentially.

### Track A — Follow the frontier

LLMs, agent systems, GPU inference, training infrastructure, and new AI capabilities.

Primary objective: maintain your edge and identify emerging opportunities.

### Track B — Understand the foundations

C/C++, Python, Linux, networking, computer architecture, electronics, and digital design.

Primary objective: build a durable mental model that survives changes in frameworks and models.

### Track C — Ship products

AI consulting, Fun Live, developer tools, customer discovery, product design, sales, and team building.

Primary objective: convert knowledge into revenue, relationships, and ownership.

Use projects to connect the tracks. For example, build a small inference server, benchmark it on your local GPU, expose it through an API, create an agent that uses it, and find a real user who needs the result. One project can teach you architecture, systems, AI, product engineering, and business simultaneously.

My rule would be: go broad in understanding, go deep where the opportunity is, and keep shipping.
