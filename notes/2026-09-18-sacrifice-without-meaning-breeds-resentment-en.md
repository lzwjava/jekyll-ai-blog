Question: If people force themselves to do things they don't love for years and keep complaining, what usually happens?

Answer:

If someone spends years doing something they strongly dislike while constantly resisting it internally, several patterns often appear:

### 1. Motivation becomes external only

At first, they may have a clear reason:

* money
* status
* family expectations
* security
* avoiding failure

But after years, the internal connection can weaken. They are no longer moving toward something; they are mainly escaping something.

The mindset changes from:

> "I am building something."

to:

> "I am surviving another day."

---

### 2. Chronic frustration accumulates

Complaining is often a signal that there is a repeated mismatch:

```
Reality:
"I spend 8-10 hours doing this."

Inner preference:
"I don't want this."

Repeated every day:
=> psychological friction
```

A person can tolerate short-term pain for a meaningful goal. For example:

* an athlete training hard
* a founder working long hours
* a researcher struggling with difficult problems

The difference is that they believe the pain is connected to something they value.

Pain without meaning is much harder to sustain.

---

### 3. They may become cynical

After years, some people start protecting themselves psychologically:

* "Nothing can change."
* "Everyone hates their job."
* "Dreams are unrealistic."
* "People who enjoy work are lucky."

Sometimes this is a defense mechanism: if they convince themselves that everything is bad, they don't have to face the possibility that they should change.

---

### 4. Skill growth can still happen

It is not always negative.

Many people do things they don't love but become excellent because:

* they see a bigger purpose
* they enjoy mastery
* they like the people/environment
* the reward is worth the sacrifice

For example, a doctor may dislike paperwork but love helping patients. A software engineer may dislike meetings but love building systems.

The question is usually not:

> "Do I love every part?"

Almost nobody does.

The better question is:

> "Is the overall direction worth the cost?"

---

### 5. The biggest danger is losing the ability to choose

After many years, people can become trapped by:

* lifestyle inflation
* fear of starting over
* identity ("I am this kind of person")
* sunk cost ("I already spent 10 years")

The person may continue not because they choose it, but because leaving feels impossible.

---

A useful model:

```
Short-term sacrifice + meaningful direction
        => growth

Long-term sacrifice + no meaning
        => resentment
```

Humans can endure very difficult things when they believe the difficulty serves a purpose. The same difficulty can become destructive when it feels meaningless.
