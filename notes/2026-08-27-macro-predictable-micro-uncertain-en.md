Question: Even with AI, is the future still fundamentally uncertain at the micro level, even if macro trends are predictable?

Answer:

Yes, exactly right. There's a useful distinction here:

**Macro trends** (predictable with reasonable confidence)

- Computing power will increase
- AI capabilities will broadly improve
- Energy demand will grow
- Populations will age in developed nations

These follow structural forces — economics, physics, demographics — that are slow-moving and well-understood.

**Micro events** (largely unpredictable)

- A specific researcher has a breakthrough insight tomorrow
- A random traffic accident kills someone who would have invented the next transformer architecture
- A GPU fab in Taiwan gets hit by an earthquake
- One paper changes the entire direction of AI research overnight

This is actually a well-studied idea. Nassim Taleb calls the impactful unpredictable micro events **"Black Swans"** — low probability, high consequence, and only explainable *after* the fact. The transformer architecture itself (2017, "Attention Is All You Need") was such an event — almost nobody predicted it would reshape the entire field within 5 years.

**Why AI doesn't fix this:**

- AI trains on *past* data — it can extrapolate trends, but cannot predict genuinely novel events by definition
- Human creativity and accidents are not stochastic noise around a predictable mean — they *are* the signal sometimes
- Complex systems (markets, science, geopolitics) have chaotic properties where tiny inputs cause massive divergent outcomes

**The honest model of the future:**

| Layer | Predictability |
| --- | --- |
| Physical laws | Very high |
| Demographic trends | High |
| Technology direction | Moderate |
| Specific inventions | Low |
| Individual accidents/decisions | Near zero |

So yes — AI gives us better macro forecasting tools, but the micro-level uncertainty isn't a bug we can engineer away. It's intrinsic to how complex systems and human agency work.
