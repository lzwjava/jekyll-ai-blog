Answer:

The correct answer is **B. Henry Thoreau (亨利·梭罗)**.

**Detailed explanation:**

The text 《蚂蚁大战》 ("The Battle of the Ants") is a famous excerpt from Henry David Thoreau's book *Walden* (《瓦尔登湖》), specifically from the chapter "Brute Neighbors" (《禽兽邻居》). In this passage, Thoreau vividly describes an epic-like battle between two groups of ants (red ants and black ants) that he observed near his cabin at Walden Pond. He uses mock-heroic language to compare the ant war to human wars, giving it both humor and philosophical depth.

This piece is one of the most well-known and frequently anthologized sections of *Walden*. It is definitely written by **Henry David Thoreau** (1817–1862), the American transcendentalist writer, not by any of the other authors listed.

- **D. O. Henry (欧·亨利)**: American short-story writer famous for surprise endings (e.g., "The Gift of the Magi"). His works are mostly urban stories with ironic twists, not nature observations.
