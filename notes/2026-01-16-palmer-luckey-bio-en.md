**Palmer Luckey** (full name: Palmer Freeman Luckey) is an American entrepreneur, engineer, and inventor born on September 19, 1992, in Long Beach, California. He is best known for founding **Oculus VR** and designing the **Oculus Rift**, a virtual reality headset that played a major role in reviving the modern VR industry.

Luckey, who was homeschooled, developed an early passion for electronics, gaming, and technology. As a teenager, he began building VR prototypes in his parents' garage (and later a camper trailer), experimenting with components like lenses and sensors. In 2012, at age 19, he launched Oculus VR and started a successful Kickstarter campaign for the Oculus Rift, which gained support from figures like Valve's Gabe Newell. In 2014, Facebook (now Meta) acquired Oculus for approximately $2 billion in cash and stock, making Luckey a billionaire in his early 20s.

He worked at Facebook/Oculus until 2017, when he left the company amid controversy related to a political donation supporting Donald Trump. Following his departure, Luckey founded **Anduril Industries** in 2017, a defense technology company focused on autonomous systems, AI-powered surveillance, drones, and advanced military hardware. Anduril aims to modernize the U.S. military by disrupting traditional defense contracting with software-driven, modular, and rapidly producible technologies.

As of late 2025, Anduril is valued at around $30.5 billion, and Luckey serves as its founder (with a net worth estimated by Forbes at approximately $3.5 billion). The company has secured major contracts with the U.S. Department of Defense, including work on counter-drone systems, border security sensors, and the Army's Integrated Visual Augmentation System (IVAS) program in collaboration with Meta. Anduril's products include systems like the Lattice software platform, Roadrunner drones, and various autonomous vehicles.

Luckey is also known for his eccentric personal style (often seen in Hawaiian shirts, sandals, and an '80s mullet), libertarian views, interest in retro gaming (he founded ModRetro and released the Chromatic handheld in 2024), and strong advocacy for American technological and military superiority.

He remains an influential and polarizing figure in tech and defense, blending consumer innovation roots with high-stakes national security work.
