Question: How to perform well in onsite interviews or customer meetings, and how to build confidence?

Answer:

## Before the Meeting / Interview

**Research thoroughly**
- Know the company's products, recent news, culture, and pain points
- For customer meetings: understand their industry, challenges, and what they've bought or are considering
- Prepare 3–5 sharp questions that show you've done your homework

**Prepare your stories**
- Use the **STAR method** (Situation → Task → Action → Result) for behavioral questions
- Have 5–7 versatile stories ready that can be adapted to different questions
- Quantify results wherever possible ("reduced costs by 30%", "closed a $200K deal")

**Rehearse out loud**
- Saying things aloud is very different from thinking them — practice with a friend or record yourself
- Time your answers: 1–2 minutes per behavioral question is ideal

---

## During the Onsite / Meeting

**First impressions (first 30 seconds matter)**
- Arrive 10–15 min early, relax outside before going in
- Firm handshake, smile, make eye contact
- Use the person's name once early in conversation

**Active listening**
- Don't rush to answer — pause for 2–3 seconds to think
- If a question is unclear, ask for clarification: *"Could you tell me more about what you mean by X?"*
- Take notes (it shows respect and helps you respond accurately)

**Handling tough questions**
- It's okay to say: *"That's a great question — let me think for a moment"*
- For things you don't know: *"I don't have that answer off the top of my head, but here's how I'd approach finding it…"*
- Never bluff — interviewers and customers can usually tell

**For customer meetings specifically**
- Focus on their problems, not your product features ("So what I'm hearing is…")
- Use the **listen 70%, talk 30%** rule
- Always end with clear next steps: *"What would a good next step look like for you?"*

## Building Genuine Confidence

**Before the day:**
- Power pose for 2 minutes in private (research supports a mild boost in composure)
- Get 7–8 hours of sleep — fatigue kills clarity
- Dress one level above what's expected

**Mental reframes:**
- Replace *"I hope I don't mess up"* with *"I'm here to learn and add value"*
- Treat it as a **two-way evaluation** — you're also deciding if this is right for you
- Nerves = excitement; the physical feelings are identical, only the label differs

**During:**
- Slow your breathing (box breathing: inhale 4s, hold 4s, exhale 4s)
- Speak slower than you think you need to — nervous people rush
- If you make a mistake, acknowledge it briefly and move on; don't dwell

**Long-term confidence building:**
- Do more of these — confidence is a skill, not a trait
- Debrief after every interview/meeting: what went well, what to improve
- Keep a "wins log" of positive feedback and accomplishments to read before high-stakes events

## Quick Checklist

| Category | Key Action |
|---|---|
| Prep | Research + STAR stories ready |
| Arrival | Early, calm, name + eye contact |
| Listening | 70/30 rule, take notes |
| Answers | Pause → structure → quantify |
| Confidence | Slow breath, slow speech, reframe nerves |
| Close | Ask smart questions + confirm next steps |
