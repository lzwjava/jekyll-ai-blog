* **瓜类 seeds**

  * 西瓜籽 (watermelon seeds): edible; usually roasted because the shell is hard.
  * 南瓜籽 (pumpkin seeds): very common snack.
  * 苦瓜籽 (bitter melon seeds): edible when mature; younger ones are softer.

* **Grains are technically seeds**

  * Rice, wheat, corn, oats — all are seeds.

* **Nuts are also seed-like structures**

  * Almonds, walnuts, cashews, etc.

* **Other edible seeds**

  * Sesame seeds
  * Chia seeds
  * Flax seeds
  * Lotus seeds

### Seeds that people usually don't eat

Some are:

* too hard to digest,
* contain toxins,
* too small/low nutrition,
* or simply not traditionally eaten.

Examples:

* **Apple seeds**: contain amygdalin, which can release cyanide compounds when crushed and eaten in large amounts. Accidentally swallowing a few whole seeds is usually not a concern, but don't eat them deliberately.
* **Cherry/apricot/peach pits**: similar issue; the hard pit protects the seed, but crushed seeds can release cyanogenic compounds.
* Some ornamental plant seeds can be poisonous.

### What about bitter melon seeds?

Yes, **苦瓜籽 is relatively soft when the bitter melon is young**.

There are stages:

1. **Young bitter melon**

   * Seeds are white or pale.
   * Soft, tender.
   * Can be eaten together with the flesh.
   * Many people don't remove them.

2. **Mature bitter melon**

   * Seeds turn yellow/orange/red.
   * The outer coating becomes harder.
   * The red pulp around seeds becomes slightly sweet.
   * Usually people remove the hard seeds.

The red part around mature bitter melon seeds is actually edible too. Some people eat it because it is mildly sweet.

A simple cooking rule:

* **Soft, white seeds → eat them.**
* **Hard, mature seeds → remove or roast/process them.**

Similar to vegetables: the "seed" itself is not automatically edible or inedible; it depends on the plant and maturity stage.
