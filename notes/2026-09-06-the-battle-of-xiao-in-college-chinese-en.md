**Question:** What is an introduction to *秦晋崤之战* (The Battle of Xiao between Qin and Jin) as studied in 大学语文 (College Chinese)?

---

**Answer:**

**Source and Text**

*秦晋崤之战* (The Battle of Xiao / Yào between Qin and Jin) is a prose selection commonly included in Chinese university-level *大学语文* (College Chinese) curricula. The text is drawn from the *Zuo Zhuan* (《左传》), specifically from the chapters covering Duke Xi's 32nd and 33rd years (*僖公三十二年、三十三年*), and narrates the events surrounding Qin's military campaign against Zheng and its eventual defeat at the hands of Jin at Mount Xiao.

**About the *Zuo Zhuan***

The *Zuo Zhuan*, often translated as *The Zuo Tradition* or *The Commentary of Zuo*, is an ancient Chinese narrative history traditionally regarded as a commentary on the *Spring and Autumn Annals*. It covers the period from 722 to 468 BC, focusing on political, diplomatic, and military affairs. For many centuries it was the primary text through which educated Chinese learned their ancient history, and its concise, flowing style served as a paragon of elegant Classical Chinese.

**Historical Background**

The Battle of Xiao took place between the states of Qin and Jin, both major principalities during the Spring and Autumn Period of the Zhou dynasty, occurring in 627 BC at the Xiao Mountains — a branch of the Qinling Range in modern-day Henan province.

The background begins in 630 BC, when Jin and Qin jointly besieged the state of Zheng. A diplomat from Zheng named Zhu Zhiwu (烛之武) visited Duke Mu of Qin at night and persuaded him that conquering Zheng would only benefit Jin, not Qin. Duke Mu withdrew, and Qin even allied itself with Zheng. This split between the two states planted the seeds of conflict.

After Duke Wen of Jin died, Duke Mu of Qin appointed Baili Mengming, Xi Qi Shu, and Bai Yibing as generals to launch a surprise attack on Zheng. However, a senior advisor named Jian Shu strongly opposed the plan, warning that it would end in disaster. Duke Mu ignored this counsel.

In 627 BC, the Qin army aborted its mission against Zheng after being tricked into believing that Zheng had discovered their plans. Jin and its Jiang Rong allies then inflicted a major defeat on the retreating Qin army at Mount Xiao.

**Literary and Thematic Significance**

The text is studied for several reasons in *大学语文*:

- **Vivid characterization:** The narrative skillfully portrays multiple characters — the wise but ignored Jian Shu, the arrogant Qin generals, and the strategically patient Jin commanders — through direct speech and action.
- **Moral and strategic lessons:** The piece illustrates the strategic principle that arrogance and ignoring wise counsel leads to defeat — *"only by taking the people's trust, choosing the right timing, and striking when the enemy is exhausted can a smaller force overcome a larger one."*
- **Classic narrative style:** The *Zuo Zhuan*'s tendency toward third-person narration and portraying characters through direct speech and action became hallmarks of Chinese narrative, imitated by historians and writers for over 2,000 years.

**Key Exam Point**

The event most directly linked to the Battle of Xiao is *烛之武退秦师* (Zhu Zhiwu's persuasion of Duke Mu to withdraw from Zheng) — because it was this diplomatic episode that broke the Qin-Jin alliance and ultimately led to the armed confrontation at Mount Xiao.
