**Question:** What does Li Xiaolai mean when he says collaboration is about giving up part of your sense of security to others and trusting they won't exploit it?

**Answer:**

Yes, your understanding is essentially correct. Li Xiaolai defines it this way: **Collaboration** is when each party voluntarily gives up a small portion of their own sense of security, and entrusts that portion to their collaborator to safeguard. **Trust** is the belief that the other party will not exploit that portion of security you have willingly surrendered.

This is a beautifully precise framing. Let's unpack what it means:

- **You can never be 100% secure in a relationship.** To collaborate with anyone — a business partner, spouse, colleague — you must accept some vulnerability. You expose a part of yourself (your resources, secrets, effort, or emotions) that the other person *could* take advantage of.

- **Trust is the bridge.** It's not blind faith — it's the specific belief that the other person *won't* weaponize the vulnerability you've offered them.

- **Why this matters practically:** Li Xiaolai warns not to collaborate with people who lack a sense of security (安全感), because in their worldview, genuine collaboration is impossible. He even extends this to personal life: avoid marrying or dating insecure people, as they are driven to pursue 100% security — which is impossible — and will exhaust both themselves and everyone around them.

In short: collaboration *requires* mutual vulnerability, and trust is the willingness to remain vulnerable because you believe the other person is decent enough not to exploit it.
