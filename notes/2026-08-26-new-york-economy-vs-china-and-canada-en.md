**Question:** Is New York's economy currently doing better than China's and Canada's?

**Answer:**

This is a nuanced comparison — it depends heavily on what "better" means: raw GDP size, growth rate, or per-capita wealth. Here's how they stack up right now in 2026:

---

**New York State**

New York's GDP reached $2.47 trillion in 2025, and the NYC metro area produced over $2.6 trillion — ranking as the largest metro GDP in the world. New York's economy grew 2.9% in 2025, third-fastest in the U.S., driven largely by finance and AI-related technology. Growth is expected to decelerate to around 2.2% in 2026. Wall Street firms posted profits of $21.1 billion in Q1 2026 — a 37% year-over-year increase — reflecting the financial sector's continued strength.

---

**China**

China's economy is far larger in total size (~$21+ trillion), but growth is slowing. China's GDP grew 4.7% year-on-year in the first half of 2026, reaching roughly RMB 69.57 trillion (US$10.3 trillion) in H1 alone. However, Q2 growth decelerated to 4.3%, down from 5% in Q1. High-tech sectors like semiconductors, robotics, and EVs are outperforming, while construction, property, and big-ticket consumer spending remain weak. In terms of **GDP per capita**, China ($14,874) is far behind New York ($123,369).

---

**Canada**

Canada is the weakest performer of the three right now. Canada's economy flatlined in Q1 2026, with zero growth following a 0.2% decline in Q4 2025 — technically a recession. The OECD expects Canada's full-year GDP growth to reach only 1.2% by end of 2026, recovering from the shock of U.S. tariffs. Business capital investment fell for a fifth consecutive quarter, with many small businesses in a holding pattern due to rising energy costs and economic uncertainty.

---

**Bottom line:** New York is doing better than Canada by a wide margin right now. Against China, New York wins decisively on wealth per person and financial sector performance, but China is growing faster in percentage terms and is a much larger economy overall.
