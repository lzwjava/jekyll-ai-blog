Question: Introduction to the Computer History Museum

Answer:

The **Computer History Museum** (often abbreviated as **CHM**) is a nonprofit museum located in **Mountain View, California**, at the heart of Silicon Valley. It is widely regarded as home to the world's largest and most significant collection of computing artifacts, preserving the history of computers, software, and the broader information age.

### Key Facts
- **Location**: 1401 N Shoreline Blvd, Mountain View, CA 94043, USA
- **Mission**: To preserve and present for posterity the artifacts and stories of the Information Age, exploring how the computing revolution came to be and its ongoing impact on society and humanity.
- **Collection**: Includes hardware, software, documents, photos, oral histories, and ephemera — from ancient calculating tools to modern smartphones and AI-related items.
- **Major Exhibit**: "Revolution: The First 2000 Years of Computing" — traces computing history globally from the abacus (over 2,000 years ago) through mechanical calculators, early electronic computers, personal computers, the internet, mobile devices, and beyond.

### History of the Museum
The roots go back to 1968 when DEC engineer Gordon Bell started collecting computing artifacts. Early efforts led to:
- 1975: First small exhibit at Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC)
- 1979: Opened to the public as The Digital Computer Museum in Massachusetts
- 1984: Moved to Boston as The Computer Museum
- Late 1990s: History center established in Silicon Valley (Moffett Field, with NASA support)
- 2002: Official opening at the current Mountain View location

Today it focuses heavily on Silicon Valley's role while covering global computing history.

### What Visitors Can Experience
- Historic machines: IBM 1401 (1959 business computer — often demonstrated live), Apollo Guidance Computer (used in moon landings), early Apple computers, Cray supercomputers, and more.
- Interactive elements: Play classic games like Spacewar! (1959–1962), experience software history (Photoshop, MP3, Wikipedia, World of Warcraft), try vintage interfaces.
- Special programs: CHM Live speaker series featuring tech pioneers (past speakers include Bill Gates, Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg), family events, virtual tours, online exhibits, and educational programs (including Roblox and Minecraft experiences).
- Other highlights: Docent-led tours, hands-on demos, café, and gift shop.

The museum appeals especially to tech enthusiasts, students, historians, and anyone interested in how computers transformed the world. It's frequently described as a "must-visit" in Silicon Valley for its comprehensive, well-presented story of computing from pre-electronic times to today's digital era.
