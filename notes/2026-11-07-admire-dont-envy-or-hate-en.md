**Question:** Is "可以羡慕，不要嫉妒，更不要恨" a valid life principle, as attributed to Wang Xing of Meituan?

---

**Answer:**

Yes, absolutely — and it's one of the most psychologically precise things Wang Xing ever wrote. The quote draws a sharp three-level gradient of emotional response to others' success:

| Emotion | Chinese | Nature | Effect on you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Admire | 羡慕 | Positive longing | Motivates, inspires |
| Jealousy | 嫉妒 | Toxic comparison | Corrodes focus |
| Hate | 恨 | Destructive resentment | Destroys the self |

**Why the distinction matters:**

- **羡慕 (admire)** is outward-looking — "they have something great; I want to build that too." It's fuel.
- **嫉妒 (jealousy)** turns inward — "why do *they* have it and not *me*?" It shifts focus from your own path to someone else's, which is a dead end.
- **恨 (hate)** is the final corruption — it wastes enormous energy on someone who likely doesn't even know you exist. Wang Xing's Fanfou posts reveal a person who is remarkably unshakeable — his failures "could hardly hurt him", which is precisely this mindset in action.

Wang Xing lived this principle. When校内网 (Campus Network) was sold and later IPO'd at over $7 billion, Wang Xing wasn't consumed by regret — he channeled his energy into the next venture. He admired what others built, learned from it, and moved on without bitterness.

The deeper insight: **羡慕 makes you a student; 嫉妒 makes you a rival; 恨 makes you a prisoner.** Only the first one is worth keeping.
