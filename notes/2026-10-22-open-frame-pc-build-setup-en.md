**Question:** How do you use an open-frame rack (open rack / test bench) to hold PC components like a motherboard instead of a traditional case?

---

**Answer:**

An **open-frame rack** (开放式机架, also called an open-frame chassis or test bench) works just like a regular case internally — it just removes the enclosure. Here's how to set one up:

---

### 1. Choose the Right Open Frame
Make sure it supports your **motherboard form factor**:
- ATX, Micro-ATX, Mini-ITX — most open frames list which they support
- Popular brands: Thermaltake Core P-series, Lian Li (vertical), ASUS ROG Hyperion, TT A500

---

### 2. Mount the Motherboard
- The frame has **pre-drilled standoff holes** matching ATX/mATX/ITX layouts
- Screw in the **brass standoffs** first, then place the motherboard on them and screw it down
- This is identical to mounting in a regular case — the standoffs prevent electrical shorts

---

### 3. Mount the PSU (Power Supply)
- Most open frames have a **dedicated PSU bracket** (usually at the bottom or back)
- Secure with the 4 screws, just like a standard case

---

### 4. Install GPU
- Seat the GPU into the **PCIe x16 slot** on the motherboard
- Many open frames support both **horizontal** (traditional) and **vertical** (showcase) GPU mounting
- Use a riser cable for vertical mounting

---

### 5. Mount Storage (SSD / HDD)
- The frame usually has **drive trays or brackets** with screw holes
- M.2 SSDs go directly on the motherboard
- 2.5"/3.5" drives can be zip-tied or screwed onto the frame's bracket

---

### 6. CPU Cooler
- Air coolers or AIO liquid coolers both work — open frames usually have **radiator mounting points** on the sides or top
- Airflow is generally better than in a closed case

---

### 7. Cable Management
- Use **zip ties** or Velcro straps — cables are fully exposed so you can route them cleanly
- Run cables behind the frame panel if there's a back channel

---

### Key Tips & Caveats

| Topic | Note |
|---|---|
| **Dust** | Open frames collect dust faster — clean more often |
| **Grounding** | Touch a metal part before handling components (no case ground) |
| **ESD** | Use an anti-static mat/wristband since there's no metal shell |
| **Shorts** | Don't let loose screws or metal contact the motherboard |
| **Pets/kids** | Spinning fans and exposed PCB are accessible — keep away |
| **Airflow** | Excellent; great for overclocking or high-TDP builds |

---

### Common Use Cases
- **Test benches** — swap components easily without removing case panels
- **Showcase builds** — show off RGB / water cooling
- **Enthusiast overclocking** — maximum thermal headroom
