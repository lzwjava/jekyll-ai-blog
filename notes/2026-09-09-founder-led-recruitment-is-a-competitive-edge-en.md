Question: Is it good behavior for a founder to directly approach and recruit talent themselves?

Answer: Yes, absolutely — and it's often one of the most effective things a founder can do, especially in the early stages. Here's why it works:

**Why hands-on founder recruiting is a strong signal:**

- **Conviction is contagious.** When a founder personally reaches out, it signals genuine belief in the mission. Candidates feel the weight of the opportunity more than when a recruiter does the same outreach.
- **Better culture fit filtering.** Founders know the DNA of the company better than anyone. Direct conversations catch alignment (or misalignment) that a hiring manager might miss.
- **Speed.** Cutting out intermediary layers means faster decisions and less candidate drop-off.
- **It attracts top talent.** A-players often respond better to a founder's personal message than a generic recruiter note — it feels like an invitation, not a transaction.
- **Builds a talent network early.** Even "not now" conversations plant seeds for future hires or referrals.

**When to watch out:**

- It can become a bottleneck if the founder is the *only* one recruiting as the company scales.
- Some founders over-index on "people like me" and accidentally narrow the talent pool.
- Time cost is real — the founder has to consciously balance recruiting with building.

**The bottom line:** Hands-on founder recruiting is not just good behavior — it's a competitive advantage, especially pre-Series B. The best founders (Zuckerberg, Bezos, Chesky) were famously obsessive about who they hired, and personally involved in early hires.
