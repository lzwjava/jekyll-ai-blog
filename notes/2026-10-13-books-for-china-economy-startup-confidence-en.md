**Question: What books can help me regain confidence in China’s economy and prepare for doing a startup in China with possible global customers?**

**Answer:**

Your reaction is actually reasonable. A book like 溃而不崩 gives a "risk lens": debt, demographics, structural problems, institutional constraints. That lens is useful, but if you only read pessimistic analysis, you may start seeing China only as a declining system.

For a founder, a better mental model is:

> China is not a simple "growth miracle" anymore. It is a huge, competitive, engineering-heavy economy entering a harder phase. The opportunity shifts from "easy growth" to "innovation, efficiency, global competition."

I would read books in several categories.

---

## 1. Understand why China became strong (build the foundation)

### China's Economy: What Everyone Needs to Know

Good balanced overview.

Key lessons:

- China is not only real estate + infrastructure.
- Manufacturing capability, supply chains, engineering talent, and entrepreneurship are deep advantages.
- Slow growth does not mean no opportunity.

---

### The Chinese Economy: Transitions and Growth

More academic, but probably the best foundation.

You understand:

```
1978 reform
    ↓
market incentives
    ↓
manufacturing expansion
    ↓
export integration
    ↓
technology upgrading
    ↓
current transition
```

It prevents emotional conclusions from short-term news. [Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/articles/china/2009-04-27/what-read-chinas-economy?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

---

## 2. Read about Chinese entrepreneurs and technology companies

### Tech Titans of China

This is closer to your situation.

Study:

- Tencent
- Alibaba
- Huawei
- Xiaomi
- ByteDance

The important lesson:

China's strongest companies often win through:

```
fast iteration
+
huge domestic competition
+
engineering execution
+
cost efficiency
```

Not just capital. [Focus](https://focus.cbbc.org/five-books-on-the-chinese-economy/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

---

### The Huawei Story

For an engineer-founder, Huawei is one of the most important cases.

Study:

- long-term R&D
- customer obsession
- surviving hard environments
- global market expansion

A Chinese technology company can become globally competitive.

---

## 3. Read books about "China going global"

Your idea is not "only China market". You want:

```
China engineering talent
        +
AI capability
        +
global customers
```

This is probably the most interesting path.

Read:

### Decoding China's Export Miracle

It explains why Chinese companies can compete globally through supply chains and manufacturing ecosystems. [CBBC](https://www.cbbc.org/news-insights/want-understand-chinese-economy-2021-start-these-books?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The startup equivalent today:

Old:

```
China factory → global products
```

New:

```
China engineers → global AI/software services
```

---

## 4. Read founder mindset books (more important than economy books)

### The Hard Thing About Hard Things

For startup reality.

Main idea:

Startup is not about a perfect environment.

It is:

```
uncertainty
+
pain
+
adaptation
+
continuous execution
```

---

### Zero to One

Useful question:

"Where is there a monopoly opportunity?"

For you:

Not:

> "Can I build another software outsourcing company?"

Better:

> "Can I become the best AI agent implementation team for a specific industry?"

---

## 5. Read China's future opportunity areas

### AI Superpowers

Some predictions aged poorly, but the strategic view is useful.

China advantage:

- engineers
- deployment speed
- huge user base
- manufacturing + AI combination
