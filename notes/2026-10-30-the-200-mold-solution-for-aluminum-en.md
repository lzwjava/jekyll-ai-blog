**Question:** How did Zhang Feng (张峰), founder of ZMI (紫米), come to open ~200 molds at aluminum profile factories in order to produce the Xiaomi power bank?

---

**Answer:**

This story comes from Xiaomi's official authorized biography *一往无前* (No Limits). Here's what happened:

**The core manufacturing problem**

When building the first generation Xiaomi power bank, ZMI founder Zhang Feng discovered a critical problem: every time aluminum was extruded and formed into a shell, then anodized, visible "ribs" (筋条) would be exposed on the front surface — a defect that virtually no factory knew how to fix.

**Zhang Feng's approach — the 200 molds**

During that period, Zhang Feng drove himself around the Jiangsu-Zhejiang (江浙) region, city by city, searching for factories that could form the aluminum shell. He visited nearly 30 cities and commissioned nearly 200 sets of molds in an attempt to solve the manufacturing defect.

He wasn't *finding* a factory that already had 200 molds — he was *creating* (开模) nearly 200 different molds across dozens of factories himself, testing whether any combination could eliminate the rib seam problem.

He described it as "dead-manning the metal shell" (死磕金属壳) — every night he would question whether choosing an aluminum shell was a mistake, but couldn't think of a better alternative, so he had no choice but to keep pushing. He noted that at least 10 mold sets went wrong early on due to design interpretation issues: when a factory optimized the CAD drawings, the curved geometry came out wrong, exposing four angular bumps, and neither side could get it right.

He described his daily life at the time: "Every night I was camped out in a factory, doing PR for the factory managers." Some factories were busy during the day doing sandblast anodizing for iPad shells, so Zhang could only talk to them about his problem at night.

**How it was finally solved**

Three months later, the problem was finally solved by a factory called **广东和胜 (Guangdong Hesheng)**. Their mold craftsmen were of exceptionally high skill — they adjusted the molds with minute precision, like lightly tapping on fine sandpaper, tweaking and re-tweaking until the one-piece extruded aluminum shell, after sandblasting, showed no more rib lines at all.

When Zhang Feng saw the successful sample, he said: "After so many years of commercial battles and brutal competition, that moment still made me want to cry."

**The result**

On December 4, 2013, Xiaomi launched its first power bank: 10,400 mAh, priced at only ¥69 — using LG/Samsung imported cells, an anodized aluminum alloy shell rated to withstand 50 kg of pressure and 300 abrasion tests.
