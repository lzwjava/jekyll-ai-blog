Question: Is it true that AI engineers and researchers from companies like OpenAI (such as Jiayi Weng and John Schulman) are becoming more publicly recognized and celebrated compared to traditional large organizations where technical people typically remain anonymous?

Answer:

You're observing a real shift in how technical talent is recognized and celebrated, though the reality is somewhat nuanced.

**The OpenAI Model**

OpenAI and similar AI-focused companies do indeed have a culture where researchers and engineers are more visible. Figures like John Schulman, Ilya Sutskever, and others have become recognizable names in the AI community because:

- AI research is often published and discussed openly, giving individual contributors visibility
- The field moves quickly, and breakthrough papers and announcements get attributed to specific people
- These companies benefit from the visibility of their talent—it's part of their brand and recruitment strategy
- The AI community itself is relatively young and values technical excellence and transparency

**The Traditional Organization Reality**

However, the contrast with traditional corporations and banks isn't as stark as it might appear:

- Large organizations absolutely do have recognized technical leaders, though perhaps less visible to the general public
- Banking and finance have their own "celebrity technologists" and recognized figures, just operating in different circles
- The visibility difference is partly about **audience scope**—AI researchers reach broader public audiences through papers, conferences, and media coverage, while bank engineers' work is more domain-specific

**The Broader Trend**

What's actually changing is that **technical expertise itself is becoming more valuable as a public-facing asset**. Companies realize that showcasing talented engineers helps with recruitment, builds trust, and creates thought leadership. This is spreading beyond AI to other tech companies, startups, and even traditional firms trying to modernize.

So yes, you're right that there's a meaningful trend toward greater public recognition of AI researchers and engineers, but it's more accurate to say this reflects changing market dynamics around technical talent rather than a completely new phenomenon.
