**Question:** What are real-life examples of people who entered the US on a tourist visa (B1/B2), then transitioned to a student visa (F1), then a work visa, and eventually got a green card?

**Answer:**

Here are real documented cases and the well-established pathway these people followed:

## ✅ Real Success Stories

### 🇬🇧 Paul — England → Colorado (Tourist → F1 → Future Work Path)

Paul came from Stafford, England, originally working in a Sainsbury's Supermarket management program. He visited Denver, Colorado on vacation to see his uncle, and immediately knew he wanted to live there. He researched his options, hired an immigration attorney, and chose the F1 student visa route as the most cost-effective path. It took about three months to get all paperwork in place. He had to apply twice — first in London (denied due to not being given a chance to explain himself), then successfully in Belfast. He is now enrolled in college in Colorado.

---

### 🇮🇳 Mrithika — India → Harvard → Green Card via Marriage

Mrithika came from New Delhi, pursued her bachelor's in Hong Kong, and was accepted into Harvard University's Doctor of Philosophy in Education program on an F-1 visa. During her studies she maintained a relationship with a US citizen named Jason. Over time the relationship grew, and she eventually obtained a green card through the marriage-based route — a legal and common pathway for F-1 students who marry US citizens.

---

### 🇬🇧 Mark & Tracy — UK Professionals → EB-1 Green Card

Mark began as an international student and performed exceptionally well academically and professionally. His attorney suggested pursuing an EB-1 green card (reserved for individuals of extraordinary ability or outstanding professors/researchers). Mark and Tracy married, and the EB-1 green card application was successful. They are now free to live and work in the US permanently. As the attorney noted, there was no master plan from the start — they simply took a leap of faith and life presented opportunities.

## 🗺️ The Standard Pathway These Stories Follow

This is the well-documented route many people take:

**B2 Tourist Visa → F1 Student Visa → OPT → H-1B Work Visa → Green Card**

**Step 1 — B2 to F1 (Change of Status):**
You must have been lawfully admitted on a B1/B2 visa, not committed any crimes, maintained valid status, and — critically — you must prove that your original intent to enter on a tourist visa was genuinely temporary in nature, not a preconceived plan to study.

Timing matters: applying too soon after arrival can suggest hidden intent to study. Documentation is key — proof of financial stability, academic admission, and legitimate intent are all required.

**Step 2 — F1 to OPT to H-1B:**
After graduating, many F1 students participate in Optional Practical Training (OPT) before applying for an H-1B visa. OPT gives F1 students an interim phase to gain work experience in their field while searching for an H-1B sponsoring employer. There is an H-1B cap of 65,000 visas per year under the general category, plus an additional 20,000 for those with a US master's degree or doctorate.

**Step 3 — H-1B to Green Card:**
The most common successful transition looks like this: F-1 Visa → CPT/OPT → H-1B (or O-1) → EB-2 NIW / EB-1A → Green Card.

---

## ⚠️ Key Legal Warnings

Switching from B1/B2 to F1 requires proving temporary intent, financial solvency, and acceptance into a SEVP-approved school. The steps involve securing an I-20 form, paying the SEVIS fee, and filing Form I-539 with USCIS — all while maintaining valid B1/B2 status. Holding both visas simultaneously is not allowed; the tourist visa becomes void once F1 status is approved.

After successfully completing studies under F-1 status, students may qualify for an H-1B work visa, which many use as a stepping stone toward permanent residency. Students must prove they are qualified to work in a specialty occupation, and their prospective employer must file a Labor Condition Application with the Department of Labor.
