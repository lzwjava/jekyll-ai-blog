Question: Can I create a Hong Kong company, apply/interview for jobs on JobsDB, then convince companies to sign a contract with my Hong Kong company instead of hiring me as an employee?

Answer:

Yes, this is **possible**, and actually fits your background better than a normal job-search path. But you need to position yourself as a **Hong Kong-based AI/software consultancy**, not as a person trying to avoid employment.

The model:

```
You (China)
   |
   | owner/director
   v
Hong Kong Limited Company
   |
   | service contract
   v
Hong Kong company client
   |
   | pays HK company monthly/project fee
   v
You deliver AI/software work remotely or on-site
```

This is a common contractor/consultant structure. Hong Kong companies do engage independent contractors and technical service providers, including software developers and IT consultants. ([HKEX News][1])

## How to approach JobsDB

Do **not** say:

> "I don't want to be your employee, please hire my company."

Many HR departments will reject this.

Instead:

### Normal interview

Position yourself as:

> "I run a small AI engineering consultancy in Hong Kong. I specialize in LLM application development, model fine-tuning, backend systems, and AI infrastructure. I can join projects as a technical consultant."

Then discuss:

* monthly retainer
* project delivery
* technical ownership

Example:

```
Option A:
HKD 80k/month employee salary

Option B:
HKD 100k/month consulting contract
through my Hong Kong company
```

For companies, B can be attractive because:

* no MPF/payroll complexity
* easier project termination
* access to specialized skills

Hong Kong already has a contractor ecosystem, especially in IT. There are IT contracting channels such as T-Contract and IT staffing companies. ([Seamatch][2])

## Company setup

A Hong Kong limited company is not enough by itself.

You need:

* company registration
* business bank account
* accounting
* contracts
* invoices
* tax compliance

If later you want to physically work in Hong Kong, immigration is separate. A company does not automatically give you a work visa. Employment/professional schemes require supporting documents such as employment contracts, company background, financial standing, etc. ([Government of Hong Kong][4])
