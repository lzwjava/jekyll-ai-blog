Question: What is an intranet, and how does it differ from the internet?

Answer:

An **intranet** is a private network accessible only to members of an organization — typically a company, school, or government agency. It uses the same web technologies (HTTP, browsers, HTML) as the internet, but is closed off from the public. Employees might use it to access internal HR systems, company wikis, shared documents, or internal dashboards.

The **internet** is the global, public network connecting billions of devices worldwide, open to anyone with a connection.

Here's a quick comparison:

| Feature | Intranet | Internet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access | Restricted (employees/members only) | Open to the public |
| Scope | Local to an organization | Global |
| Security | High — behind firewalls/VPNs | Variable — must be secured per-service |
| Content | Internal docs, tools, HR portals | Websites, social media, email, etc. |
| Ownership | Single organization | No single owner |
| Speed | Usually faster (local network) | Depends on ISP and distance |

A related term is **extranet** — a controlled extension of an intranet that allows access to specific outsiders (partners, suppliers, clients), sitting between the two in terms of openness.
