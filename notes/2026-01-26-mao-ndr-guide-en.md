### Introduction to the New Democratic Revolution (新民主主义革命)

The **New Democratic Revolution** (Chinese: 新民主主义革命) is a core concept in modern Chinese Communist Party (CCP) theory, primarily developed and theorized by **Mao Zedong** in the 1930s and 1940s. It served as the guiding strategy for the Chinese revolution from approximately 1921 (founding of the CCP) until 1949 (foundation of the People's Republic of China).

#### 1. Historical Background

After the **May Fourth Movement (1919)** and the founding of the Chinese Communist Party (1921), China was still a **semi-colonial and semi-feudal society**. The old democratic revolution (led by Sun Yat-sen and the Kuomintang) had failed to thoroughly solve China's problems of imperialism, feudalism, and bureaucratic capitalism.

Mao Zedong argued that China needed a **new type** of democratic revolution — one that was led by the **proletariat (working class)** through the **Communist Party**, rather than by the bourgeoisie as in Western bourgeois democratic revolutions.

#### 2. Main Characteristics of the New Democratic Revolution

Mao Zedong defined it with these key features:

- **Leadership**: Led by the **proletariat** (through the Communist Party), not by the bourgeoisie.
- **Main Force**: The **peasantry** was the main revolutionary force (because China was overwhelmingly rural and peasants made up ~90% of the population).
- **United Front**: A broad alliance that included:
  - Working class (leadership)
  - Peasants (main force)
  - Urban petty bourgeoisie
  - National bourgeoisie (patriotic part of the capitalists who opposed imperialism)
- **Two Main Enemies**:
  1. **Imperialism** (foreign powers controlling parts of China)
  2. **Feudalism** (landlord class and warlords)
  - Later, **bureaucratic capitalism** (collaboration between big capitalists and the Kuomintang government) was also added as a target.

#### 3. Two-Stage Theory of Chinese Revolution

Mao Zedong outlined that the Chinese revolution would proceed in **two distinct but continuous stages**:

1. **New Democratic Revolution** (1949 and before)
   - Goal: Overthrow **imperialism, feudalism, and bureaucratic capitalism**
   - Establish a **new-democratic republic** (a joint dictatorship of several revolutionary classes under proletarian leadership)
   - Not yet socialism

2. **Socialist Revolution** (after 1949)
   - After winning state power in 1949, gradually transition to **socialist revolution** and socialist construction

This **two-stage, continuous revolution** theory was a major innovation of Maoism.

#### 4. Key Historical Periods of the New Democratic Revolution

| Period                        | Years          | Main Events / Characteristics                              |
|-------------------------------|----------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| First United Front            | 1924–1927      | CCP–KMT cooperation against warlords                       |
| Agrarian Revolutionary War    | 1927–1937      | After KMT betrayal → Rural Soviet bases, Long March       |
| War of Resistance Against Japan | 1937–1945    | Second United Front (CCP–KMT against Japan)                |
| War of Liberation             | 1945–1949      | Civil war → Defeat of Chiang Kai-shek → Founding of PRC    |

#### 5. Main Outcomes (1949)

- **Overthrew the "three big mountains"**:
  - Imperialism
  - Feudalism
  - Bureaucratic capitalism
- Established the **People's Republic of China** on **October 1, 1949**
- Implemented **New Democracy** in the early years (1949–1953/1956):
  - Mixed economy (state, private, cooperative)
  - Multi-class united front government
  - Gradual transition toward socialism

#### 6. Core Theoretical Works by Mao Zedong

- **"On New Democracy"** (1940) — the most important document
- **"Analysis of the Classes in Chinese Society"** (1926)
- **"The Chinese Revolution and the Chinese Communist Party"** (1939)
- **"On Coalition Government"** (1945)
- **"On the People's Democratic Dictatorship"** (1949)

#### 7. Significance

- Adapted **Marxism-Leninism** to China's concrete conditions (a semi-colonial, semi-feudal agrarian society)
- Proved that revolutions in colonial and semi-colonial countries could be led by the **proletariat** rather than the bourgeoisie
- Became a model for many national liberation movements in Asia, Africa, and Latin America during the 20th century
- Laid the theoretical foundation for the **People's Republic of China** and its early policies

In summary, the **New Democratic Revolution** was Mao Zedong's creative application of Marxism-Leninism to China's specific national conditions, successfully guiding the Chinese people to national independence and the establishment of the People's Republic of China in 1949.
