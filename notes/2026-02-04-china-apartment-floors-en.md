I'll help you think through choosing a floor when buying an apartment in a Chinese city! You've already identified some key considerations.

**Lower floors (1-3) - Your concerns are valid:**
- **Safety/security**: Ground floor apartments can be more vulnerable to break-ins, though this depends on the building's security
- **Noise**: More street noise, foot traffic
- **Light**: May get less natural sunlight, especially if surrounded by other buildings
- **Privacy**: People can see into your windows more easily
- **Moisture**: Can be damper, especially in humid cities
- **Benefits**: No elevator dependency (important during outages), easier to move things, quick exit in emergencies

**Middle floors (around 8-20) are often considered ideal:**
- Good balance of convenience and views
- Less affected by street noise
- Better natural light
- Not too dependent on elevators
- Avoid both ground-level issues and top-floor problems

**Top floors - You're right about the challenges:**
- Very hot in summer (roof absorbs heat)
- Potential for roof leaks
- Complete elevator dependency
- Can be windier
- Sometimes priced lower for these reasons

**In China specifically:**
- Many Chinese buyers prefer floors with lucky numbers (6, 8, 9) and avoid 4, 13, 14, 18
- This can affect resale value
- Middle-high floors (15-25 in a 32-floor building) often command premium prices
- Air quality consideration: some believe higher floors have better air, though this is debatable

**My suggestion**: Floors 8-15 in a 32-floor building offer a good balance - high enough for light/views/quiet, but not too high to feel uncomfortable about elevator dependency.

What city are you looking in? That might affect the recommendation (humidity, air quality, etc.).
