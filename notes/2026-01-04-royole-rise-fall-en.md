Question: Provide a comprehensive introduction to the collapse of Royole, the screen company.

Answer: 

**Royole Corporation (also known as Royole Technologies or Shenzhen Royole Technologies Co. Ltd.)** was a Chinese technology company specializing in flexible display screens and foldable devices. Founded in 2012 by Liu Zihong (a Stanford graduate), it positioned itself as a pioneer in flexible OLED (Organic Light-Emitting Diode) technology.

### Key Milestones in Royole's Rise
- **Innovation and Early Success**: Royole gained global attention in 2018 by launching the FlexPai, widely recognized as the world's first commercially available foldable smartphone. This device featured a fully flexible display that could fold into a compact form or unfold into a tablet-sized screen, beating major competitors like Samsung to market.
- **Valuation and Funding**: By 2020, Royole was valued at around US$6–8 billion, earning "unicorn" status. It completed multiple funding rounds and ranked highly on lists of promising Chinese tech firms.
- **Infrastructure**: The company invested heavily, building a large quasi-G6 flexible AMOLED production facility in Shenzhen, China.

### Beginning of Financial Troubles (2021–2022)
- **Failed IPO**: Royole attempted to list on Shanghai's STAR Market in 2020–2021 but withdrew its application in February 2021 due to issues with shareholder structure and regulatory scrutiny.
- **Cash Flow Crisis**: Post-IPO failure, the company faced severe liquidity problems. It delayed salary payments for months, leading to employee unrest. By late 2021–2022, reports emerged of unpaid wages, supplier debts, and inability to secure new funding.
- **Layoffs and Operations Halt**: In 2022, Royole began large-scale layoffs after months of non-payment of wages. It effectively ceased operations, with assets put up for public auction.

### Reasons for Collapse
- **Lack of Major Customers**: Despite technological breakthroughs, Royole's flexible screens failed to attract orders from big smartphone manufacturers (e.g., Apple, Samsung, Huawei), who developed their own in-house technologies or preferred established suppliers.
- **Overambitious Investments**: Heavy spending on R&D and production facilities (e.g., the $1.7 billion Shenzhen plant) outpaced revenue, leading to unsustainable debt.
- **Market Competition**: The foldable market grew, but dominated by giants with better supply chains and brand power.
- **Broader Context**: Financial struggles were exacerbated by challenges in China's tech sector, including regulatory pressures and funding droughts for high-risk ventures.

### Official Bankruptcy (2024)
- In November 2024, the Shenzhen Intermediate People's Court declared Royole bankrupt, ruling that its assets were insufficient to cover debts. This followed bankruptcy applications for Royole and subsidiaries earlier in the year.
- Unpaid obligations included significant wage arrears (over 160 million yuan when including options) and other liabilities.
- Attempts to auction assets reportedly failed to attract buyers, marking a complete downfall from its peak valuation.

Royole's story exemplifies the risks in the high-tech display industry: groundbreaking innovation alone is insufficient without commercialization, customer adoption, and financial prudence. Once hailed as a foldable pioneer, its collapse highlights how even highly valued startups can fail amid intense competition and economic pressures.
