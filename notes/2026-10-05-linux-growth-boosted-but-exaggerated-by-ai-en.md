Question: Has Ubuntu/Linux become much more popular in recent years because of the AI boom?

Answer: Partly yes, but the picture is more mixed than the headlines suggest.

**Where AI clearly helps Linux (servers and development):**

- AI training and cloud workloads run overwhelmingly on Linux. One market report says Ubuntu Server's share in AI training scenarios exceeds 21.6%, and describes AI workloads as a structural tailwind for Linux adoption.
- Linux was already dominant on servers, and industry trackers project Linux server share to keep growing.

**Desktop Linux: real growth, but the numbers are inflated:**

- In mid-2026, StatCounter showed Linux at 10.65% of North American desktop usage in July, up from 5.52% in June. That is a huge jump in one month.
- Much of that jump is probably not new human users. Some suggest the surge may be bots or AI agents running on users' behalf via Linux-based servers. Cloudflare's data supports this: 19% of Linux desktop traffic is bots, and excluding them, North American Linux desktop share was 6.4% in July 2026, up from 4.1% a year earlier.
- One analysis also says part of the jump is due to changes in analytics, though desktop Linux is still seeing steady growth.
- Even so, a real increase appears to be happening. Steam crossed 5% Linux share for the first time in March 2026, and some commentators argue that modern LLMs can fix almost any Linux problem, lowering the barrier for ordinary users.

**Ubuntu specifically:** In the Steam survey's July 2026 distro breakdown, Ubuntu 26.04 LTS sat at 2.92%, tied with Fedora 44 KDE at the top. So Ubuntu is popular but not the only winner; Fedora, Mint and gaming-focused distros are also gaining.

**Bottom line:** AI has definitely boosted Linux on servers and in developer environments, and it may be helping desktop adoption indirectly. But the dramatic desktop percentages are exaggerated by bot traffic and measurement changes. The real human desktop share is probably around 5-6% in North America, up meaningfully from a year ago but far from the 10%+ headline figures.
