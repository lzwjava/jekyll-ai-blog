Hating less isn't really a knowledge problem, so I'll skip the search-and-cite format for this one — it wouldn't fit.

A few things that actually work, mechanically:

**Separate the person from the pattern.** Hate usually locks onto a person, but what actually hurt you was a specific action or repeated behavior. Naming the specific thing ("I hate that he took credit for my work in that meeting") is more useful and less corrosive than "I hate him." It gives you something to solve instead of something to carry.

**Check what the hate is doing for you.** Sometimes it's protecting you from feeling something worse — like grief, or powerlessness, or fear that you were wrong to trust someone. Hate can feel more agentic than sadness does. Worth asking what's underneath it.

**Reduce exposure before you try to reduce feeling.** If you're actively hating something (a person, a comment section, a competitor), the fastest lever isn't willpower, it's input. Mute, unfollow, stop checking. You can't think your way out of a feeling that gets re-triggered daily.

**Time-box it if you need to.** Suppressing hate often makes it louder later. Give yourself an explicit window to be angry — write it out uncensored, say it out loud to a wall — then close it. This isn't about being nice, it's about not letting it run in the background 24/7 eating your attention, which is the actual cost.

**Watch for the identity trap.** If hating someone/something has become part of how you explain yourself to yourself ("I'm the guy who got screwed by X"), that's the hardest kind to let go of, because letting go feels like losing a piece of your story. Worth noticing if that's happening.
