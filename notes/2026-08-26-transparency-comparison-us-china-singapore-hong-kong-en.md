**Question:** How does government transparency compare across China, Hong Kong, Singapore, and the USA?

**Answer:**

Here's a comparative overview based on the 2025 Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI) by Transparency International — the world's leading measure of public-sector corruption and governance accountability (scale: 0 = highly corrupt, 100 = very clean):

---

### 🇸🇬 Singapore — Rank #3 | Score: 84

Singapore is the top-ranked country in the Asia-Pacific region, placing 3rd globally with a CPI score of 84. It has held this position consistently. However, despite its high CPI score, Singapore has faced scrutiny for facilitating the movement of illicit funds across borders — a dimension the CPI does not measure.

---

### 🇭🇰 Hong Kong — Rank #12 | Score: 76

Among 182 countries in the 2025 CPI, Hong Kong ranked 12th least corrupt — 5 places higher than the previous year — making it one of the top performers in Asia-Pacific. Hong Kong gained two points compared to the prior year, reaching a score of 76. It has historically remained in the top 20 since the CPI began in 1995.

---

### 🇺🇸 USA — Rank ~24th | Score: 64

The US scored 64 out of 100 on the 2025 CPI, continuing a longer-term drop of 12 points over the previous decade. Beyond the corruption index, US government transparency is also under structural pressure: the US federal Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) of 1966 now ranks only 78th in strength out of 140 nations, as other countries have adopted stronger laws. Since 2025, the data transparency landscape has deteriorated markedly, with major cuts to statistical agencies, removal of senior data officials, and increasing constraints on oversight and accountability.

---

### 🇨🇳 China (Mainland) — Rank #76 | Score: 43

China retains a CPI score of 43 in 2025, ranked 76th globally, presenting what analysts describe as a multifaceted corruption risk profile for businesses operating there. China showed no year-on-year change, maintaining the same score and ranking as 2024, despite ongoing enforcement campaigns such as Operation Sky Net 2025 and new anti-bribery guidelines for the healthcare sector. Government information access for citizens and foreign journalists remains heavily restricted, which is not captured in the CPI score alone.
