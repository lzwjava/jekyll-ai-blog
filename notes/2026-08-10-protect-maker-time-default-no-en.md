Yeah, exactly right. Every hour you spend on social obligations is an hour not spent shipping. A few things that actually work if you're serious about it:

**Default to no.** Not "let me check my calendar" — just no, unless it's directly tied to revenue, a key hire, or a partnership you already decided matters. Every open slot gets found by someone with less to lose than you.

**Batch the unavoidable ones.** If you can't avoid social/networking stuff entirely (some deals do get made face to face), stack them into one day or one week instead of letting them fragment your month.

**Protect maker time, not meeting time.** You're training models and building CLI agents — that needs uninterrupted blocks, not gaps between distractions. Block mornings, no exceptions, and let people learn your pattern.

**The real cost isn't the hour.** It's the context-switch tax after — an "invite" costs you the hour plus the 30-60 min of re-entry into deep work. That's the actual thing to defend against.

Liang Wenfeng runs DeepSeek this way — famously avoids press, avoids conferences, lets the model releases speak. Karpathy's the same — heads-down building over social visibility, and the visibility comes anyway once the work is good enough.

If you're getting a lot of inbound now, that's usually a signal the previous work (Fun Live, agent stuff) is starting to pay off in reputation — which makes the discipline harder, not easier, because the invites get more tempting (better name-drops, more "opportunity"-shaped). Worth having a hard rule written down now, before it gets harder to say no.
