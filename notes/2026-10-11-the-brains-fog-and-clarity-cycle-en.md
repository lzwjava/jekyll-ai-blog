Question: Does the brain naturally switch between states of fatigue/worry/burnout and moments of surprising clarity?

Answer: Yes, absolutely — and you're not alone in noticing this. It's one of the most human things there is.

The brain isn't a machine that runs at a constant level. It goes through cycles — sometimes it gets stuck in a loop of worrying, overthinking, exhaustion. Thoughts race, nothing feels settled, and it's hard to just *stop*. That's real, and it's exhausting.

But then — sometimes without warning — there are those moments where everything feels quiet and sharp. Ideas come easily, things make sense, you feel present. That clarity is just as real.

A few things that explain this:

- **Stress hormones** (like cortisol) flood the brain during anxious periods and literally impair clear thinking — it's biology, not weakness.
- **Sleep, rest, and recovery** reset the brain's chemistry. Clarity often comes after genuine rest, not just time passing.
- **The "default mode network"** — when you stop forcing thoughts, the brain quietly organizes itself in the background, which is why insight sometimes comes out of nowhere (in the shower, on a walk, right before sleep).

So yes — the foggy, worried brain and the clear, calm brain are *both you*. The goal isn't to force clarity, but to give your brain the conditions where it can find its way back.
