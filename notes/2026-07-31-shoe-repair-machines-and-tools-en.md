Question: How does 裁缝 (tailoring/cobbling) repair clothes or shoes via machine, and what tools are used?

Answer:

**The core machine: the "patcher" (cylinder-arm sewing machine)**

Regular flatbed sewing machines can't get inside a shoe or a tubular garment (pant leg, sleeve, boot shaft). So repair shops use a **cylinder-arm patcher** — a narrow horizontal arm (12–18") replaces the flat bed, so you can slide a shoe, boot, bag, or sleeve over it and stitch the inside seam directly.

Mechanically it's still a standard **lockstitch** machine — needle thread + bobbin thread interlock — but the feed mechanism is different:
- No feed dog under the fabric (there's nothing under a shoe upper to push against).
- Instead the **presser foot itself rotates 360°** and does the feeding — it revolves to whatever direction the seam curves, since a shoe throat isn't a straight line. This is the single distinguishing trick of a patcher vs. a normal machine.
The are used to repair stitching and tears in shoe and boot uppers. These machines are unique in that they are fed entirely by the presser foot, which revolves in a full 360º circle.

**Thread**: bonded nylon or polyester thread, typically #69 or smaller, up to #138 for heavier work — much stronger/more abrasion-resistant than cotton, since sole/upper seams take constant flex + friction.

**Full toolkit for shoe/leather repair:**
| Tool | Job |
|---|---|
| Cylinder-arm patcher (e.g. Cowboy CB29) | stitch uppers, seams, patches |
| Awl + waxed thread (hand, no motor) | quick fixes, welt stitching, spots the machine can't reach |
| Skiving knife | thins leather edges before gluing/folding so seams don't bulge |
| Sole press / clamp | bonds new sole with contact cement under pressure |
| Finisher (buffing/sanding wheel stack) | shapes and smooths edge after resoling |
| Last (shoe-shaped anvil) | shoe sits on it while nailing/gluing so shape holds |
| Heat gun / iron | reactivates contact cement, softens old glue |

**For clothing (not shoes)**, it's just a regular flatbed **lockstitch or overlock machine** — no cylinder arm needed since fabric lies flat — plus scissors, seam ripper, and an iron for pressing.
