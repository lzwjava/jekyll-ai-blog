Question: **Who founded Shennan Circuits (深南电路), who were the co-founders, and what is the story behind its creation?**

Answer:

The interesting thing is that **Shennan Circuits does not really have a “Steve Jobs-style founder.”** It was created in 1984 as a **three-party state-owned joint venture**, rather than as a private startup founded by an individual entrepreneur.

### 1. The original founders

On **May 14, 1984**, the Shenzhen government approved the establishment of **“深南电路公司”**.

The three founding organizations were:

| Founder                | Original stake | Background                                                                   |
| ---------------------- | -------------: | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **中航技深圳工贸中心 / 中航国际深圳** |            40% | China Aviation Technology / aviation-industry trading & manufacturing system |
| **南方动力机械公司**           |            30% | Aviation-engine / machinery industry                                         |
| **上海长江科学仪器厂**          |            30% | Shanghai scientific-instrument manufacturer                                  |

The initial investment was **RMB 6.94 million**. The business was specifically established to manufacture **printed circuit boards (PCBs)**. ([DFC Financial Website][1])

So if you ask:

> **“Who is the founder of 深南电路?”**

The historically accurate answer is:

**There was no single individual founder. The founders were these three institutional shareholders.**

The company itself also describes its history as beginning in **1984** rather than attributing its founding to a particular entrepreneur. ([SCC][2])

---

## 2. But there is a fascinating twist

The original three-party structure didn't last long.

Only a few months after establishment, **Shanghai Changjiang Scientific Instrument Factory decided not to participate** because it lacked sufficient manpower, resources and financial capacity.

So the remaining two parties:

```text
1984

中航国际深圳       40%
南方动力机械公司   30%
上海长江科学仪器厂 30%
       │
       │ Shanghai withdraws
       ▼

中航国际深圳       60%
南方动力机械公司   40%
```

They then signed a supplementary agreement and continued operating the company. ([Sina Finance][3])

That's actually a pretty important part of the company's origin story.

It wasn't:

> “Entrepreneur has an idea → raises VC → starts PCB company.”

It was more like:

> **China's aviation-industrial system + Shenzhen's early economic reform environment → identify PCB manufacturing as a strategic industrial capability → establish a joint venture → gradually build technical capability.**

---

# 3. Why Shenzhen + PCB in 1984?

This is where the historical context becomes interesting.

1984 was **very early in Shenzhen's development**.

China was beginning to open up economically, Shenzhen was becoming a special economic zone, and electronics/communications were becoming increasingly important.

PCB is a particularly foundational technology:

```text
IC
 ↓
component
 ↓
PCB
 ↓
electronic equipment
 ↓
communications / computer / industrial system
```

If you wanted to build a domestic electronics industry, you needed domestic PCB manufacturing capability.

And the founders weren't random companies.

Two of the three founding parties came from the **aviation/machinery industrial system**.

That gave the new company access to:

* engineering talent
* manufacturing experience
* industrial customers
* capital
* quality-control experience

while Shenzhen provided a rapidly developing manufacturing environment.

---

# 4. The first big transformation: communications

The really important strategic move came in the **1990s**.

Initially, SCC wasn't an AI/server company, obviously. It was a PCB manufacturer.

But during **1993–1994**, the company began shifting toward **communications equipment** and started producing higher-end double-sided and multilayer PCBs. ([SCC][4])

Then:

**1995**

> It achieved mass production of **12-layer PCBs**, described in its historical timeline as the highest-end domestic product at that time. ([SCC][4])

And it subsequently became a domestic pioneer in **communication backplanes**.

That's a crucial strategic trajectory:

```text
1984
basic PCB
   ↓
1990s
multilayer PCB
   ↓
communications PCB
   ↓
high-speed / high-density PCB
   ↓
Huawei / telecom ecosystem
   ↓
servers / data centers
   ↓
AI computing infrastructure
```

So today's AI-server business wasn't created overnight.

It's the result of **~40 years of climbing the electronics-complexity curve**.

---

# 5. It gradually became an engineering organization rather than just a factory

The company progressively invested in:

* multilayer PCB
* high-density interconnect
* high-frequency/high-speed PCB
* flexible/rigid-flex technology
* packaging substrates
* electronic assembly
* testing
* R&D

Its official history records milestones such as joining IPC in 1991, ISO9002 certification in 1997, and development of rigid-flex technology in the late 1990s. ([SCC][4])

This is an important distinction.

A low-end PCB factory competes mostly on:

```text
labor
price
capacity
```

A high-end PCB company competes increasingly on:

```text
process engineering
materials
signal integrity
layer count
yield
reliability
automation
customer qualification
IP
manufacturing know-how
```

That's how SCC moved up the value chain.

# 6. The next major move: packaging substrates

Then SCC made another very interesting transition:

```text
PCB
 │
 ├── traditional PCB
 │
 ├── high-speed PCB
 │
 └── IC packaging substrate
             ↑
             │
          closer to
          semiconductor
```

The company calls its current structure **“3-In-One”**:

```text
             Shennan Circuits

          ┌──── PCB ────┐
          │             │
          │             │
 Packaging Substrate ── Electronic Assembly
```

The three businesses share considerable technology, manufacturing capabilities and customers. ([Sina Finance][5])

This is actually a pretty smart industrial evolution:

**PCB → more sophisticated PCB → package substrate → electronics assembly**

Rather than abandoning its original capability, SCC repeatedly moved into adjacent higher-value layers.

---

# 7. What happened to the ownership?

This is also unusual compared with a typical startup.

SCC remained connected to the **China Aviation Industry / AVIC system**.

For example, at the time of its IPO prospectus, **中航国际控股** was its controlling shareholder, holding **92.99%**. ([Cninfo][6])

Then the company was reorganized into **深南电路股份有限公司** in 2014 and eventually went public on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange on **December 13, 2017**, ticker **002916**. ([10jqka][7])

So the ownership evolution is roughly:

```text
1984
institutional joint venture
        │
        ▼
1980s–2000s
AVIC-controlled industrial company
        │
        ▼
2014
joint-stock restructuring
        │
        ▼
2017
IPO → 002916
        │
        ▼
today
large publicly listed electronics manufacturer
```

---

# 8. The really interesting entrepreneurial lesson

If you're looking at SCC from a **startup/business-building perspective**, I think its story is more interesting than simply “Chinese PCB company.”

It is a case of **compounding manufacturing capability for 40+ years**.

The progression is approximately:

```text
1984
PCB manufacturing
        ↓
technical capability
        ↓
multilayer PCB
        ↓
communications
        ↓
high-end PCB
        ↓
packaging substrates
        ↓
electronic assembly
        ↓
global customers
        ↓
servers / data centers
        ↓
AI infrastructure
```

The company didn't need to correctly predict **AI in 1984**.

It only needed to keep asking:

> **What is the next technically adjacent, higher-value problem that our existing capabilities allow us to solve?**

That's a very different growth mechanism from a software startup.

And it explains why a company founded around **PCB manufacturing in 1984** can suddenly look like an **AI infrastructure beneficiary in 2026**: the AI boom is sitting on top of decades of accumulated manufacturing, materials, process and customer-qualification knowledge.
