Question: **Has Hong Kong been bypassed because Chinese businesses can now go directly from Shenzhen/Guangdong to global customers through the Internet and mainland ports?**

Answer: **Yes. This is one of the biggest structural changes from the 1980s/90s to today.** Your mental model is basically correct.

The key change is:

> **Hong Kong used to be the gateway. Today, much of the gateway function has moved directly into mainland China.**

### 1980s–90s

A simplified supply chain looked like:

```text
Guangdong factory
      ↓
   Hong Kong
      ↓
Hong Kong trader
      ↓
international buyer
      ↓
Hong Kong port / airport
      ↓
      world
```

Hong Kong provided a lot of the missing infrastructure:

* international banking
* foreign exchange
* lawyers/accountants
* international buyers
* shipping
* air cargo
* business information
* relatively frictionless international trade

So a Shenzhen/Dongguan factory might have been **physically in China but commercially connected to the world through Hong Kong**.

---

### Today

A Shenzhen company can increasingly do:

```text
Shenzhen factory
      ↓
Amazon / Shopify / Alibaba.com / TikTok Shop
      ↓
US / Europe / Japan customers
      ↓
Amazon FBA / overseas warehouse
```

And the entrepreneur can manage much of it from a laptop.

Amazon itself now offers logistics directly from **mainland China to its overseas fulfillment network**, without requiring Hong Kong as an intermediate commercial hub. ([Sell on Amazon][1])

And the development is getting even more explicit: in **April 2026, Amazon opened a Global Warehousing & Distribution facility in Shenzhen**, allowing Chinese sellers to put inventory into Shenzhen and have Amazon handle warehousing, customs, cross-border transportation and replenishment to the US Amazon network. ([Global Selling Amazon][2])

That's almost the perfect example of the transition you're describing.

---

## Shenzhen is basically doing something Hong Kong used to do

Look at this:

**1990**

```text
Factory → Hong Kong trader → foreign customer
```

**2026**

```text
Factory → Shenzhen entrepreneur → Amazon → foreign customer
```

The Shenzhen entrepreneur can now have:

* a US Amazon store
* US dollar revenue
* overseas warehouse inventory
* international advertising
* foreign customers
* international logistics

without necessarily having a Hong Kong company or Hong Kong intermediary.

And Shenzhen is extremely strong in this.

Official Shenzhen data said that in Q1 2024, the city had about **150,000 cross-border e-commerce exporters**, and roughly **one-third of Chinese Amazon sellers were from Shenzhen**. ([Shenzhen Government][3])

That's an extraordinary concentration.

---

## And yes, the Internet removes another layer

This is perhaps even more important than the port.

In 1990, imagine a Chinese factory owner wanting to sell to America.

He needs:

> Hong Kong agent → trade fair → foreign buyer → telephone/fax → bank → shipping company → paperwork

Today:

```text
Alibaba / Amazon / Google / social media
             ↓
       find customers
             ↓
       online payment
             ↓
       fulfillment API
             ↓
          logistics
             ↓
          customer
```

The **information layer has collapsed**.

You don't necessarily need to physically meet the foreign buyer anymore.

That's why someone sitting in Shenzhen can operate a genuinely global business while rarely interacting with Hong Kong.

---

# And you're right about ports — with one correction

It's not simply:

> "Cargo now goes to Shanghai or Guangzhou."

It depends heavily on where the factory is and what route/carrier is being used.

For a Shenzhen/Dongguan manufacturer, the major nearby gateways include:

* **Yantian** in Shenzhen
* **Shekou** in Shenzhen
* **Nansha** in Guangzhou
* **Hong Kong**
* and, depending on the supply chain, other mainland ports

So the geography increasingly looks like:

```text
                 WORLD
                   ↑
       ┌───────────┼───────────┐
       │           │           │
   Hong Kong     Yantian     Nansha
                   ↑           ↑
                   └─────┬─────┘
                         │
              Shenzhen / Dongguan
                         │
                    Guangdong
```

Hong Kong hasn't disappeared from the system. Its port still handled about **13 million TEUs in 2025**, but that was down 5.1% year-over-year. ([GovHK][4])

So the important story isn't:

> **Hong Kong → dead**

It's:

> **Hong Kong → no longer the unavoidable gateway.**

That's a huge difference.

# And this explains something interesting about Shenzhen

In the 1980s, **Shenzhen needed Hong Kong**.

Today, **Shenzhen increasingly has its own complete ecosystem**:

```text
hardware
   +
factories
   +
R&D
   +
capital
   +
software
   +
e-commerce
   +
cross-border logistics
   +
international customers
```

That's why Shenzhen feels so different from a normal Chinese city.

It isn't merely a manufacturing city anymore.

It's becoming a **global business operating environment sitting directly on the Chinese mainland**.

And Amazon choosing Shenzhen for its first global-origin warehouse is almost symbolically perfect: Amazon is effectively saying,

> "We don't need the seller to first move inventory through Hong Kong. **Let's put the global distribution infrastructure next to the factory.**" ([Global Selling Amazon][2])

---

## The really deep change

I think this is what you are noticing when comparing old Hong Kong with modern Shenzhen:

**1990:**

> Geography determines business.

**2026:**

> Software partially determines business.

Previously, being physically close to Hong Kong's banks, ports, traders and international companies was incredibly valuable.

Now you can be physically in Shenzhen, put your product on Amazon, communicate with a customer in California, receive USD revenue, advertise globally, and have an algorithm coordinate your inventory.

The physical world **still matters enormously** — factories, ports, airplanes, ships, warehouses — but the **coordination layer has moved onto the Internet**.

That's why the old Hong Kong model hasn't disappeared; **it has been decomposed and distributed across mainland China + global platforms.**

And honestly, this is probably one of the most important reasons **Shenzhen became what Hong Kong was 30–40 years ago, but with a much larger industrial base behind it.**
