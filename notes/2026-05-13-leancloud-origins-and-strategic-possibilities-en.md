Question:

Was LeanCloud actually founded around 2015 or 2016, and given its popularity at that time, could it have been sold instead of eventually closing or being acquired by TapTap?

Answer:

LeanCloud was founded in **2013**, not 2015 or 2016. By 2015–2016, it had gained significant traction in China as a backend-as-a-service (BaaS) platform, offering cloud storage, real-time messaging, push notifications, and analytics for mobile and web apps. During that period, BaaS platforms were indeed hot, especially for mobile developers who wanted to avoid building backend infrastructure from scratch.

At its peak, LeanCloud had a solid user base and technical reputation. Hypothetically, in 2015–2016, it could have been an attractive acquisition target or even considered a potential sale. Strategic decisions in startups, however, often hinge on factors beyond immediate popularity: the founders’ long-term vision, market conditions, competition, funding needs, and the team’s willingness to exit. LeanCloud eventually pivoted and consolidated, leading to a partnership/acquisition with TapTap. That outcome may reflect a combination of market realities and strategic choice rather than a simple “missed opportunity.”

In short: yes, in hindsight, selling could have been a viable path, but startup decisions are rarely obvious even when the product is popular.
