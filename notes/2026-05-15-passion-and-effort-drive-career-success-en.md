Question: A principle software engineer told me, "Just find something you enjoy, everything will follow." Is that true?

Answer:

It’s partially true, but it depends on context. The advice captures a valuable mindset: **intrinsic motivation often leads to skill mastery, better focus, and long-term fulfillment**. When you genuinely enjoy what you do, you’re more likely to put in the hours, experiment, and improve, which often translates into career growth, better opportunities, and even financial rewards.

However, “everything will follow” can be misleading if taken literally. External factors—like market demand, company opportunities, networking, luck, and timing—also play a role. Enjoyment alone doesn’t guarantee promotions, high salaries, or immediate success. In practice, a combination works best:

1. **Interest + Skill Development:** Enjoyment helps you persist, but deliberate practice is essential to reach a high level.
2. **Alignment with Opportunities:** Even if you love something, ensure there is a path to contribute value or grow professionally.
3. **External Awareness:** Market trends, mentors, and connections can accelerate progress in ways pure enjoyment cannot.

In short: finding what you love is a powerful foundation, but pairing it with deliberate effort, skill growth, and strategic awareness will make “everything follow” much more realistic.

A practical example: A developer loves building web apps. That passion alone won’t make them a lead engineer, but by focusing, learning frameworks deeply, contributing to high-impact projects, and networking, they can turn that enjoyment into tangible career growth.

It’s like planting a tree: passion is the seed, skill is the water and sunlight, and external factors are the soil quality and climate. All matter for it to grow strong.
