Question: Can someone understand a country deeply without living there for a long time, because most information is online?

Answer:

Yes, **to a large degree**. The internet has fundamentally changed how we learn about places.

For many practical layers of a country:

* **Food**: restaurants, menus, prices, reviews, delivery apps, videos
* **Housing**: rental platforms, property prices, neighborhood tours
* **Transportation**: Google Maps, local apps, transit guides
* **Banking/finance**: app screenshots, API docs, user experiences, regulations
* **Shopping**: e-commerce, product reviews
* **Culture**: YouTube, TikTok, forums, blogs
* **Immigration/business**: official websites, communities, case studies

A motivated person can build a very detailed "virtual model" of a country before visiting.

Your Hong Kong example is actually interesting. Spending only a few days physically there does not prevent someone from understanding a specific domain deeply. If you worked on Hong Kong banking projects in an international bank, you may understand parts of the **banking system, product flows, compliance requirements, and user experience** better than many residents who only use banking apps as consumers.

This is similar to software:

A developer who maintains a payment system may understand payment architecture better than millions of users who have used the payment product for years.

But there is a distinction:

**Information knowledge ≠ embodied experience.**

Online research can give you:

* maps
* rules
* prices
* workflows
* opinions
* statistics

But living somewhere gives you:

* social intuition
* hidden norms
* how people actually behave
* small inconveniences
* emotional feeling of a place
* relationships

For example, someone can know Hong Kong:

* MTR routes,
* bank products,
* restaurants,
* visa rules,
* salaries,

but after living there they discover:

* how people communicate at work,
* what neighborhoods feel like at 11pm,
* how colleagues actually think,
* which "official rules" are flexible in practice.

The ratio of online knowledge is probably much higher than before. For many modern cities, maybe **70–90% of transactional knowledge** can be acquired remotely. But the last 10–30% of tacit knowledge often requires being there.

The bigger change in the AI era is that this gap is shrinking further. An agent can continuously collect:

* local news,
* regulations,
* restaurant changes,
* price changes,
* user discussions,
* videos,

and maintain a living knowledge base.

So a future "digital citizen" may know many countries better than a casual local resident. A person who systematically studies a place can indeed outperform someone who was born there but never explored deeply.
