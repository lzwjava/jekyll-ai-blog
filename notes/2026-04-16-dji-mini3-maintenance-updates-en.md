**Question:**
After 1–2 years of using a DJI Mini 3, do we need to upgrade hardware or software, deal with QuickTransfer issues and Wi-Fi passwords, recalibrate alignment (gimbal/IMU), and are there stricter flight altitude limits (e.g., 50–100 m) in big cities in China?

---

**Answer:**

You’re mixing several real issues that happen over time with drones like the DJI Mini 3—let’s break them down clearly:

---

## 1. Do you need to upgrade hardware after 1–2 years?

**Usually: no.**

* DJI drones are not like phones—you don’t *need* hardware upgrades just because of time.
* The Mini 3 can easily last **3–5 years** if:

  * Battery health is maintained
  * Motors are not physically damaged
  * Firmware remains supported

**What *does* degrade:**

* **Battery** (most important): after ~200–300 charge cycles, flight time drops
* **Propellers**: may need replacement (cheap and normal)
* Motors/gimbal: only if damaged, not time-based

👉 So this is more **maintenance**, not “upgrade.”

---

## 2. DJI Fly app & firmware updates (iOS)

The DJI Fly app **does need updates**:

* DJI frequently updates:

  * Firmware (drone + controller)
  * App compatibility (especially iOS updates)
  * Geo-restriction rules (important in China)

**Common issues after long gaps:**

* App version too old → cannot connect
* Firmware mismatch → forced update before flying
* iOS system upgrade → breaks older DJI Fly versions

👉 Best practice:

* Update app + firmware **before flying again after long inactivity**

---

## 3. QuickTransfer & Wi-Fi connection issues

This part you mentioned is very real.

### What happens:

* QuickTransfer relies on **direct Wi-Fi connection** between phone and drone
* After time:

  * Password may be forgotten
  * Auto-connect may fail
  * You must **manually connect to drone Wi-Fi**

### Typical fixes:

* Reset Wi-Fi on drone (hold power button)
* Manually enter password (shown in app or drone)
* Re-pair in DJI Fly

👉 This is normal friction—not a hardware issue.

---

## 4. Calibration (alignment issues)

You mentioned “align vertically/horizontally”—this refers to:

* **Gimbal calibration**
* **IMU calibration**
* **Compass calibration**

### Do you need it regularly?

* Not frequently, but needed when:

  * Drone hasn’t been used for months
  * You changed location significantly
  * App shows warnings like:

    * “Gimbal horizon tilted”
    * “IMU error”
    * “Compass interference”

### Good news:

* DJI automates most of this
* You only recalibrate when prompted

👉 No need to “manually align motors”—that’s not a real user task.

---

## 5. Flight restrictions in China (important)

Yes—this has become **much stricter**, especially in big cities.

### Typical rules (vary by city):

* **Altitude limits**:

  * Common default: **120 meters**
  * Some areas: **≤100 m or even 50 m**
* **No-fly zones**:

  * Airports (very strict)
  * City centers
  * Government areas
* **Real-name registration required**
* Some areas require **pre-approval**

### DJI enforcement:

* Built-in **geofencing**
* App may:

  * Limit altitude automatically
  * Prevent takeoff
  * Require unlocking authorization

👉 In big cities like Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou:

* Flying freely is much harder now than before

---

## 6. Overall reality (what actually changed)

After 1–2 years, the “friction” you feel comes from:

| Area        | What changed                      |
| ----------- | --------------------------------- |
| Hardware    | Mostly fine                       |
| Software    | Needs updates                     |
| Connection  | Less smooth (QuickTransfer/Wi-Fi) |
| Calibration | Occasional                        |
| Regulations | **Much stricter**                 |

---

## 7. Practical advice

If you take your Mini 3 out after a long time:

1. Update DJI Fly app
2. Update firmware (drone + controller)
3. Charge all batteries
4. Check propellers
5. Reconnect Wi-Fi manually if needed
6. Calibrate only if prompted
7. Check local flight restrictions **before takeoff**

---

## Bottom line

* You **don’t need hardware upgrades**
* Most issues are **software + connectivity + regulation**
* The biggest real change is **airspace control in China**, not the drone itself
