Question: Why do many Chinese companies prefer direct hiring over outsourcing/contractors, and what does this reveal about their organizational culture?

Answer:

You're touching on something real and nuanced. Here's how to think about it:

**Why Chinese companies (especially startups) avoid outsourcing**

- **Control and loyalty culture** — Many Chinese firms, especially founder-led ones, prefer people who are "all in" on the mission. Contractors feel transactional; direct hires feel like soldiers.
- **IP and trust paranoia** — Outsourcing code or sensitive work to third parties is seen as a security risk. Keeping everything in-house means tighter control over trade secrets.
- **Speed through alignment** — A full-time employee absorbs the company culture, understands unspoken priorities, and can pivot fast. Contractors need briefing every time.
- **Cost tolerance tradeoff** — Chinese startups often accept the overhead of HR/payroll as worthwhile, because they want deep commitment, not deliverables.

**What the hiring process reveals about hierarchy**

You're right that when a company hires directly — even for junior roles — it pulls in tech leads, CEOs, or senior managers into interviews. This reflects:

- **High power distance culture** — Decisions flow from the top. Even "small" hiring decisions carry weight because the person joining becomes part of the inner circle.
- **Long-term relationship framing** — Hiring isn't just filling a role; it's an implicit long-term commitment, so it deserves senior attention.
- **Bureaucracy as quality signal** — The more hoops, the more the company signals it takes team composition seriously.

**The tradeoff they accept**

| | Outsourcing/Contractor | Direct Hire |
|---|---|---|
| Speed to fill | Fast | Slow |
| Overhead | Low | High (HR, benefits, culture) |
| Commitment | Low | High |
| Control | Low | High |
| Hierarchy involved | Minimal | Often senior-heavy |

So the "diligence" you observe is real — but it's also a **cultural and strategic choice**: they're betting that a tight, loyal, in-house team outperforms a flexible contractor network over time. Whether that's always right depends on the industry and growth stage.
