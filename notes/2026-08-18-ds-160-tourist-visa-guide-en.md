Question: **How should I fill out the DS-160 for a U.S. tourist visa?**

Answer: Yes. If your purpose is **tourism / visiting the U.S. temporarily**, you would normally apply for a **B-2 visitor visa** (or B1/B2 if the application offers the combined category). The DS-160 is mostly a detailed record of your identity, passport, travel plans, work/education, family, and security/travel history.

The official DS-160 is here:

[DS-160 Online Application — U.S. Department of State](https://ceac.state.gov/genniv/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The State Department explicitly says answers must be **in English using English characters**, except where the form asks for your native-alphabet name. ([Travel.state.gov][1])

### 1. Before you start

Have these beside you:

* Current passport
* Old passports, especially if they contain previous U.S. visas/travel
* Your home address and phone number
* Your employment information
* Education history
* Your parents' information
* Spouse/children information
* Approximate dates of previous international travel
* U.S. contact information, if you have someone there
* Rough travel plan
* Digital visa photo

The State Department specifically recommends having your passport, travel itinerary if already arranged, previous U.S. travel dates, and CV/resume available. ([Travel.state.gov][1])

**You do NOT need to buy an air ticket before applying.** If you haven't finalized the trip, don't invent a reservation.

---

## 2. Getting Started

When you start a new application, the site gives you an **Application ID** and asks you to choose a security question.

Save these somewhere secure.

You can stop and continue later. The State Department says an online application can be retrieved for 30 days; you can also save the application to your computer for longer-term access. ([Travel.state.gov][1])

I recommend:

```text
DS-160 Application ID: <PLACEHOLDER>
Security question: <PLACEHOLDER>
Security answer: <PLACEHOLDER>
```

Don't send me the actual security answer.

# 3. Personal Information

### Surname

Use exactly what appears in your passport.

For example, if passport says:

```text
Surname: LI
Given Names: ZHIWEI
```

then:

```text
Surname: LI
Given Names: ZHIWEI
```

Don't convert it to Chinese pinyin differently from your passport. The State Department specifically says surnames should match the passport. ([Travel.state.gov][1])

### Full Name in Native Alphabet

For a Chinese passport, enter your Chinese name here if the field asks for it.

For example:

```text
李智维
```

### Other Names Used

If you've never legally or professionally used another name:

```text
No
```

If you have used another legal name, answer accordingly.

---

# 4. Travel Information

This is one of the most important sections.

If your purpose is tourism:

### Purpose of Trip

Select something equivalent to:

```text
TEMPORARY VISITOR (B)
    TOURISM / MEDICAL TREATMENT (B2)
```

or the B1/B2 option if that's what the current form presents.

### Have you made specific travel plans?

If you haven't booked anything:

```text
No
```

Then you can provide an approximate intended trip.

For example:

```text
Intended Date of Arrival: <MONTH/DAY/YEAR>
Intended Length of Stay: 14 days
```

Don't claim you have booked hotels/flights if you haven't.

### Address Where You Will Stay

If you have a hotel:

```text
Hotel Name: <HOTEL NAME>
Address: <HOTEL ADDRESS>
```

If staying with a friend/relative, use their actual information.

---

# 5. U.S. Point of Contact

If you're staying at a hotel and don't have a personal contact in America, the hotel can generally be used as the point of contact where the form permits it.

Don't invent an American friend just to make the application look stronger.

Truthfulness matters much more than trying to optimize individual answers. The State Department warns that false or misleading statements can have serious consequences. ([Travel.state.gov][1])

---

# 6. Family Information

You'll be asked about:

* Father
* Mother
* Spouse
* Children
* Other relatives in the U.S.

Answer literally.

For example:

```text
Father: <REAL INFORMATION>
Mother: <REAL INFORMATION>
Spouse: <REAL INFORMATION>
Children: <REAL INFORMATION>
```

If you have relatives in the U.S., **don't hide them**.

---

# 7. Present Work / Education / Training

This section deserves particular attention in your case.

Use your **actual current employment**.

For example, if you're currently employed:

```text
Primary Occupation: COMPUTER SCIENCE / ENGINEERING
Present Employer: <ACTUAL EMPLOYER>
Job Title: <ACTUAL TITLE>
Employer Address: <ACTUAL ADDRESS>
Monthly Salary: <ACTUAL MONTHLY SALARY>
```

Don't change your occupation to "business owner" or "AI researcher" merely because it sounds stronger.

Use the closest truthful category available.

You'll also provide previous employment and education.

For education, give your actual history. If a school asks for degree information, don't claim a degree you haven't received.

---

# 8. Previous U.S. Travel

If you've never been to the U.S.:

```text
Have you ever been in the U.S.? → No
```

If you've previously visited, give the dates as accurately as possible.

The State Department says applicants may need the dates of their **last five U.S. trips** and may also be asked about international travel during the previous five years. ([Travel.state.gov][1])

---

# 9. Previous Visa Applications

You'll encounter questions such as:

```text
Have you ever been issued a U.S. visa?
```

If you've never had one:

```text
No
```

If you've previously applied and were refused, **say yes** and provide the requested details.

Do not try to hide a previous refusal.

---

# 10. Social Media

The DS-160 may ask for social-media identifiers.

Use your **actual identifiers/accounts requested by the form**.

Don't fabricate accounts.

Also don't give passwords.

---

# 11. Security Questions

You'll see a long series of questions concerning things such as:

* Criminal history
* Immigration violations
* Visa fraud
* Terrorism
* Certain diseases
* Illegal drugs
* Military service
* Organizations
* Security-related activities

Answer based on the actual facts.

Most ordinary applicants will answer **No** to many of these, but don't blindly select No without reading each question.

---

# 12. Photo

You'll upload a U.S. visa-compliant digital photo.

The State Department provides the official photo requirements. ([Travel.state.gov][2])

If the upload fails, don't panic. The DS-160 FAQ says that if the confirmation page shows an **X** where the photo should be, you may need to bring a compliant printed photograph according to the instructions of the consulate where you're applying. ([Travel.state.gov][1])

---

# 13. Review EVERYTHING

Before submitting, go through the entire form.

Especially verify:

```text
Passport number
Surname
Given names
Date of birth
Place of birth
Nationality
Marital status
Employment
Education
Travel history
U.S. travel history
Previous visa applications
Family information
U.S. contact
Intended trip
Social media information
```

A typo in your passport number is much more important than whether your English sounds elegant.

---

# 14. Sign and Submit

At the end you'll electronically sign the DS-160.

The State Department says your electronic signature certifies that the information is true and complete to the best of your knowledge. ([Travel.state.gov][1])

After submission:

**Print/save the DS-160 confirmation page with the barcode.**

You do **not** need to print the entire DS-160 for the interview; the State Department says the confirmation page is what you need to bring. ([Travel.state.gov][3])
