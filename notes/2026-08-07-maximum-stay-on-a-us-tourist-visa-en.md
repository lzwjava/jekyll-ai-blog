**Question: How many months can someone stay in the USA with a travel visa?**

If you mean a **US tourist visa (B-2) / visitor visa**, the usual rule is:

* **Up to 6 months per entry** is common.
* The exact allowed stay is decided by the **CBP immigration officer when you enter** the US.
* The official date is recorded on your **I-94 arrival record**, not the visa expiration date. ([U.S. State Department Travel][1])

Example:

* You have a **10-year B1/B2 visa**.
* You enter the US on January 1.
* CBP gives you an I-94 until July 1.
* You must leave by July 1.

The visa validity (for example, 10 years) only means you can **request entry during that period**; it does not mean you can stay 10 years. ([U.S. State Department Travel][1])

Important:

* You **cannot work** in the US on a tourist visa.
* Staying close to 6 months repeatedly (for example, 6 months in the US, a short trip abroad, then another 6 months) may cause CBP to question whether you are actually living in the US rather than visiting. ([CBP Help Center][2])
* If you need more time, a B1/B2 visitor can apply for an extension before the I-94 expires, but approval is not automatic. ([USCIS][3])

For comparison:

* **ESTA (Visa Waiver Program):** maximum **90 days** per visit. ([USAGov][4])
* **B1/B2 tourist visa:** commonly **up to 6 months** per visit. ([USCIS][3])

For someone from China planning to visit the US, the practical safe pattern is usually:

* normal tourism: a few weeks to 3 months;
* longer family visit: up to ~6 months if you can explain your purpose and have strong ties back home. ([CBP Help Center][2])
