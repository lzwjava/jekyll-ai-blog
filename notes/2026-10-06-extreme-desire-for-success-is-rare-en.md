Question: Is it true that "people with extreme desire for success are rare" (as Wang Xing from Meituan said)?

Answer:

Yes, this idea is close to a famous view attributed to Wang Xing: people who are **"extremely eager for success" are not many, and people willing to pay an extraordinary price are even fewer**. ([Zhihu][1])

The key point is not just **wanting success**. Many people say:

* "I want to become rich."
* "I want to build a great company."
* "I want freedom."

But fewer people are willing to accept the cost:

* 5–10 years of uncertainty
* repeated failures
* social misunderstanding
* working when others relax
* learning continuously without immediate reward
* risking reputation and money

The difference is:

```
Normal desire:
"I want success if the path is comfortable."

Extreme desire:
"I want success enough to redesign my life around the goal."
```

Many people like the **result** but dislike the **process**.

For example, many people admire startup founders after success. But before success, the founder often looks irrational:

* leaving a stable job
* spending years with no guarantee
* building something nobody believes in
* facing competitors with more money

Wang Xing himself had multiple entrepreneurial attempts before Meituan succeeded. His path included failures before reaching Meituan's success. ([TechNode][2])

However, extreme desire alone is not enough.

A useful formula:

```
Success ≈ Desire × Ability × Learning Speed × Time × Luck
```

Extreme desire gives energy, but without:

* correct direction
* ability to execute
* ability to learn from reality

it can become blind persistence.

The strongest founders usually combine:

```
high ambition
+
long patience
+
fast learning
+
accurate judgment
```

This is why Wang Xing also emphasizes patience and understanding trends, not only hard work. ([Sina Finance][3])

For someone building AI products/startups, this trait matters because the feedback loop is often slow:

```
Build → nobody cares → improve → try again → small users → revenue → scale
```

Most people quit during the "nobody cares" phase.

The rare people are not necessarily those who work 18 hours every day. They are those who can maintain a 10-year direction while continuously adapting.

I can also analyze the difference between "extreme desire for success" in founders like Wang Xing, Musk, and Liang Wenfeng versus ordinary career ambition.
