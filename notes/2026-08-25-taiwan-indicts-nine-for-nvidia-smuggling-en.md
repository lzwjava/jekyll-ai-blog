**Question:** What is the current status of the Taiwan case involving people who bought Nvidia B300 Supermicro servers (without actually using them in Taiwan) and smuggled them to mainland China?

**Answer:**

This case has moved rapidly, with major developments just in the past day (August 24–25, 2026):

**The Core Scheme**

The alleged scheme centered on 130 B300 servers ordered from Super Micro. False documents were created to make it look like the servers would be installed and used at a rented facility in Taiwan. This is exactly the pattern you described — purchased but never actually deployed locally.

Of those 130 servers, 74 were shipped to Chinese customers through multiple routes, including direct shipments and transshipments through Indonesia, Japan, and Hong Kong. The remaining 56 were caught by Taiwan customs after officials detected irregularities.

**The Five-Step Cover-Up**

The indictment sets out how "a compliance regime designed to track every unit was defeated from the inside," thanks to a scheme set up to run through five different points in the supply chain.

Some defendants set up a company in Japan to help transfer the servers, and some allegedly created fake websites and falsified information to evade export restrictions.

**Supermicro Insiders Were Involved**

A senior sales manager at Supermicro's Taiwan subsidiary had reportedly coached the buyers through the review process. A second Supermicro manager reportedly caught wind of where the servers were really ending up, but was cut in on the commissions rather than speaking up.

**The Indictments (August 24, 2026)**

Taiwan has indicted nine people — including two senior employees of Supermicro and one Nvidia employee identified by his surname Chang — along with senior employees of several other companies involved in arranging customers and shipments.

Prosecutors are seeking a five-year sentence for Chang, whom they describe as the central figure in the alleged scheme. The charges involve forgery and breach of trust.

**Separate U.S. Case (Even Larger)**

There is also a parallel U.S. federal case involving a much bigger operation: U.S. prosecutors charged Super Micro co-founder and board member Yih-Shyan "Wally" Liaw, Taiwan sales manager Ruei-Tsang "Steven" Chang, and contractor Ting-Wei "Willy" Sun with conspiring to violate export control laws, smuggling, and defrauding the U.S. The trio's scheme allegedly diverted $2.5 billion in Supermicro servers containing restricted Nvidia AI GPUs (including banned H200 and B200 models) to China between 2024 and 2025. Two of the three have been arrested; the third remains a fugitive.
