Question: Explain the meaning of “高潜力-高风险” in the talent nine-box grid.

Answer:

In a **talent nine-box grid（人才九宫格）**, “高潜力-高风险” means:

> A person who has strong future potential, but whose current performance, behavior, or stability is uncertain.

The phrase you quoted:

> “绩效波动大但学习能力强、或思想超前但缺乏经验/稳定的‘野马型’员工”

means:

* **绩效波动大** — performance is inconsistent:

  * Sometimes produces amazing results.
  * Sometimes fails to deliver.
  * Not predictable yet.

* **学习能力强** — learns very fast:

  * Can absorb new technology quickly.
  * Can solve problems beyond current experience.
  * Often improves dramatically.

* **思想超前** — thinks ahead:

  * Has unusual ideas.
  * Sees trends earlier than others.
  * Challenges existing processes.

* **缺乏经验/稳定** — lacks maturity:

  * May not know how to execute consistently.
  * May underestimate organizational constraints.
  * May have weak communication or management skills.

“野马型” is a metaphor:

A **horse** that runs very fast and has great energy, but is difficult to control.

Example:

A young AI engineer:

* Builds a prototype using a new model architecture in 3 days.
* Understands papers from OpenAI/DeepMind quickly.
* Has creative ideas nobody else has.

But:

* Misses deadlines.
* Doesn't document code.
* Changes direction frequently.
* Has difficulty collaborating.

Management may classify this person as:

```
Performance: medium / unstable
Potential: very high
Risk: high
```

The key idea:

**High potential ≠ high current value.**

A high-potential/high-risk person is an investment:

```
Today:
    unstable contributor

Future possibility:
    technical leader / founder / researcher

Company challenge:
    provide freedom + mentorship + structure
```

Many famous innovators would have looked like this early:

* Steve Jobs — extremely visionary but difficult personality.
* Elon Musk — high-risk style, unconventional execution.
* Many early-stage startup engineers are similar.

The opposite categories:

```
                 Potential

              Low          High

Performance
High      Reliable star   Future leader

Low       Poor performer  Wild horse
```

The “wild horse” problem is not lack of ability. It is **conversion**:

```
raw ability
     +
discipline
     +
experience
     +
team skills
     =
high-impact leader
```

A good manager does not immediately suppress this type. They try to **channel the energy**:

* Give challenging problems.
* Reduce unnecessary bureaucracy.
* Add experienced mentors.
* Define clear goals.
* Build execution habits.

A bad manager often sees only the risk:

> “This person is difficult.”

A good manager sees the potential:

> “This person is not mature yet, but may become exceptional.”

For engineering/AI fields, this category appears frequently because frontier work rewards people who are unconventional, curious, and willing to explore. The challenge is turning exploration ability into reliable shipping ability.
