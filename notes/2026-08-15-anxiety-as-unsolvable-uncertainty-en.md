Question: How do you deal with anxiety, especially when your mind keeps running and worrying about the future?

Answer:

The most useful framing is: **anxiety is often your brain trying to solve uncertainty that cannot be solved right now.** You don't need to eliminate the feeling; you need to stop letting it control your next action.

### 1. Separate the problem from the prediction

When anxiety says:

> “What if I fail?”
> “What if I can't make it?”
> “What if something goes wrong?”

turn it into something concrete:

```text
Fear:
    "My future is uncertain."

Actual problem:
    "I don't know whether X will work."

Action:
    "What experiment can I run this week to get information?"
```

This is particularly powerful for an engineer because **uncertainty becomes an experiment rather than a threat**.

### 2. Don't argue with anxiety for hours

A common failure mode is:

```text
anxiety
  ↓
think harder
  ↓
temporary relief
  ↓
new uncertainty
  ↓
think even harder
  ↓
more anxiety
```

The solution isn't necessarily another 3 hours of reasoning.

Instead:

```text
Notice → name → act
```

For example:

> “I'm anxious about whether this project will succeed.”

Then:

```bash
# not:
think_about_project.py --hours 3

# instead:
ship_one_small_experiment
```

Action gives your brain new evidence.

### 3. Control the physical state first

Anxiety isn't purely intellectual. If you're sleep-deprived, overstimulated, caffeinated, sitting at a computer for 12 hours, etc., your reasoning system is operating on top of an activated nervous system.

Try something extremely boring:

* walk for 10–20 minutes
* slow breathing, especially a **longer exhale**
* eat normally
* reduce caffeine if you're highly activated
* get enough sleep
* step away from the screen

You don't have to *feel calm* before doing these things.

### 4. Use a tiny “worry budget”

Give yourself a fixed period:

```text
20:00–20:20
    worry / write down everything

20:20
    stop
    choose one actionable item
```

During the day, when the thought returns:

> “Not now. That's in my 20-minute slot.”

This sounds almost stupidly simple, but it teaches your brain that **a thought doesn't automatically deserve immediate computation**.

### 5. Accept some anxiety

This is probably the deeper point.

If you try to reach:

> “I will start only when I feel confident.”

you can become trapped.

A healthier target is:

> **“I'm anxious, and I'm doing it anyway.”**

Researchers, founders, engineers, parents, athletes—all encounter uncertainty. The difference isn't necessarily that successful people don't experience fear. They become better at **functioning while uncertainty is present**.

Think:

```text
confidence ≠ prerequisite for action

action → evidence → competence → confidence
```

not:

```text
confidence → action
```

### 6. If anxiety becomes persistent or disabling

If anxiety is happening most days, seriously disrupting sleep/work/relationships, causing panic attacks, or becoming difficult to control, don't treat it as merely a productivity problem. A mental-health professional can help, and evidence-based treatments such as CBT are quite effective.

And if you ever feel that you might hurt yourself or you're in immediate danger, seek urgent local help rather than trying to reason through it alone.

The key idea I'd keep:

> **You don't need to convince yourself that everything will be okay. You only need to figure out what the next controllable action is.**

For an engineer, anxiety can often be converted from **“predict the entire future”** into **“run the next experiment.”**
