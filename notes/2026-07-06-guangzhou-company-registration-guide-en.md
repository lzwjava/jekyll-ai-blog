**Question:** Explain this Chinese article about registering a company in Guangzhou (materials, process, costs).

**Answer:**

It's a marketing/guide post from a corporate-services agency ("财税小行家") walking through how to register a limited company in Guangzhou. Here's the substance, stripped of the sales pitch:

**1. Five things you need before registering**

- **Company name** — format is 广州 + brand name + industry word + 有限公司 (e.g., "Guangzhou XX Technology Co., Ltd.").
- **Business scope (经营范围)** — selected from a government dropdown system. The article's advice: don't list too many activities (can raise your tax burden) or too few (limits what you can legally invoice for).
- **Shareholder info** — ID cards (front/back) for the legal representative (法人) and supervisor (监事). Key rule: the same person cannot be both. So a solo founder technically needs a second person (often a spouse or family member) to serve as supervisor. You also define ownership split, capital contributions, and voting arrangements.
- **Registered capital (注册资金)** — China uses a *subscribed* capital system (认缴制): you declare a number but don't actually deposit the money upfront. Too small looks unserious to clients/banks; too large increases your legal liability (shareholders are liable up to the subscribed amount). Benchmark against similar companies.
- **Registered address** — must be commercial-use. This is the article's main pitch: if you don't need a physical office, you can use a "business secretary address" (商务秘书地址, i.e., a virtual/hosted address) which is much cheaper than renting office space. Legal for tech, trading, and media companies. You can switch to a real address later when you hire.

**2. The process**

1. Name check (核名) — free, same-day, via the Guangzhou Market Supervision Administration website.
2. Application submission — free, 3–7 working days for approval; resubmit if rejected.
3. Collect business license (营业执照) — free, same-day pickup with your ID.
4. Carve official seals (刻章) — company seal, finance seal, legal-rep seal, invoice seal. 0–120 CNY, 1 day, done at a designated police-approved engraver.
5. Open a corporate bank account — 500–1,500 CNY depending on the bank. Banks scrutinize new companies heavily now; book ~1 month ahead.
6. Tax registration — you must register with the tax bureau and provide an accountant's info. From month one after incorporation, **monthly bookkeeping and tax filing is mandatory even with zero revenue** (零申报, zero-declaration filing).

**3. The consequences section (the scare list)**

If you skip bookkeeping/tax filing: personal credit blacklisting, fines of 2,000–10,000 CNY/year from the tax bureau, inability to get mortgages, travel restrictions (no flights/high-speed rail if you owe taxes), and problems with social insurance or emigration applications. The scare framing is salesy, but the underlying point is real — a dormant Chinese company is not free to own; it has recurring compliance obligations, and abandoning one without proper deregistration (注销) genuinely does damage the legal rep's credit record.

**4. Costs**

- Agency handles registration only (you have an address): 600–1,000 CNY.
- Agency provides a virtual address too: ~1,300–3,500 CNY.
- Ongoing bookkeeping: hiring an accountant is 3,000–5,000 CNY/month, vs. outsourcing to an agency at 1,800–3,200 CNY/**year** for a small-scale taxpayer (小规模纳税人). This is why nearly all small companies outsource.

**Relevance to your situation:** since your playbook includes revenue experiments before the full startup launch, the useful takeaway is that a Guangzhou entity costs roughly 2,000–5,000 CNY in year one (virtual address + agency + bookkeeping) with real ongoing compliance overhead. For side-project revenue at the 1K CNY/month validation stage, it's often not worth incorporating yet — many people wait until revenue or a client contract *requires* an entity (e.g., a customer needs a fapiao, or you need a corporate bank account for payment processing). The one structural detail worth remembering: you'll need a supervisor who isn't you, and your wife being the natural candidate means her buy-in becomes practically necessary at incorporation time anyway.

One caveat: this is an agency's promotional post from Zhihu, so treat the price ranges as directional and the "free virtual address is fine for everyone" framing as optimistic — some districts and banks are stricter about hosted addresses than the article implies, especially for bank account opening. Verify current rules with the Guangzhou Market Supervision Administration when you actually incorporate.
