Question: What is the trend in recent years for native vs cross-platform mobile development (Flutter, React Native)?

Answer:

## Mobile Development Trends in Recent Years (2019–2026)

---

### 1. Cross-Platform Has Risen Sharply, Eating Into Pure Native's Share

The overall trend is clear: cross-platform frameworks have grown significantly in developer adoption, with businesses increasingly preferring "build once, run everywhere" to reduce cost and time-to-market. Pure native development (Swift/Kotlin) still holds a strong position in enterprise and performance-critical apps, but its share of new projects has declined relative to cross-platform solutions.

---

### 2. Flutter Has Become the Dominant Cross-Platform Framework

After gaining on React Native for over two years, in April 2020 Flutter became a more frequently searched query globally and continues to be so in 2025 worldwide.

Stack Overflow's 2024 Developer Survey ranked Flutter as the most-used cross-platform framework with 46% adoption versus React Native's 35%.

GitHub statistics show Flutter with 170,000 stars compared to React Native's 121,000 as of 2025.

---

### 3. Market Share Snapshot (2024–2025)

According to the 2024 Stack Overflow Developer Survey, Flutter and React Native together account for 60% of all cross-platform mobile projects, with Flutter at 32.8% and React Native at 27.2%.

Statista's latest research indicates that Flutter holds approximately 46% of the cross-platform market share, compared to React Native's 35%.

---

### 4. React Native Is Still Relevant — But Losing Ground

React Native was the undisputed king of cross-platform from 2015 through ~2020. Since then, it has been steadily overtaken by Flutter in search trends, GitHub activity, and developer surveys. However, despite Flutter's ascending popularity, the high demand for React Native developers indicates it continues to be a popular choice in the market. React Native may offer developers more job opportunities due to its longer presence and larger adoption.

---

### 5. Agencies Are Increasingly Supporting Both

43% of development agencies with more than 20 developers now maintain teams proficient in both Flutter and React Native. This dual capability allows these agencies to match the right tool to each project's specific requirements rather than forcing all clients into a single technical approach.

---

### 6. Flutter Is Maturing — Entering Its "Production Era"

Google considers Flutter to now be in its Production Era — meaning it's mature enough that even enterprise-grade projects are not a challenge for it. Flutter 3.29 was released in February 2025 bringing performance optimizations and widget improvements, cementing this maturity.

---

### 7. Native Development Is Not Dead — It's Evolving

While cross-platform frameworks have grown, native is adapting too. SwiftUI and Jetpack Compose have modernized native development significantly, reducing the productivity gap. Flutter's growing acceptance in sectors with stringent performance and security requirements is putting pressure on pure native, but for mission-critical, hardware-intensive, and enterprise apps, native still dominates.

---

### Summary Timeline

| Period | Trend |
|---|---|
| 2015–2019 | React Native dominates cross-platform; native is the default |
| 2019–2020 | Flutter rises fast, overtakes React Native in search interest |
| 2021–2022 | Flutter becomes the most loved framework; React Native still leads in jobs |
| 2023–2024 | Flutter takes 46% cross-platform market share; native adopts SwiftUI/Compose |
| 2025–2026 | Flutter in "Production Era"; both cross-platform frameworks co-exist; native still preferred for enterprise/performance apps |

The overall narrative is: **cross-platform is the default for new startups and MVPs, while native remains the gold standard for performance-demanding and enterprise applications.**
