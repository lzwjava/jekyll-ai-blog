Question: Is meeting someone offline relatively expensive in modern city life?

Answer: **Yes, often surprisingly expensive—not necessarily in money, but in total coordination cost.**

A “let's meet for one hour” can actually consume:

* 🚗 **Travel:** 30–90 min round trip
* 🅿️ **Parking / transport:** money + hassle
* 🍜 **Food/coffee:** often expected socially
* ⏰ **Scheduling:** finding a mutually available time
* 🧠 **Context switching:** stopping your current work, preparing, traveling, returning
* 🏙️ **Urban friction:** traffic, crowded places, weather, distance
* 💬 **Opportunity cost:** those 2–3 hours could have produced something else

So the real cost is closer to:

$$
C_{\text{meeting}}
=
C_{\text{transport}}
+C_{\text{food}}
+C_{\text{time}}
+C_{\text{coordination}}
+C_{\text{opportunity}}
$$

And **the opportunity cost can dominate everything else**.

For someone building software with agents, this becomes even more obvious. If you can stay at your computer and have an agent implement, test, research, and iterate for two hours, going across the city for a casual meeting is a much more expensive decision than it was when physical presence was necessary for most knowledge work.

But there is an important asymmetry:

**Offline meetings are expensive, but high-value offline meetings can be extremely cheap relative to their return.**

For example:

> 2 hours + ¥100 of expenses → meet a potential long-term client → ¥100k project

That's a fantastic trade.

Whereas:

> 2 hours + transportation + food → casual conversation that could have been a 20-minute call

is expensive.

So I would think about it as **offline meetings becoming a scarce resource**. Use them when physical presence creates something that remote communication cannot easily create: trust, relationship depth, negotiation, inspecting hardware, closing a deal, or doing something physical.

For ordinary information exchange, **text → voice/video → offline** is a sensible escalation ladder.
