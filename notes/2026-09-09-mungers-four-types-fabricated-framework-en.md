**Question:** Is the "4 types of people Munger cuts off" article accurate and genuinely from Munger?

**Answer:**

The article is **partially true in spirit, but misleading in framing**. Here's the breakdown:

**What's genuine:**
Munger did consistently warn against toxic people in real, documented quotes. He told shareholders: "A great lesson of life is get them the hell out of your life — and do it fast." And he specifically cited "avoiding toxic people and toxic activities" as one of the keys to avoiding major mistakes in life.

He also gave broader, documented guidance: "Give a whole lot of things a wide berth… Crooks, crazies, egomaniacs, people full of resentment, people full of self-pity, people who feel like victims — there's a lot of things that aren't going to work for you. Figure out what they are and then avoid them like the plague."

**What's fabricated or oversimplified:**
As the article itself honestly admits upfront, the **specific "4 categories"** (dishonest people, emotional people, self-deceiving people, incentive-driven people) are **not a real Munger framework from any speech or book**. They are a Chinese internet editorial reconstruction — someone organized Munger's scattered, general remarks into a neat four-part list that Munger never actually presented that way.

The quote about the friend who "always felt unlucky and complained for 70 years" is also **not verifiable** as a real Munger anecdote from *Poor Charlie's Almanack* or any known speech. It has the feel of an illustrative story added by the article's author.

**What's accurately captured:**
The underlying values — prioritizing integrity over talent, avoiding self-pity, being wary of people driven purely by incentives — do reflect Munger's real thinking across multiple verified sources.

**Bottom line:** Think of it as a **well-intentioned paraphrase**, not a factual transcript. The core attitude is Munger's; the tidy four-category structure is someone else's packaging.
