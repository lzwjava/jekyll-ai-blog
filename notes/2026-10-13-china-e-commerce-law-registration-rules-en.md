**Question:** What is the current status of China's 电商法 (E-Commerce Law), and do e-commerce sellers need to register a company in China?

---

**Answer:**

**Current Law — 电商法 (2019, still in force)**

China's E-Commerce Law was promulgated on August 31, 2018 and came into effect on January 1, 2019. It remains the primary legal framework today.

**Registration Requirement (Article 10)**

E-commerce operators are required to register themselves as market subjects under the law — *except* for:
- Individuals selling self-produced agricultural products or byproducts
- Individuals selling family handicrafts
- Individuals using their own skills for labor convenience services (e.g. tutoring, small repairs)
- Occasional small-amount transactions that don't require a license under the law

So the short answer is: **yes, most e-commerce sellers must register** — but a full company (法人) is not always required. You may register as an **individual business (个体工商户)** rather than a formal company.

There was considerable debate during the legislative process about whether *all* e-commerce operators should be required to conduct registration. The law ultimately established registration as a general principle, with the exceptions above.

**Daigou (代购) sellers**

People who shop abroad on behalf of consumers in China and resell goods at a higher price must register as e-commerce businesses and pay taxes.

**What about opening stores on Taobao/Pinduoduo without registration?**

Until further notice (and until "low-value transactions" are more explicitly defined), individual Taobao stores can continue to operate as before without forced closure, as long as they meet the regulations. You can still open a Taobao store as an individual.

---

**2026 Draft Amendment (Proposed — Not Yet Enacted)**

On July 4, 2026, China's SAMR and MOFCOM opened a 20-provision draft amendment to the 2019 E-Commerce Law for public comment. The draft does not scrap the existing law — it expands its scope.

Key changes proposed:
- Merchants would face **clearer requirements** relating to business registration, licensing, tax compliance, and the provision of accurate and truthful information about products and services.
- Enhanced platform responsibilities: e-commerce platforms would have greater responsibility for **verifying sellers**, monitoring products, and taking action on illegal or non-compliant items.
- Social media, livestreaming commerce, and content-commerce platforms (e.g. short video sellers) would be **explicitly included** under the E-Commerce Law's scope.

The two authorities (SAMR and MOFCOM) said they will optimize the draft in light of public feedback and work to advance the revision as soon as possible. It is **not yet finalized or enacted**.
