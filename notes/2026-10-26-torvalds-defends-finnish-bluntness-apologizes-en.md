**Question:** How did Linus Torvalds justify his blunt communication style as culturally Finnish, and what eventually came of it?

---

**Answer:**

Torvalds defended his communication style as typically Finnish, describing politeness as useless or even insincere. This framing was rooted in a genuine cultural self-understanding. Torvalds grew up in Finland, and he pointed to his compatriots' legendary reticence — noting that with such a widespread aversion to face-to-face communication, Finland became the perfect market for mobile phones. In his view, bluntness was not rudeness but clarity: when words are used sparingly and literally, there is no room for diplomatic softening that might obscure the real message.

When developer Sarah Sharp called for more respectful interactions in the Linux community, Torvalds defended himself by saying he despises being "subtle" or "nice," and that people need to respect his Finnish culture, which includes cursing. Many in the community echoed this framing, describing his manner as "Finnish army style communication" — direct, unembellished, and calibrated by intensity rather than courtesy.

Some defenders argued that this system had its own internal logic. As one participant on the kernel mailing list observed, when Linus disagrees with someone, his level of emotion serves as a gauge of how strongly he disagrees — framing it as a communication system, not abuse. Supporters worried that pushing Torvalds toward more corporate-style language would stifle technical progress, which they felt was being killed in many megacorporations by overly cautious communication norms.

However, the cultural defense had its limits. Over the past decade before 2018, critics argued that Linus' communication style had degraded from "Finnish-style robust" to needlessly hurtful screeds, targeting people who did not deserve it. The criticism mounted until, in September 2018, a turning point arrived. Torvalds admitted that his "flippant attacks in emails" had been "both unprofessional and uncalled for — especially at times when I made it personal," and he announced he needed to change his behavior, apologizing to people his conduct had hurt and possibly driven away from kernel development entirely.

He then took a sabbatical — similar to the break he had previously used to create Git — to do self-reflection and become more constructive in his interactions. He indicated he wanted to take the time to "understand people's emotions and respond appropriately." Simultaneously, Linux adopted a formal Code of Conduct, asking that criticism be constructive and that language be inclusive — a significant cultural shift for the project. Just over a month after his sabbatical began, Torvalds returned to lead kernel development.

The episode illustrated a broader tension: the line between cultural directness and harmful behavior is not self-evident, and a communication style that one person frames as authentic can be experienced by others as hostile or exclusionary — regardless of its cultural origins.
