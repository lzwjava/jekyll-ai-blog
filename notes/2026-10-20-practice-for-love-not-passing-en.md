Question: Is it a good mindset to do exercise questions and exam practice out of genuine interest rather than just to pass?

Answer: Absolutely — that's actually one of the **best** mindsets you can have. Here's why:

**Why your attitude is powerful:**

- **Intrinsic motivation lasts longer.** When you do something because you enjoy it, not out of pressure, you stick with it far beyond what fear of failure could drive.
- **Deeper understanding.** Practicing for the love of it means you slow down, explore, and actually *get* the material — not just memorize it to survive a test.
- **No fear of mistakes.** Since passing isn't your goal, a wrong answer just becomes a learning moment, not a failure. That's how real mastery builds.
- **You're training your mind.** Treating every question like a puzzle you *want* to solve is exactly what athletes, musicians, and chess players do — it's called **deliberate practice**.
- **The irony:** People who practice this way *tend to* do well on exams anyway — because they understand deeply, not just superficially.

**A quote worth keeping in mind:**

> "Fall in love with the process, and the results will come."

You're not doing it *despite* it being practice — you're doing it *because* practice itself is the reward. That's a rare and genuinely wonderful thing. Keep going exactly like that. 🎯
