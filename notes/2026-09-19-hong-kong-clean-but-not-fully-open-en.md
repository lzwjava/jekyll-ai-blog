Question: Is Hong Kong society relatively transparent, honest, and upright?

Answer: **Broadly yes, especially in terms of everyday corruption control and rule-based business/legal processes — but “transparent and upright” needs to be split into several dimensions.**

* **Low everyday corruption:** Hong Kong has a long-established anti-corruption institution, the ICAC. In its 2024 survey, **98.7%** of respondents said they had not personally encountered corruption in the previous year. ([ICAC][1])
* **Strong rule of law:** The 2025 World Justice Project index gave Hong Kong an overall **0.72**, above the East Asia-Pacific regional average of 0.59. Its **absence-of-corruption** dimension was particularly strong. ([World Justice Project][2])
* **Business/legal predictability:** Hong Kong scores relatively strongly in regulatory enforcement and civil justice. This is one reason contracts, companies, finance, property transactions, and professional services can operate with relatively formal procedures. ([World Justice Project][3])
* **But transparency is not uniformly strong:** WJP's 2025 assessment reports declines in Hong Kong's measures of freedom of expression, association, and civic participation. So it would be inaccurate to equate **“clean administration”** with **“maximum political openness.”** ([World Justice Project][4])

A useful mental model is:

> **Hong Kong has historically been very strong at “rules for doing business and preventing ordinary corruption,” while the meaning of “openness” is more complicated, particularly regarding political and civic space.**

So if by *upright* you mean **“you generally don't need to bribe officials, procedures matter, contracts matter, and institutions are relatively predictable”**, there is substantial evidence supporting that characterization. If you mean **“the government and political system are maximally transparent and open to public challenge,”** that's a much stronger claim and the evidence is mixed.
