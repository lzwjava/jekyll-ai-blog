Question: Give more common vegetables in China

Answer:

Here are additional common vegetables widely used in Chinese cuisine, building on the previous list. These are popular across regions, often featured in stir-fries, soups, hotpots, and pickles.

- **Daikon radish (white radish or luóbo)**: Large white root vegetable; used in soups, stir-fries, pickled, or shredded in salads. China is a major producer.
- **Carrots (húluóbo)**: Commonly stir-fried, added to soups, or used in fillings; sweet and crunchy.
- **Cauliflower (càihuā)**: Popular in stir-fries; China is one of the top producers and consumers.
- **Choy sum (cài xīn or flowering cabbage)**: Tender leafy green with yellow flowers; mild and sweet, often stir-fried with garlic.
- **Winter melon (dōngguā)**: Mild-flavored gourd; used in cooling soups or braised dishes.
- **Bamboo shoots (sǔn)**: Crunchy shoots; added to stir-fries, soups, or braised with meat.
- **Snow peas (hélán dòu or pea pods)**: Flat pods; quick stir-fried for crisp texture.
- **Garlic chives (jiǔcài)**: Flat, garlicky leaves; used in stir-fries, dumplings, or with eggs.
- **Celery (Chinese celery or qíncài)**: Thinner and more aromatic than Western celery; stir-fried or in soups.
- **Spinach (bōcài)**: Tender leaves; blanched, stir-fried, or in soups.
- **Onions (yángcōng)** and green onions (cōng)**: Essential aromatics; green onions used as garnish or in stir-fries.
- **Peanuts (huāshēng)**: Often treated as a vegetable in dishes; boiled, fried, or in kung pao.

Regional favorites include mustard greens (jiècài) for pickling in Sichuan, and amaranth (xiàncài) in southern dishes. Mushrooms like shiitake and wood ear are also staples but technically fungi.
