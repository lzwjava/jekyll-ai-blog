Question: Who is Robert O. Ritchie?

Answer: Materials scientist, not a computer scientist — probably not who you're expecting if you were thinking ML/AI.

He's the H.T. and Jessie Chua Distinguished Professor of Engineering at UC Berkeley, and Senior Faculty Scientist at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL). He earned his MA, PhD and ScD at Cambridge University in the UK.

Career path: Miller Research Fellow at Berkeley starting 1974, then joined MIT faculty in 1977 as Class of 1922 Associate Professor in Mechanical Engineering, returning to Berkeley in 1981. He chaired UC Berkeley's Materials Science & Engineering Department from 2005 to 2011.

Research focus: mechanics and micro-mechanisms of fracture and fatigue across structural and biological materials — providing a microstructural basis for damage-tolerance and fatigue resistance. Current interests include high-entropy alloys, bulk-metallic glasses, structural integrity of human bone, and bio-inspired structural materials.

Recognition: Foreign fellow of the Royal Society and Royal Academy of Engineering (UK), member of the National Academy of Engineering, AAAS, and NAS (US). Over 800 papers, 19 edited books, h-index of 115 on Web of Science (1491 on Google Scholar). One notable academic lineage note: his doctoral advisor was John F. Knott, and one of his own doctoral students was Subra Suresh (former NSF Director, MIT Dean of Engineering).
