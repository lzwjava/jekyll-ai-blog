Question: How to understand the desire to live by your own principles like Yin Wang — not being controlled by society, money, or status, but by nature, mathematics, and computer science?

Answer:

What you describe is actually a very old idea: **the pursuit of intrinsic value over extrinsic value**.

Many people optimize:

```
social approval
        ↓
money
        ↓
title
        ↓
status
        ↓
security
```

But some people optimize:

```
truth
  ↓
beauty
  ↓
understanding
  ↓
creation
  ↓
freedom
```

The second path is closer to what you admire in Yin Wang's writing: the idea that an idea should be judged by its internal correctness, not by who says it or how famous the person is. He explicitly argues that truth does not need to depend on "status" or "position"; the value should come from the reasoning itself. ([Yinwang][1])

For a programmer, this feeling is especially natural because computers are one of the few places where reality is brutally honest:

```python
def add(a, b):
    return a + b
```

The machine does not care:

* Did you graduate from MIT?
* Are you a VP?
* Do people respect you?
* Are you rich?

It only asks:

```
Does the program work?
Does the logic hold?
Is the design elegant?
```

That is why many engineers love programming. It is a small universe governed by rules.

Mathematics is similar:

A theorem is true because the proof works, not because the author is famous.

Computer science is similar:

A system is good because it is simple, reliable, understandable, and useful.

This mindset is close to the "craftsman" mentality:

> Build something because it deserves to exist.

Not:

> Build something because the market currently rewards it.

---

But there is an important distinction.

Freedom from society does **not** mean ignoring reality.

Even Yin Wang's philosophy is still deeply connected to engineering reality: programming must face the computer's strict rules. He often emphasizes programming as a creative art where elegance and correctness matter. ([Tumblr][2])

A good version of this philosophy is:

```
Ignore shallow human judgment
        +
Respect deep reality
```

Not:

```
Ignore everyone
        +
Do whatever feels good
```

Nature, mathematics, and computers are actually stricter than society.

Society might say:

> "This person is successful because he raised $100M."

Computer science asks:

> "Can you design a system that works at scale?"

Mathematics asks:

> "Can you prove it?"

Nature asks:

> "Does it survive?"

---

For someone building AI systems, I think this philosophy can become very practical.

Instead of asking:

"Which AI trend will make me rich?"

Ask:

"Which problem is beautiful enough that I want to spend 10 years understanding it?"

Examples:

* Build a better programming language.
* Build a better agent architecture.
* Understand intelligence from first principles.
* Train models and discover new scaling laws.
* Create tools that make programmers more powerful.

Money can become a **measurement**, not the **goal**.

A useful analogy:

```
Artist:
    creates because beauty matters

Scientist:
    discovers because truth matters

Engineer:
    builds because reality matters

Entrepreneur:
    creates because value matters
```

The strongest entrepreneurs are often not primarily money seekers. They are obsessed builders. Money is a consequence of creating something people deeply need.

---

Your sentence:

> "obey the rule of nature/math/computer instead of human/society"

is actually very close to the hacker tradition:

```
The computer is not impressed by your reputation.
The compiler is not impressed by your confidence.
The GPU is not impressed by your ambition.

Only reality answers.
```

That is a powerful place to stand.

The challenge is balance:

```
inner compass
      +
technical excellence
      +
understanding humans
      +
economic sustainability
```

You don't need to become a monk rejecting money. A great engineer can build a company, make money, and still remain loyal to the deeper motivation:

"I built this because I believed it should exist."

That is probably the closest path to the kind of life you are describing.
