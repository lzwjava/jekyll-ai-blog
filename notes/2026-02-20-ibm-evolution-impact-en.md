Here’s a comprehensive introduction to the history of IBM, highlighting its key milestones and transformations:

---

### **Founding and Early Years (1911–1930s)**
- **1911**: IBM was founded as the Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company (CTR) through the merger of three companies specializing in punch-card tabulators, time clocks, and scales. The company was later renamed International Business Machines (IBM) in 1924.
- **1924**: Thomas J. Watson, Sr. became CEO and instilled a strong corporate culture, emphasizing innovation, customer service, and the famous motto “THINK.”
- **1928**: IBM introduced the 80-column punched card, doubling data capacity and setting an industry standard for decades.

---

### **Expansion and Technological Leadership (1940s–1960s)**
- **1940s**: IBM played a crucial role in World War II by developing electromechanical calculators, precursors to electronic computers. The company also entered the electric typewriter market, becoming an industry leader.
- **1952**: Under Thomas Watson, Jr., IBM made a strategic push into electronic data processing, leading to the development of the IBM 701, its first commercial scientific computer.
- **1964**: The launch of the System/360 mainframe family revolutionized computing. System/360 was the first to offer compatible hardware and software across a range of models, solidifying IBM’s dominance and earning it the nickname “Big Blue”.

---

### **Innovation and Challenges (1970s–1990s)**
- **1971**: IBM invented the floppy disk, a breakthrough in data storage.
- **1981**: IBM introduced its first personal computer (PC), using Microsoft’s MS-DOS and Intel chips. This move popularized the term “PC” and set the standard for the industry.
- **1993**: IBM faced its largest corporate loss in U.S. history ($8 billion). Lou Gerstner was hired as CEO to turn the company around, shifting focus to services and software, and acquiring Lotus Development Corporation.

---

### **Modern Era and Transformation (2000s–Present)**
- **2005**: IBM sold its PC division to Lenovo, marking a shift toward software, services, and high-value technologies like AI and cloud computing.
- **2011**: IBM’s Watson AI system gained fame by winning the TV quiz show *Jeopardy!*, showcasing advancements in natural language processing and machine learning.
- **2014–2026**: IBM continued to innovate in quantum computing, AI (including generative and agentic AI), and hybrid cloud solutions. The company also expanded its global partnerships and digital services, focusing on enterprise-grade AI and data platforms.

---

### **Key Inventions and Contributions**
IBM’s innovations include:
- The ATM, DRAM, hard disk drive, magnetic stripe card, relational database, SQL, UPC bar code, and the first self-learning AI program (1956).

---

### **Legacy and Impact**
IBM’s history reflects its ability to adapt and lead through technological revolutions—from punch cards to AI and quantum computing. Its contributions have shaped modern computing, business, and society.
