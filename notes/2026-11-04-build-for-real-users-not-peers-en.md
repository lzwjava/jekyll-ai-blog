**Question:** What does Mark Pincus's quote mean — "If you truly have ambition, burn your resume. Your goal is not to win the respect of your peers, but to win the hearts of real users"?

---

**Answer:**

This quote comes from Mark Pincus, the founder of Zynga — the company behind Words With Friends, FarmVille, and Zynga Poker — who has arguably created more hit consumer products than anyone in history. It contains two interconnected ideas:

---

### 🔥 "Burn your resume"

Your resume represents your identity built for *other professionals* — your credentials, your prestigious past, your reputation among peers. "Burning" it means **stop building your career for an audience of insiders**. Stop asking "will this look impressive?" and start asking "will this actually work for users?"

Pincus shares his own painful lesson: after early successes like Freeloader and Support.com, he felt he could "do anything." This led to over-ambition with his venture Tribe, which ultimately failed. His subsequent venture, Zynga, started with an "embarrassingly small" idea — a poker game on Facebook. This *reduced* ambition, focusing on a manageable initial step, was crucial to its success.

In other words, the resume-chasing mindset almost killed him. Letting it go saved him.

---

### 👥 "Win the hearts of real users, not peer respect"

The point is direct: "You became a founder, an entrepreneur because you wanted to go be an innovator, but you're trying to win the hearts and minds of nurses in Indiana — like for FarmVille. You're not trying to win awards and respect from your peers."

FarmVille was mocked by the gaming elite as shallow and uncool. But it reached tens of millions of real people who loved it. Pincus advises against seeking peer respect, suggesting that true ambition often leads to doing things differently, which can naturally alienate those who adhere to conventional paths.

---

### The Deeper Point

He encourages listeners to redefine their ambition from a peer-focused perspective to one that centers on delivering value to consumers. Most people unconsciously optimize for how their work is *judged by colleagues* — which leads to products that are sophisticated-looking but not genuinely loved.

Real ambition is harder: it means building something that *ordinary people* actually want, even if it makes you look unsophisticated to the industry.

---

**One-line summary:** Stop performing for your professional tribe. The only scoreboard that matters is whether real users love what you built.
