Question: **Does who we admire strongly influence our decisions and mindset?**

Answer: **Yes — much more than most people realize.**

Admiration is not just “I like this person.” It is often a form of **implicit value selection**.

When you repeatedly admire someone, your brain starts treating their behavior as evidence of what is *good, possible, and desirable*.

A rough model:

```text
Who you admire
      ↓
What traits you notice
      ↓
What you consider "success"
      ↓
What risks seem acceptable
      ↓
What opportunities you notice
      ↓
How you make decisions
      ↓
What kind of person you become
```

For example:

* Admire **Karpathy** → value deep technical understanding, first principles, building, learning.
* Admire **Liang Wenfeng** → value technical independence, research depth, long-term bets.
* Admire **a great entrepreneur** → tolerate uncertainty and think in terms of ownership rather than salary.
* Admire **a corporate executive** → potentially optimize more for title, status, organizational power.
* Admire **a very wealthy person** → may gradually normalize capital accumulation as a primary objective.
* Admire **a highly agreeable person** → may unconsciously start treating being liked as more important than negotiating aggressively.

The important part is that **you also inherit their blind spots**.

You might copy:

```text
their strengths
+
their worldview
+
their risk tolerance
+
their definition of success
+
their weaknesses
```

That's why I'd distinguish:

> **Admiration ≠ imitation.**

A better approach is to decompose someone:

```text
Person X
├── technical ability       → copy
├── work ethic              → copy
├── decision framework      → inspect
├── values                  → evaluate
├── lifestyle               → maybe copy
├── weaknesses              → explicitly avoid
└── circumstances           → don't assume transferable
```

This is especially important when you're making major decisions. If your heroes disproportionately come from **research/startup/engineering**, you'll naturally perceive building an AI company or doing deep technical work as more meaningful than climbing a conventional corporate ladder. Someone whose heroes are bankers, politicians, or executives could look at exactly the same choices and reach very different conclusions.

So one useful question before a major decision is:

> **"Would I still want this if the people I admire didn't exist?"**

If the answer changes dramatically, your decision may be partly **borrowed preference** rather than your own.

And there's an even deeper point: **your environment determines who you encounter, and who you encounter determines who you can admire.** So deliberately exposing yourself to unusually different successful people is a way of expanding your possible futures.

I can also help you build a **“admiration audit”**—break down the people you currently admire into traits to copy vs. traits to consciously reject.
