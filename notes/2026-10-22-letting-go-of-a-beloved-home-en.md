## 2. Why it can be particularly difficult

A house is not just an investment. It often represents several things at once:

- Security: “My family has somewhere to live.”
- Achievement: “I've worked hard and finally own something.”
- Identity: “I'm a responsible adult who provides for my family.”
- Hope: “One day, this property will be worth more, and life will become easier.”

Selling can feel like losing all four, even though the underlying reality is more complicated.

There is also a psychological trap: the more money and effort you've already put into something, the harder it becomes to let go. But money already spent cannot be recovered merely by continuing to hold the asset. The relevant question is what choice gives you and your family the better future from today onward.

## 3. What I would want you to remember

You have several ambitions beyond property ownership: deepening your understanding of AI, training models, building products, completing your education, and creating a sustainable business.

Imagine two possible futures.

### Future A: Keep the house

You preserve a familiar home and avoid the immediate emotional pain of selling. But if the mortgage continues to create substantial pressure, you may have less freedom to experiment, study, or take career risks.

### Future B: Sell the house

You experience grief, uncertainty, and possibly regret. But if the sale materially reduces your financial burden, you may gain the time and flexibility to rebuild your income and pursue your goals.

Neither future is guaranteed to work out. Keeping the house isn't necessarily cowardice, and selling it isn't automatically courage.

The real measure of success isn't whether you keep the property. It's whether you make a sound decision for your family and preserve your ability to build the life you want.

You can sell a house without giving up on homeownership forever. You can rent for a while, rebuild your income, and buy again later if that becomes the right choice. The next home doesn't need to be purchased on the same timeline as the previous one.

And if you ultimately decide to keep your house, make that an intentional decision supported by realistic finances—not a decision driven solely by the pain of letting go.

## 4. One question worth asking yourself

Imagine that you had never bought this house. Today, with your current finances, your family responsibilities, and your AI career ambitions, would you choose to buy this exact property at its current market value, with this mortgage, all over again?

Don't answer based on what you paid in 2022. Answer based on what you know today.

That question can help separate your attachment to the life you've already invested in from your judgment about the life you want to build next.

One thing I'd like to understand: what hurts the most when you imagine selling?

- The possibility of losing money on the property.
- The feeling that you failed or went backward in life.
- Losing the home and the security it provides your wife and daughters.
- The fear that prices will recover after you sell.
- Or the feeling that, after working so hard, you have to give up something you've earned.

You don't have to choose just one. The distinction matters because each kind of pain calls for a different response.
