Question: What is Marxism (马克思主义)?

Answer:

Marxism is a political, economic, and social philosophy developed by Karl Marx (and Friedrich Engels) in the 19th century. Its core ideas include:

**Historical Materialism** — History is driven by material and economic conditions, not ideas or religion. Societies evolve through stages (feudalism → capitalism → socialism → communism).

**Class Struggle** — Society is divided into classes with conflicting interests. Under capitalism, the **bourgeoisie** (owners of capital) exploit the **proletariat** (workers), who sell their labor for wages.

**Surplus Value & Exploitation** — Workers produce more value than they receive in wages; the difference (surplus value) is captured by capitalists as profit. This is seen as the root of exploitation.

**Alienation** — Workers become estranged from the products of their labor, from the act of working itself, and from their fellow human beings under capitalist production.

**Critique of Capitalism** — Capitalism contains internal contradictions (e.g., overproduction, falling profits) that will eventually lead to its collapse.

**Vision of Communism** — The ultimate goal is a classless, stateless society where the means of production are collectively owned and resources are distributed according to the principle *"from each according to his ability, to each according to his needs."*

**Key Texts:**

- *The Communist Manifesto* (1848) — Marx & Engels
- *Das Kapital* (1867) — Marx

Marxism has since branched into many currents — Leninism, Maoism, Western Marxism, Marxist humanism, etc. — and has had an enormous influence on modern politics, sociology, economics, and philosophy worldwide.
