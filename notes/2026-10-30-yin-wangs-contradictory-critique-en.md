Question: What are the problems with Yin Wang's (王垠) opinions, as reflected in the essay at lzwjava.com/success-en?

Answer:

Yin Wang is a genuinely sharp thinker — his technical critiques of OOP, type systems, and AI hype often have real merit. But his broader worldview has several recurring structural problems:

**1. Self-contradiction at the core**

He says he doesn't need external validation, yet the entire essay is a public rebuttal of critics. He dismisses the idea of chasing mainstream success for approval, but he simultaneously demands "genuine respect" from others — which is still a form of external validation, just on his own terms. He can't have it both ways.

**2. Dismissal instead of engagement**

He is famous for debunking industry myths, but he is also known for highly subjective personal takes. His "brilliant technical insights" coexist with "personal grievances" that are very hard to separate. When critics appear, he labels them "noise," "street thugs," or "low-quality people" rather than addressing their specific objections. This makes his position unfalsifiable — anyone who disagrees is, by definition, beneath him.

**3. Sweeping claims without evidence**

His assertion that many celebrated tech companies are "bubbles" and "toxins" holds some truth for specific cases, but he generalizes without naming companies, citing financials, or acknowledging counterexamples. Strong claims need strong evidence.

**4. The elitism trap**

He lambasts Chinese academia as a "cult of mediocrity," dismisses figures like Turing Award winners as "paper tigers," and views Ivy League credentials as "worthless signaling tools." There's genuine insight here about credentialism — but the tone frames everyone not sharing his views as morally or intellectually deficient. That's not critique; it's contempt.

**5. The grader who refuses to be graded**

He attacks the "grading culture" that conditions people to seek approval — but he himself grades others harshly and constantly (calling people "slaves," "leeches," "societal tumors"). He objects to *being* judged, not to *judging*.

**6. Unfalsifiable self-image**

His influence on young engineers is cited entirely from his own account. His writing style is "self-referential, confrontational, with conspiratorial undertones, and a strong conviction in solo intellectual superiority." This makes it very hard to distinguish genuine insight from ego.

**7. AI skepticism overstated**

He dismisses AI achieving human-like vision as "marketing hype," asserting current models are just statistical pattern-matching far from biological vision. While there are legitimate debates here, his framing is often absolutist — and given rapid progress in AI since 2019–2022, some of his dismissals have aged poorly.

**The bottom line:** Yin Wang is most valuable when read as a *technical provocateur* — someone who forces you to question lazy assumptions about OOP, testing dogma, or framework hype. He's least valuable when he writes about people, society, and success, because those essays substitute rhetorical contempt for actual argument.
