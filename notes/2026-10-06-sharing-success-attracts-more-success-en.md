**Question: If people share more success, will they attract more success?**

**Answer:**

Often yes, but the mechanism is not magic. Sharing success creates **information flow, trust, and opportunity surface area**.

A simple model:

```
Your ability × Visibility × Trust = Opportunity
```

Many capable people have low visibility:

```
High ability × Low visibility = few opportunities
```

When you share useful achievements publicly:

* people discover what you can do
* potential customers know your capability
* collaborators know where you fit
* investors/employers can evaluate you
* your reputation compounds over time

For example:

* An engineer writes technical blog posts → another company finds them → job offer or consulting opportunity.
* A founder shares product progress → users trust the journey → early adopters appear.
* An open-source developer publishes projects → contributors and companies notice.

This is why companies like Netflix and Meituan publish engineering blogs. They are not only giving information away; they are also demonstrating competence and attracting engineers, partners, and ecosystem attention.

However, the key is **signal quality**.

Low-quality sharing:

> "I am amazing. Look at me."

Usually creates weak trust.

High-quality sharing:

> "Here is what I built, what problem it solves, what failed, and what I learned."

creates credibility.

A good pattern is:

```
Build → Measure → Share → Connect → More opportunities → Build more
```

Success attracts success because successful people usually become **more visible nodes in the network**. More connections create more chances for unexpected opportunities.

For a technical founder/engineer, sharing:

* experiments
* benchmarks
* open-source code
* engineering lessons
* customer outcomes

is especially powerful because the content itself becomes proof of ability.

The important balance is: **share evidence, not just claims**. The strongest reputation is built when other people can independently verify your work.
