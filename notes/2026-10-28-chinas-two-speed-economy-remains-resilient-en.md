**Question:** Is China's economy still resilient?

**Answer:**

Yes — broadly speaking, China's economy has continued to show resilience in 2026, though the picture is more nuanced than a simple "yes."

**The positive side:**

- GDP grew 5% year-on-year in Q1 2026, accelerating from the previous quarter, with stronger domestic demand and new growth drivers taking shape.

- In the first half of 2026, GDP expanded 4.7% year-on-year to 69.6 trillion yuan (~$10.25 trillion), with officials noting it demonstrated "solid resilience" against geopolitical tensions and trade frictions.

- New industries — high-end manufacturing, the digital economy, and modern services — now account for more than 40% of economic growth in the first half of 2026.

- Officials attributed the economy's strength to two key pillars: resilient industrial and supply chains and strong energy security, reinforcing China's dual role as the "world factory" and the "world market."

**But real structural challenges remain:**

- The property market downturn continues to weigh heavily on the economy, and ongoing property sector headwinds are keeping Chinese consumers cautious, with weak domestic demand persisting in 2026.

- Youth unemployment has been officially reported above 19%, and analysts describe China as an "I-shaped economy" where technology dominates while broader consumer sectors struggle.

- China set its 2026 growth target at 4.5%–5%, its lowest range since 1991, with policymakers explicitly acknowledging difficulties from a prolonged property slump, geopolitical risks, and US tariff pressures.

- Demographic headwinds are building: the UN projects China's working-age population will decline about 0.6 percentage points per year over the next decade.

**Bottom line:** China's economy is holding up — beating many forecasts — but it's essentially a **two-speed economy**: tech exports and advanced manufacturing are strong, while domestic consumption and the property sector lag. Resilient, yes, but with meaningful structural vulnerabilities that could limit growth over the longer term.
