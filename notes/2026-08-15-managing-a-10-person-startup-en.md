Question: How to manage a tech startup like a 10-person team?

Answer:
At 10 people, a startup sits right at the transition point between "everyone just talks to everyone" and "we need some structure." Here's how to manage it well:

**1. Keep communication lightweight but intentional**
- A daily 10–15 min standup (even async in Slack) keeps everyone aligned without heavy meetings.
- A weekly all-hands (30–45 min) to share metrics, wins, and blockers builds shared context.
- Avoid meeting bloat — at this size, most decisions can happen in small huddles or written docs.

**2. Clarify roles without over-formalizing**
- People will wear multiple hats, but each person should have one clear primary owner area (e.g., "you own backend infra," "you own customer support").
- Write a simple one-page doc per person: what they own, what they're not responsible for. This avoids duplicated work and dropped balls.

**3. Set a small number of shared priorities**
- At this stage, focus beats scope. Pick 1–3 company-level goals per quarter (OKRs or even simpler "themes") so the whole team rows in the same direction.
- Revisit and communicate priorities often — a 10-person team can pivot fast, but only if everyone knows what "fast" means right now.

**4. Build lightweight processes early, not late**
- Documented onboarding, a basic PR/code review process, and a simple way to track tasks (Linear, Notion, or even a shared doc) save huge pain later.
- Don't over-engineer — you want just enough process that a new hire #11 could ramp up without asking 20 questions.

**5. Protect culture deliberately**
- At 10 people, culture is still mostly "how the founders behave." Model the values you want (transparency, urgency, curiosity) rather than writing a culture deck.
- Create rituals — demo days, retro sessions, small celebrations — that reinforce identity as the team grows.

**6. Manage 1:1s and feedback loops**
- Regular 1:1s (biweekly is often enough at this size) matter more than performance reviews. Use them to catch issues early and give real-time feedback.
- Make feedback bidirectional — ask what's blocking people, not just what they're doing.

**7. Watch burn rate and hiring pace closely**
- At 10 people, payroll is often your biggest expense. Tie hiring decisions directly to runway and revenue/usage milestones, not just ambition.
- Hire slowly for culture fit and fast for proven skill gaps — a bad hire at this size disproportionately hurts morale and velocity.

**8. Delegate real ownership, not just tasks**
- As a founder/manager, resist the urge to be the bottleneck on every decision. Give people the "why," then let them own the "how."
- This is also the stage where you start identifying future team leads — people who can own a function as you scale past 15–20.

The core theme: stay lean and human at 10 people, but put just enough lightweight structure (roles, priorities, rituals) in place that growth to 20–30 doesn't require reinventing everything from scratch.
