**Question:** Can one register a company in Guangzhou and then liquidate/dissolve it after just one month?

**Answer:**

Technically **yes, it is legally possible** — Chinese law has no minimum operating period before dissolution. However, **completing the full process within one calendar month is extremely difficult in practice**. Here's why:

---

### Two Exit Routes

Two principal exit routes exist in China: **general liquidation** (governed by PRC Company Law) and **simplified deregistration** (an expedited pathway available to entities with no outstanding liabilities).

---

### Route 1 — Simplified Deregistration (Fastest)

This is your best option for a brand-new company with zero debts and no employees:

- A pre-application public announcement must be made. During the publicity period, the SAMR passes deregistration information to tax, HR, and social security authorities. If there is no objection, **within 20 days after the publicity period expires**, the company can apply for simplified deregistration.
- The simplified track compresses the public announcement to **20 days**.

So the 20-day public notice window alone nearly fills one month — and then the actual filings with SAMR, tax authorities, and banks come *after* that. **Realistic minimum timeline: ~1.5–3 months.**

---

### Route 2 — General (Standard) Liquidation

Much slower:

- Within 15 days of the dissolution resolution, a liquidation committee must be established. Within 60 days of establishing the liquidation team, it must make at least one public announcement in a national or provincial newspaper. Within 30 days of submitting the liquidation report, the company should perform deregistration with the authorities.
- The creditor-claims period runs for a minimum of **45 days** from the date of the public announcement under PRC Company Law.
- In practice, failure modes can turn a 6-month closure into a **24-month one** — and since 2025–2026, enforcement has tightened materially, with the State Tax Administration digging deeper into pre-closure transactions.

---

### Key Risks to Know

- Skipping steps or rushing the process can leave shareholders and directors **personally exposed** to unpaid debts, tax penalties, or blacklisting on China's National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System, even years after the business has stopped operating.
- Where a company completes deregistration without undergoing proper liquidation procedures, the **liable persons shall be responsible for repaying all debts** of the company.
- Tax clearance from the State Tax Administration is typically the most time-consuming step and cannot be skipped.

---

### Bottom Line

| Factor | Simplified Route | General Route |
|---|---|---|
| Debts/employees? | Must have none | Any situation |
| Public notice period | 20 days | 45 days |
| Realistic total time | **2–3 months** | **6–18+ months** |
| Feasible in 1 month? | ❌ Barely | ❌ No |

If your company is newly registered, has **no employees, no debts, no tax filings, and no contracts**, the simplified route is your only shot at a fast exit — but plan for at least 2–3 months, not one.
