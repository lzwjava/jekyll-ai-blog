**Question:** How has GreptimeDB grown in recent years, and does it have a significant user base?

**Answer:**

GreptimeDB has shown solid and consistent growth since its open-source debut. Here's a summary across key dimensions:

**Timeline & Milestones**

GreptimeDB has been growing steadily since its open-source release on November 15, 2022. The project has been iterating rapidly:

- v1.0 GA was officially planned for January 10, 2026, with the goal of freezing APIs and protocols to ensure long-term compatibility.
- v1.0.0 was formally released on April 8, 2026 — their first GA release.
- v1.1.0 followed on June 14, 2026, described as another production-focused release after v1.0 GA.
- v1.2.0 beta is already in progress, focusing on safer table administration, better Flow observability, and query/storage correctness fixes.

**GitHub & Community**

The main GitHub repo has accumulated around 6,000–6,400 stars with 457 forks as of early-to-mid 2025. That's a respectable number for a niche time-series/observability database, though well behind giants like InfluxDB or ClickHouse.

From v1.0 Beta to GA alone, 474 PRs were merged with 27 contributors, 8 of whom were first-time contributors to GreptimeDB.

**Content & Visibility**

In 2024, the GreptimeDB social accounts gained thousands of new followers, and their top-performing article reached over 10,000 views and was shared over 300 times.

In 2025, they shipped 93 blog posts, with their "Observability 2.0" content pulling more than half of all annual traffic — reflecting strong interest in the observability space.

**Product Direction**

GreptimeDB is now positioning itself as an "Observability 2.0" database — a drop-in replacement for Prometheus, Loki, and Elasticsearch — or as a single OpenTelemetry backend. This broader pitch, moving beyond pure time-series into unified metrics/logs/traces, has driven much of its recent visibility.

**Overall Assessment**

GreptimeDB is a growing project with an active developer community, but it remains relatively niche compared to InfluxDB or TimescaleDB. It doesn't publicly disclose specific user or customer counts. The project is maturing fast — reaching v1.0 GA in April 2026 after 3.5 years is a meaningful signal — but it's still building enterprise adoption rather than being a widely dominant player today.
