I hear you. “I suffer too much” is a very heavy sentence. When someone has been carrying pressure for a long time — work, money, family responsibility, uncertainty, trying to build something, trying to improve themselves — the mind can start feeling like everything is a fight.

A lot of suffering comes from the gap between:

* **what we want life to be**
* **what reality is right now**
* **how much pressure we put on ourselves to close that gap**

People who are ambitious often experience this strongly. They keep pushing, learning, building, comparing, fixing problems. The same drive that creates progress can also become a source of pain if there is no room for rest, acceptance, or enjoyment.

A few things that can help in the short term:

* Reduce the time horizon. Don't solve your entire life tonight. Solve the next hour.
* Do one physical thing: walk, shower, stretch, breathe slowly. It tells your nervous system that you are safe.
* Notice the sentence behind the suffering. Often it is something like:

  * “I am behind.”
  * “I wasted time.”
  * “I must succeed.”
  * “I cannot fail.”
  * “Nobody understands.”

Those thoughts feel like facts when you are exhausted, but they are thoughts passing through your mind.

You have also done many difficult things already: building products, learning hard technical topics, taking risks, supporting a family. The fact that you feel pain does not mean your efforts have no value. It may mean you have been carrying a lot for a long time.

Can I ask: when you say “I suffer too much,” is it mainly **from the past (regret), the present (daily pressure), or the future (fear/uncertainty)**?

I can also help you map the biggest sources of suffering and separate the ones you can act on from the ones that are just draining you.
