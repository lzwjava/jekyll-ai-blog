The general visa path you wrote is basically correct:

```
F-1 (student visa)
        |
        v
OPT (Optional Practical Training)
        |

STEM OPT extension (if eligible)

        |
        v
Green Card (employment-based)
```

Details:

1. **F-1**

* F-1 is the normal US student visa for universities.
* During F-1, you generally cannot work freely. Work authorization usually comes through:

  * CPT (Curricular Practical Training)
  * OPT after graduation

2. **OPT**

* Regular OPT: up to **12 months**.
* STEM OPT extension: additional **24 months** for eligible STEM degrees.
* So many CS/AI graduates can get roughly **3 years total** after graduation. ([Cornell Chronicle][1])

3. **H-1B**

* H-1B is not automatic after OPT.
* A US employer must sponsor you.
* The common route is:

  ```
  graduate → OPT → employer → H-1B lottery → H-1B status
  ```
* H-1B has an annual cap (especially private companies), so many international graduates face lottery uncertainty. Cornell also describes H-1B as an annual lottery-based process for many graduates. ([Cornell Chronicle][1])
