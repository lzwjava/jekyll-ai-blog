**Question:** Is it legal in China to set up a TikTok channel (to teach English) using methods like a Macau SIM card to bypass restrictions?

**Answer:**

This involves two separate legal questions — TikTok's status in China, and the legality of bypassing the Great Firewall.

---

### 🔴 TikTok in Mainland China

TikTok is not officially available in mainland China, and TikTok itself states it "does not operate in mainland China." ByteDance built Douyin (抖音) as the domestic counterpart instead.

If the app detects a Chinese mobile country code or a domestic IP address, it blocks access to the feed. TikTok is also not downloadable from mainland Chinese app stores.

---

### 🟡 Does a Macau SIM Card Work as a Bypass?

Technically, partially — but it's complicated:

- Unlike mainland China, Macau does not implement internet censorship, so global platforms are freely accessible there.
- Macau's three carriers — CTM, 3 Macau, and SmarTone Macau — are independent from mainland Chinese networks. However, **China Telecom Macau** uses the same censorship criteria as mainland China, so it's not a good choice for bypassing the firewall.
- A connection routed through mainland Chinese infrastructure hits the same firewall a local SIM would. A connection routed through servers *outside* mainland China — which many international SIM/eSIM providers do — can bypass it entirely.
- Foreign roaming SIMs function in China but may face throttling on certain services.

So a Macau SIM *may* bypass the firewall depending on how the carrier routes traffic, but it is **not a guaranteed or legal workaround**.

---

### 🔴 Is Bypassing the Great Firewall Legal?

**No — it is technically illegal, though enforcement varies:**

- While VPN technology itself isn't illegal, using an unapproved service to access websites and services blocked by the Great Firewall is against Chinese law. Unlicensed VPNs are illegal for both personal and business users.
- The same logic applies to using a foreign SIM or eSIM *specifically to circumvent* the Great Firewall — VPN usage for accessing blocked services is legally restricted under Decree No. 11 of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology.
- In a precedent-setting case, a programmer surnamed Ma was fined over 1 million yuan for using a VPN, with authorities confiscating his earnings as "illegal income."
- Enforcement primarily targets unauthorized commercial VPNs and gateway services, not casual individual users. However, there are documented cases of forced app removals, phone service suspension, and police interviews for VPN use in sensitive regions.

### ✅ Legal Alternatives

If your goal is to **teach English to Chinese learners**, consider these fully legal options:

| Platform | Available in China | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Douyin (抖音)** | ✅ Yes | TikTok's mainland equivalent |
| **Bilibili** | ✅ Yes | Popular for educational content |

Using Douyin is the safest and most effective path — it has hundreds of millions of users and is the natural home for short-video English education content in China.

---

**Bottom line:** Using a Macau SIM (non-China Telecom) *can* technically bypass the Great Firewall in practice, but it sits in a gray/illegal area under Chinese law. Running a public TikTok channel from mainland China this way carries real legal risk, especially if it gains visibility. The recommended approach is to use **Douyin** instead.
