Question: If an employee in a Chinese real-estate company sells a ~¥2 million house, how much does the employee personally earn from the commission?

Answer: It depends heavily on **new-build vs. second-hand** and the company's commission split, but the rough economics look like this:

### 1. New-build, employee works at the developer's sales office

A typical developer sales person's personal commission can be around **0.3%–0.5% of the transaction price** according to industry reporting. ([Xinhua Net][1])

For a ¥2,000,000 house:

```text
¥2,000,000 × 0.3% = ¥6,000
¥2,000,000 × 0.5% = ¥10,000
```

So **~¥6k–¥10k per house** is a reasonable ballpark for the salesperson's commission.

Their total income might therefore look like:

```text
base salary       ¥4k–¥8k/month
+ one sale        ¥6k–¥10k commission
--------------------------------
                  ~¥10k–¥18k that month
```

But commission plans can have thresholds, team splits, bonuses, clawbacks, etc.

### 2. New-build, employee is an external intermediary/channel agent

This is quite different.

The **agency/company** might receive something like **2%–5%** of the property price from the developer in some projects. Recent reporting in Guangzhou, for example, described new-home channel commissions around **2%–4.5%** in a particular market/project period. ([Sina Finance][2])

For ¥2m:

```text
2%   = ¥40,000
3%   = ¥60,000
4.5%  = ¥90,000
```

But **the salesperson doesn't necessarily receive all of this**.

If the agency gives the salesperson, say, 30%–50% of its commission:

```text
agency commission    ¥60,000
salesperson 40%      ¥24,000
company/team         ¥36,000
```

Historical industry reporting gives examples of salesperson splits around **20%–30%**, with some companies/levels reaching ~50%. ([ZJ News][3])

So a channel salesperson might personally make roughly **¥10k–¥30k+ from a ¥2m transaction**, depending on the project and their split.

### 3. Second-hand house

For second-hand housing, the structure is usually:

```text
house price
    ↓
agency service fee
    ↓
branch/company
    ↓
salesperson commission
```

The actual service fee is market-negotiated; current national guidance explicitly says real-estate brokerage fees should be determined through negotiation and encourages lower rates for higher-priced homes. ([SAMR][4])

As a simplified example, suppose the agency charges **2%**:

```text
¥2,000,000 × 2% = ¥40,000 agency revenue

if salesperson gets 30%:
¥40,000 × 30% = ¥12,000

if salesperson gets 50%:
¥40,000 × 50% = ¥20,000
```

So again, **~¥10k–¥20k** from a ¥2m transaction is quite plausible, although individual companies' plans differ substantially.

### The key distinction

Don't confuse:

> **"The company earns ¥60k commission"**

with

> **"the employee earns ¥60k."**

The employee usually receives only a fraction of the brokerage commission.

| ¥2m house              |                Company/agency revenue | Employee might receive |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------: | ---------------------: |
| Developer sales office | ~¥6k–¥10k equivalent sales commission |          **~¥6k–¥10k** |
| New-home channel       |                            ~¥40k–¥90k |        **~¥10k–¥30k+** |
| Second-hand brokerage  |                       ~¥40k at 2% fee |         **~¥10k–¥20k** |

These are **illustrative ranges, not a national fixed rate**. China's regulations require real-estate agencies to publicly disclose their service items and fee standards, and brokerage staff generally cannot independently collect the transaction fee—the agency collects it. ([jyg.gov.cn][5])

If you're asking because you're evaluating **how lucrative being a real-estate salesperson in China actually is**, I can also model a salesperson's monthly income at **0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 sales per month**, including base salary and commission.
