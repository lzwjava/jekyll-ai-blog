**Question:** Is the essay at <https://lzwjava.com/ceo-en> a good public essay to announce a company?

---

**Answer:**

The essay — *"With 21-Year-Old CEO, Achieve Small Goals"* (2016, Zhiwei Li) — is a sincere personal narrative, but as a **company announcement essay**, it has notable strengths and significant weaknesses.

---

### ✅ Strengths

1. **Authentic voice.** The essay is honest and personal. Readers feel the founder's genuine motivation, not polished PR language.
2. **Credibility-building journey.** It walks through real experience: LeanCloud, Y Combinator visit, two prior startups, mentors at Microsoft/Google-level circles. For a 21-year-old, the background is impressive.
3. **Inspirational tone.** The closing call to "build dreams together" could attract like-minded co-founders and early believers.
4. **Concrete ask.** It clearly lists what roles are being recruited (design, backend/Android, operations) and offers 10–30% equity — which is unusually transparent.

---

### ❌ Weaknesses (as a company announcement)

| Issue | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **No company name** | The company being announced is never actually named. |
| **No product description** | The business is only vaguely described as "a new direction... in cloud computing, e-commerce, and IT education." |
| **No problem statement** | It never clearly states what customer pain the company solves. |
| **No business model** | How will it make money? Unclear. |
| **Structure is scattered** | The narrative jumps across years of experiences without a clear arc toward "here's the company." |
| **Title undersells** | "Achieve Small Goals" is oddly modest for a launch announcement. |
| **Reads like a personal blog** | The audience feels like friends and followers, not investors or customers. |

---

### 🔑 Verdict

It works well as a **personal founding story** or a **co-founder recruitment post**, but it fails as a proper **company announcement**. A strong company announcement should lead with: *what the company is, what problem it solves, and why you*. This essay buries all three under a long personal memoir.

If the goal was to attract early co-founders who would bet on the founder personally — it may have worked. If the goal was to announce the company to the broader public or potential customers, it falls short.
