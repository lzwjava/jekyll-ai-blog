**Question:** What is the full story of the conflict between Apple and Micron — where Micron suffered losses around 2022–2023, Apple pushed for lower prices during that time, and then Apple complained about Micron's high prices in recent months?

---

**Answer:**

This is actually a fascinating and very current drama that just exploded publicly in mid-2026. Here's the full arc:

---

### 🔵 Phase 1 — The Memory Boom & Bust (2021–2023)

In fiscal 2021, Micron was riding high — DRAM average selling prices rose 8% as pandemic demand for PCs and gadgets outpaced supply, and Micron churned out $5.86 billion in net income on $27.7 billion in revenue.

But that reversed hard. Oversupply arrived and prices began collapsing rapidly — one forecast saw a 13–18% sequential decline in consumer DRAM in a single quarter. Micron reported a net loss of around $1.6 billion for its fiscal year 2023, as weak pricing and elevated inventories crushed profitability.

---

### 🔴 Phase 2 — Apple Squeezed During the Down Cycle (2022–2023)

Here's where Apple enters as the villain of Micron's story. Micron's Chief Business Officer Sumit Sadana argued that when memory prices collapsed during the 2023 downturn, big customers used the slump to negotiate rock-bottom prices — gutting suppliers' margins at the exact moment they needed capital to build new capacity. Micron's gross margin turned sharply negative, bottoming at minus 17.8% in its fiscal third quarter of 2023.

Without directly naming Apple, Sadana disclosed that Micron had informed a couple of its customers "who were being very aggressive with pricing" that their strategy was "not constructive," and that "a lot of the industry investments got shut down in 2023 because of really poor pricing and really poor margins."

So during the period when Micron was bleeding losses, Apple was aggressively using its massive buying power to drive prices down even further — which deterred Micron from investing in new factory capacity.

---

### 🟡 Phase 3 — AI Demand Explodes, Supply Can't Keep Up (2024–2026)

Generative AI from 2023 to 2025 initially drove up High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) demand for AI training, prompting major memory makers to shift production toward HBM, DDR5, and LPDDR5 — tightening global DRAM supply. Then with the rise of agentic AI in 2026, standard DRAM demand also surged, while capacity was already squeezed.

Because Micron had cut investments during the 2023 downturn (partly due to aggressive buyer pressure), there was no spare capacity to meet the new demand wave. Micron's gross margins recovered to nearly 85%, and its stock jumped roughly 15% after earnings, with a reported 346% revenue surge.

---

### 🟠 Phase 4 — Apple Complains & Raises Prices (June 2026)

On June 25, 2026, Apple unveiled price increases across its entire MacBook and iPad portfolio, with certain MacBook configurations rising by up to $300. Apple CEO Tim Cook called the increases "unavoidable," pointing to memory chip manufacturers for imposing substantial cost increases.

Micron's Sadana fired back almost simultaneously. Memory chip makers, having had enough of Apple using them as a scapegoat to preserve its own margins, pushed back — blaming Apple's penny-pinching during the down cycle for years of under-investment and the resulting capacity shortage.

---

### ⚫ Phase 5 — The Nuclear Option: Apple Tries to Buy Chinese Chips (2026)

Apple is now lobbying the Trump administration for permission to source memory from China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) — a company blacklisted by the U.S. government due to ties to the Chinese military — arguing that Micron is charging excessive prices in today's tight market.

Apple's suppliers — Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron — are also now facing a class-action lawsuit alleging they coordinated DRAM production cuts since 2022, driving prices up approximately 700% over four years. Micron denied the claims and said it will defend itself.

---

### 🧩 The Bottom Line

The story is essentially a **classic supply-chain power struggle**:

- Apple used its dominance to crush memory prices when suppliers were weak → suppliers couldn't invest in new capacity.
- When AI demand exploded and supply was tight, memory prices skyrocketed.
- Apple complained loudly — even though Micron argues Apple's own behavior caused the shortage.
- As Micron's CEO put it bluntly: "For the past decade, Apple bought our chips for $5, put them inside a metal box, sold it to consumers for $99, and then laughed at us for wanting to raise the price to $7. Today we sell the chip for $50, and Apple simply passes that on by charging consumers $250."
