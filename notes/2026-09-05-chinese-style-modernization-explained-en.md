**Question:** What is 中国式现代化 (Chinese-style Modernization)?

**Answer:**

**中国式现代化** (Zhōngguó shì xiàndàihuà) translates as **"Chinese-style modernization"** or **"the Chinese path to modernization."**

**Origins**

The term dates back to 1979, when Chinese leader Deng Xiaoping mentioned it in a speech on the Four Cardinal Principles. However, the first half of Deng's phrase never became a standalone concept in its own right — not until Xi Jinping appropriated the idea in 2021. While Deng's version was broadly about introducing new approaches while accommodating China's national circumstances, Xi Jinping's is much bolder — an assertion of the uniqueness, and even superiority, of China's approach to development as opposed to that of the West.

**Core Claim**

The concept encapsulates the political assertion that modernization as pursued by China abides by principles such as equitable income distribution, peaceful national development, and ecological sustainability — distinguishing it from how modernization unfolded in the developed West. Simultaneously, it makes the political claim that the prerequisite of such modernization is the rule of the CCP.

**Five Distinctive Features** (as defined at the 20th Party Congress, 2022)

The Chinese Path to Modernization is characterized by five key features and nine essential requirements. According to official CCP doctrine, the five features are:

1. Modernization of a huge population
2. Common prosperity for all
3. Coordination of material and cultural-ethical advancement
4. Harmony between humanity and nature
5. Peaceful development (rather than colonialism or hegemony)

**Nine Essential Requirements**

These were formally set out in Xi Jinping's report to the 20th Party Congress in October 2022, including: upholding CCP leadership, upholding socialism with Chinese characteristics, achieving high-quality development, and developing whole-process people's democracy.

**Geopolitical Framing**

The concept is described in official discourse as "creating a new form of human civilization" and "offering a Chinese proposal for humanity's search for a better social system." It defines itself explicitly in opposition to Western-style modernization.

Crucially, it modernizes industry, agriculture, military, and science & technology — but deliberately excludes political liberalization or Western-style democracy, which the CCP argues has led to social conflict in Western societies.
