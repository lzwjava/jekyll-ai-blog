Question: **What U.S. graduate programs are similar to Illinois Tech’s M.A.S. in Artificial Intelligence?**

Answer: Yes. If you mean **practical, industry-oriented AI master's programs** rather than CMU/Stanford-style research-heavy programs, there are quite a few worth putting on your application list.

Illinois Tech's M.A.S. is a good reference point: it is STEM-designated, F-1 eligible, and covers ML, NLP, computer vision, and other applied AI areas. ([Illinois Institute of Technology][1])

### 1. NJIT M.S. in Artificial Intelligence

This is probably one of the **closest matches to Illinois Tech**.

[NJIT M.S. in Artificial Intelligence](https://ds.njit.edu/ms-artificial-intelligence?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The curriculum is unusually aligned with what you actually do:

* Probability
* Big Data
* Machine Learning
* Reinforcement Learning
* Deep Learning
* NLP
* Graph Neural Networks
* Trustworthy AI

It's 30 credits / 10 courses, and there is a thesis option. ([NJIT Catalog][2])

For someone who wants **AI engineer / ML engineer rather than theoretical AI researcher**, I like this one.

**Big issue for you:** NJIT explicitly requires a bachelor's degree, although it has a graduate-certificate pathway for certain applicants. ([Department of Data Science][3])

### 2. Stevens Institute of Technology — Applied AI

[Stevens M.S. in Applied Artificial Intelligence](https://www.stevens.edu/program/applied-artificial-intelligence-masters?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is another very good match.

It is a 30-credit program and combines AI theory with engineering applications. Concentrations include:

* Computer Engineering
* Data Engineering
* Software Engineering
* Machine Learning-related work
* Robotics
* etc.

Stevens also has a dedicated AI institute and separate master's programs in **Machine Learning, Robotics, CS and Data Science**. ([Stevens Institute of Technology][4])

For you, **Stevens + NYC** is particularly interesting because you could potentially combine the degree with your existing software/AI engineering experience.

### 3. DePaul University — M.S. Artificial Intelligence

[DePaul M.S. Artificial Intelligence](https://www.depaul.edu/academics/programs/ms-in-artificial-intelligence?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is another very direct Illinois Tech alternative.

The program covers:

* Machine learning
* NLP
* Big data systems
* Computer vision
* Image processing
* Robotics
* Cybersecurity

And DePaul explicitly describes it as a **professional master's degree**, emphasizing practical applications. It is also STEM-designated. ([DePaul University][5])

I would put **DePaul and Illinois Tech in roughly the same "practical Chicago AI master's" bucket**.

### 4. Drexel — M.S. Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning

[Drexel M.S. Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning](https://catalog.drexel.edu/graduate/collegeofcomputingandinformatics/artificialintelligenceandmachinelearning/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This one is attractive if you want something more technically serious.

It explicitly emphasizes:

> mathematical foundations + algorithms + tools + applications

and has **Applied** and **Computational** tracks. More importantly, full-time on-campus master's students have a **co-op option**. ([Drexel University Catalog][6])

That co-op component can be quite valuable if your objective is:

**degree → U.S. internship/job → AI engineer**

rather than simply obtaining the credential.

### 5. Northeastern — M.S. Artificial Intelligence

[Northeastern M.S. Artificial Intelligence](https://graduate.northeastern.edu/programs/ms-artificial-intelligence/master-of-science-in-artificial-intelligence/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

I'd put Northeastern a little above Illinois Tech/DePaul/NJIT in terms of overall university/industry positioning.

The AI program has specialization areas including:

* Machine Learning
* Robotics and agent-based systems
* Vision
* Intelligent interaction
* Knowledge management/reasoning

and offers specialization, thesis, or coursework-only paths. ([Northeastern University Academic Catalog][7])

The interesting part for you is **Boston + Northeastern's co-op/industry ecosystem**.

The downside is obvious: **cost**. Northeastern estimates roughly $60k tuition for the 2025–26 AI program. ([Northeastern Grad Programs][8])

### 6. ASU — AI Engineering

[ASU AI graduate programs](https://ai.asu.edu/asu-programs?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

ASU is particularly interesting because it is moving toward **AI Engineering**, rather than merely calling everything "Computer Science."

For example, it has AI Engineering programs focused on:

* Software Engineering
* Robotics
* Human-Centered AI
* Operations/Decision Science

The **Software Engineering** concentration is especially relevant to you because it combines AI/ML with advanced software development and intelligent applications. ([Artificial Intelligence at ASU][9])

ASU has also approved a broader M.S. in Artificial Intelligence in 2026, so its AI graduate portfolio is expanding. ([University Senate][10])

## Then there is the next tier

I wouldn't only search for degrees literally named **"Master of Artificial Intelligence."**

For your goals, these can actually be **better**:

**MS Computer Science + Machine Learning/AI specialization**

For example:

* Georgia Tech MSCS — Machine Learning
* UMD MSCS / AI-related programs
* NYU Tandon MSCS
* UIUC MCS / CS
* UT Austin MSCS
* UC Irvine MCS
* ASU MSCS

The title **"MS Computer Science" isn't worse than "MS Artificial Intelligence."**

For an AI engineer, I'd often prefer:

```text
MS Computer Science
    +
ML
DL
NLP
Distributed Systems
GPU / Parallel Computing
Systems
```

over:

```text
MS Artificial Intelligence
    +
AI fundamentals
AI ethics
business applications
generic AI courses
```

The **course selection matters more than the degree title**.
