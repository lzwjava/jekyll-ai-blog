Question: **How can a Chinese citizen get a Canadian tourist visa?**

Answer: If you hold an **ordinary Chinese passport**, you generally need a **Canadian Visitor Visa (Temporary Resident Visa / TRV)** before travelling to Canada. You can apply online; you do not need to use an immigration agent. ([Canada][1])

### 1. Apply online

Use the official IRCC portal:

[Canada — Apply for a Visitor Visa](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada/apply-visitor-visa.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The basic flow is:

```text
Create IRCC account
      ↓
Complete visitor visa application
      ↓
Upload documents
      ↓
Pay CAD $100 visa fee
      ↓
Receive Biometrics Instruction Letter
      ↓
Give fingerprints + photo
      ↓
Wait for decision
      ↓
If approved → submit passport
      ↓
Visa sticker placed in passport
      ↓
Travel to Canada
```

The current visitor-visa fee is **CAD $100**, and biometrics are generally **CAD $85** for an individual. ([Canada][2])

### 2. The important part: prove you will return

Canada isn't simply checking whether you have enough money. The key question is:

> **Are you a genuine temporary visitor who will leave Canada after the trip?**

IRCC explicitly says you need to convince the officer that you have ties—such as a **job, home, financial assets or family**—that will take you back, and that you have enough money for the trip. ([Canada][3])

For a Chinese applicant, I'd prepare something like:

```text
Identity
├── Chinese passport
├── ID
└── household registration, if useful

Employment / business
├── employment certificate
├── employment contract
├── recent payslips
└── business/company documents if self-employed

Financial
├── 3–6 months bank statements
├── savings
├── investment/brokerage statements if relevant
└── proof of income

Travel
├── proposed Canada itinerary
├── hotel reservations
└── flight itinerary/reservation

Travel history
├── previous US visas/trips
├── Japan
├── Europe
├── Singapore
└── other countries

Purpose
└── short explanation of why you're visiting Canada
```

You **don't need to buy a non-refundable flight ticket before getting the visa**.

### 3. Biometrics

For most applicants, you need fingerprints and a photograph. After submitting the application, IRCC sends a **Biometrics Instruction Letter**, then you book an appointment at a Visa Application Centre (VAC). ([Canada][4])

If you're applying from China, Canada has VAC services there. If you're legally living in another country, you can also apply online from there and give biometrics at an appropriate VAC. ([IRCC][5])

### 4. How long can the visa be valid?

A Canadian visitor visa **can be issued for up to 10 years**, but it cannot extend beyond the expiry of your passport or biometrics, and the officer can issue a shorter validity period. ([Canada][2])

Importantly:

```text
10-year visa
    ≠
10 years continuously staying in Canada
```

The visa lets you travel to Canada and request entry. The border officer determines whether you can enter and how long you can stay.

### 5. If you are currently in the US

If you're currently legally in the US, you **can apply online from the US**; you don't necessarily need to return to China. Canada explicitly allows people legally admitted to another country to apply from there. ([IRCC][5])

And interestingly, **US immigration biometrics do not substitute for Canadian biometrics**. If Canada asks you for biometrics, you still need to provide them. ([IRCC][6])

### What I would do in your situation

If your goal is simply:

> **Chinese passport → visit Canada for 1–3 weeks as a tourist**

I'd make the application very straightforward:

```text
Purpose:
Tourism

Duration:
~10–20 days

Itinerary:
Vancouver → Banff → Calgary
(or another realistic route)

Funding:
Personal savings/income

Return reason:
Employment/business/family/residence outside Canada

Evidence:
Passport
+ bank statements
+ income/employment evidence
+ travel history
+ itinerary
+ accommodation
+ short letter explaining the trip
```

Don't manufacture a complicated story. **Consistency between your income, bank balance, employment/business situation, itinerary, and stated purpose is much more important.**
