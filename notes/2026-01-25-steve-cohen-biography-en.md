Question: Provide a comprehensive introduction to Steve Cohen.

Answer:

**Steve Cohen** (full name: Steven A. Cohen) is an American billionaire hedge fund manager, investor, philanthropist, and sports team owner. He is widely regarded as one of the most successful and influential figures in modern Wall Street history, known for his aggressive trading style, exceptional performance record, and ability to generate high returns.

### Early Life and Education

- Born on **June 11, 1956**, in Great Neck, Long Island, New York.
- Grew up in a middle-class Jewish family; his father was a factory worker and his mother a part-time piano teacher.
- Developed an early interest in investing: reportedly bought shares in Apple as a teenager and made profits trading stocks.
- Graduated from the Wharton School at the University of Pennsylvania in 1978 with a degree in economics.

### Career Beginnings

After college, Cohen started his career on Wall Street in 1978 as a junior trader and options specialist at Gruntal & Co., a brokerage firm.
He quickly stood out for his trading prowess and reportedly generated significant profits for the firm, earning a reputation as a highly talented and aggressive trader.

### Founding SAC Capital Advisors

- In 1992, at age 36, he founded **S.A.C. Capital Advisors** (named after his initials) with approximately $25 million, much of it his own capital.
- SAC became one of the most successful hedge funds in history, achieving average annual returns of around 25–30% over two decades.
- The firm was known for its intense, high-pressure culture, rapid decision-making, and heavy reliance on short-term trading and information flow.
- At its peak, SAC managed billions in assets and employed hundreds of people.

### Insider Trading Scandal and Transition

- In the early 2010s, SAC faced intense scrutiny from federal prosecutors over insider trading allegations.
- Several SAC employees were convicted or pleaded guilty to insider trading charges.
- In November 2013, SAC Capital itself pleaded guilty to insider trading violations (a rare event for a major hedge fund firm) and agreed to pay a record **$1.8 billion** in penalties and restitution.
- Cohen was not personally charged with insider trading, but was barred from managing outside money for several years.
- Following the settlement, SAC stopped managing external capital and restructured.

### Point72 Asset Management

- In 2014–2018, Cohen relaunched his firm as **Point72 Asset Management**, initially managing only his personal and employee capital.
- Point72 began accepting outside investors again in 2018.
- As of recent data, Point72 manages approximately **$40 billion** in assets under management and operates as a global multi-strategy investment firm with a focus on equities, macro, quantitative strategies, and more.
- Cohen serves as Chairman and CEO of Point72.

### New York Mets Ownership

- In September 2020, Cohen purchased the **New York Mets** (MLB team) for **$2.4 billion** — at the time, the highest price ever paid for a Major League Baseball franchise.
- He serves as the team's Chairman and CEO.
- Under his ownership, the Mets have invested heavily in talent, analytics, and infrastructure (including stadium improvements), aiming to build a perennial contender.

### Personal Life and Philanthropy

- Lives in Greenwich, Connecticut.
- Known as an avid art collector, with one of the world's most valuable private art collections (works by Picasso, Warhol, and others).
- Has donated hundreds of millions to philanthropy, including significant gifts to hospitals, education (e.g., supporting hospitals and medical research), and Jewish causes.
- Estimated lifetime philanthropic giving exceeds **$1 billion**.

### Net Worth and Recognition

- As of 2024–2025 estimates, Cohen's net worth is around **$21 billion**, placing him among the richest people in the United States (frequently ranked in the top 50 on Forbes lists).
- Often described as "the hedge fund king" or one of the most feared and respected traders on Wall Street.

Cohen's career combines legendary investment performance with controversy, remarkable resilience, and diversification into sports ownership and philanthropy.
