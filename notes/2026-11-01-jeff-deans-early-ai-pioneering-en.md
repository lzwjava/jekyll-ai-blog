**Question:** Did Jeff Dean start working on AI quite early?

**Answer:** Yes, Jeff Dean's involvement with AI goes back surprisingly far:

- **Undergraduate level (late 1980s):** Even as a student, Dean worked on neural networks in C programming, advised by Vipin Kumar — this was at a time when neural networks were deeply unfashionable in the research community.

- **2007 — Early language models:** He worked on language modeling starting in 2007, training 300 billion parameter language models on trillions of tokens of text, demonstrated in the paper *Large language models in machine translation* — remarkably early for large-scale LM work.

- **2011 — Google Brain founder:** He joined a project that morphed into Google Brain, a team that studies large-scale artificial neural networks, formed in 2011. He was an early member alongside Andrew Ng.

- **DistBelief & deep learning infrastructure:** Some of the earliest research work using DistBelief explored unsupervised learning on video frames — also used to develop word2vec, speech recognition models, and early embedding models like RankBrain.

- **2018 onward:** In April 2018, Dean was appointed the head of Google's artificial intelligence division, and later became Google's Chief Scientist when DeepMind and Google Brain merged.

So yes — he was deeply involved in AI **well before** the modern deep learning boom, making his commitment to the field notably early and sustained.
