**Question:** Did Charlie Munger say that jealousy is toxic and leads to an unhappy life?

**Answer:** Yes, that's essentially correct — though a small correction on the name: it's **Charlie Munger** (not "murger"). He was the vice-chairman of Berkshire Hathaway and one of Warren Buffett's closest partners.

Munger spoke about jealousy and envy extensively throughout his life. He famously stated, "The world is not driven by greed but by jealousy," and called jealousy a great cause of human suffering.

When asked about the secret to a long and happy life, Munger kept it simple: don't have a lot of envy, don't have a lot of resentment, don't overspend your income, stay cheerful in spite of your troubles, deal with reliable people, and do what you are supposed to do.

In 2019, Munger spoke out against jealousy, claiming that staying away from this emotion is one of the simple secrets to a long and happy life.

He also practiced what he preached. "I have conquered envy in my own life. I don't envy anybody," Munger said. "I don't give a damn what someone else has. But other people are driven crazy by it."

In his famous 1986 Harvard commencement speech, Munger used inversion to relay his message — listing envy and resentment as things that lead to a life of misery, sarcastically saying he "cannot recommend them highly enough if you desire misery."

So your summary is spot on: Munger viewed jealousy/envy as deeply destructive to happiness.
