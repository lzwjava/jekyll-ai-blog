Question: Is "the customer is god" a true principle in business?

Answer: It depends on context and interpretation.

The phrase "the customer is always right" (or "customer is god," especially in Japanese business culture — **お客様は神様です**) captures the spirit of **prioritizing customer satisfaction**. There's real truth in it:

- Businesses exist because customers pay for products/services
- Listening to customers drives product improvement
- Good service builds loyalty and reputation

**However, it's not an absolute truth.** Most business thinkers today push back on it:

- Customers can be wrong, abusive, or have unreasonable demands
- Blindly obeying every customer can demoralize employees, harm team culture, and even hurt the business
- Some customers are simply not your target audience — trying to please everyone pleases no one
- Companies like Apple famously succeeded by *telling* customers what they wanted, not just asking

A more balanced modern view: **Customers are the reason you exist, but not infallible gods.** Treat them with respect, listen carefully, but also have the wisdom to know when to say no or push back constructively.

The Japanese version (神様/kami-sama) is often taken more literally in service culture there, which is why Japanese customer service is famously exceptional — but even in Japan, industry insiders debate whether it goes too far.
