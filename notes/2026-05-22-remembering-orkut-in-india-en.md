**Question:** What was the Facebook-era messaging app popular in India around 2004–2010? Something called "Ola" or similar?

**Answer:**

You're thinking of **Orkut**.**Orkut** — not Facebook, actually. It was a **Google** product. Here's the full picture:

## What Was Orkut

Orkut was a social networking platform developed by Google, launched on January 24, 2004. It was named after its creator, Orkut Büyükkökten, a Turkish Google employee.

It was actually the result of a "20% project" — where Google workers got to spend a fifth of their time on ideas not necessarily related to their job responsibilities.

## Why India Specifically

In places like Brazil and India, it was the internet's social fabric — it introduced millions of people to the idea that your online identity could be a profile, a wall, and a web of friendships.

In India, Orkut fell to Facebook in 2010. So your Indian colleague's 2004-era memories are exactly on point — Orkut was *the* platform then.

## Shutdown

Google announced the shutdown in June 2014, and the platform officially ceased operations on September 30, 2014, after a decade of existence.

## The Parallel to QQ

| | QQ (China) | Orkut (India) |
| -- | ----------- | -------------- |
| Launch | 1999 | 2004 |
| Peak | 2000s–still active | 2005–2010 |
| Killed by | WeChat (same company) | Facebook |
| Shut down | Still running | 2014 |

Key difference: Tencent *owned* both QQ and WeChat, so they managed the transition. Google let Orkut die while Facebook took the market. Same generational story, different ending.
