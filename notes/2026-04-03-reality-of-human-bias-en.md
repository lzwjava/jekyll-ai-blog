Question: nobody in this world will actually treat people equally , everyone has bias , have people they like more , have people they hate or unlike , right? have different trust to different people , right?

Answer:

Yes — in reality, humans naturally treat people **unequally**. This happens because of psychology, experience, incentives, and social dynamics. But there’s an important distinction:

* **Equality of value (ideal)** → treat everyone with fairness and respect
* **Equality of behavior (reality)** → people behave differently toward different individuals

These two are not the same.

---

## 1. Humans naturally have bias

People automatically:

* like some people more
* distrust some people
* feel closer to familiar personalities
* avoid people with bad past experiences

This comes from:

* past interactions
* personality similarity
* shared interests
* group identity (team, culture, language)
* perceived competence
* reputation

Psychology calls this **in-group bias** and **implicit bias** — it’s automatic, not always intentional.

So yes — **no one treats everyone exactly the same**.

---

## 2. Trust is always unequal

Trust is built on:

* history
* reliability
* competence
* emotional connection
* risk

Example:

* You trust a long-time coworker more than a new hire
* You trust a careful engineer more than a careless one
* You trust a friend more than a stranger

This is rational — not unfair.

**Equal trust for everyone would actually be dangerous.**

---

## 3. Even "fair" people still behave differently

A fair manager:

* gives equal opportunity
* but assigns harder tasks to stronger engineers
* asks reliable people to handle critical work
* double-checks risky people

This is **fair but unequal treatment**.

So:

* fairness ≠ identical treatment
* fairness = appropriate treatment

---

## 4. Humans prioritize limited energy

People have limited:

* time
* attention
* emotional bandwidth
* risk tolerance

So they naturally invest more in:

* people they like
* people who help them
* people they trust
* people with shared goals

This is unavoidable.

---

## 5. But there is an important boundary

Healthy bias:

* trusting proven people more
* liking compatible personalities
* preferring reliable collaborators

Unhealthy bias:

* discrimination
* unfair exclusion
* ignoring competence
* tribal thinking

Everyone has bias — **but good people try to control unfair bias.**

---

## 6. The most realistic view

The real world works like this:

* Everyone has bias ✅
* Everyone prefers some people ✅
* Everyone trusts unequally ✅
* Nobody treats everyone identically ✅

But also:

* Good people try to be fair
* Professionals separate feelings from decisions
* Mature people update bias with evidence

---

## One sentence summary

Humans cannot treat everyone equally in behavior, but they **can try to be fair in principle**.

That’s the realistic balance.

This is actually an important social skill:

* understand bias exists
* don’t expect equal treatment
* build trust gradually
* become the person others prefer to trust
