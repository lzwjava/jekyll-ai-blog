Question: Why do people stay in big corporations and live comfortable lives instead of taking the leap into startups?

Answer:

It comes down to a few deep human forces working together:

**Loss aversion beats ambition**
Psychologically, losing something hurts roughly twice as much as gaining the same thing feels good (Kahneman's prospect theory). A stable $200K salary *feels* more valuable to protect than the *chance* at $2M feels worth chasing. The brain is wired to preserve what you have.

**Identity gets fused with the job**
After years at Google or Goldman, "I work at X" becomes *who you are* — to yourself, your parents, your partner, your LinkedIn. Quitting means a small identity death. That's genuinely painful, not just irrational.

**Golden handcuffs are real**
RSUs vesting over 4 years, health insurance, pension, visa sponsorship — these aren't just perks, they're engineered retention mechanisms. Every year you wait, you lose something concrete. The sunk cost trap keeps people anchored.

**Social pressure from loved ones**
Parents, spouses, friends often reinforce staying. "You have such a good job" is well-intentioned, but it trains you to interpret risk as recklessness rather than courage.

**Comfort degrades risk tolerance**
Paradoxically, the more comfortable life becomes — nice apartment, lifestyle inflation, a kid — the higher the psychological cost of uncertainty. The people *most* equipped with skills to start something are often *least* willing to sacrifice the life those skills bought them.

**The startup narrative lies**
Most people see Zuckerberg, not the 999 founders who burned through savings and quietly went back to employment. Survivorship bias makes the odds look better from the outside. Once inside a big corp, you see colleagues who *tried* and came back humbled — that's a more honest data set.

**Optionality illusion**
"I'll do it someday" feels low-cost to believe. The big corp job feels like it keeps options open, when in reality habits, obligations, and age quietly close them. The "right time" never arrives on its own.

---

The brutal honest summary: **comfort is a slow sedative**. It doesn't feel like fear — it feels like wisdom, responsibility, and patience. That's what makes it so effective at keeping people exactly where they are.

The founders who do leap usually have one of: nothing to lose, something burning inside they can't ignore, or a co-founder who pulls them over the edge.
