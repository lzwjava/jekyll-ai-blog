Question: What school does Elon Musk want to make for kids and young people?

Answer:

Elon Musk has talked about building a new kind of education system, starting with a school called **Ad Astra** (“to the stars” in Latin). He originally created it in 2014 for his own children and some SpaceX employees' children because he felt traditional schools were not teaching the way he wanted. ([Wikipedia][1])

The core ideas:

### 1. No traditional grade system

Musk criticized the "assembly line" model:

> Put kids of the same age together → same textbook → same speed.

Instead, Ad Astra tried mixed-age learning, where students progress based on ability and interest. ([VentureBeat][2])

### 2. Teach problem-solving, not memorization

His philosophy:

Traditional:

```
Learn tools:
  screwdriver
  wrench
  engine theory

Then:
  maybe build engine
```

Musk's approach:

```
Give students an engine.

Question:
  How does it work?
  How can we improve it?

Then learn:
  physics
  math
  engineering
  programming
```

Basically **learning from first principles**. ([VentureBeat][2])

### 3. STEM + building things

The school emphasizes:

* mathematics
* science
* engineering
* AI
* robotics
* hands-on projects

The goal is not just "get good grades"; it is creating builders and inventors. ([Forbes][3])

### 4. Scale from preschool to university

More recently, Musk's education efforts expanded around Texas. The Ad Astra project has been described as aiming toward a broader education ecosystem, potentially including elementary, secondary education, and eventually a university. ([Kut.org][4])

There was also discussion of a future university called the Texas Institute of Technology and Science, though it remains a proposal. ([Wikipedia][5])

---

The deeper idea is very similar to how Musk runs companies:

```
Traditional education:
  memorize -> test -> credential

Musk style:
  curiosity -> build -> fail -> iterate -> create
```

It is closer to a **startup incubator for children** than a normal school.

This connects with ideas from people like Seymour Papert (learning by making) and modern AI-era education: children should learn how to use powerful tools and create, not only consume information.

For AI education today, this model is very relevant: a future "Ad Astra for AI" might look like:

```
Age 8:
  build games with AI

Age 10:
  train small models

Age 12:
  create AI agents

Age 15:
  launch real products
```

The interesting question is whether this can scale from a small elite school into a system for millions of kids. That is the hard part. ([astranova.org][6])
