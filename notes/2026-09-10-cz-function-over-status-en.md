**Question:** Is Binance founder Changpeng Zhao (CZ)'s lifestyle mostly functional rather than luxury, with the private jet being one of the major luxuries?

**Answer:** Yes — **that is a pretty accurate characterization of how CZ describes himself**, with one correction: it isn't literally *only* a private jet.

CZ has repeatedly said that he is **function-driven rather than status/luxury-driven**:

* He said his house is an old, third/fourth-hand house with recurring water leaks, but he keeps it because **“it works for me”** and is in the right location. ([The Singju Post][1])
* He says he doesn't own Ferraris/Lamborghinis, expensive watches, or an art collection. Instead, he buys **functional things** related to activities he actually enjoys: skiing, snowboarding, windsurfing, kite surfing, and a small home gym. ([The Singju Post][2])
* His vehicle is a **Lexus van**, chosen partly because its rear seat can lie flat, which is useful for his back and long trips. That's a very "utility over status" purchase despite being expensive. ([The Singju Post][2])
* **Private aviation is the conspicuous exception**, but even his stated reason is practical: privacy, security, avoiding airports, and avoiding being recognized and approached during commercial flights. He described having a long-term lease arrangement rather than simply emphasizing ownership of a jet. ([The Singju Post][2])

There's an interesting evolution here. Earlier in his career, CZ said he didn't even have a private jet, yacht, or car; he bought electronics and flew business class. He said that once he had enough money, he realized **time was more valuable than money**. ([Unchained][3])

So the pattern is roughly:

```text
CZ's wealth
    │
    ├── Status consumption       → relatively low
    │     ├── Lamborghini        → no
    │     ├── Ferrari             → no
    │     ├── luxury watches      → no
    │     └── art collection      → no
    │
    ├── Functional consumption   → high
    │     ├── normal/old house
    │     ├── practical vehicle
    │     ├── home gym
    │     └── outdoor sports
    │
    └── Time/privacy purchases   → high
          └── private aviation
```

And that's an important distinction: **being extremely wealthy doesn't necessarily mean maximizing luxury consumption**. At some wealth level, the rational purchase is often not "more beautiful stuff," but **removing friction**:

> money → time
> money → privacy
> money → convenience
> money → health
> money → freedom

That also resembles a broader pattern among some newer tech wealth: recent reporting describes younger AI-rich people spending disproportionately on things that are **high-performance, private, convenient, and functional**, rather than traditional conspicuous luxury. ([ft.com][4])
