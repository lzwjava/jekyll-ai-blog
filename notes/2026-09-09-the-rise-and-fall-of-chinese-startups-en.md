**Answer:**

One important caveat first: the founder of IT Juzi himself disputed the exact figures reported in the Western press, saying "the cited data is inaccurate" and that the 1,202 figure represented startups recorded over a specific period, not the total count for the year. So treat the numbers as indicative of a steep trend, not precise statistics.

That said, the overall shape of the story is widely corroborated. Based on available data points, here's how the 15-year arc looks:The story breaks into three distinct phases:

**Rise (2010–2018):** China's startup boom was fueled by government "mass entrepreneurship" campaigns, massive foreign VC inflows, and the explosive growth of the mobile internet. China became the world's second-largest breeding ground for unicorns, with 3,683 early-stage investments totaling $20.2 billion in 2016 alone. Startups multiplied nearly 100x in under a decade.

**Peak (2018):** The confirmed high-water mark. China had 51,302 new startups in 2018, according to IT Juzi. This was also the year China briefly surpassed the US as the top destination for VC investment globally.

**Collapse (2019–2024):** The decline began before COVID and accelerated sharply. By 2024, partial data showed only ~260 startups founded, putting the full year on pace for around 400 — one VC-backed startup per 3.5 million citizens. In the US, the comparable ratio was one per 107,000 citizens, 33x more. The drivers include government crackdowns on private sector leaders, foreign capital exodus, and predatory VC terms that make founding a company personally financially ruinous for entrepreneurs.

Note: confirmed data points are 2018 (51,302), 2023 (1,202), and 2024 (~400). Intermediate years are estimates based on trend reporting.
