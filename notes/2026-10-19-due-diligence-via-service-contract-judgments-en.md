**Question:** When finding a collaborator or purchasing expensive services (like migration), should we check 服务合同纠纷一审民事判决书 (first-instance civil judgments for service contract disputes) as part of due diligence?

---

**Answer:**

Yes — checking those judgment records is one of the smartest and most specific due diligence moves you can make. Here's the full picture:

**Why it works**

China Judgments Online (wenshu.court.gov.cn) reports on judgments from the courts of all PRC provinces and provincial-level cities. The search field allows searches by court name, parties, counsel, or other keywords. The database yields case details including the name of the opposing party, the nature of the complaint, the amount in controversy, and the amount in judgment.

Searching specifically for **服务合同纠纷** (service contract disputes) against a company name tells you:

- Have clients previously sued them for non-delivery or poor performance?
- Were they typically the **defendant** (被告) — a red flag?
- How often do they end up in court vs. resolving issues themselves?

**Important caveat — post-2022 anonymization**

Since 2022, the names of individuals and entities referenced in most cases are now anonymised, but records prior to 2022 have not been redacted. This change may make litigation checks in China challenging going forward, though the understanding is that certain licensed providers can continue to provide litigation checks on individuals and entities for specific purposes such as due diligence and investigations.

This means for recent cases, commercial aggregators like **天眼查 (Tianyancha)** or **企查查 (Qichacha)** — which have licensed access — are more reliable than searching wenshu.court.gov.cn yourself.

**Don't stop there — a complete checklist**

Companies can incorporate enforcement record information into their due diligence analysis and perform a more meticulous evaluation of an entity's financial stability. This can be critical for determining a potential business partner's dependability in commercial contracts, as well as for evaluating target companies.

Layer these checks together:

| What to Check | Where |
| --- | --- |
| 服务合同纠纷判决书 (court judgments) | wenshu.court.gov.cn or 天眼查/企查查 |
| 失信被执行人 (dishonest debtor blacklist) | zxgk.court.gov.cn/shixin |
| 被执行人 (outstanding enforcement judgments) | zxgk.court.gov.cn/zhixing |
| Business registration & scope | gsxt.gov.cn (SAMR) |
| Credit history | creditchina.gov.cn |

The "Information System of Person Subject to Enforcement" helps look up outstanding judgments against an enterprise or person with records dating back to January 1, 2007. The platform is run by the Supreme People's Court.

**Practical tip for expensive services like migration**

Before signing, run the vendor's **exact registered Chinese legal name** (not just their trade name) through these systems. Do not rely only on an English trading name, website name, or salesperson's email signature. For high-value contracts, consider requesting their **统一社会信用代码 (Unified Social Credit Code / USCC)** and verifying it matches their business license.
