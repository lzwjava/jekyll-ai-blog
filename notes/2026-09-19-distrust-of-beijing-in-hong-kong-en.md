Question: **Do many people in Hong Kong distrust the CCP / Beijing central government?**

Answer: **Yes, a substantial minority do, but “many” should not be interpreted as “most Hong Kong people.”** Public opinion is quite heterogeneous.

A useful distinction is between **distrust of the CCP/Beijing central government** and **dissatisfaction with the Hong Kong SAR government**. They aren't the same thing.

For example, a CUHK survey conducted in **Feb–Mar 2025** found:

| Attitude toward Beijing central government | Hong Kong respondents |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------: |
| Trust                                      |             **34.2%** |
| Distrust                                   |             **27.4%** |
| In-between / neutral                       |             **34.9%** |

The sample was 711 adults, with an estimated ±3.68 percentage-point sampling error at 95% confidence. ([CUHK Communications][1])

Interestingly, the same survey found **21.8% trusted the Hong Kong government while 34.1% distrusted it**, showing that attitudes toward the local government can be different from attitudes toward Beijing. ([CUHK Communications][1])

And historically, distrust has sometimes been substantially higher. In September 2023, for example, the corresponding Central Government figures were **29.8% trust vs. 33.5% distrust**; by October 2024 they were **31.6% vs. 26.2%**. ([CUHK Communications][2])

So I would describe Hong Kong society as having **a significant and historically persistent segment that is skeptical or distrustful of Beijing/CCP**, alongside people who trust Beijing and a large group whose views are somewhere in between.

One important methodological point: surveys generally ask about **“the Beijing Central Government”**, not literally “the CCP.” So we shouldn't automatically equate a respondent saying “I distrust the Central Government” with “I distrust the Communist Party” without additional evidence. ([CUHK Communications][1])
