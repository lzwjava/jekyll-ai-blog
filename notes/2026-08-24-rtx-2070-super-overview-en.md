**Question:** What is an introduction to the NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2070 Super graphics card?

**Answer:**

The **NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2070 Super** is a desktop GPU released in July 2019, sitting in the high-end segment of NVIDIA's Turing-generation lineup.

**Architecture & Manufacturing**

It is based on the Turing architecture, manufactured on a 12nm TSMC process. The Turing generation was revolutionary for introducing RT Cores for ray tracing and Tensor Cores for AI-based algorithms.

**Key Specifications**

It features 2,560 shader (CUDA) cores, 8 GB of GDDR6 memory on a 256-bit bus, and 448 GB/s of memory bandwidth. Its theoretical compute performance is 9.243 TFLOPS, with a TDP of 215W.

**Notable Features**

The card supports Real-Time Ray Tracing (RTX) for realistic lighting and shadows in games, and DLSS 2.0, which uses AI to upscale image resolution with minimal quality loss — critical for 1440p and 4K gaming.

**Display Outputs**

It provides HDMI, three DisplayPort outputs, and a USB-C port.

**Performance Position**

At launch, reviewers noted that the RTX 2070 Super delivered performance on par with GPUs costing $200 more, making it an excellent value proposition. Performance benchmarks show it competing closely with both the RTX 2080 (approximately 8% slower) and modern cards like the RTX 5050.

**Power Requirements**

A build using this card needs at least a 550W power supply, with 650W recommended for headroom. Reference cooler models can reach 75–80°C under load, so models with 2–3 fans (such as the ASUS Dual EVO or MSI Gaming X) are worth considering.

In summary, the RTX 2070 Super was one of the best value-for-money cards of its generation and remains a capable GPU for 1080p and 1440p gaming today.
