Question: **What is “自雇专才” in Hong Kong?**

Answer: There isn't actually a Hong Kong immigration scheme officially called **“Self-Employed Talent / 自雇专才.”** People usually use that phrase to describe a few different routes where you can effectively work for your own company.

For someone like you—an AI engineer/founder—the important one is **GEP Entrepreneur / 企业家签证**.

### 1. GEP Entrepreneur — probably what you mean

Hong Kong's **General Employment Policy (GEP)** allows overseas/Taiwan/Macao entrepreneurs to establish or join a business in Hong Kong. The key requirement is that your business can make a **substantial contribution to Hong Kong's economy**. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][1])

It is not simply:

> “I register a Hong Kong company → I automatically get a visa.”

Immigration looks at things such as:

* business plan
* amount of investment
* technology/skills introduced
* projected/actual turnover
* jobs created in Hong Kong
* financial resources
* business viability

A startup supported by certain Hong Kong government programmes can receive preferential consideration. ([HK Engage][2])

So conceptually:

```text
You
 │
 ├── own Hong Kong company
 │      │
 │      ├── AI consulting
 │      ├── AI agents
 │      ├── model training
 │      └── software products
 │
 └── apply as entrepreneur
          ↓
      Hong Kong GEP
          ↓
   residence/work permission
```

### 2. This is different from a normal employment visa

For a Mainland Chinese resident, the normal professional route is **ASMTP**.

That requires:

```text
Hong Kong company
      ↓
gives you a genuine job offer
      ↓
your qualifications / experience match the job
      ↓
salary at market level
      ↓
ASMTP application
```

The company is effectively your employer. The scheme is quota-free and non-sector-specific. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][3])

So if you want to **be your own boss**, GEP Entrepreneur is conceptually much closer than ASMTP.

### 3. QMAS is another interesting route

There is also **Quality Migrant Admission Scheme (QMAS)**.

Unlike a normal employment visa, you **don't need a Hong Kong job offer before applying**. The General Points Test considers things including:

* age
* academic qualifications
* language
* work experience
* annual income
* business ownership

After entering, you can establish/join a business or take suitable employment. ([HK Engage][4])

This can be interesting for a founder because you're being evaluated more as an **individual high-value talent**, rather than simply as an employee.

### 4. TTPS can be even better if you qualify

The **Top Talent Pass Scheme (TTPS)** is another route worth checking.

The particularly interesting part is **Category A**:

> annual income ≥ **HK$2.5 million** in the preceding year.

Importantly, Hong Kong explicitly says business income can include profits from **self-owned companies** for this calculation. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][5])

So founder income can potentially count.

---

### For your situation

I'd think about the Hong Kong routes like this:

| Route                | Core idea                        |  Founder-friendly? |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------- | -----------------: |
| **GEP Entrepreneur** | Build/run your HK business       |              ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ |
| **QMAS**             | Qualify as high-value individual |               ⭐⭐⭐⭐ |
| **TTPS A**           | ≥ HK$2.5M annual income          | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ if qualified |
| **ASMTP**            | HK company employs you           |                ⭐⭐⭐ |
| Normal employment    | Join another HK company          |                 ⭐⭐ |

And there's an important distinction:

**“Self-employed” ≠ “I can just freelance in Hong Kong on any visa.”**

Your immigration status determines what work/business activities you're allowed to conduct. Hong Kong generally requires a visa/entry permit to work or establish/join a business unless you already have the relevant right of abode/land. ([Immigration Department Hong Kong][6])

For an AI founder, **GEP Entrepreneur + Hong Kong company** is probably the thing people casually call **“自雇专才.”**
