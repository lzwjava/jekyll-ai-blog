Question: Should I go to the USA for a master's program with ~700k RMB mortgage/debt already, even if family can help?

Answer:

This is a **high-upside but high-risk bet**. The key question is not "Can I afford the tuition?" but:

> "Does this master's program significantly increase my probability of reaching a better long-term position than staying in China and building AI business/engineering capability?"

Given your background (11 years engineering, AI/LLM experience, IELTS 6.5, startup experience), this is not the same decision as a fresh graduate. The opportunity cost matters more.

Let's model it.

## Option A: USA AI master's

Typical cost:

* Tuition + fees: ~$40k–100k USD total (depends heavily on school)
* Living: ~$20k–40k/year
* Total: roughly **700k–1.2M RMB**

With existing 700k RMB mortgage:

You are effectively making a **1.5M–2M RMB financial commitment**.

Potential upside:

* US degree opens doors:

  * US AI engineer jobs
  * research labs
  * immigration pathways
  * stronger global network
* A top school can be a major signal:

  * CMU
  * Stanford
  * UIUC
  * NYU
  * Georgia Tech, etc.

But a mid-tier master's has weaker ROI. The US market does not automatically reward "having an American degree".

The biggest risk:

You spend 2 years and a lot of money, then return to China with:

* more debt
* lost engineering momentum
* no strong US job offer

That outcome is possible.

---

## Option B: Stay China + OPC AI company

Your current position is unusual:

You already have:

* real engineering experience
* production systems experience
* LLM training experience
* GPU experience
* content/community assets
* previous company revenue experience

Many master's students are trying to build what you already have.

Your possible path:

```
AI consulting
       |
       v
small AI projects
       |
       v
agent products / SaaS
       |
       v
AI company
```

The capital requirement is much lower.

For example:

* cloud GPU: 10k–100k RMB/year
* website/content/community: almost free
* consulting projects: can generate revenue

Your downside risk is lower.

---

## The important question: what problem does the master's solve?

A master's helps if your bottleneck is:

### 1. Immigration / US entry

Then yes, it can be valuable.

A degree is a legal bridge into the US ecosystem.

### 2. Lack of credibility

A CMU/Stanford-level brand can change how people perceive you.

### 3. Research transition

If you want:

```
Engineer
    |
    v
Research engineer
    |
    v
Frontier AI lab
```

Then a master's/PhD path makes sense.

---

But if your bottleneck is:

* "I need more AI skill"
* "I need more projects"
* "I need more confidence"

Then spending 1M RMB is probably inefficient.

You can probably learn/build faster yourself.

---

My decision framework would be:

### Go USA if

✅ admission from strong AI program
✅ scholarship or manageable cost
✅ goal is US career/immigration
✅ family support does not create pressure
✅ you are willing to restart network from zero

### Do not go if

❌ expensive unknown school
❌ only reason is "need a degree"
❌ you would return after 2 years with debt
❌ you stop building during the master's

---

For your specific situation, I would treat it like a startup investment:

Do not ask:

> "Can my family pay?"

Ask:

> "Will this investment increase my expected future value by more than 1M RMB?"

A strong CMU/Stanford/Top-30 AI program: **possibly yes**.

A random master's: **probably no**.

A third strategy may be strongest:

```
2026-2027:
- apply US master's
- continue AI consulting/OPC
- publish technical work
- build open-source reputation

If top admission arrives -> go.
If not -> continue building.
```

This keeps optionality.

The mistake would be treating the master's as an escape route. The best candidates use it as a multiplier.
