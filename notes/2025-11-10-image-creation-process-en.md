### How a Projector Works

A projector is a device that takes a digital or analog input signal (like video from a computer, Blu-ray player, or streaming device) and projects a large image onto a screen or wall. It works in three main stages:

1. **Input and Processing**: The source signal (e.g., HDMI) is received and processed by an internal chip or processor. This decodes the video, scales it to the projector's native resolution, and applies corrections like keystone (to fix trapezoidal distortion if the projector is angled).

2. **Light Generation and Modulation**: A bright light source illuminates the image-forming element, which modulates (shapes) the light pixel-by-pixel to create the picture. The modulated light is then focused through lenses.

3. **Projection**: The shaped light beam passes through a projection lens and is thrown onto a surface. The image size depends on throw distance (how far the projector is from the screen) and lens zoom.

Projectors differ by technology:

- **DLP (Digital Light Processing)**: Common in portable models like the Jimi H6. Uses a spinning color wheel and a DMD (Digital Micromirror Device) chip with millions of tiny mirrors that flip to reflect light on/off.
- **LCD (Liquid Crystal Display)**: Uses three LCD panels (for red, green, blue) to filter white light.
- **LCOS (Liquid Crystal on Silicon)**: Hybrid of LCD and DLP, higher-end.
- **Laser/LED**: Modern light sources for better brightness/longevity.

Portable mini projectors like the Jimi H6 are typically DLP-based with LED lamps for compactness, lower heat, and battery options.

### Key Components

Here's a breakdown of typical components in a projector (using a DLP mini projector like the Jimi H6 as an example; specs can vary by model, but Jimi H6 is a budget Android-powered DLP projector with ~300-500 ANSI lumens, 1080p support, and built-in OS):

1. **Light Source**:
   - LED lamp (in Jimi H6: multi-color LEDs lasting 20,000-30,000 hours). Provides the raw white/multicolor light. Brighter than old bulbs, cooler operation.

2. **Image Engine/Chip**:
   - DMD chip (in DLP like Jimi H6: usually 0.23-0.47 inch size with 720p or 1080p native resolution). Contains microscopic mirrors (one per pixel) that tilt rapidly to direct light toward or away from the lens.

3. **Color Wheel (DLP-specific)**:
   - Spinning disk with red, green, blue (and sometimes white/cyan/yellow) segments. Light passes through it sequentially to create full color. (Jimi H6 uses this for vibrant colors; some models are "single-chip" with sequential coloring to reduce rainbow effect.)

4. **Optics/Lens System**:
   - Projection lens (fixed or zoom; Jimi H6 has short-throw for small rooms).
   - Mirrors/prisms to fold the light path in compact bodies.
   - Focus/zoom rings for manual adjustment.

5. **Processing Board**:
   - CPU/GPU (Jimi H6: Android-based SoC like quad-core ARM, with 1-2GB RAM, 8-16GB storage). Handles OS, apps (e.g., Netflix via built-in Android), Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, and image scaling.

6. **Cooling System**:
   - Fans and heatsinks (essential in minis like Jimi H6 to prevent LED/DMD overheating; noise is a common complaint in budget models).

7. **Power Supply**:
   - AC adapter or battery (Jimi H6: often portable with optional battery packs; internal PSU converts to low-voltage DC).

8. **Inputs/Outputs**:
   - HDMI, USB, AV, audio out (Jimi H6: HDMI in, USB for media, 3.5mm audio; built-in speakers ~3-5W).

9. **Housing and Controls**:
   - Plastic/metal body, remote, touchscreen/on-body buttons. Includes keystone correction, auto-focus in some.
