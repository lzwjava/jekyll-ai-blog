**Question:** What is CSTU (加州科技大学 / California Science and Technology University)?

**Answer:**

CSTU is a small, private, for-profit postsecondary institution in Silicon Valley — not to be confused with any major research university. Key facts:

- Founded September 26, 2011, located at 1601 McCarthy Boulevard in Milpitas, California, offering Bachelor's and Master's degrees in Computer Systems and Engineering (BSCSE/MSCSE), Business Administration (BSBA/MBA), plus an Emerging Technology Training Program (ETTP)
- Its curriculum focuses on Silicon Valley technologies like AI and Data Science — the Chinese text you pasted is essentially its marketing pitch (founded by Silicon Valley tech leaders to teach practical, cutting-edge skills traditional colleges don't)
- Licensed to operate by the BPPE (California Bureau for Private Postsecondary Education), and accredited by ACCSC (a national career-school accreditor, not regional accreditation like Stanford/Berkeley have)
- Degree programs (MSCSE, MBA) were approved by BPPE in April 2016; training targets unemployed professionals via government funding, employer reimbursement, or scholarships
- It's tiny: a 2020 state filing shows just 1 MBA degree awarded that year, with total program charges of $18,000, and 38% of institutional income derived from public funding
- Co-founder and Chairman is Elizabeth Xu, Ph.D.

Honest read: it's a legitimate, licensed vocational-style school popular with Chinese-speaking professionals in the Bay Area (hence the bilingual marketing), useful mainly for career retraining or visa-adjacent degree needs. It carries essentially no research reputation or brand weight. If you're evaluating it for credibility signaling, partnerships, or client trust in your consulting work — a CSTU credential won't move the needle the way your open-source work, trained models, and blog already do.
