Question: **What do “公有制” and “所有制” mean in Chinese politics/economics?**

Answer:

They are related but **not the same concept**.

### 1. 所有制 = ownership system

**所有制 (suǒyǒuzhì)** means **the legal/economic system that determines who owns productive assets**.

Think of it as:

> **Who legally owns the factory, land, company, machines, capital, etc.?**

Typical categories:

| Chinese   | Meaning           | Example                                            |
| --------- | ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **公有制**   | public ownership  | State-owned enterprises, collectively owned assets |
| **私有制**   | private ownership | Privately owned companies                          |
| **混合所有制** | mixed ownership   | State + private shareholders                       |

So **所有制 is the broader category**.

---

### 2. 公有制 = public ownership

**公有制 (gōngyǒuzhì)** literally means **public ownership**.

In China's political-economic theory, it primarily consists of:

> **全民所有制 + 集体所有制**

Historically, **全民所有制** is generally represented by **state ownership**.

For example:

```text
公有制
├── 全民所有制
│   └── State ownership / state-owned assets
│
└── 集体所有制
    └── Collective ownership
```

A large state-owned enterprise such as PetroChina is therefore normally described as part of the **公有制经济**.

---

### 3. The important distinction: 公有制 ≠ 国家所有制

This is where Chinese political terminology gets interesting.

**国家所有制** = state ownership.

**公有制** = public ownership.

State ownership is **one important form of public ownership**, but the concepts are not logically identical.

For example:

```text
所有制
│
├── 公有制
│   ├── 国家/全民所有
│   └── 集体所有
│
└── 非公有制
    ├── 私人所有
    ├── 民营企业
    └── 外资等
```

---

### 4. Why does China emphasize “公有制为主体”?

The famous formulation in China's constitutional/economic framework is:

> **公有制为主体、多种所有制经济共同发展**

Meaning roughly:

> **Public ownership is the principal basis, while multiple forms of ownership develop together.**

This is an important distinction from the simplistic idea:

> “China = everything is state-owned.”

That isn't how the current Chinese economic system is officially defined.

China explicitly recognizes **non-public ownership**, including private businesses.

So conceptually:

```text
China's official economic structure

        所有制
           │
     ┌─────┴─────┐
     │           │
   公有制       非公有制
     │           │
     │        私营企业
     │        外资等
     │
  国家/全民
  集体
```

The political claim is that **public ownership remains dominant**, while private and other ownership forms are legitimate components of the socialist market economy.

---

### 5. Why “所有制” is politically important

This comes from Marxist political economy.

The key question isn't simply:

> “Who manages the company?”

It is:

> **Who owns the means of production, and therefore ultimately controls the economic surplus?**

For example, imagine:

```text
Factory
  ↓
Workers produce ¥100M value
  ↓
Who owns factory?
  ↓
Who owns profits/assets?
```

Under **private ownership**:

```text
Private owner
      ↓
owns capital
      ↓
owns residual profits
```

Under **state/public ownership**:

```text
State / public
      ↓
owns assets
      ↓
profits ultimately belong to public/state
```

This is why **所有制** is much more fundamental in Marxist theory than simply whether a company has a particular management structure.

---

### 6. A subtle example: Alibaba

Suppose a company is:

```text
Alibaba
├── private shareholders
├── public shareholders
├── foreign investors
└── government regulation
```

Government regulation **doesn't automatically make it 公有制**.

The key question is **ownership**.

Likewise:

> A company being strategically important does not automatically mean it is state-owned.

You have to distinguish:

```text
Ownership      所有制
     ↓
Who owns it?

Control        控制权
     ↓
Who controls decisions?

Management     经营权
     ↓
Who operates it?

Regulation     监管
     ↓
Who sets the rules?
```

These four things can be different.

---

### 7. The Chinese political vocabulary

You'll frequently see these terms together:

```text
所有制
Ownership system

公有制
Public ownership

全民所有制
Ownership by the whole people
→ practically associated with state ownership

集体所有制
Collective ownership

非公有制经济
Non-public economy

私营经济
Private-sector economy

混合所有制
Mixed ownership
```

And the central formulation is:

> **公有制为主体、多种所有制经济共同发展**

This is essentially China's official description of its **“社会主义基本经济制度”**.

The easiest mental model is:

> **所有制 = the ownership system as a whole.**
> **公有制 = one major type of ownership within that system, centered on public/state and collective ownership.**

The really interesting next question is **what “全民所有” actually means in practice—if the state owns something, in what sense does “the whole people” own it?** That's where Chinese political theory and actual property rights start to diverge in a very interesting way.
