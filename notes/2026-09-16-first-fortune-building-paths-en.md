Question: How do people typically build their first significant fortune?

Answer:

There's no single path, but history and research point to several recurring patterns:

**1. Entrepreneurship / Starting a Business**
The most common route. People identify a problem, build a solution, and scale it. Early-stage equity is where most modern fortunes begin — think tech startups, local businesses that grow into franchises, or niche service companies.

**2. High-Income Skills + Aggressive Saving**
Some accumulate wealth by mastering a highly paid skill (medicine, law, finance, software engineering), living well below their means, and investing the surplus consistently over time.

**3. Real Estate**
Buying undervalued properties, leveraging debt wisely, and benefiting from appreciation and rental income. Many first-generation wealthy individuals started with a single rental property.

**4. Investing Early and Consistently**
Compound interest over long time horizons — index funds, stocks, or early investment in a single winning asset (crypto, a startup, etc.) — has created wealth for disciplined, patient investors.

**5. Sales & Commission-Based Careers**
High-performing salespeople in industries like real estate, finance, or SaaS can earn outsized income relative to peers through performance-based pay.

**6. Inheritance or Windfall Events**
Less controllable, but IPOs, acquisitions of companies people worked at (via stock options), insurance payouts, or family wealth transfers play a role.

**Common Threads Across Most Paths:**
- Taking calculated risks early
- Avoiding lifestyle inflation
- Reinvesting profits rather than consuming them
- Building networks and relationships
- Developing a skill or asset that scales beyond hours worked
