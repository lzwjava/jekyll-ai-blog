**Question:** When a married couple has a mortgage together, should it be split fairly between both spouses?

**Answer:**

Yes — fairness matters, but "fair" doesn't always mean exactly 50/50. Here are the common approaches:

**1. Equal Split (50/50)**
Each spouse pays half the monthly mortgage. Simple and symmetric. Works well when both partners earn similar incomes.

**2. Income-Proportional Split**
Each pays a share based on their income. Example: if you earn 60% of household income, you cover 60% of the mortgage. This is often considered the most *equitable* (vs. equal) approach.

**3. One Pays, Other Contributes Elsewhere**
One spouse handles the mortgage; the other covers other major expenses (utilities, childcare, groceries). The contributions balance out differently but aim for overall fairness.

**Key considerations:**
- **Both names on the mortgage?** Both are legally responsible for the full debt regardless of internal agreements.
- **Ownership stake:** If one pays more, does that give them a larger equity share? This should be discussed and possibly documented.
- **Changing circumstances:** Income changes, career breaks, or child-rearing roles may shift what's "fair" over time.
- **Jurisdiction matters:** Community property states/countries may treat marital assets and debts differently.
- **Prenuptial or postnuptial agreements** can formalize who pays what and what equity each person holds.

The most important thing is **transparency and agreement between both partners** — ideally put in writing, especially if the split is unequal.
