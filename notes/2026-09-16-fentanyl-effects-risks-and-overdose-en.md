Question: **What is fentanyl, and what do people experience when they use it?**

Answer:

**Fentanyl is a synthetic opioid**—a very powerful painkiller. Doctors legitimately use pharmaceutical fentanyl for severe pain and during surgery. Illicitly manufactured fentanyl is a major driver of opioid overdose in the U.S. ([CDC][1])

It is roughly **50× stronger than heroin and 100× stronger than morphine**, which makes dosing errors particularly dangerous. ([CDC][2])

### What does fentanyl feel like?

As an opioid, it can produce:

* Euphoria / a strong feeling of pleasure
* Relaxation and calm
* Sleepiness or drowsiness
* Reduced physical and emotional pain
* Feeling warm or detached
* Slowed thinking and reaction time

The dangerous part is that it also **suppresses breathing**. With too much, someone can become unconscious and stop breathing.

### What does a fentanyl overdose look like?

Typical warning signs are:

* Can't wake the person up
* **Very slow, shallow, or absent breathing**
* Gurgling/snoring or choking sounds
* **Very small “pinpoint” pupils**
* Limp body
* Blue/gray or otherwise discolored lips/fingernails
* Cold or clammy skin ([CDC][3])

If you see this in someone, **call 911 and give naloxone (Narcan) if available**. Naloxone can rapidly reverse an opioid overdose, including fentanyl. ([CDC][4])

One particularly scary thing about illicit fentanyl is that people may **not know they're taking it**. It can be mixed into cocaine, heroin, methamphetamine, or counterfeit pills made to look like prescription drugs. You generally can't identify it by sight, smell, or taste. ([CDC][1])
