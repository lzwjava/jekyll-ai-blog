Question: What's the difference between a B1 and B2 visa?

Answer:

B1 and B2 are both non-immigrant visitor visas to the US, often issued together as a combined **B1/B2** stamp. The distinction is *purpose of travel*, not separate physical visas in most cases.

**B1 — Business visitor**
- Attending meetings, conferences, negotiations
- Consulting with business associates
- Settling an estate
- Participating in short-term training (non-paid)
- **Not allowed**: working for a US employer, receiving US-sourced salary, or providing labor for hire

**B2 — Tourism/visitor**
- Tourism, vacation
- Visiting family/friends
- Medical treatment
- Participating in social events, amateur contests (unpaid)

**Key rules for both:**
- No employment, no US salary — this is the main line CBP/USCIS enforces
- Typical admission period: up to 6 months per entry (decided by CBP officer at port of entry, not by the visa itself)
- Visa validity (how long you can use it to apply for entry) is separate from admission period (how long you can stay per visit) — for Chinese citizens, B1/B2 visas are commonly issued with 10-year validity, but each entry still caps at ~6 months
- Extensions possible via Form I-539, filed before I-94 expiry
- Dual intent is NOT allowed — unlike H-1B/L-1, you must show non-immigrant intent (ties to home country, no plan to stay permanently)

**Practical case relevant to you**: if you're going to the US for client meetings/consulting business trips (not doing paid work physically in the US), B1 is correct. If you're speaking at a conference unpaid or just visiting, B1/B2 combined covers both without needing to declare separately.
