Question: How do YouTube creators earn money, and how is their pay calculated?

Answer:

YouTube creators don't receive a traditional "salary" — instead, they earn revenue through several mechanisms, primarily **AdSense/ad revenue**, but also other streams.

---

## 1. Ad Revenue (Main Source)

**How it works:**

- YouTube places ads on videos through **Google AdSense**
- Creators earn a share of the ad revenue — typically **55% goes to the creator**, 45% to YouTube

**How it's counted — key metrics:**

| Term | Meaning |
| ------ | --------- |
| **CPM** | Cost Per Mille — what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions |
| **RPM** | Revenue Per Mille — what the *creator* actually receives per 1,000 views (after YouTube's cut) |
| **Monetized views** | Not every view shows an ad; only a portion counts |

**Typical RPM ranges:**

| Niche | RPM (USD) |
| ------- | ----------- |
| Finance / Tech | $10 – $30+ |
| General Entertainment | $2 – $5 |
| Gaming | $2 – $6 |
| Kids content | $1 – $3 |

So a channel with **1M views/month** in finance might earn **~$15,000–$25,000**, while a gaming channel might earn **~$2,000–$6,000**.

---

## 2. Other Income Streams

- **YouTube Premium revenue** — share of Premium subscribers' fees based on watch time
- **Super Chats / Super Thanks** — viewer tips during live streams or on videos
- **Channel Memberships** — monthly subscriptions from fans
- **Sponsorships** — brand deals (often *more* lucrative than ads)
- **Merchandise** — sold via YouTube Shopping integration
- **Affiliate links** — commission from product referrals

---

## 3. Eligibility Requirements

To monetize, creators must join the **YouTube Partner Program (YPP)**:

- 1,000+ subscribers
- 4,000+ watch hours in the past 12 months (or 10M Shorts views in 90 days)
- Linked AdSense account

---

## Realistic Earnings Example

| Channel Size | Monthly Views | Est. Monthly Ad Revenue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small (10K subs) | ~50K | $50–$200 |
| Mid (100K subs) | ~500K | $500–$2,500 |
| Large (1M subs) | ~5M | $5,000–$25,000 |
| Top (10M+ subs) | ~50M+ | $50,000–$300,000+ |

Sponsorships and other streams can **double or triple** these numbers for established creators.
