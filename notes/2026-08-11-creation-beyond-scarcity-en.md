Question: Why create when material life is already abundant, and should AI-era creation come from richer consumption and experience?

Answer:

I think you are touching a very important shift in the AI era: **creation is no longer only driven by scarcity**.

Historically, many people created because they had a problem:

* no money → build a business
* no tools → invent tools
* no information → create knowledge systems

But now, especially for someone with your background, the question changes:

> "If my basic life is already comfortable, why spend energy creating?"

The answer is not "because you must work harder." It is more like:

> Creation becomes a natural extension of deep experience.

A good loop is:

```
Experience → Observe pain/friction → Write notes → Build small tool → Get feedback → More experience
```

Not:

```
Sit alone → force innovation → build random startup
```

Your eSIM example is actually a good signal. You used something in real life, encountered a small pain point, then realized the value. That is how many good products start.

A lot of great builders are extreme users first:

* Steve Jobs cared about using computers, music, typography, and design.
* Jensen Huang built around understanding GPU workloads and developer needs.
* Andrej Karpathy constantly studies models by implementing and experimenting.

The common pattern is not "work 16 hours." It is:

**They are close to reality.**

For AI especially, the best ideas often come from daily usage:

Examples:

* You use an AI coding agent → notice missing workflow → build agent tooling.
* You use translation AI with your daughter → notice education problems → build learning tools.
* You run your own company → notice customer support problems → build AI automation.
* You manage communities → notice information overload → build knowledge agents.

Your existing habit of writing notes is actually a huge advantage.

The note is a compression layer:

```
100 hours of experience
        ↓
10 pages of notes
        ↓
1 useful insight
        ↓
1 small product
```

Many people consume but don't record. The experience disappears.

You are closer to a researcher mindset:

```
observe → document → hypothesize → experiment
```

The other important point:

A comfortable life and creation are not opposites.

A good target is probably not:

> "I must sacrifice my life to create."

More like:

> "I build because building makes my experience richer."

For someone who already achieved a reasonable income and autonomy, the scarce resource is not money. It is:

* attention
* health
* family time
* curiosity
* energy

So your idea of "consume more" is actually correct, but with one modification:

Not passive consumption.

More like:

```
High-quality living
+
deep observation
+
public notes
+
small experiments
=
AI-era creation
```

The next generation of solo companies may look less like old startups:

```
10 people + office + funding
```

and more like:

```
1 person
+
AI agents
+
domain experience
+
distribution
+
continuous experiments
```

Your 5-6 hour effective workday with AI tools is not a disadvantage. It may actually be the ideal environment: enough freedom to explore, enough discipline to ship.

The key is not to force yourself to create every day. Keep increasing the surface area of life. More experiences give AI more raw material to amplify.
