The QYL2 specification is typically around **148–158 mm minimum height**, with roughly **80–90 mm hydraulic lift**, plus an adjustable screw section. ([Tongrun Jacks][1])

### But there is one BIG issue

For a **broken front-left wheel**, the question isn't simply "2T vs 1T."

You need to know **where the jack can physically reach the car's designated lifting point**.

A bottle jack:

```text
       CAR
 ─────────────────
       chassis
          ↑
        [QYL2]
          │
        █████
        ground
```

The QYL2 needs enough clearance to fit under the manufacturer's jack point.

A scissor jack:

```text
       CAR
 ─────────────────
          ↑
         /\
        /  \
       /    \
      /______\
```

is much easier to position in some cars because it's narrow and can fit into the factory jack point.

### For a normal passenger car

I'd rank them:

**QYL2 2T > QCL10 1T**

especially if you want something you can keep and use repeatedly.

The **1T scissor jack isn't necessarily unsafe just because it's 1T**. A car weighing 1.5–2T doesn't put the entire car's weight on one wheel when lifting one corner. But you have less rated-capacity margin, and the mechanism is more laborious.

### One more thing: wheel removal

If your "front-left wheel got broken" means **flat tire / damaged tire and you need to remove the wheel**, you'll also need a **lug wrench**. The jack only lifts the car.

And after lifting:

**Do not work underneath the car while it is supported only by the jack.** Hydraulic-jack safety guidance explicitly says the jack is for lifting, not supporting, and requires a hard supporting surface. ([河南克鲁德重工有限公司][2])

**My practical setup for your car:**

```text
QYL2 2T
   +
lug wrench
   +
wheel chock
   +
jack stand
```

That's a much better emergency kit than buying only the 1T scissor jack.
