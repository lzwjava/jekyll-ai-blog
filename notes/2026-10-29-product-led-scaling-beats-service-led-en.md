**Question:** Does ByteDance have a fundamentally better scaling model compared to traditional IT services companies like Tata (TCS) and Infosys?

**Answer:**

Yes, and the difference is structural — it's the classic **product-led vs. service-led** scaling divide.

**ByteDance's scaling model:**

ByteDance's secret weapon is its "Shared Services Platform" (SSP), which includes algorithms and technical staff (engineers, analysts) that work across multiple products rather than being siloed in a single product line. This lets product teams stay lean while drawing on shared infrastructure.

Powered by this SSP, ByteDance pursues broad exploration (140+ apps in 2 years), rapid iteration (releasing fast, killing underperformers), and selective focus — all simultaneously.

Its data-driven culture means one insight can be leveraged into multiple breakthroughs — e.g., a spike in video views on Toutiao directly led to the development of Douyin, and eventually TikTok.

At the infrastructure level, ByteDance's HeteroScale system runs LLMs at industrial scale across clusters of more than 10,000 GPUs, processing trillions of prefill tokens and hundreds of billions of decode tokens every day — an engineering capability that compounds the advantage further.

**TCS/Infosys's scaling model:**

IT services companies like Infosys and TCS typically bill clients based in part on the number of people deployed on a project. GenAI has now made it possible to handle IT projects with fewer people, forcing them to pivot toward value-based pricing pegged to outcomes rather than costs.

Service companies sell human labor — their revenue scales *linearly* with headcount. More projects means more hires. Lower margins, harder to differentiate, and limited scalability because they can't easily automate consulting or bespoke software.

**The numbers tell the story:**

TCS's revenue per employee sits around $49,902, Infosys at $60,164. These are growing slowly — just 4–5% compared to FY22. In contrast, product engineering firms often see growth rates of 9–11% even in volatile markets. And ByteDance/Big Tech product companies generate orders of magnitude more revenue per employee than that.

**The core asymmetry:**

Product companies can automate value delivery — once built, the product serves millions with minimal incremental cost. That's why they command high valuation multiples: investors bet on exponential growth. Service companies grow arithmetically; product companies grow geometrically.

So yes — ByteDance's model is fundamentally more scalable because its revenue is decoupled from headcount, driven by algorithms and shared tech infrastructure. TCS and Infosys have known this for years and are scrambling to shift, but it's a very hard transition from a labor-arbitrage model to a software/product model.
