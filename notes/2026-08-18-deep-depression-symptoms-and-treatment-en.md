Question: What is deep depression?

Answer: Deep (or major) depression is a serious mental health condition characterized by persistent, intense feelings of sadness, hopelessness, and emptiness that significantly interfere with daily life. It goes beyond ordinary sadness — it affects how a person thinks, feels, and functions.

**Core symptoms include:**

- Persistent low mood lasting two weeks or more
- Loss of interest or pleasure in activities once enjoyed (anhedonia)
- Fatigue and low energy
- Changes in sleep (insomnia or sleeping too much)
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
- Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
- In severe cases, thoughts of death or suicide

**What makes it "deep":** The term suggests a severe form where symptoms are pervasive — the person may struggle to get out of bed, maintain relationships, or perform basic self-care.

**Causes** are typically a mix of genetics, brain chemistry imbalances (serotonin, dopamine), trauma, chronic stress, or medical conditions.

**Treatment** usually involves psychotherapy (especially CBT), antidepressant medication, lifestyle changes, or a combination.
