I'll address your question about screen protectors on a MacBook Air M2 and their effects in bright light.

**Screen protectors and light reflection:**

Yes, adding a screen protector (贴膜) to your MacBook Air M2 will generally increase light reflection. Most screen protectors add an extra layer of glass or plastic that reflects more light than the original screen surface, even if they're designed to be "anti-glare." This is because:

- They create an additional air gap or interface that causes reflections
- Even anti-glare protectors reduce clarity slightly and can cause some light to scatter, which can make the screen appear washed out in bright conditions
- The MacBook Air M2's original display already has good anti-reflective coating, so adding a protector degrades this

**Using it in bright sunlight/bedroom:**

If you're using the laptop in bright sunlight or very bright bedroom conditions, a screen protector will make things noticeably worse:

- More glare and reflections will wash out the image
- You'll likely need to increase brightness significantly, which drains battery faster
- The viewing angle and color accuracy will be reduced
- Text and fine details become harder to read

**My recommendation:**

For a MacBook Air M2, I'd suggest **skipping the screen protector**. The MacBook's display is already well-designed with good durability and anti-reflective coating. If you're concerned about glare in bright conditions, instead:

- Adjust your screen angle to reduce reflections
- Use the built-in brightness controls
- Consider an anti-glare screen filter designed for laptops (though these have the same downsides)
- Work in areas with better ambient lighting control

The M2's display is one of its strong points—adding a protector would diminish that advantage.
