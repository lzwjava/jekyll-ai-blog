**Question: Provide a comprehensive introduction to Citron, the short sell company.**

**Answer:**

Citron Research is a prominent online investment research platform and newsletter founded by Andrew Left, known primarily for its activist short-selling activities. It publishes reports and commentary highlighting companies it believes are overvalued, involved in questionable practices, or facing fundamental issues, often taking a bearish (short) stance on those stocks.

### History and Founding
- Citron Research began in 2001 under the name StockLemon.com as a blog-style site focused on exposing potentially fraudulent or overhyped companies.
- It was rebranded to Citron Research in 2007.
- Andrew Left, born in 1970, has led the operation since its inception. He positions Citron as an independent voice "representing the other side of Wall Street," aiming to provide truthful, entertaining analysis to protect retail investors from fraud and poor investments.
- Over more than two decades, Citron has published hundreds of reports, building a reputation for identifying problematic companies (e.g., early criticism of Valeant Pharmaceuticals in 2015, which later faced major scrutiny).

### Approach and Style
- Citron's reports are typically detailed, sarcastic, and provocative, often accusing targets of fraud, unsustainable business models, or hype-driven valuations.
- Left frequently appears on financial media like CNBC, Fox Business, and Bloomberg to discuss his views.
- The platform uses its website (citronresearch.com) and social media (especially X/Twitter @CitronResearch) to share insights, short positions, and market commentary.
- While historically focused on shorts, Left announced in 2021 (post-GameStop events) a shift away from public bearish reports toward long-side opportunities, though Citron has continued to announce short positions in recent years.

### Notable Activities and Impact
- Citron gained fame for high-profile calls that sometimes moved markets significantly upon release.
- It has targeted companies across sectors, including tech, pharma, and more recently memory chips.
- As of February 2025–2026, Citron remains active, recently announcing a short position on SanDisk (SNDK), citing cyclical risks in the NAND flash memory market, competition from Samsung, and signs of a potential peak in the cycle. This caused a notable drop in SanDisk's stock price despite its prior massive gains.

### Legal Challenges
- In July 2024, Andrew Left and Citron Capital LLC faced serious charges from the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and Department of Justice.
- Allegations include a multi-year scheme (profiting at least $16–20 million) involving market manipulation: publishing misleading recommendations (long or short) that moved stock prices, then quickly reversing positions to profit against followers.
- Claims also involve undisclosed compensation from hedge funds and false statements about independence.
- As of late 2025, efforts to dismiss the criminal case failed, with a trial potentially looming into 2026.
- These issues have significantly impacted Citron's reputation, though the platform continues to publish and announce positions.

Citron exemplifies the controversial world of activist short selling—providing scrutiny that can expose real issues but also drawing criticism for potential conflicts, market influence, and (in this case) alleged impropriety.
