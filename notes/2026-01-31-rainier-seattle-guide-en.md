Mount Rainier is a large active stratovolcano standing at 14,410 feet, making it the highest peak in Washington state and the most topographically prominent mountain in the contiguous United States. Located about 60 miles south-southeast of Seattle, this iconic mountain dominates the landscape so prominently that locals often simply call it "the Mountain."

## Geography and Location

Mount Rainier sits within Mount Rainier National Park, which was established in 1899 and spans nearly 240,000 acres. The mountain is easily visible from Seattle on clear days, and can be seen from viewpoints throughout the city including Kerry Park, the Space Needle, and along the waterfront. The peak has a topographic prominence of 13,210 feet and on exceptionally clear days can be seen from as far as Oregon and British Columbia.

## Volcanic Nature

As an active volcano, Mount Rainier is considered one of the most dangerous volcanoes in the world due to its high probability of future eruption and proximity to major urban areas. It's classified as a Decade Volcano, meaning it's monitored extremely closely by the USGS. The mountain features significant glacial coverage, with volcanic craters at its summit.

## Glaciers and Rivers

Mount Rainier is the most glaciated peak in the contiguous United States, with 26 major glaciers and 36 square miles of permanent snowfields and ice. These glaciers feed five major rivers: the Nisqually, Puyallup, White, Carbon, and Cowlitz Rivers, which flow into Puget Sound and beyond.

## Visiting the Park

**Paradise**: The most popular visitor area, located at 5,400 feet on the mountain's south side. Paradise is nestled in high alpine meadows offering close-up views of Rainier's peak and set a world record with 93.5 feet of snowfall during the 1971-1972 winter. The Jackson Visitor Center here offers exhibits and ranger-led snowshoe walks in winter.

**Sunrise**: Located on the northeast side, Sunrise is the highest point accessible by car in the park and offers spectacular sunrise views and marmot sightings during summer months.

**Longmire**: Home to the year-round National Park Inn, a museum, and various trailheads.

**Getting There**: The drive from Seattle takes approximately two hours via the Nisqually Entrance, which provides the closest access to Paradise and is the main gateway from Seattle.

## Activities

The park offers hundreds of miles of hiking trails through old-growth forests, wildflower meadows, and past waterfalls. Popular activities include hiking, camping, mountaineering (though only about half of the 10,000 annual summit attempts succeed), snowshoeing, and wildlife viewing. The park experiences its highest visitation during July and August, with nearly 2.5 million visitors annually.

## Cultural Significance

Indigenous peoples have lived near Mount Rainier for millennia and have at least 20 different names for the mountain in various languages, including "Tahoma" and variations meaning "snow-covered mountain" or "sky wiper."

Mount Rainier remains one of the Pacific Northwest's most spectacular natural landmarks, offering year-round recreation and unforgettable views whether you're visiting from Seattle for a day trip or exploring the park more extensively.
