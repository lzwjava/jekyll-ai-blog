Question: Does society/internet still lack highly skilled engineers who also have millions or 10M+ followers, like George Hotz, Andrej Karpathy, or Zhihuijun?

Answer:

Yes, this combination is extremely rare: **deep technical ability + original research/building + communication ability + mass audience growth**.

The bottleneck is not just engineering skill. These are usually four different skill sets:

```
               Mass influence
                    ^
                    |
          Karpathy   |       MrBeast-type creators
                    |
                    |
Engineering --------+-------- Entertainment
                    |
          Linus     |
          Torvalds  |
                    v
              Low public reach
```

Most people occupy one corner:

### 1. Elite engineers, low public visibility

Examples:

* Many NVIDIA CUDA engineers
* Google DeepMind researchers
* OpenAI researchers
* Linux kernel maintainers

They may influence millions of developers indirectly, but their personal accounts don't have millions of followers.

Reason:

Their output is embedded in products/research, not personality-driven media.

---

### 2. Big technical creators, but not frontier engineers

Examples:

* Programming YouTubers
* AI tutorial creators

They can have millions of followers, but usually they are explaining technology rather than creating frontier systems.

---

### 3. The rare hybrid

These people are unusual.

Andrej Karpathy

Karpathy is almost a perfect example:

* Former Tesla AI director
* OpenAI founding member
* Built neural network education content
* "Zero to Hero" series reached huge developer audiences
* Makes complex AI understandable

His influence comes from:

```
research credibility
        +
teaching ability
        +
clear communication
        +
consistent public presence
```

---

* PS3 reverse engineering

His audience follows him because he **builds unusual things publicly**.

The pattern:

```
interesting project
        ↓

        ↓

        ↓

```

---

But 10M+ followers with true frontier engineering ability is almost impossible.

Why?

Because the time allocation conflicts:

```
Frontier engineering:
  80 hours/week coding/research

Mass creator:
  content production
  community management
  sponsorship/business

CEO:
  hiring
  fundraising
  strategy
```

A person can usually optimize for only one.

---

The next AI era may create more of these people.

LLMs reduce the cost of explanation/content creation:

A single engineer can now:

```
build agent system
        +
train small model
        +
write technical notes
        +
generate videos
        +
answer community questions
```

So the "solo AI engineer celebrity" may become a new category.

Potential future examples:

* people building open-source agents
* people training small foundation models publicly
* people running personal AI labs
* researchers streaming experiments

The closest historical analogy is:

* Linus Torvalds → created Linux, huge developer influence, but not mainstream celebrity
* Yann LeCun → world-class researcher + public communication
* Lex Fridman → technical audience at massive scale, though not primarily a builder

The missing archetype is:

> "A person who can train a frontier model, write the kernels, build the product, and communicate daily to 10M people."

That person basically does not exist yet.

AI may create this role because the leverage of one engineer is increasing dramatically. A single person with enough technical depth and distribution can now look more like a small research company.
