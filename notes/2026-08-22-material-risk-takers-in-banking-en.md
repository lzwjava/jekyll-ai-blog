Question: What are "Material Risk Takers" (MRTs) in banking?

Answer:

Material Risk Takers (MRTs) are employees at financial institutions whose professional activities have a material impact on the institution's risk profile. The concept is a regulatory classification used to identify staff whose decisions could significantly affect a bank's financial health or stability.

**Who qualifies as an MRT?**

Banks must identify MRTs based on qualitative and quantitative criteria:

- **Qualitative criteria** — Roles that inherently carry significant risk authority, such as senior management, heads of control functions (risk, compliance, audit), and business line heads.
- **Quantitative criteria** — Employees whose total remuneration exceeds certain thresholds (e.g., €500,000/year in the EU) or falls within the top-paid bracket of the institution.

**Why does it matter?**

MRT status triggers special regulatory requirements, particularly around **remuneration (pay)**:

- A portion of variable pay must be **deferred** over several years (typically 3–5 years)
- Part of pay must be in **instruments** (shares, bonds) rather than cash
- Pay can be subject to **malus** (reduction before payout) or **clawback** (recovery after payout) if risks materialize
- Guaranteed bonuses are largely prohibited

**Regulatory Framework**

| Region | Key Rule |
|---|---|
| EU | CRD IV/V — EBA Guidelines on MRT identification |
| UK | PRA/FCA Remuneration Rules (post-Brexit adaptation) |
| US | Comparable rules under Dodd-Frank for large banks |

**Purpose**

The MRT framework was born out of the 2008 financial crisis, where excessive short-term risk-taking incentivized by uncapped bonuses was seen as a key contributor to systemic failure. By tying compensation structures to long-term outcomes, regulators aim to **align incentives** with prudent risk management.
