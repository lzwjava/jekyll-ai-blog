Question: **Is Pune poorer than Guangzhou, especially in GDP per person?**

Answer: **Yes — substantially, if you compare nominal economic output/income per person.** But Pune is not a poor Indian city; it is one of India's richer and more industrialized urban economies.

| Metric                   |               Guangzhou |                              Pune |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------: | --------------------------------: |
| Latest comparable period |                    2025 |                           2023–24 |
| GDP / income per person  | **¥168,279 ≈ US$23.6k** |            **₹375,000 ≈ US$4.3k** |
| Relative level           |          **~5.4× Pune** |                 ~19% of Guangzhou |
| Economic character       |  Major Chinese megacity | Major Indian IT/manufacturing hub |

Guangzhou's official 2025 statistics report GDP of **¥3.204 trillion**, population of **19.10 million**, and GDP per capita of **¥168,279 / US$23,559**. ([Guangzhou Statistical Bureau][1])

For Pune, Maharashtra's official district estimates put **2023–24 per-capita district income at ₹374,257**. Pune is actually **#3 in Maharashtra**, behind Mumbai and Thane, so it is already relatively affluent by Indian standards. ([CDN BBSR][2])

### But the interesting part is the PPP adjustment

The **5× nominal difference overstates the difference in everyday living standards** because:

* Housing, food, services and labor are generally much cheaper in Pune.
* A ₹100 purchase in Pune does not correspond to a ¥100-equivalent purchase in Guangzhou.
* Pune has a very strong **IT/software/engineering ecosystem**, plus automotive and manufacturing.
* Guangzhou has a much larger industrial, logistics, financial and trade economy.

So I would characterize them roughly as:

**Guangzhou**

> Rich Chinese megacity → closer to a developed/high-middle-income East Asian metropolitan economy.

**Pune**

> Rich Indian metropolitan area → economically much stronger than the Indian average, but with substantially lower productivity and income per capita.

For reference, India's Maharashtra government puts **Maharashtra's 2023–24 per-capita income at ₹278,681**, while Pune is ₹374,257 — about **34% above the state average**. ([CDN BBSR][2])

And Guangzhou's 2025 per-capita GDP is about **1.69× China's national figure of ¥99,665**. ([Guangzhou Statistical Bureau][1])

So if you're thinking about **Pune as an alternative city for an engineer/business**, I wouldn't interpret the numbers as "Pune is poor." A better mental model is:

**Pune = one of India's strongest engineering cities, but operating inside a much poorer country.**
**Guangzhou = one of China's strongest engineering/business cities, inside a much richer economy.**

That difference becomes very visible in **infrastructure, public transportation, housing quality, consumer purchasing power, industrial supply chains, and salaries**.
