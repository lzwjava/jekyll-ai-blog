**Question:** What is the KiCad project hosted at the GitLab repository `gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad`?

**Answer:**

**KiCad** is a free, open-source Electronic Design Automation (EDA) suite primarily used for designing electronic schematics and printed circuit boards (PCBs).

**Origins & License**

KiCad was originally developed by Jean-Pierre Charras in 1992 and has since grown into one of the most powerful open-source EDA tools available today. It is licensed under GNU GPL v3 and runs on Windows, Linux, and macOS.

**What It Does**

KiCad provides integrated tools for schematic capture, PCB layout, footprint and symbol library management, 3D visualization, electrical and design rule checking, and generation of manufacturing outputs such as Gerber files, drill files, BOMs, and pick-and-place data. Unlike fragmented toolchains that require exporting netlists between separate programs, KiCad keeps schematic and board data synchronized inside a single project.

**Key Features**

- It supports PCBs with up to 32 copper layers and is capable of creating designs of all complexities, including schematic capture, PCB layout, SPICE simulation, bill of materials generation, and 3D PCB models.
- It exports standard manufacturing formats including Gerber (RS-274X), Excellon drill files, IPC-2581, ODB++, STEP and VRML 3D models, and includes extensive official libraries with thousands of symbols, footprints, and 3D models.

**Who It's For**

The goal of the KiCad project is to provide the best possible cross-platform electronics design application for professional electronics designers, while remaining approachable by new and inexperienced users.

**The GitLab Repository**

The repository is written primarily in C++ and has over 57,000 commits, 11 branches, and 159 tags — reflecting decades of active development.
