Question: Is IELTS 6.5 sufficient for the QMAS language proficiency requirement?

---

Answer:

**IELTS 6.5 itself is likely fine for English — but that's probably not the real problem.**

Looking at your rejection letter closely, **two separate language criteria** were flagged as deficient:

| Criterion | Requirement | Your Status |
|---|---|---|
| (4) | Proficient in **two languages**, written & spoken | ❌ Missing docs |
| (5) | Good proficiency in **Chinese** (written & spoken) | ❌ Missing docs |

---

### What's actually required

The QMAS General Points Test includes as one of its 12 assessment criteria: whether the applicant is proficient in **two languages**, both written and spoken.

Under the updated QMAS rules, applicants need to demonstrate proficiency in **two languages**. Notably, there is also a separate criterion specifically for Chinese language proficiency.

An IELTS score of 6.0 or above is generally considered sufficient to satisfy the English proficiency component of the scheme.

So your **IELTS 6.5 should be acceptable for English**. The problem is almost certainly one of these:

1. **You only submitted IELTS (English only)** — but the scheme requires proof of a **second language** too (typically Chinese: Mandarin/Cantonese via HSK or PSC test)
2. **You didn't submit a Chinese language certificate** at all — the letter flags criterion (5) (Chinese proficiency) as separately deficient

---

### What you need to fix this

- ✅ **English**: IELTS 6.5 — already covered
- ❌ **Chinese**: Submit a **PSC (普通話水平測試)** or **HSK** certificate, OR proof that you studied/worked in Chinese medium — this is what's missing

> If Chinese is your native language, you can likely use your **Chinese education certificates** (diploma, transcripts from a Chinese-medium school/university) as evidence, rather than a formal test score.

---

### Bottom line

Don't worry about your IELTS score — it's not too low. The issue is that you need **two separate language proofs**, and the Chinese language proof is what appears to be missing from your application.
