Question: Is maturity basically about stopping the blame toward one's parents and taking responsibility for one's own life and family?

Answer: **Yes, with one important distinction.**

A mature person gradually moves from:

> “My parents caused my problems.”

to:

> “Whatever they did or didn't do, **my life is my responsibility now.**”

That doesn't mean pretending the parents did nothing wrong. Childhood circumstances can genuinely affect someone. Understanding that history can be useful. But **understanding is different from assigning responsibility for your present life**.

A useful model is:

```text
Past
  ↓
Parents / environment / luck
  ↓
What happened to me
  ↓
My responsibility now
  ↓
What do I do with it?
```

You cannot choose the first half. You *can* increasingly choose the second half.

### The mature position

It is something like:

* **I can acknowledge that my parents hurt me.**
* **I don't need to hate them or excuse them.**
* **I don't expect them to repair my life.**
* **I take responsibility for my own decisions.**
* **I take care of my spouse and children.**
* **I build the life I want rather than endlessly explaining why I don't have it.**

And there's an important second-order point:

**Taking responsibility doesn't mean carrying everything alone.**

A mature person can say:

> “I need help.”

> “I made a mistake.”

> “I can't solve this by myself.”

> “My parents were wrong about this.”

> “I'm going to set a boundary.”

Those are all compatible with responsibility.

The unhealthy extremes are:

```text
Blame everything on parents
        ↓
"I am a victim, therefore I cannot act."

versus

Blame nobody, including myself
        ↓
"I must suffer silently and handle everything alone."
```

The mature middle is:

```text
Understand the past
      +
Accept reality
      +
Take responsibility
      +
Act
      +
Protect the next generation
```

Perhaps the strongest version is:

> **You don't choose the family you were born into. But eventually, you become responsible for the family and life you create.**

And once you have children, this becomes especially powerful: **your job isn't to give them a perfect childhood; it's to avoid unnecessarily passing your unresolved problems down to them.**

That is a pretty deep form of maturity.
