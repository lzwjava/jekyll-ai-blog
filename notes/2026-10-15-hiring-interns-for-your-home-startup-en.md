**Question:** For the same setup (house in Zengcheng, AI/software consultancy), can you hire people specifically as interns?

**Answer:**

Yes, you can — and for a small home-based startup, **the intern route is genuinely the most practical starting point.** Here's everything that matters:

---

### What Makes Interns Different (and Simpler)

In the case of an internship, no employment relationship is established, and the Labor Contract Law does not apply — so it is not necessary to sign a formal labor contract with the student. This means:

- ❌ No social insurance contributions (五险一金) required
- ❌ No formal labor contract
- ❌ No mandatory severance protections
- ✅ Far less administrative overhead to get started

---

### The Catch: They Must Still Be Students

These exceptions cease to apply after the intern graduates. Once they graduate, the relationship is treated as an employment relationship under the Labor Contract Law. So:

- If the person is **still enrolled** at a Guangdong university → intern rules apply ✅
- If they have **already graduated** (even by one day) → they become employees with full rights immediately ⚠️

This is the single most important line to watch.

---

### What You Must Do (Even for Interns)

China's Labor Contract Law is silent on how student interns should be treated, but this does not mean a company can retain student interns without written documentation.

In Guangdong/Guangzhou specifically:

**1. Tripartite Agreement (三方实习协议)**
You need a signed agreement between **you (the company) + the student + their school/university**. It should cover:
- Duration and schedule
- Compensation
- Work hours and shifts
- Labor protection provisions
- Responsibilities of each party

Without the school's involvement, you're exposed if something goes wrong (injury, dispute, etc.).

**2. Pay Them**
Virtually none of China's student intern rules allow companies to use student interns as free labor, especially when labor protections are ignored. In Guangdong, Guangzhou has a formal intern base program that requires paying interns at least at the local minimum wage rate. Even outside that program, paying at or near minimum wage (¥2,680/month for full-time, or ¥25.4/hour part-time) is both legally safer and the right thing to do.

**3. Accident Insurance (工伤/意外险)**
Since interns aren't covered by the social insurance system, you should purchase commercial accident insurance for them. This is low cost (a few hundred RMB per year per person) and protects both sides.

---

### Living in Your Home

No legal issue here for interns either. Providing a room is simply a private arrangement — think of it as part of the compensation package (accommodation + stipend). Just make sure:
- Basic fire safety (smoke alarm, clear exits)
- The room is livable (ventilation, bedding)
- It's mutually agreed and ideally noted in the internship agreement

---

### Practical Setup for 2–3 Interns

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Agreement | Tripartite (you + student + their school) |
| Pay | ~¥2,000–2,680+/month recommended |
| Social insurance | Not required |
| Labor contract | Not required (while still enrolled) |
| Accident insurance | Buy commercial policy — cheap & important |
| Company registration | Still needed — register your consultancy first |
| Business address | Your home address is valid for AI/software consultancy in Guangzhou |

---

### One Risk to Watch

If the employer does not want to hire the former intern on a full-time basis after graduation, they have the option of offering a part-time contract of no more than four hours per day. So when your interns approach graduation, have a plan: either convert them to employees with a proper contract, offer part-time, or end the internship before they graduate — don't let it drift past graduation date without formalizing it.

---

**Bottom line:** The intern model works well for your situation. Register your company first, reach out to the career offices of Guangdong universities (南方科技大学, 华南理工, 广东工业大学 are close to Zengcheng area), and set up a simple tripartite internship agreement. It's legal, lighter on paperwork, and a great way to test-fit people before committing to formal employment.
