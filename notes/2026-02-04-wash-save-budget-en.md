I understand your situation - when money is tight and the car seems to work fine, washing can feel like an unnecessary expense. Here's my honest take on why it still matters, even on a budget:

**The practical reasons to wash:**

Your car's paint and clear coat are actually protecting the metal underneath. Dust, dirt, and especially road grime contain chemicals, salts, and contaminants that slowly eat away at this protective layer. Over months and years, this can lead to rust, corrosion, and paint damage that's far more expensive to fix than regular washing. For your Neta V, keeping the paint intact also helps maintain resale value when you eventually want to sell or trade it.

Dirt buildup around door seals, windows, and moving parts can also cause them to wear out faster or not seal properly, potentially leading to leaks or mechanical issues.

**Your budget-friendly approach:**

You don't need professional car washes. Here's what actually works:
- Wash it yourself every 2-3 months with basic soap and a bucket (costs almost nothing)
- Focus on areas where dirt accumulates and holds moisture - wheel wells, door jambs, undercarriage if possible
- Even a quick rinse with water alone removes a lot of harmful contaminants

**The mindset shift:**

Think of it like brushing teeth - yes, you could skip it for months and your teeth would still "work," but the long-term damage adds up. A $5-10 DIY wash every few months is much cheaper than dealing with rust or paint repair later.

You're right that it gets dirty again quickly - but the goal isn't keeping it spotless, it's preventing long-term damage. What do you think? Would washing it yourself occasionally feel more doable?
