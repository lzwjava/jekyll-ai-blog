Question: In the AI era, is spending many hours traveling to meet people often a waste compared with learning LLMs and building?

Answer:

Yes, for many knowledge workers and builders, the opportunity cost has changed dramatically.

Before AI, a 3-hour coffee meeting or 10-hour trip might be valuable because:

* information was scarce;
* expertise was concentrated in certain people;
* collaboration required physical presence;
* building software required large teams.

Now the equation is different.

A developer can spend 3 hours and:

* read a new paper;
* fine-tune a model;
* build an agent prototype;
* deploy an AI product;
* generate content;
* talk with multiple AI agents;
* learn from thousands of experts through models.

For someone like you who is already building AI systems, the marginal value of a random social meeting is often low.

A simple calculation:

```
3 hours meeting:
- travel: 1.5h
- waiting: 0.5h
- talking: 1h

Output:
- maybe one connection
- maybe one idea
- uncertain ROI


3 hours building:
- 500 lines of code
- one open-source contribution
- one AI demo
- one blog post
- one customer prototype

Output:
- compounding asset
```

The key difference is **compounding**.

A conversation disappears.
A shipped project remains.

A blog post, GitHub repo, AI demo, or trained model can:

* attract users;
* attract customers;
* prove capability;
* create opportunities.

This is similar to what many AI-era builders do: reduce passive consumption and maximize "artifact creation."

However, physical meetings are not useless. The filter should change.

High-value meetings:

* meeting a potential customer;
* meeting a strong technical collaborator;
* meeting someone who can open a new market;
* deep relationship with close friends/family.

Low-value meetings:

* "let's hang out";
* networking without purpose;
* repeating the same conversations;
* social obligations.

A good rule:

```
If the meeting can change your trajectory → go.
If it only fills your calendar → skip.
```

In the AI era, the advantage goes to people who protect large blocks of uninterrupted creation time.

For a person building an AI company, a 6-hour deep work block can be worth much more than a day of random social activity.

The scarce resource is no longer information.
It is **attention + execution speed**.
