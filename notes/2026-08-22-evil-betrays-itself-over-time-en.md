Question: Does evil tend to expose itself over time, even without intervention?

Answer: Yes, this is a well-observed pattern in human history and society. A few reasons why:

**Truth has a way of surfacing**
Corruption, abuse, and wrongdoing tend to leave traces — in people's memories, records, patterns of behavior. The more people are harmed, the more witnesses exist. Secrets become harder to keep at scale.

**Systems eventually break under dishonesty**
A union that's corrupt, a company covering up misconduct, a leader abusing power — these create internal contradictions. The gap between what's claimed and what's real widens until it collapses. People notice inconsistencies over time.

**Victims and witnesses speak**
Even when silenced initially, people eventually find moments, platforms, or safety to tell the truth. The #MeToo movement, whistleblowers, investigative journalism — these show that suppressed truth often finds a release.

**Accountability is delayed, not always denied**
History repeatedly shows figures who seemed untouchable — later exposed, tried, or at minimum condemned by historical record. Not always in their lifetime, but the record often corrects itself.

**The limits of this pattern**
It's important to be honest: *not all evil gets exposed*. Some injustices remain buried. Some perpetrators die without consequence. This is why active effort — journalism, legal systems, courageous individuals — still matters enormously. Passive waiting isn't enough.

So yes — truth tends to reflect itself, but it usually needs *people willing to carry it forward* to actually land.
