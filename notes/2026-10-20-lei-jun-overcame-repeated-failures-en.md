**Question:** How did Xiaomi founder Lei Jun overcome his difficulties and hard times?

**Answer:**

Lei Jun's journey is marked by repeated failures before his eventual success. Here's a chronological look at the key struggles and how he pushed through each one:

---

### 🎓 1. Humble Beginnings & First Failure (Late 1980s)

Lei Jun was born in 1969 in Xiantao, Hubei province, China, and studied computer science at Wuhan University, where he was influenced by the works of Bill Gates and Steve Jobs. Inspired by reading about Steve Jobs, the 21-year-old Lei set up his very first company — but it soon failed because he lacked a long-term plan. Rather than giving up, he learned from the failure and went on to join Kingsoft as a software engineer in 1992.

---

### 💼 2. The Kingsoft Grind — 16 Years of Struggle (1992–2007)

Kingsoft lasted for eight years and went through five rounds of funding before finally going IPO. A source told Caixin he was burnt out on running the company, which had made four failed attempts at a listing before finally succeeding.

During this period, Kingsoft also suffered a devastating blow: before Microsoft Office broke into China's market, Kingsoft's WPS had captured 90% of the market share in China during the early 1990s — but Microsoft's entry largely crushed that dominance. Lei endured this collapse and kept going.

---

### 🛒 3. The Joyo.com Regret (Early 2000s)

Lei co-founded Joyo.com, a promising e-commerce platform. The failure of Joyo was attributed to "bad luck" — it was founded after the bursting of the internet bubble and before the rise of the e-commerce trend; no matter how smart or diligent one can be, one may lose at the starting line. He eventually sold Joyo to Amazon in 2004, and it never reached its full potential.

---

### 🚀 4. Founding Xiaomi: Convincing Suppliers from Scratch (2010)

When Lei left Kingsoft in 2007 to reflect and eventually start Xiaomi in 2010, the company faced enormous early challenges. Before launching the company's first phone, Lei had to find a chip supplier to power his new devices — a near-impossible task for a brand-new, unproven startup. He reportedly made a passionate two-hour presentation that convinced Qualcomm to sell chips to Xiaomi.

"I had my dream not realized and it was calling me every night," said Lei. At the age of 38, he left Kingsoft and started his own company.

---

### 📉 5. The 2015–2016 Sales Collapse — "The Most Challenging Period"

This was arguably Xiaomi's darkest hour. At the beginning of 2015, Lei set an ambitious target of 100 million smartphone units for the year. But it ultimately shipped 71 million units. Smartphone sales further declined 25% year-on-year to 53 million units in 2016.

Xiaomi fell from the world's most valuable unicorn. Sales plunged in 2016, pushing the company from first to fifth place among China's smartphone makers.

Xiaomi's business strategy contributed to its struggle in 2015 and 2016. With online sales as its primary means, Xiaomi potentially neglected key consumers in lower-tier cities and rural areas, where individuals relied more on local retailers.

**How he turned it around:** The company that was built upon a platform that rejected brick-and-mortar retail decided to bring its sales offline. By opening physical Mi Home stores and expanding aggressively into international markets like India, Xiaomi's quarterly smartphone shipments hit a record high in the second quarter of 2017 — a 70% increase from the same period the prior year. Lei declared: *"No other smartphone company globally has been able to resume growth after a decline in sales."*

---

### 🚗 6. The EV Tragedy (2025) — "The Hardest Time"

Lei Jun said he experienced "the hardest time" since founding Xiaomi in 2010, following a fatal crash involving the autonomous-driving feature of a Xiaomi SU7 that killed three people in late March 2025. "I was feeling down, cancelled some meetings and business trips, and stopped interacting on social media for a while," Lei said. "I have been very busy in the past few years, but this time I was able to calm down and think carefully."

---

### 🔑 Key Lessons from Lei Jun's Resilience

| Challenge | How He Overcame It |
|---|---|
| First company failure | Joined an established firm, learned from scratch |
| Kingsoft burnout & IPO failures | Persisted through 5 funding rounds, finally succeeded |
| Joyo sold short | Accepted "bad luck," moved on |
| Xiaomi supply chain | Persuaded Qualcomm through sheer determination |
| 2015–16 sales collapse | Pivoted to offline retail, expanded globally |
| EV tragedy (2025) | Self-reflection, stepped back, then re-engaged |

His core philosophy: *keep learning, reflect after every failure, and never stop moving forward.*
