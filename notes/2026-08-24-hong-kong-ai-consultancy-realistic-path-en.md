Question: Is setting up a Hong Kong business and going there to do software/AI consultancy a more realistic path for me?

Answer: **Yes — I think this is substantially more realistic for you than betting everything on a US master’s degree or trying to immediately enter frontier AI research.**

The key is that you're not starting from zero. You already have:

* ~10+ years software engineering experience
* backend/full-stack + Python/ML
* LLM training and inference experience
* real GPU/AI infrastructure experience
* prior freelance/consulting experience
* ability to build and ship products independently

So the Hong Kong route can be:

```text
China technical base
       │
       ▼
Hong Kong company
       │
       ├── AI / LLM consulting
       ├── AI agent implementation
       ├── GPU / inference infrastructure
       ├── private deployment / RAG
       └── software engineering outsourcing
       │
       ▼
HK clients
       │
       ├── SMEs
       ├── traditional enterprises
       ├── startups
       └── mainland/HK companies
```

### Why I think it fits you

**1. Lower capital requirement**

You don't need to spend hundreds of thousands of CNY on an overseas degree before discovering whether the market wants your services.

You can validate demand first.

For example:

```text
Month 1
  HK company + website + portfolio

Month 1–2
  contact 50–100 companies

Month 2
  first HK$20k–50k project

Month 3–6
  2–3 recurring clients

Then decide:
  scale consultancy
  → productize
  → hire
  → pursue bigger AI infra contracts
```

That's a much more favorable risk/reward curve.

**2. Hong Kong is unusually suitable for your China ↔ international positioning**

You can potentially operate as:

> technically strong mainland engineer + Hong Kong commercial entity + international-facing AI consultancy.

That is a much more interesting position than competing as another generic software outsourcing shop in mainland China.

**3. Your AI infra experience gives you a better wedge than generic web development**

I wouldn't market yourself as:

> "We build websites and mobile apps."

That's commoditized.

I'd position around things like:

```text
Private LLM deployment
AI agent systems
Enterprise RAG
LLM gateway / model routing
GPU inference infrastructure
Fine-tuning
AI application security
On-prem AI
AI automation
```

For example:

> "We help Hong Kong companies deploy private AI systems using their own data and infrastructure."

That's much easier to charge serious money for.

### The important part: don't move first

I would **not** do:

```text
open HK company
↓
move to HK
↓
rent office
↓
hope clients appear
```

I'd do:

```text
Guangzhou
   ↓
build HK company
   ↓
sell remotely
   ↓
visit HK for meetings
   ↓
close first client
   ↓
repeat
   ↓
only then spend substantial time in HK
```

Your first objective isn't "move to Hong Kong."

It's:

> **Can I make HK$30k–100k/month from Hong Kong clients?**

If yes, then physical relocation becomes a business optimization problem rather than a career gamble.

My ranking for **your current situation** would be:

**1. AI/software consultancy + HK market**
**2. AI infra/LLM engineering contracts/jobs**
**3. GPU repair as a parallel cash-flow business**
**4. US master's as an option, not the default plan**
**5. Pure frontier-model research**

The particularly interesting strategy is actually **combining #1 + #2 + #3** rather than choosing one identity.

```text
             Zhiwei
```

You have enough technical depth that **consulting can finance your research**, instead of waiting for research to finance your life.

That's the part I think is especially attractive for you.

One caveat: **company formation, business registration, tax, and immigration/right-to-work are separate questions**. Having a Hong Kong company does not automatically mean you can freely live/work there. I'd verify the current HK visa/business rules before making relocation decisions.
