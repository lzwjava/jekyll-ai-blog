Question: What is the difference between a market economy (市场经济) and a planned economy (计划经济)?

Answer:

These are two fundamentally opposing systems for organizing a nation's economic activity.

**Market Economy (市场经济)**
Prices and production decisions are driven by supply and demand, with private individuals and firms making most economic choices. The government plays a limited role, mainly enforcing contracts and property rights. Resources flow toward wherever they earn the highest return. Examples: the United States, Germany, Japan.

**Planned Economy (计划经济)**
Also called a command economy — a central authority (typically the government) decides what to produce, how much to produce, and at what price. Private ownership is limited or absent. Resources are allocated by directive, not by price signals. Classic examples: the Soviet Union, Maoist China, North Korea.

**Side-by-Side Comparison**

| Dimension | Market Economy | Planned Economy |
|---|---|---|
| Price mechanism | Set by supply & demand | Set by the state |
| Resource allocation | Decentralized (millions of actors) | Centralized (planners) |
| Ownership | Primarily private | Primarily state/collective |
| Innovation incentive | Strong (profit motive) | Weak |
| Efficiency | Generally high (competitive pressure) | Generally low (no price signals) |
| Equality | Can produce wide inequality | More uniform distribution, but often at low levels |
| Risk of failure | Market bubbles, monopolies | Misallocation, shortages, surpluses |

**Key Insight**

The core tension is the **information problem** (articulated by economist F.A. Hayek): no central planner can gather and process the vast, dispersed knowledge that millions of individual market participants use every day. Prices in a market economy transmit this information efficiently; planners cannot replicate it.

Most modern economies are **mixed economies** — primarily market-based but with government intervention in healthcare, education, infrastructure, and regulation. China today is a notable case: officially socialist but heavily market-driven in practice.
