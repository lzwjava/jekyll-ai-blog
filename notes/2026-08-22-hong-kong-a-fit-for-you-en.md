Question: Would Hong Kong fit me because I value Bitcoin, freedom, truth, English ability (IELTS 6.5), and want to start an AI/software consultancy company there?

Answer:

I think **Hong Kong could fit many parts of your personality and career direction**, but not simply because "Bitcoin + freedom = Hong Kong". The match is more practical:

Your profile:

* 11 years software engineering
* AI/LLM engineering direction
* English ability (IELTS 6.5)
* international mindset
* dislike of rigid systems
* want to sell expertise globally
* already thinking about US/Canada/HK markets

Hong Kong has several advantages for this:

### 1. Hong Kong is a good "bridge" location

Hong Kong sits between:

```
China engineering talent
        |
        |
    Hong Kong
        |
        |
Global clients (US, Singapore, Europe)
```

For an AI consultancy:

* build AI agents
* LLM fine-tuning
* enterprise automation
* software outsourcing
* technical consulting

Hong Kong gives you a more international business image than a mainland-only company.

A HK Ltd company can be incorporated relatively easily, including by non-Hong Kong residents. ([Companies Registry][1]) The government also positions Hong Kong as a startup and international business hub connecting Mainland China and global markets. ([HK Engage][2])

### 2. Your "freedom/truth" mindset may feel more comfortable

You may like some aspects:

* English is widely used in business
* common-law legal system
* international finance culture
* many entrepreneurs, investors, expats
* more exposure to global ideas

You may find more people who discuss:

* startups
* investing
* AI
* Bitcoin
* technology trends
* global opportunities

Compared with many traditional Chinese cities, the social atmosphere is more internationally oriented.

But remember:

Hong Kong is still a Chinese city. It is not Silicon Valley or a libertarian island. It has its own political, economic, and social realities.

The better framing:

> "Hong Kong is a place where a Chinese engineer can connect with global markets."

That is probably closer to your situation.
