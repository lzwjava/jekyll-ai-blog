### In Personal Career (ACM/ICPC Example)
For a freshman at a top Chinese university (985/211) with no competitive programming background from high school, immediately targeting world-class performance in ACM/ICPC (International Collegiate Programming Contest) by graduation is often unrealistic. Competitive programming demands thousands of hours of deliberate practice, pattern recognition, algorithm mastery, and contest experience — skills rarely built from zero in just 3–4 years while handling a full university curriculum.

A more realistic path:
- Year 1: Learn basics (C++/Python, data structures, simple algorithms), solve 100–300 easy/medium problems on platforms like LeetCode, Codeforces, or Luogu.
- Year 2: Participate in regional contests, build speed and accuracy, aim for rating milestones (e.g., Codeforces Specialist → Expert).
- Year 3–4: Target provincial/national awards or consistent performance in major contests.

### Overall Principle
- **Unrealistic goals** can demotivate when repeatedly missed, waste energy on fantasy, or lead to risky overcommitment (business equivalent: burning cash on unproven ideas).
- **Realistic, sequential goals** build momentum, skills, confidence, and credibility — creating a flywheel effect. Small wins compound, often leading to outsized success that seemed "unrealistic" at the start.
- Stretch goals are valuable for motivation, but pair them with honest assessment of current reality and a clear ladder of intermediate milestones.
