Question: Why do some people stagnate after achieving success, becoming arrogant, less hands-on, and disconnected from future trends?

Answer:

Yes, that's a common pattern. When people achieve a certain level of success, several psychological and behavioral factors can contribute to stagnation:

1. **Pride and ego inflation** – Success can make people overestimate their own abilities, leading to arrogance and resistance to feedback.

2. **Reduced curiosity** – Feeling “secure” in their status, they may stop actively learning, exploring new ideas, or keeping up with emerging technologies and trends.

3. **Delegation without engagement** – They may rely heavily on subordinates, losing touch with the actual work and becoming less effective in decision-making.

4. **Overemphasis on judgment** – Instead of guiding by example or fostering growth, they become critical, bossy, or micromanaging, which can demotivate their team.

5. **Past knowledge bias** – They tend to rely on methods or knowledge that worked in the past, ignoring shifts in the market, technology, or society, which makes their approach increasingly outdated.

6. **Activity over impact** – They speak more and act less, creating the illusion of leadership while contributing little to real progress.

This combination creates a self-reinforcing cycle: their lack of hands-on involvement and openness to learning gradually erodes their adaptability, causing professional and personal stagnation despite outward success. The key to avoiding this is **maintaining humility, curiosity, and engagement** regardless of status.
