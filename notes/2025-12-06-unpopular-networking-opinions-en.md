Question: What are some unpopular opinions about professional networking?

Answer:

Here are some genuinely unpopular (but often defended by experienced people) opinions about networking that tend to trigger strong reactions:

- **Most networking events are a complete waste of time.** The signal-to-noise ratio is terrible. You’ll collect 50 business cards and maybe one useful contact after hours of small talk.
- **“Building genuine relationships” is mostly a polite fiction.** People network for mutual advantage. Pretending otherwise just adds unnecessary emotional labor. Being transparently transactional often works better and faster.
- **Your network’s value is wildly overestimated.** Weak ties rarely move the needle for big opportunities (jobs, funding, deals). Strong prior relationships or raw proof of work (GitHub, revenue, published research) beat “who you know” almost every time.
- **Cold outreach has a higher ROI than warm intros in many cases.** A well-researched, value-first cold email to the right person converts better than a lukewarm intro from someone who barely remembers you.
- **LinkedIn is performative theater, not networking.** Posting cringe thought-leadership, commenting “🔥” on everything, and humble-bragging about your morning routine fools no one who actually matters.
- **Giving to get is manipulative and obvious.** The people who keep meticulous “favor spreadsheets” are usually the first to get quietly phased out when they’re no longer useful.
- **Introverts don’t need to “get out of their comfort zone” to network effectively.** Remote, asynchronous, and written communication (email, Twitter/X, forums) can build stronger connections than forcing yourself into crowded happy hours.
- **Name-dropping and signaling which famous people you know is inversely correlated with actual influence.** Truly powerful people rarely mention who they know—it’s evident in results.
- **Most “networking advice” is cargo-cult nonsense for people who don’t have anything substantial to offer yet.** Once you build something impressive, inbound opportunities drown out any need to “network.”
- **Attending Ivy League schools or working at FAANG is often just expensive signaling disguised as networking.** The alumni network effect is overstated; individual performance still dominates.

These takes tend to upset people who’ve invested heavily (time, money, identity) in traditional networking culture, which is exactly why they remain unpopular despite being repeatedly validated in practice.
