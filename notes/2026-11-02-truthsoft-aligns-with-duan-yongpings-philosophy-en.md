## 2. Where I would disagree with your current strategy

There are three important issues.

### A. Four business lines are too broad

Your website offers:

1. Model training and inference.
2. AI transformation consulting.
3. AI applications and agents.
4. General application development.

This covers almost the entire software industry.

The problem isn't your technical ability. It's customer perception.

A customer visiting your website might wonder:

"Are these people an AI research company, an outsourcing company, an AI consultancy, or an app development agency?"

Duan's approach to business is to understand what you're good at and focus on it.

Remember his explanation of OPPO and vivo: they deliberately avoided OEM because they understood that it wasn't their competitive advantage.

My recommendation:

Make AI engineering your primary identity.

Primary business

AI Engineering & Research

Model training, inference optimization, AI infrastructure, agents and AI-native software.

Revenue engine

AI Consulting & Development

Help companies understand, integrate and deploy AI.

Long-term investment

Truth Lab

Open research, model training, GPU infrastructure, inference benchmarks and engineering experiments.

Selective business

General Software Development

Accept projects when they are profitable, strategically useful or aligned with your capabilities.

You don't need to reject general software development. Just don't let it define the company.

### B. Token-based pricing is philosophically attractive but commercially risky

I like your idea:

¥20 / 1 million tokens.

It communicates transparency and experimentation.

But there is a fundamental economic problem.

Tokens are not equivalent to engineering value.

Consider two tasks:

- Task A: Generate 100,000 tokens of boilerplate code.
- Task B: Spend three hours debugging a distributed inference system and discover a critical race condition.

Task B might create 100 times more value despite using fewer tokens.

A better pricing architecture:

| Service                             | Pricing                                        |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| AI coding and iterative development | Token-based                                    |
| Model API consumption               | Actual usage + disclosed infrastructure margin |
| Consulting                          | Fixed fee                                      |
| Model training                      | Compute + engineering fee                      |
| Production deployment               | Project fee + maintenance                      |
| Long-term collaboration             | Monthly budget or prepaid credits              |

Keep token pricing as your differentiator, but don't force every service into it.

Duan would probably ask: Does this pricing model genuinely benefit customers and create a sustainable business?

The answer should be yes for both sides.

### C. Don't confuse technical exploration with a business model

Truth Lab is one of my favorite parts of your website.

The public training logs, 124M/760M GPT-2 experiments, GPU cluster, nanoGPT modifications and inference research demonstrate actual engineering work.

That's much stronger than simply claiming to be an AI company.

But there is an important distinction:

- Research creates knowledge.
- Products create user value.
- Businesses capture some of that value.

You need all three.

Don't spend three years continuously training models without developing a product or finding customers who need your expertise.

Duan's philosophy isn't about doing interesting things indefinitely. It's about creating sustainable value.

## 3. What would Duan Yongping do if he were building TruthSoft?

I obviously cannot know what he would personally decide.

But applying his principles, I would establish five rules.

| Principle                    | TruthSoft implementation                                |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 本分 (integrity)               | Never exaggerate capabilities                           |
| 消费者导向 (customer orientation) | Optimize customer outcomes, not billable hours          |
| 做对的事 (do the right thing)    | Reject projects that don't create real value            |
| 长期主义 (long-termism)          | Accumulate technology, trust and reputation             |
| Stop Doing List              | Stop unprofitable, distracting and dishonest activities |

The last one is particularly important.

I would create an explicit Stop Doing List:

1. No projects just because someone offers money.
2. No unnecessary custom software when open source works.
3. No hiring before predictable revenue justifies it.
4. No expensive GPU purchases without a measurable purpose.
5. No exaggerated marketing claims.
6. No chasing every new AI framework.
7. No abandoning a promising direction simply because results take time.

The distinction is that you should persist in the right direction, not blindly persist in every project.

## 5. One thing I would emphasize: Don't sacrifice your family and financial stability

This is where I would apply Duan's rationality particularly strictly.

You have two young daughters, a mortgage, and a business that is being restarted.

Don't confuse long-termism with taking unlimited financial risks.

A company should be able to survive long enough to discover its business model.

I would:

- Keep fixed expenses low.
- Avoid large infrastructure investments.
- Use cloud GPUs when economically justified.
- Keep a consulting revenue stream.
- Build products gradually from customer problems.
- Avoid hiring full-time employees before revenue supports them.

Your first milestone isn't building a billion-dollar AI company. It's building a company that can sustainably support its founder, employees and customers.

That's a much better foundation.
