Question: Will the Iran Strait (Strait of Hormuz) closure make material prices like plastics go higher because oil prices rise?

Answer:

Yes — your understanding is **basically correct**, and there is a clear chain of cause and effect:

---

### 1. Strait of Hormuz → Oil supply shock → Oil price rises

The **Strait of Hormuz** is one of the most critical energy chokepoints in the world:

* About **20% of global oil supply passes through it** ([Axios][1])
* When traffic is disrupted or blocked, global oil supply tightens
* This quickly pushes oil prices higher (already happening in recent news)

---

### 2. Oil → Petrochemicals → Plastics

This is the key link many people miss:

* **~99% of plastics are made from fossil fuels (mainly oil)** ([Greenpeace][2])
* Oil is refined into **naphtha → ethylene/propylene → plastic (PE, PP, etc.)**

So:

> Oil price ↑ → petrochemical cost ↑ → plastic cost ↑

---

### 3. Supply disruption makes it worse

It’s not just price — it’s also **availability**:

* The Middle East is a major exporter of petrochemicals
* Disruption reduces supply of raw materials for plastics
* This creates **shortages + higher prices**

Real effects already observed:

* Plastic and polymer prices hit **multi-year highs** ([Hydrocarbon Processing][3])
* Some plastics surged **~40%** in price ([@IntellectiaAI][4])
* Packaging, automotive parts, toys, etc. all affected ([IDN Financials][5])

---

### 4. Transmission to everyday goods

Because plastics are everywhere:

* Food packaging
* Electronics
* Clothing (synthetic fibers)
* Logistics (containers, wraps)

→ Costs spread across the entire economy

Example:

* Food and beverage companies expect **higher packaging costs** ([FoodNavigator.com][6])

---

### 5. Summary (simple chain)

```
Strait disruption
    ↓
Oil supply ↓
    ↓
Oil price ↑
    ↓
Petrochemical cost ↑
    ↓
Plastic price ↑
    ↓
Consumer goods price ↑
```

---

### Bottom line

Yes — if the Strait of Hormuz is closed or heavily disrupted:

* Oil prices rise
* Plastic and material costs rise
* And eventually, many everyday goods become more expensive

This is a **real and already observed effect**, not just theoretical.
