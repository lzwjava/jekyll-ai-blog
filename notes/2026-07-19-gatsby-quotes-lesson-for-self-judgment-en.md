Question: Is the Gatsby quote applicable to this situation?

Answer:

Yes, the quote fits — but with a subtle twist.

The original line from The Great Gatsby:

> “Whenever you feel like criticizing anyone, just remember that all the people in this world haven’t had the advantages that you’ve had.”

is usually interpreted as a reminder of **humility and empathy**: before judging someone's choices, remember that their starting point, information, timing, family situation, resources, and luck may be completely different.

Your story actually illustrates the reverse side too: **don't judge yourself too harshly either**.

Looking at the timeline:

* 2022: bought a house in Guangzhou, then the market changed dramatically.
* The mortgage became a daily psychological weight.
* You considered selling the house, selling the EV, cutting losses.
* 2023 H2: AI excitement → career uncertainty → many failed interviews.
* Eventually found a job and held it for 16 months.
* Now: instead of staring at the debt number every day, you restarted building:

  * AI community
  * AI research
  * sharing knowledge
  * helping people
  * writing and experiments

The interesting psychological shift is not that the debt disappeared. The debt is still there. The external situation may not have improved much.

What changed is **the allocation of your attention**.

Before:

```
attention → mortgage → house price → regret → fear
```

Now:

```
attention → AI → people → learning → creating → opportunities
```

That is actually a very important transition for an entrepreneur/engineer.

Many people underestimate how much human energy is consumed by a "sunk cost wound". A falling house price is not only financial loss; it creates a repeated mental loop:

```
I made a wrong decision
    ↓
I lost money
    ↓
I need to fix it immediately
    ↓
I cannot move forward
```

Breaking that loop does not mean ignoring reality. It means returning to the things that can create future options.

The Gatsby quote also applies to housing debates in China around 2022:

A lot of people say:

> "Why did they buy at the peak?"

But people forget:

* interest rates were low;
* many believed housing was a stable asset;
* family expectations pushed ownership;
* previous decades rewarded that decision;
* everyone was making decisions with incomplete information.

Someone buying in 2015 and someone buying in 2022 were not playing the same game.

The same applies to career:

A person who got an AI job in 2023 may have had:

* stronger network,
* better English environment,
* existing ML background,
* geographic advantages,
* financial runway.

Another person with a mortgage, family responsibility, and a traditional software background faces a different optimization problem.

The more interesting sentence in your story is actually:

> "过去几个月我已经没关心这个房贷了"

That sounds like acceptance, not surrender.

There is a big difference:

**Surrender:**

```
Nothing can change.
```

**Acceptance:**

```
This is my current constraint.
Now what can I build from here?
```

For someone building AI products, training models, and running a community, the second state is much more productive.

The Gatsby quote is about judging others. But your story suggests another lesson:

> Before criticizing yourself, remember that you were making decisions with the information, environment, and pressures you had at that time.

The goal is not to prove the 2022 decision was correct. It is to make sure the 2026 version of you is not trapped by the 2022 version of you.
