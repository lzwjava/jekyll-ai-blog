Question:
Will weak job and real-estate markets in China push global banks (like HSBC, DBS, etc.) to lower salaries, since they can hire cheaper talent? Will China pay fall even more compared to Singapore/Hong Kong in recent years?

Answer:

Yes — **your reasoning is mostly correct**, and this is already happening in parts of the banking industry. But the reality is nuanced:

* Some roles → **pay pressure downward**
* Some roles (AI, senior, revenue-generating) → **pay stable or rising**
* China vs HK/SG gap → **likely stays or widens**

Let’s break it down.

---

# 1. When job supply increases, banks gain bargaining power

Your logic is basic labor economics:

* More candidates (weak economy, layoffs, real estate crash)
* Same or fewer jobs (banks restructuring, automation)
  → Employers can **offer lower pay**

And this is already happening:

* HSBC is **considering cutting ~20,000 roles globally** as part of AI-driven cost reduction. ([Reuters][1])
* HSBC also **cut about 900 employees in its China wealth unit** due to costs and underperformance. ([Reuters][2])
* Financial firms broadly are doing layoffs and restructuring to reduce expenses. ([The Business Times][3])

This means:

* fewer openings
* more applicants
* weaker salary negotiation

So **yes — downward pressure on pay is real.**

---

# 2. China banking hiring demand is weaker than before

Another structural shift:

* Chinese graduates increasingly **avoid finance and tech** due to slower hiring and shrinking workforces. ([Business Insider][4])
* Some banks are reviewing **salary structures and costs** in China units. ([Sports Radio KWSN][5])

This shows:

* banks trying to reduce cost base
* China business not as profitable
* compensation becoming more conservative

This usually leads to:

* lower base salary
* smaller bonus pool
* slower promotions

---

# 3. Why China pay is already lower than HK / Singapore

This is structural, not temporary:

Banks set pay mainly by:

* local market salary
* cost of living
* revenue generated
* talent scarcity

China typically has:

* larger talent pool
* lower cost of living (outside tier-1 cities)
* more back-office roles
* less profit per employee

So pay is lower.

And when economy weakens:
→ **gap often increases**, not decreases.

---

# 4. But NOT all roles will see pay cuts

Important exception:

High-value roles still expensive:

* AI / quant / data
* trading / revenue roles
* senior architects
* cross-border specialists

Example:
Chinese tech firms are **raising AI salaries by up to 150%** to attract talent. ([Tom's Hardware][6])

Same happens in banks:

* AI risk modeling
* automation engineering
* trading tech

These may even increase.

---

# 5. What likely happens in next 3–5 years

Most likely scenario:

### China banking tech / ops roles

* salary growth slows
* more competition
* lower new-hire offers
* bonus smaller

### HK / Singapore

* remain higher
* still compete for talent
* limited talent supply

### Gap

* China vs HK → gap widens
* China vs SG → gap widens slightly

---

# 6. But there is one counter-force

Global banks also optimize location:

They may shift work:

* HK → China (cheaper)
* SG → India / China
* London → Asia

This can:

* increase China hiring
* but still **at lower salary band**

So jobs may increase, but **pay stays low**.

---

# Final conclusion

Yes — your intuition is correct:

* Weak China economy → more job seekers
* Global banks cutting costs → lower offers
* China already cheaper than SG/HK → gap may widen
* Only high-skill niche roles resist this trend

So in recent years:

* **China pay likely stagnant or slightly lower**
* **HK/SG pay more stable**
* **Relative gap probably increases**

This is especially true for:

* IT
* back office
* operations
* support engineering

Less true for:

* AI
* quant
* trading tech
* senior architects
