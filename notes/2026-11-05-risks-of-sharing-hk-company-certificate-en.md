**Question:** Is sharing a Hong Kong company's Certificate of Incorporation a concern?

**Answer:**

Generally, **it is low-risk to share**, but with some caveats:

**What the Certificate of Incorporation contains (limited info):**

The key items on a Hong Kong Certificate of Incorporation are:

- Registration Number
- Certificate Type
- Official Company Name (Chinese, English, or both)
- Date of Establishment

The Certificate of Incorporation provides a formal acknowledgment of a company's registration but **does not disclose detailed shareholder information**.

**Why it's commonly shared:**

Companies are routinely asked to provide it when opening a corporate bank account, signing contracts in the company's name, or applying for licenses and registrations. It is a standard business document requested by counterparties to verify legitimacy.

**The concern to watch for:**

There is a potential risk of **identity theft or fraud** — in some cases, malicious actors may attempt to use the certificate to deceive third parties, such as banks, suppliers, or government agencies.

**Bottom line:**

| Scenario | Risk Level |
| --- | --- |
| Sharing with a bank, lawyer, or business partner for KYC/due diligence | ✅ Normal & expected |
| Posting publicly online (e.g., social media) | ⚠️ Unnecessary, avoid |
| Sharing with an unverified third party | ⚠️ Verify their identity first |

The document itself contains **minimal sensitive data**, but share it only with **trusted, identifiable parties** for a clear business purpose — not publicly or indiscriminately.
