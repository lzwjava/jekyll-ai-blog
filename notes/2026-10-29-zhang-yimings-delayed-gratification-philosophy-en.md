**Question:** What is the full story and meaning behind Zhang Yiming's 2016 *Caijing* interview, where "delayed gratification" appeared 10 times, and his claim that it causes half of life's problems?

**Answer:**

## Why 10 Times Matters

When Zhang Yiming gave his 2016 *Caijing* interview, the term "延迟满足感" appeared 10 times in the resulting article. He had also posted about it on Weibo 12 times. This repetition wasn't coincidental — it signals that this wasn't a passing opinion but the **central operating system of his entire life philosophy**.

Zhang Yiming went further, saying: "People whose level of delayed gratification is not on the same scale simply cannot communicate effectively with each other." That's a radical statement — he's essentially saying the *capacity to delay gratification* determines who you can even have a meaningful conversation with.

---

## What "Half of Life's Problems" Actually Means

His claim that lacking delayed gratification causes half of people's problems maps directly onto **5 real-life failure patterns** he observed:

---

### ❌ Problem 1 — Chasing Short-Term Salary Over Long-Term Growth

He advised younger students to go to Baidu rather than IBM or Microsoft, even though the foreign companies offered more prestige and slightly higher pay. A salary gap of a few thousand yuan right after graduation is truly negligible. Short-term pay differences don't matter much — but in practice, not many people can actually see past them and make a clear-headed judgment.

**The life problem:** You optimize for this year's salary and miss the company where you'd grow ten times faster.

---

### ❌ Problem 2 — Doing Only What's "Your Job"

After finishing his own work, if he could help solve a colleague's problem, he would. He had looked through most of the codebase. When new employees joined, if he had time, he'd walk them through it — and through explaining, he grew himself. In the first two years, he worked until midnight or 1am almost every day, going home to continue coding — out of genuine interest, not because the company required it.

He described a former colleague who had solid theoretical knowledge but always clocked out the moment his assigned work was done, never learning new technologies. Delayed gratification makes you willing to proactively do more, gain more training, rather than calculating "is this really my job?"

---

### ❌ Problem 3 — Staying in a Failing Situation Out of Sunk-Cost Feelings

Before founding ByteDance he had been part of 4 startup companies but didn't stay until the end in any of them. He concluded: if a startup isn't going well, the sooner it ends the better — just look forward. Delayed gratification keeps you looking ahead rather than staying satisfied with current circumstances or already-achieved results.

---

### ❌ Problem 4 — Taking the "Exciting Offer" Too Early

Less than a year into founding Toutiao (今日头条), a major player made him a very tempting investment offer. He struggled with it for a full week before turning it down. He felt it was like a stimulant — before his internal capabilities were fully developed, it would suppress his own organic growth. Accepting a giant's help would also force him to take sides, making his thinking less free.

In situations like this, the benefits are obvious but the costs are hidden. Most people overestimate the benefits and underestimate the costs. This is a classic sign of insufficient delayed gratification — insufficient long-term confidence.

---

### ❌ Problem 5 — Setting Apartment Goals Instead of Life Goals

Many of his college classmates and former colleagues were very talented people — better grades, better technical skills — yet 10 years later, many didn't reach what he had expected of them. The reason: right after graduation, they set their targets too low. Some joined bank IT departments just to get a Beijing *hukou* (residency permit) or housing subsidies. If you're truly ambitious, you wouldn't need to worry about those things.

If your very first goal after graduation is to buy a small two- or three-bedroom apartment inside Beijing's 5th Ring Road, and you spend all your energy on that, your work suffers greatly. Your behavior changes — you become unwilling to take risks.

---

## The Core Insight

The opposite of delayed gratification is immediate gratification. Most life pain comes from caring too much about immediate satisfaction — putting in a little effort and feeling pain if there's no instant reward.

Zhang Yiming said the essence of delayed gratification is overcoming human weakness — it is a long-term practice. If something feels good, delay it a little further — this raises your standards.

The higher your level of delayed gratification, the more patient you are, the higher your standards, the bigger your goals, the more composed and focused you are in your work — unconcerned with immediate gains, unbothered by winning or losing a single battle, taking short-term success lightly, and not fearing temporary failure.

## The Most Human Example — His Wife

The article ends with a telling personal story: A female classmate's computer broke down. Zhang helped fix it, came back excited, telling his roommate he liked her and would pursue her. He kept going back to fix computers, chatting with her online, asking her out. She rejected him flatly — not even offering the polite "let's just be friends." His roommates expected him to be upset. Instead, Zhang's reaction was something like: "I just told her how I felt, she had a different opinion — no big deal, we continue." He kept showing up. Two months later, his roommates saw them walking back from a trip holding hands. That girl was his first love — and later became his wife.

The point: delayed gratification isn't just about money or careers. It's a **character trait** — the ability to tolerate the gap between where you are and where you want to be, without flinching.
