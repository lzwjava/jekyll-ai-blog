The exact model is listed on JD as **锢力耐 QYL2, 2T, 国标液压千斤顶**, with thousands of reviews. ([JD.com][1])

### What I like

* **2-ton rated capacity** — plenty for lifting one corner of a normal car.
* **Very simple mechanism** — hydraulic cylinder + pump + release valve.
* **Small and cheap**, good for keeping in a workshop.
* QYL2 is a conventional bottle-jack specification. A comparable QYL2 specification is around **158 mm minimum height + 90 mm lifting stroke**. ([Hugongsh][2])
* The current Chinese national standard for vertical hydraulic bottle jacks is **GB/T 27697-2024**, effective June 1, 2025. ([Standardization Administration China][3])

### But I would NOT use it like this

```text
       CAR
  ─────────────
       ↑
      [QYL2]
       │
      floor
```

and then **crawl underneath the car while it is supported only by the jack**.

A hydraulic jack is for **lifting**, not for being the permanent safety support.

Use:

```text
       CAR
  ─────────────
       ↑
   [JACK STAND]
       │
      floor

       ↑
     QYL2
```

Once raised, put proper **jack stands/supports** under the vehicle.

### For your GPU/PC repair work

If you're buying it because you're building a little **hardware/GPU repair workshop**, I'd actually rate it:

**QYL2: 7/10**

It's useful for:

* lifting a car
* moving heavy equipment
* experimenting with mechanical structures
* pressing/lifting fixtures if you build something around it
* general garage work

But **don't buy QYL2 specifically for GPU repair**. A 2T bottle jack has *far* more capacity than you need for electronics. For BGA/GPU work, a small arbor press or controlled mechanical press is much more appropriate.

If you're choosing between **QYL2 2T vs QYL3 3T vs QYL5 5T**, I'd pick **QYL3** if the price difference is small. The QYL family is available in 2T, 3T, 5T, 8T, etc. ([JD.com][4])

**Bottom line:** QYL2 is a perfectly sensible **cheap garage jack**, but don't interpret "2T" as "I can safely put 2 tons on it in every situation." Stability, jack placement, ground condition, and safety supports matter just as much.
