Question: Is it easier for Chinese students to go to the US for master's/PhD/undergraduate programs or migration in 2026 compared with COVID years or earlier?

Answer:

For Chinese applicants, **2026 is generally easier than the COVID period (2020–2022)**, but **not necessarily easier than the golden period before 2018–2019**. The situation is more mixed.

A rough timeline:

| Period            | Difficulty for Chinese going to US | Reason                                                                         |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Before 2018       | Relatively easier                  | Strong China-US academic exchange, more visas, more funding                    |
| 2018–2019         | Starting to become harder          | US-China tech competition began                                                |
| 2020–2022 (COVID) | Much harder                        | Consulates closed, travel restrictions, visa delays, geopolitical restrictions |
| 2023–2025         | Recovery period                    | Visa processing recovered, universities welcomed international students again  |
| 2026              | Better than COVID, but selective   | Normal process restored, but competition + geopolitics remain                  |

## 1. Master's programs: easier than COVID, but competitive

For master's programs (CS, AI, engineering):

**2026 advantages:**

* US universities need international tuition revenue.
* Many programs expanded AI/data science offerings.
* Visa processing is much more normal than COVID.
* Chinese applicants can apply normally.

But compared with 2015:

* More Chinese students are applying.
* More applicants have CS/AI backgrounds.
* Top schools are extremely competitive.

For example:

* CMU AI programs
* Stanford MSCS
* MIT EECS
* Berkeley

are still extremely difficult.

But mid-tier universities are much more accessible.

A strong engineer with:

* 5–10 years experience
* AI projects
* publications/open source
* good English

can have a stronger profile than a fresh undergraduate applicant.

---

## 2. PhD programs: still attractive

PhD is actually one of the better routes.

Why?

US universities value:

* research ability
* publications
* engineering experience
* open-source contributions

A Chinese engineer who has:

* trained LLMs
* contributed to PyTorch/vLLM/SGLang
* published papers
* built datasets

can compete globally.

Funding is the key:

PhD students usually receive:

* tuition waiver
* stipend

So the financial barrier is much lower than master's.

---

## 3. Undergraduate: harder than before

For Chinese undergraduate applicants:

Competition increased significantly.

Reasons:

* More wealthy Chinese families send children overseas.
* More students attend international schools.
* More applicants have:

  * SAT/ACT
  * Olympiad awards
  * research experience
  * startup experience

Top US undergraduate admission is extremely difficult.

A normal Chinese high school student faces much stronger competition than 10 years ago.

---

## 4. Immigration/general migration

This is the most complicated.

For Chinese nationals:

### Easier routes

* Study → OPT → H1B → green card
* EB-1/EB-2 NIW (for qualified professionals)
* Startup/business routes

### Harder parts

* US-China geopolitical tension
* Some sensitive technology fields have restrictions
* Green card waiting times for Chinese-born applicants remain long

However, compared with COVID:

* travel is normal
* interviews are available
* immigration systems operate normally

So 2026 is much easier operationally.

---

## 5. AI changes the calculation

For someone in AI, 2026 is actually a special moment.

Before:

> "Need a US degree to prove yourself."

Now:

> "Can you build valuable AI systems?"

A strong AI engineer can create evidence:

```
GitHub
  |
  +-- LLM training experiments
  +-- open-source contributions
  +-- papers/blogs
  +-- deployed AI products
  |
University / employer interest
```

The signal from real AI work is much stronger than before.

For example, someone who trained GPT models, contributed to inference frameworks, or built AI products may have a more interesting profile than a normal master's applicant.
