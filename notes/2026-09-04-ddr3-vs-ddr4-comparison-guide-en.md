Question: What do DDR3 and DDR4 mean?

Answer:

**DDR** stands for **Double Data Rate**, referring to a type of SDRAM (Synchronous Dynamic Random-Access Memory) used as the main RAM in computers. The numbers (3 and 4) indicate the generation.

**How "Double Data Rate" works:** Unlike older memory, DDR transfers data on both the rising and falling edges of each clock cycle — effectively doubling throughput without increasing clock speed.

**DDR3 vs DDR4 at a glance:**

| Feature | DDR3 | DDR4 |
|---|---|---|
| Released | 2007 | 2014 |
| Voltage | 1.5V (1.35V low-power) | 1.2V (more efficient) |
| Speed | 800–2133 MT/s | 1600–5100+ MT/s |
| Max module size | 16 GB | 64 GB+ |
| Bandwidth | Lower | ~50% higher than DDR3 |
| Compatibility | Not interchangeable | Not interchangeable |

**Key takeaway:** DDR4 is faster, more power-efficient, and supports larger capacities than DDR3. They use different physical slots and **cannot be swapped** — your motherboard supports only one type.

> DDR5 is now the latest generation, found in newer platforms like Intel 12th gen+ and AMD Ryzen 7000+.
