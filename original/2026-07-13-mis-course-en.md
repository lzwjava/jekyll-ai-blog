## Course 1: Myopia Reversal

I have spent over three years experimenting on myself to reverse myopia. Starting in March 2022, I've documented my prescription history, tested different reduction levels, and developed practical frameworks — not just theory.

### What I Can Teach You

- **The "just barely clear" principle** — the single most important concept for reversing myopia. How to pick the right prescription reduction so your eyes work correctly.
- **Why full-prescription glasses make myopia worse** — most people wear glasses that are too strong for near work, causing their eyeballs to elongate further over time.
- **The multi-pair approach** — keep a strong pair for driving and essential tasks, and a weaker pair (~150–200 degrees below full prescription) for phones, computers, and reading.
- **Progressive and bifocal lenses** — what I tried, why it didn't work for me, and how to decide if they're right for you.
- **How to track your progress** — simple methods to measure prescription changes at home, with realistic expectations (~0.25D reduction every three months).
- **Understanding your eye mechanics** — how external eye muscles, lens flexibility, and axial length actually work in daily life.
- **My 3-year experiment data** — real prescription measurements from 2022 to 2025, documented with timestamps.
- **The science** — Todd Becker's research, Yin Wang's methods, and why near-work is the primary environmental driver of myopia.
- **Common mistakes** — why "just take off your glasses" doesn't work, the dangers of over-correction, and how to avoid the same pitfalls I hit.
- **Practical tips** — ordering cheap glasses online (~60 RMB per pair), testing new prescriptions, and integrating the approach into daily life.

### Sample Topics

- Why wearing full prescription for phone/computer use is like wearing high heels to run a marathon
- The 150-degree reduction rule and how to find your sweet spot
- How to tell if you're making progress without an optometrist visit
- Astigmatism and myopia — why both changed in my experiment
- Driving with under-corrected glasses — the real risks and how I hit a road divider
- Hyperopia (farsightedness) as a sanity check — why the optical industry already accepts scenario-based prescriptions
